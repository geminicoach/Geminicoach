## B4 — Reading Your Bloodwork  (Advanced, 10 chapters)
Angle: read your own common lab panels, normal vs optimal, what training and supplements shift. Strong boundary: interpretation and diagnosis belong to a clinician.
DESIGN NOTE (LOCKED 2026-08-09, applies to every marker in B4): teach ranges by AGE BAND and LIFE STAGE, not one broad population interval. For each marker present three layers: (1) the lab reference range and that it is a population 95% interval, not a health target; (2) the age/sex/life-stage-specific EXPECTED position, calling out where a healthy person of that age should typically sit (the mid-range point); (3) the context that moves interpretation (training status, draw timing, concurrent markers, meds/supplements like biotin). Core literacy lesson, made concrete in Ch1 and repeated: "in range" is NOT "optimal for you," and a value near either edge of the range warrants understanding context, not reflexive reassurance. Worked example to include: a 22-year-old with TSH 0.7 is flagged "in range" but sits near the floor; a young adult is typically expected mid-range (functional view ~1.0-2.0), and because low TSH points to the hyper side, the correct read is to check free T4 and free T3, not assume all is well. Present functional/optimal ranges as evidence-graded and contested where they are (cite strongest sources, name disagreement), never as a self-treat target. TSH must always be paired with free T4 (plus free T3 and TPO antibodies when unclear); note age-drift (older adults run higher and that can be neutral/protective) and trimester-specific pregnancy ranges. Audience for ClassBuild: Professional.
1. What Bloodwork Is (and Is Not): Reference Ranges vs Decision Limits
2. The Complete Blood Count (CBC): Red Cells, White Cells, Platelets
3. The Metabolic Panel: Glucose, Electrolytes, Kidney Markers
4. Lipids: Cholesterol, Triglycerides, and What the Numbers Mean
5. Liver Markers: ALT, AST, and Why Lifters Can Read High
