Part B · The Resting Membrane as a Charged Battery

Part D · Myelin and Conduction Speed

  1. What is myelin, what cells produce it, and what are the small unwrapped gaps between segments called?
  2. Explain saltatory conduction in a full sentence or two: why does a myelinated axon conduct so much faster than a bare one covering the same distance?
  3. Myelin is expensive for the body to build and is not applied to every axon equally. Give one example of the kind of information that tends to travel on heavily myelinated axons, and one example of information that tends to travel on thin, unmyelinated ones, and explain why that allocation makes sense.

Part E · Frequency Coding: Digital in Strength, Graded in Frequency

Use the chapter's central principle to reason through intensity.

  1. If every action potential is identical in size, how does the nervous system communicate the difference between a light touch and a hard press? Name the concept and explain it in a full sentence.
  2. Explain what it means to say neural signals are "digital in strength but graded in frequency." Use both of those phrases correctly in your answer.
  3. A sensory fiber is firing at 5 spikes per second, and the stimulus intensity then doubles. In frequency-coding terms, what changes about the fiber's output, and what stays exactly the same?
