Chapter 6 : Listening from the Inside

Transduction turns a stimulus into spikes, adaptation reports change instead of constancy, and interoception is the vague, predicted sense of the body's own state.

Transduction: The Universal Grammar

TransductionConverting a physical stimulus into a change in a neuron's membrane potential, and ultimately into action potentials. Every sense uses this same basic chain.
Receptor potentialThe graded, analog voltage shift that occurs when a stimulus opens channels in a receptor. Its size scales with stimulus strength.
Rate codingBecause axons fire all-or-nothing spikes, stimulus intensity is encoded as firing rate (spikes per second), not spike size.
Labeled linesMeaning comes from which pathway a spike travels, not from anything different in the spike itself. Pressing a closed eyelid produces a false spot of light because the signal still arrives on the optic nerve.

Sensory Adaptation

  • Adaptation : receptors reduce their response to an unchanging stimulus. A coding strategy, not a failure.
  • Fast adapting (phasic) : fires at onset and offset, then goes quiet. Good for vibration and texture.
  • Slow adapting (tonic) : keeps firing throughout a sustained stimulus, at a declining rate. Good for tracking ongoing state, such as joint position.
  • Efficient coding: limited bandwidth is spent on what is new, not on what is already settled.

Two Directions of Sensing

  • Exteroception : senses the outside world (light, sound, touch); high spatial and temporal precision.
  • Interoception : senses the body's own physiological condition, heart, breath, gut, temperature, chemistry.
  • Craig (2002) reframed interoception as the sense of the whole physiological condition of the body, calling it "the material me."
  • Interoception is the substrate of emotion, effort, and the basic feeling of being alive.

The Vagus and the Ascending Route

  • The vagus nerve is roughly eighty percent afferent: mostly a sensory nerve, not mainly a command line.
  • Route: vagal and other visceral afferents arrive at the nucleus tractus solitarii (NTS) in the brainstem, then relay onward through the parabrachial nucleus and thalamus.
  • The insular cortex integrates these signals into one unified sense of the body's state.
  • Interoception is the sensory partner of the autonomic and stress systems: a brake you cannot feel is a brake you cannot use.

Why Signals Are Vague, and Prediction

  • A stable, healthy body is a constant stimulus, and constant stimuli get adapted away, so the interior is often quiet.
  • Interoceptive inference (Seth, 2013) : the brain predicts the body's expected state and resolves any mismatch either by updating the belief or by acting to change the body.
  • Felt experience is a resolved inference, not a raw readout. The same racing heart can be labeled excitement or dread depending on context.
  • Interoceptive accuracy (how well felt sense tracks physiology) and interoceptive awareness (how confident a person is) can diverge sharply (Garfinkel et al., 2015).

Reading a Wearable Number, Carefully

  • A heart rate variability ring is a transducer: it measures one narrow physiological signal and converts it into a number.
  • The number itself is not a direct readout of nervous system function. It becomes a prior that shapes how the predictive brain interprets an otherwise ambiguous bodily state.
  • A low number can prime a threat reading of an ordinary morning; a high number can prime discounted fatigue signals during real exertion.
  • The responsible stance: treat any single number as one imperfect data point, held loosely, not a verdict.
Where education ends   This builds an accurate model of how sensing works, not a license to diagnose or treat. A persistent or worrying bodily signal, a racing heart with no clear trigger, or a pattern that concerns you is not something this course can sort out from the outside. Treat any such signal as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
