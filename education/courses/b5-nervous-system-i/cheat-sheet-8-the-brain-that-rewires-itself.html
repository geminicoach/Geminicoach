Chapter 8 : The Brain That Rewires Itself

Experience physically reshapes the nervous system: synapses strengthen or weaken, and those changes aggregate into moving cortical maps.

The Core Mechanism

NeuroplasticityThe nervous system's capacity for structural and functional change throughout life, driven by experience. Not limited to childhood or injury recovery.
Hebbian learning"Cells that fire together, wire together." Neurons that are repeatedly co-active build stronger connections between them.
Use it or lose itThe mirror image of Hebb's principle: connections that go unused tend to weaken and are gradually pruned.
Long-term potentiation (LTP)A persistent, use-dependent increase in synaptic responsiveness, first shown by Bliss and Lomo (1973). The physiological demonstration of Hebb's idea.
Long-term depressionLTP's mirror-image partner: a persistent weakening of a synapse from low-frequency, poorly timed, or absent activity.

Cortical Maps

  • Cortical map : an orderly cortical representation of the body or a skill, more like a crowd of neighbors competing for territory than a chart drawn once.
  • Practiced skills claim more territory; unused ones cede it, as shown directly in string players (Elbert et al., 1995).
  • Structural map reorganization happens in the late, consolidating phase of learning, not the early, fast-gains phase (Kleim et al., 2004).
  • A visible plateau in progress may mean the slower, structural rewriting is underway, not that ability has run out.

The Four Conditions That Favor Plasticity

  • Attention (salience) : the experience has to matter; passive exposure barely changes anything.
  • Repetition and intensity : durable change needs enough total, concentrated practice.
  • Spacing : distributing practice across days beats cramming it into one session; consolidation needs time between sessions.
  • Sleep : an active phase of remodeling, not downtime; the brain renormalizes and locks in the day's changes (Tononi and Cirelli, 2014).

The Double Edge

  • A habit is, mechanically, a well-potentiated pathway; the mechanism does not check whether the wiring helps.
  • An unwanted habit can weaken over time by starving it of activation while a competing pathway is repeated instead.
  • Chronic stress, acting through the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, impairs LTP in the hippocampus and can facilitate its weakening counterpart (Kim and Diamond, 2002).
  • Rehearsed stress patterns are strengthened by the same repetition-driven mechanism as any skill.

Reframe

  • Neurons, synapses, autonomic patterns, sensory maps, and motor skills are all sculptable by experience, not fixed circuitry.
  • Improvement is genuinely possible at any age; adult plasticity is real, if not always as fast as a child's.
  • Changes accrue whether or not a person directs them, so attention, repetition, spacing, and sleep tilt the odds either way.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how experience reshapes the nervous system, not a license to diagnose or treat. Persistent memory or concentration problems, an inability to disengage from a stress pattern, or chronic sleep loss are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
