Chapter 3 : The Chemical Handshake

Electrical in, chemical across, electrical out. Calcium triggers release, the receptor decides the effect, and clearance ends the message.

The Handshake Sequence

1. Spike arrivesDepolarizes the presynaptic terminal and opens voltage-gated calcium channels at the active zone.
2. Calcium entersCa2+ floods in along its concentration gradient and binds synaptotagmin, the sensor on primed vesicles.
3. Vesicles fuseFusion (exocytosis) releases a quantum, a fixed packet, of neurotransmitter into the cleft; more calcium means more vesicles fuse.
4. DiffusionTransmitter crosses the narrow synaptic cleft by simple diffusion in microseconds.
5. Receptor bindingTransmitter docks onto ionotropic or metabotropic receptors on the postsynaptic membrane.
6. ClearanceReuptake or enzymatic breakdown removes the transmitter so the synapse can reset for the next signal.

Ionotropic vs Metabotropic

  • Ionotropic : receptor and channel fused into one protein; opens directly and fast (a fraction of a millisecond); delivers point-to-point excitatory postsynaptic potentials (EPSPs) or inhibitory postsynaptic potentials (IPSPs).
  • Metabotropic : binding launches a G-protein cascade; slower (tens of milliseconds to minutes); broader reach; the molecular basis of neuromodulation.
  • Heuristic only: glutamate and gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) lean ionotropic and fast; dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine lean metabotropic and slow.

The Message Is in the Lock

  • A transmitter has no fixed meaning of its own; excitation and inhibition are properties of the receptor, not the molecule.
  • Acetylcholine on skeletal muscle : nicotinic, ionotropic receptor, excites, drives contraction.
  • Acetylcholine on the heart : muscarinic, metabotropic receptor, inhibits, slows the pacemaker.
  • Same key, different lock, opposite outcome.

Major Neurotransmitters by Function

GlutamateThe accelerator, the brain's principal fast excitatory transmitter, central to learning and synaptic strengthening.
Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA)The brake, the principal fast inhibitory transmitter of the mature brain, held in tension with glutamate.
DopamineA reward prediction error signal tied to motivation and learning, not simply pleasure; acts via D1 and D2 metabotropic receptors.
SerotoninA broad modulator of mood, arousal, appetite, and overall state; acts through a large, mostly metabotropic receptor family.
AcetylcholineThe transmitter at the nerve-muscle junction and the chemical signature of the parasympathetic branch; uses both nicotinic and muscarinic receptors.
NorepinephrineThe transmitter of alertness and vigilance, and the chemical signature of the sympathetic branch.

Clearance

  • Reuptake : transporter proteins pump transmitter back into the presynaptic terminal; the main route for serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine.
  • Enzymatic breakdown : an enzyme chops transmitter apart in the cleft; acetylcholinesterase does this for acetylcholine.
  • Blocking either route strengthens and prolongs the signal without releasing a single new molecule; this is how reuptake-inhibiting medications, stimulants, and nerve agents each work, at very different scales.
