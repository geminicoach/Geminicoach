Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Rafe, 34
Consider Rafe, who finds that after tense calls at work, he feels keyed up and unable to settle for an hour or more afterward: a racing pulse, restless hands, and trouble focusing on the next task. It always resolves on its own by the time he gets home. There is no chest pain, no fainting, and it only happens after specific tense exchanges, not during ordinary quiet stretches of his day. Rafe wonders: "Is my stress response broken? Why does it take so long to go away?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what happens to Rafe during and immediately after the tense call, using the two-speed cascade from the chapter, the fast sympathetic-adrenal-medullary (SAM) surge and the slower hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis.
  2. Account for why the wired feeling outlasts the call itself, using the different timescales of the two waves.
  3. Decide whether this reads as a normal, resolving stress response or something to be concerned about, and explain how you know, referencing recovery and autonomic flexibility.
  4. Give one practical, general-education suggestion, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies the fast sympathetic-adrenal-medullary (SAM) surge and explains that it resolves within minutes of the call ending.
  • Explains the slower hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and cortisol timescale (peak roughly fifteen to thirty minutes, lingering an hour or more) as the reason the wired feeling outlasts the call.
  • Frames the pattern as normal, resolving physiology, not a disorder, referencing active recovery and autonomic flexibility.
