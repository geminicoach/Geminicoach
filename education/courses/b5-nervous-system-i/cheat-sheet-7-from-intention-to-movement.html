Chapter 7 : From Intention to Movement

One outbound relay: a command converges on a single cell, crosses a chemical junction, grades its force by two levers, and never runs without feedback.

The Path from Command to Contraction

1. PlanningMotor cortex, basal ganglia, and cerebellum shape a plan, drawing on practice and prior sequences.
2. Descending commandThe command travels down pathways such as the corticospinal tract toward the spinal cord.
3. Final common pathwayEvery source of influence converges on the alpha motor neuron, the last neural link to muscle.
4. Neuromuscular junction (NMJ)The motor neuron's spike is relayed to the muscle fiber by acetylcholine (ACh) across a reliable synapse.
5. ContractionThe muscle fiber fires its own all-or-nothing action potential and produces a twitch.

The Neuromuscular Junction (NMJ)

  • Acetylcholine (ACh) : the chemical messenger released at the junction.
  • Calcium entry : spike opens voltage-gated calcium channels, triggering vesicle release.
  • Endplate potential : the depolarization ACh produces on the muscle fiber; built oversized on purpose.
  • Safety factor : the oversized endplate potential makes one nerve spike reliably fire one muscle action potential.
  • Acetylcholinesterase : the enzyme that rapidly clears acetylcholine (ACh), resetting the junction for the next spike.

The Motor Unit

  • Motor unit : one alpha motor neuron plus every muscle fiber it controls; the true smallest unit of movement.
  • Size principle : units recruited smallest to largest as force demand rises.
  • Small units : few fibers, slow, fatigue resistant; fine control (eyes, fingers) and posture.
  • Large units : many fibers, fast, powerful, fatigue quickly; held in reserve for maximal effort.

Grading Force: Two Levers Only

  • Recruitment : turning on more motor units, smallest to largest.
  • Rate coding : firing already-active units faster.
  • Twitch to tetanus : fast enough firing fuses individual twitches into a smooth, stronger contraction.
  • Both levers work together across most of a contraction; individual spikes and twitches are always all or nothing.

Closed-Loop Feedback

  • Proprioception : the sense of body position and movement, fed by sensors in muscle and tendon.
  • Muscle spindles : report muscle length and rate of change.
  • Golgi tendon organs : report tension in the tendon.
  • Stretch reflex : a fast spinal correction, roughly thirty to fifty milliseconds, running alongside slower cortical loops.
  • Movement is never open loop; feedback and reflex correction run continuously, in parallel with the outbound command.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how a command becomes a movement, not a license to diagnose or treat. Sudden loss of movement or speech, progressive or use-dependent weakness, uncontrolled tremor, or other alarm signs are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
