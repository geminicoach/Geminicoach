Chapter 3 : GH Secretagogues II, Ghrelin Agonists

A second gate onto the same pump: one shared outcome, two independent receptor pathways.

Two Receptors, Same Somatotroph

GHRH receptorGs-coupled; adenylate cyclase, cAMP, PKA; slow-building, also raises new GH synthesis.
Ghrelin receptor (GHS-R)Gq-coupled; phospholipase C, IP3/DAG, calcium, PKC; fast, exocytosis-focused. Also expressed in the hypothalamic arcuate nucleus (appetite).

Five Class Members

  • GHRP-6 : strong pulse, most pronounced appetite effect.
  • GHRP-2 : more potent, ACTH/cortisol spillover.
  • Hexarelin : most potent, most spillover (ACTH, cortisol, prolactin).
  • Ipamorelin : preclinical data show no measurable ACTH/cortisol rise.
  • MK-677 : oral, nonpeptide, once-daily, sustained IGF-1 elevation.

Potency Is Not Selectivity

  • Potency: how strongly a compound triggers GH release.
  • Selectivity: how cleanly it does so without moving ACTH, cortisol, or prolactin.
  • Hexarelin scores high on potency, low on selectivity. Ipamorelin is the reverse, in preclinical data.

Two-Receptor Synergy

  • Combining a GHRH-pathway and a ghrelin-pathway agonist raises GH more than either alone.
  • Arvat et al. (2001): combined response significantly exceeded the simple sum, a true synergy.
  • Mechanism, not a validated regimen; risks scale with the larger signal, they do not cancel out.

Known Risks (Class-Wide)

  • Appetite stimulation, most pronounced with GHRP-6 and MK-677.
  • Fluid retention and mild edema, joint or muscle discomfort.
  • Reduced insulin sensitivity and rising fasting glucose (documented for MK-677).
  • Long-term safety data, including cancer incidence with extended use, remain genuinely limited.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of a second GH-releasing pathway and its evidence base, not a license to diagnose, treat, or recommend use. Persistent swelling, new glucose concerns, or other alarm signs warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
