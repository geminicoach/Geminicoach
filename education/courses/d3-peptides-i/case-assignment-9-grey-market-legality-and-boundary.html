Chapter 9: Reason Through a Vial's Label

Take the three-tier framework from the chapter and use it to explain what a label does and does not tell you, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Talia, 38
Talia is considering ordering a peptide sold online with a label reading "research use only, not for human consumption." A friend told her the label is just a legal formality and does not really mean anything about the product itself. Talia wants to understand what the label actually does, what tier of product she would be getting, and what specific quality or safety unknowns would apply to a vial like that.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain what the "research use only" label legally accomplishes, and what it does not certify about the vial.
  2. Place this vial in the correct tier of the three-tier legitimacy framework, and explain what oversight exists, or does not exist, at that tier.
  3. Name and explain at least two distinct categories of quality or safety unknown that would apply to this specific vial.
  4. State clearly where this explanation ends: give no sourcing recommendation, and note that legal status varies by jurisdiction and this is not legal advice.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding of sourcing tiers and quality risk, not recommending a source or answering a legal question. A responsible response explains the label's real function, places the product in the correct tier, names the relevant risk categories accurately, and identifies where professional expertise, clinical or legal, would actually be needed. This course does not offer legal advice, and diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains what the "research use only" label legally does (shifts liability) and does not certify (safety, purity, identity).
  • Correctly describes the three tiers of legitimacy (FDA-approved medicine, compounded product under 503A/503B, unapproved research chemical).
  • Accurately names and explains at least two of the three risk categories (sterility/endotoxin, identity/mislabeling, heavy metal/process impurities).
  • Connects the evidence ladder to the sourcing layer, distinguishing "what the molecule does" from "what is in this vial."
  • Stays in scope: gives no sourcing recommendation, notes legal status varies by jurisdiction, and identifies where professional expertise is needed without diagnosing.
