The Scenario

Scenario · Nadia, 38
Nadia's coworker started an approved weekly GLP-1 injection for type 2 diabetes and mentioned losing a noticeable amount of weight on it. Around the same time, Nadia saw an online ad for "research peptide" tirzepatide, sold with no prescription required, at a much lower price. Nadia's reasoning: "The drug itself is so well proven at this point, tens of thousands of people in trials, full approval, so a vial with the same name should basically be the same thing, just cheaper." She wants an honest read on whether that reasoning holds up.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain the three-site mechanism (pancreas, stomach, brain) that drives GLP-1's effects on blood glucose and appetite.
  2. Explain specifically why semaglutide and tirzepatide sit at the top of this course's evidence ladder. What makes that evidence strong, and how is it different from the evidence behind most other compounds covered in this course?
  3. Evaluate Nadia's reasoning: does the strength of the trial evidence for the approved drug transfer to an unregulated vial claiming the same chemical name? Explain why or why not.
  4. Give one practical, general-education takeaway for Nadia, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.
