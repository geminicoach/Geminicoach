Chapter 1 : What a Peptide Actually Is

A chemistry term, not a safety rating: three structural categories, two signaling routes, and a four-rung evidence ladder.

Three Structural Categories

Small moleculeRoughly under 500 daltons; diffuses through membranes; often oral. Examples: aspirin, testosterone.
PeptideRoughly 2 to 50 amino acids, ~200 to 5,000 daltons; too large and charged to diffuse in, usually injected. Examples: oxytocin, semaglutide, BPC-157.
ProteinLarger, folded into complex 3D structure, generally over ~6,000 daltons; almost always injected. Examples: growth hormone, insulin, antibodies.

Peptide vs. Steroid Signaling

  • Peptide : hydrophilic, binds a surface receptor (often a GPCR), fast cascade, seconds to minutes.
  • Steroid : lipid soluble, diffuses through the membrane, binds a nuclear receptor, alters gene transcription over days to weeks.
  • Fast at the receptor is not always fast at the level of a visible whole-body outcome.

Key Terms

  • Peptide bond : the link joining amino acids, formed with loss of water.
  • GPCR : G protein-coupled receptor; the largest family of surface receptors peptides use.
  • Hydrophilic : water soluble, charged; cannot cross the membrane by simple diffusion.
  • Off-label use : an approved drug used for a purpose or population its trials did not test.
  • Grey market : a compound sold outside pharmaceutical regulation, content unverified.

The Evidence Ladder (top to bottom)

  • 1. Human RCT : randomized, often blinded, placebo-controlled; controls for chance and placebo effect.
  • 2. Smaller human studies : open-label, pilot, case series; real human data, weaker controls.
  • 3. Animal / preclinical : rodent, primate, cell-culture; useful for mechanism, not proof in humans.
  • 4. Anecdote / vendor marketing : forum posts, testimonials, product pages; no built-in control for placebo effect or bias.

One Word, Three Worlds

  • Approved medicine : full preclinical work, phase 1 to 3 trials, formal regulatory review.
  • Off-label use : an approved compound, used outside its reviewed indication or population.
  • Grey-market compound : sold as "not for human consumption," no independent verification of content, purity, or sterility.
Where education ends   This builds structural and evidentiary literacy, not a license to diagnose, treat, or recommend use. Unexplained fatigue, vision changes, persistent swelling, or other alarm signs warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
