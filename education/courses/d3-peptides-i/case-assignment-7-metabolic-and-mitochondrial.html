The Scenario

Scenario · Priya, 29
Priya found a vendor page listing MOTS-c, SS-31 (elamipretide), SLU-PP-332, and 5-amino-1MQ together under one heading, "mitochondrial optimization stack," described as "clinically studied, exercise in a vial." She wants to understand what each name actually is, what separates them mechanistically, and whether the "clinically studied" language is accurate for all four, or just some of them.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain why SLU-PP-332 is not a peptide, and identify what that reclassification actually changes (administration route and the category of off-target risk that has to be evaluated).
  2. Give a one- or two-sentence mechanism for MOTS-c and for 5-amino-1MQ, and state each one's current, honest evidence status.
  3. Grade the evidence for elamipretide specifically: what has actually been shown in Barth syndrome versus in the broader MMPOWER-3 trial, and why the phrase "clinically studied" needs more precision than the vendor page gives it.
  4. State clearly where this explanation ends: give no recommendation about using any of the four compounds, and note what would call for a specialist's involvement.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating mechanistic understanding and honest evidence grading, not recommending any of these compounds. A responsible response corrects the vendor page's claims, explains each mechanism accurately, states each compound's real evidence status, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Correctly identifies SLU-PP-332 as a small molecule ERR agonist, not a peptide, by structure.
  • Explains what the reclassification concretely changes: administration route and the category of off-target risk to evaluate.
  • Accurately states MOTS-c's mechanism (AMPK activation via the folate cycle and purine synthesis) and its preclinical status.
  • Accurately states 5-amino-1MQ's mechanism (NNMT inhibition sparing NAD+ and SAM) and its preclinical status.
  • Accurately grades elamipretide's evidence: positive Barth syndrome result and mixed MMPOWER-3 result, distinguishing "published/preclinical" from "clinically studied."
  • Stays in scope: recommends no compound, and correctly routes a diagnosed condition to the treating specialist without diagnosing.
