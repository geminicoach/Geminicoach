Chapter 1: Reason Through a Real Claim

Take the chemistry and the evidence ladder from the chapter and use them to evaluate a real claim, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Priya, 27
Priya follows a longevity-focused social media account. It recently posted about a peptide, describing it as "clinically proven to extend lifespan and reset your biological clock," and linking to a single rodent study from the 1990s as its support. The post has thousands of likes, and the comments are full of people saying they "feel amazing" on it. Priya has not taken it and has not decided whether she wants to. A friend of hers is considering trying it and asked Priya what she thinks. Priya wants to reason through the claim honestly before she says anything back.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, structurally, what kind of molecule this peptide most likely is, and what that predicts about how it would have to signal inside the body and how it would likely need to be delivered.
  2. Using the evidence ladder from the chapter, place the claim's actual supporting evidence (one decades-old rodent study, plus social-media testimonials) on the ladder, rung by rung, and justify the placement.
  3. Explain why "a real mechanism" and "clinically proven" are not the same sentence, and why a mechanism can be entirely genuine while the human evidence behind a specific claim stays weak.
  4. Decide what a responsible, non-diagnostic response to Priya's friend would sound like, and state clearly where general education ends and an individual health decision belongs with a clinician.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language evaluation of a claim, not diagnosing anyone or telling Priya's friend what to do. A responsible response explains the mechanism and the evidence honestly, names what if anything would warrant professional input, and does not diagnose. Decisions about using any compound belong with a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies the compound's likely structural class and predicts surface-receptor, fast-cascade signaling rather than the slow nuclear route steroids use.
  • Places the rodent study on the animal/preclinical rung and the testimonials on the anecdote rung, with justification.
  • Explains that a real mechanism can still sit low on the evidence ladder.
  • Distinguishes "clinically proven" marketing language from what the cited evidence actually supports.
  • Gives a responsible, non-diagnostic response and correctly notes there is no acute red flag here, while naming where general education ends.
