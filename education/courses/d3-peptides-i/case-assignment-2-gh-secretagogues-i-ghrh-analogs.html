Chapter 2: Reason Through a Marketing Claim

Take the GHRH-receptor mechanism and the evidence ladder and use them to evaluate a real marketing claim, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Dmitri, 45
Dmitri, generally healthy and training regularly, received an email from a wellness clinic advertising tesamorelin injections. The ad promises it will help him "look leaner, recover faster, and feel twenty-five again," and mentions in passing that tesamorelin is "FDA-approved," without saying for what. Dmitri has no HIV diagnosis and no known fat-redistribution condition. He is not asking whether to try it; he wants to understand what the "FDA-approved" claim actually means before he reads the rest of the pitch.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, how tesamorelin acts on the GHRH receptor and why that pathway tends to produce something closer to a pulsatile pattern than a tonic one.
  2. State tesamorelin's actual FDA-approved indication and the population it was tested in.
  3. Reason through whether the clinic's marketed use, general leanness and anti-aging in a healthy adult, is supported by the same evidence behind the approval, or whether it is an extrapolation, and place that distinction on the evidence ladder.
  4. Explain, as pharmacology, why the mechanism being plausible does not by itself make the marketed claim well-supported, and state where general education ends and an individual prescribing decision begins.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding of a receptor mechanism and an evidence base, not recommending a dose, cycle, or protocol, or telling Dmitri what to do. A responsible response explains the mechanism and the evidence gap honestly and names what if anything belongs with a licensed physician. It does not diagnose.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the GHRH-R mechanism (Gs, cAMP, PKA) and connects it to pulsatile release.
  • States tesamorelin's actual approved indication (HIV-associated lipodystrophy, visceral fat reduction) and population.
  • Distinguishes approved-indication evidence from the marketed, off-label use, and grades the gap on the evidence ladder.
  • Does not propose a dose, cycle, or protocol, and does not diagnose.
  • Identifies that an off-label prescribing decision belongs with a licensed physician, without making referral the whole answer.
