Chapter 5 : Rewriting the Signal

Sort every method by mechanism, using four questions: ovulation, the cycle, bleeding, and return of fertility.

The Four Questions

  • 1. Does it stop ovulation?
  • 2. Does it leave the natural cycle running, or replace it?
  • 3. Is the resulting bleed a true menstrual period or a medically induced event?
  • 4. How quickly does fertility return after stopping?

Combined Hormonal (Pill, Patch, Ring)

  • Suppresses gonadotropins, blocks the LH surge. No ovulation.
  • Bleed during the hormone-free interval is a withdrawal bleed, not a true period.
  • Lower-dose formulations lean more on surge-blocking than on shutting follicles down entirely.

Progestin-Only Family

  • Progestin-only pill: mucus thickening often cited, but direct evidence is thin; ovulation suppression inconsistent.
  • Etonogestrel implant: dominant route is reliable ovulation suppression; irregular bleeding is the top reason people discontinue.
  • Injectable (DMPA): large sustained dose, reliable whole-axis suppression; amenorrhea common with continued use; longest, most variable fertility delay after stopping.

Two Devices, Opposite Logic

  • Hormonal IUD: local endometrial effect (glandular atrophy, mucus thickening); minimal systemic ovarian suppression; many users still ovulate while barely bleeding.
  • Copper IUD: no hormones; spermicidal, prevents fertilization; axis, ovulation, and true periods continue unchanged; bleeding often heavier.
  • Neither device works by disrupting an already-implanted embryo; both act before fertilization.

Non-Hormonal Methods

  • Barrier and spermicidal methods: no axis effect at all; true periods continue.
  • Fertility awareness based methods: depend on reading an intact, legible cycle accurately.
  • Typical-use effectiveness can trail perfect-use effectiveness for methods that require action every time.

Return of Fertility

  • Pooled pregnancy rate of about 83% within 12 months of stopping a reversible method; no significant difference by method type or duration of use.
  • Exception: the injectable progestin, with a longer and more variable delay tied to its slow-clearing depot dose.
Where education ends   This builds mechanism literacy, not a license to diagnose, prescribe, or recommend a method for anyone. Explain how each mechanism works. Method selection, side-effect management, and candidacy questions belong to a person and their clinician.
