Chapter 2 : The Conversation Between Brain and Ovary

A pulse becomes a message: the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis, level by level.

The Axis at a Glance

HypothalamusKNDy neurons (kisspeptin, neurokinin B, dynorphin) generate rhythmic GnRH pulses; tempo is the language.
PituitaryGonadotropes reply to pulse tempo with FSH (slow pulses) and LH (fast pulses).
OvaryTheca cells (LH) make androgens; granulosa cells (FSH) convert them to estradiol via aromatase.
Corpus luteumForms after ovulation; produces progesterone, which slows the pulse tempo.
FeedbackEstradiol and progesterone travel back to the brain and change what it does next.

The Tempo Code

  • Slow pulses (~1 per 2-3 hrs) favor follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH).
  • Fast pulses (<1 per hour) favor luteinizing hormone (LH).
  • Neurokinin B is the accelerator that triggers each kisspeptin burst; dynorphin is the brake that shuts it off.
  • Continuous, non-pulsed GnRH desensitizes the pituitary within days.

The Ovary's Reply

  • Estradiol (E2) : the potent, dominant estrogen of the reproductive years.
  • Estrone (E1) : weaker sibling estrogen, more prominent after menopause.
  • Progesterone : made by the corpus luteum after ovulation; prepares the lining and slows the tempo.

Negative vs. Positive Feedback

  • Negative feedback : moderate estradiol restrains GnRH/LH. The default mode most of the cycle.
  • Positive feedback : estradiol sustained high (roughly >200 pg/mL for ~50 hrs) flips the switch and provokes the LH surge.
  • Progesterone reinforces restraint, slowing the tempo further after ovulation.

Reading a Lab Value

  • A hormone level only means something in the context of cycle day and the rest of the axis.
  • Day-3 FSH answers a different question than day-21 FSH.
  • A single number is a snapshot of a moving system, never a standalone verdict.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of the axis, not a license to diagnose or treat. A persistently absent cycle, especially with heavy training and reduced eating, warrants evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
