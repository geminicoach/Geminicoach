Chapter 4 : The Fifth Vital Sign

A regularly ovulatory cycle reports on whole-system health. Read it with curiosity and restraint.

The Axis, In Order

HypothalamusReleases GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) in timed pulses. This pulse generator drives the whole loop.
PituitaryAnswers GnRH pulses by releasing FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) and LH (luteinizing hormone).
OvaryFSH matures a dominant follicle; a sharp LH surge triggers ovulation about a day later; the corpus luteum then makes progesterone and feeds back to the brain.

Three Self-Observed Signs

  • Basal body temperature : confirms after the fact. Progesterone raises resting temperature roughly 0.3 to 0.5°F after ovulation. Read as a trend, never one morning.
  • Cervical mucus : forecasts ahead of time. Rising estrogen produces clear, stretchy "peak" mucus in the day or two before ovulation.
  • LH test strip : predicts imminently. Detects the surge directly; ovulation follows about a day later.

Surge Versus Confirmation

  • A positive LH strip is a prediction, not proof ovulation occurred.
  • Confirmed ovulation comes from a sustained luteal-phase progesterone rise (seen indirectly via a temperature shift).
  • A regular, normal-length cycle is not automatically ovulatory: in one large study, 37% of cycles studied were anovulatory despite looking ordinary.

How Wide Is Normal

  • Everyday band: roughly 26 to 35 days, about 5 days of bleeding. Population mean is 29.3 days, not 28.
  • Variability is greatest in the first years after a first period and again approaching menopause; narrowest in the middle reproductive years.
  • A deviation from a person's own established pattern is more informative than a deviation from the population average.

Signal Versus Noise

  • Weigh three things: size of the deviation, persistence across months, and context (travel, illness, training, sleep).
  • A single unusual month is usually noise. Patterns that persist and cluster deserve a clinician's look.
  • Amenorrhea and RED-S (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport) are the recognize-and-refer thread: low energy availability (below roughly 30 kcal/kg lean mass/day) disrupts LH pulsatility.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the axis and its signals work, not a license to diagnose or treat. A cycle that stops for months, bleeding at the extremes, or a sustained departure from someone's own pattern are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. A chart is a tool for a better conversation with a professional, not a verdict on its own.
