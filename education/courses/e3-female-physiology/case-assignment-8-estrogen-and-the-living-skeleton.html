The Scenario

Scenario · Bianca, 21
Bianca rows for her university team. Ten months ago, aiming to feel "lean and fast" for the spring season, she increased her training volume and quietly reduced her portions. Her periods stopped soon after and have not returned. She says she feels completely fine: no pain most days, good energy, strong erg scores, and she has read that missing periods are "just part of being a serious athlete." Three weeks ago she developed shin soreness after a hard training block, and it has not settled the way her usual muscle soreness does. She asks you, "Since I feel fine and nothing hurts most of the time, is there actually anything to worry about here?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanistically, how a missing period for ten months could be connected to Bianca's shin soreness through the estrogen and bone remodeling pathway.
  2. Explain why "I feel fine" does not rule out bone loss, using what the chapter says about the timescale of a full remodeling cycle.
  3. Explain why simply recommending more calcium and vitamin D would not adequately address what might be happening.
  4. State clearly what your response to Bianca should and should not include, and where the responsibility for assessment and any treatment decision lies.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing Bianca or telling her what to do about her training or eating. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Interpreting a bone scan, ordering labs, and deciding on treatment belong entirely to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains estrogen's mechanism as bone's primary brake on resorption (the RANKL to osteoprotegerin ratio and/or cell survival effects).
  • Connects the missing period to possible low estrogen and therefore to bone risk.
  • Explains why feeling fine does not rule out bone loss, referencing the months-long remodeling timescale.
  • Explains why calcium and vitamin D alone would not adequately address a hormonal driver.
  • Refers clearly to a qualified clinician without diagnosing, ordering a scan, or recommending treatment.
