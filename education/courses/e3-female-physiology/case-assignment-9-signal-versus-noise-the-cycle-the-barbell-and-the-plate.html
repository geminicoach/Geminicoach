The Scenario

Scenario · Marisol, 26
Marisol competes in CrossFit at a regional level. A popular cycle-syncing app tells her which days to lift heavy, which days to back off, and what to eat, all generated from a calendar built off her last period date. For the past two cycles her lift numbers have dipped for no clear training reason, and she has felt more tired than usual, even on days the app marked as a "green light" high-output day. She eats very little red meat. She asks you two things: "Should I trust the app's calendar more, or trust how I actually feel that day? And could this tiredness be something I should just push through, or is it worth looking into?"

Your Tasks

  1. Evaluate the app's phase-based lifting prescription against the evidence covered in the chapter. Is it signal, noise, or depends, and why?
  2. Explain what a more honest, evidence-grounded alternative to the calendar prescription would look like for Marisol's day-to-day training.
  3. Explain, mechanistically, why Marisol's fatigue and low red meat intake are worth taking seriously as a possible iron issue.
  4. State clearly what Marisol should and should not do about the possible iron issue, and where that boundary sits.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating honest evidence grading and plain-language communication, not diagnosing Marisol or telling her what supplement to take. A responsible response grades the training claim against the evidence, explains the iron mechanism, and does not diagnose. Iron testing, interpretation, and any supplementation decision belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Correctly grades the phase-based lifting claim as not well supported, citing the umbrella review or performance meta-analysis findings.
  • Describes load-and-recovery mindfulness (autoregulation) as the more defensible alternative.
  • Explains the mechanism of menstrual iron loss and why it is a stronger, more measurable claim than phase-based lifting.
  • Does not recommend self-supplementation, and identifies that iron testing and any treatment decision belongs to a clinician.
  • Communicates clearly without overstating certainty in either direction.
