Chapter 6 : When the Cycle Goes Quiet

Amenorrhea is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Sort the bucket, then recognize and refer.

Vocabulary

AmenorrheaAbsence of menstruation. Primary: never started by expected age. Secondary: stopped for 3+ months after a prior regular pattern, or 6+ months if previously irregular.
Diagnosis of exclusionCan only be applied after pathological causes are ruled out with labs and, if needed, imaging.

Three Buckets

  • Medically induced: expected, deliberate, benign; e.g. an absent bleed from a progestin-based method.
  • Functional / hypothalamic: the axis powers itself down under low energy availability, stress, or overload. Reversible, but still needs a clinician to confirm.
  • Pathological: PCOS, thyroid disease, prolactinoma, primary ovarian insufficiency, and others. Cannot be told apart from functional by looking.

The Energy Availability Lever

  • Energy availability = intake minus exercise cost, per kilogram of lean mass.
  • Threshold around 20 to 30 kcal/kg lean mass/day; below it, GnRH pulsatility is disrupted.
  • Body fatness itself is not the regulator; the net energy left over is.
  • Physique athlete data: 58.65% reported menstrual disorders; 46.15% screened at risk of low energy availability.

Pathological Red Flags

  • PCOS: fast GnRH pulses, high LH:FSH ratio, elevated androgens.
  • Thyroid disorder: raises prolactin, which suppresses GnRH.
  • Prolactinoma: excess prolactin plus possible headaches, visual changes, galactorrhea.
  • Primary ovarian insufficiency: high FSH, low estradiol (opposite pattern from functional amenorrhea).

Recognize and Refer

  • Recognizing: noticing the cycle is quiet and knowing which broad bucket it might plausibly fit.
  • Referring: saying plainly that evaluation is warranted, then stepping back.
  • Referring does not include naming a condition, suggesting a fix, or reassuring someone it is "probably nothing."
  • Evaluation thresholds: cycle interval persistently longer than 45 days, or amenorrhea of 3+ months.
Where education ends   This builds recognition skills, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. A quiet cycle outside a clearly expected medically induced change is a reason to refer to a qualified clinician, not a puzzle to solve yourself.
