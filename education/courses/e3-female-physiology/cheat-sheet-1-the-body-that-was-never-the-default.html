Chapter 1 : The Body That Was Never the Default

A field built on a male reference body, an evidence gap that persists, and two habits that protect a careful learner.

The Evidence Gap, in Numbers

1977 / 1993FDA policy excluded women of reproductive age from early trials in 1977; the 1993 NIH Revitalization Act finally required inclusion in federally funded research.
Costello et al. 2014Audit of 1,382 articles: women were about 39% of the more than 6 million participants studied.
Cowley et al. 2021Audit of 5,261 publications (2014-2020): women about 34% of participants; 31% of studies were male-only, only 6% female-only.
Kuikman et al. 2022Performance-enhancement research drew only about 3% of participants from women; acute carbohydrate-intake research about 11%.

Why “Complexity” Became an Excuse

  • The menstrual cycle was treated as noise to control for, not a system worth designing for.
  • Recruiting men produced a cheaper, faster, "cleaner" sample.
  • Absence from a study is not evidence of irrelevance; often it is the opposite (Sims & Heather, 2018).
  • Elliott-Sale et al. (2021) show the barrier was a lack of specialist knowledge and design standards, not an unsolvable problem.

Grade Every Claim in Three Tiers

  • Strong and replicated : multiple independent studies, female participants with defined reproductive status, same direction.
  • Mixed or limited : real evidence, but thin, inconsistent, or extrapolated from men.
  • Weak or speculative : mechanism alone, one small study, or confident extrapolation with no direct test.

Average vs. Individual

  • An average is a place on a curve. No one lives there.
  • Cycle length, ovulation, contraceptive use, age, and energy availability all move the needle for one specific person.
  • The hormonal landscape shifts across a lifetime: adolescence, reproductive years, and the menopause transition each differ.

Recognize and Refer: FHA & RED-S

  • Low energy availability can impair metabolic rate, menstrual function, bone, immune, and cardiovascular health (Mountjoy et al., 2018).
  • Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea accounts for roughly a third of secondary amenorrhea cases (Saadedine et al., 2023).
  • The role here is to notice the pattern and refer it onward, not to diagnose or manage it.
Where education ends   This builds evidence literacy about female physiology, not a license to diagnose or treat. A missed or irregular cycle, especially alongside heavy training and reduced eating, warrants evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
