Chapter 3 : One Cycle, Two Acts, One Pivot

Follicular phase, ovulatory pivot, luteal phase: one loop, read as a story with a beginning, a turn, and a fork.

The Cycle at a Glance

Follicular phaseDay 1 to ovulation. FSH recruits a cohort of follicles; one dominant follicle is selected; estradiol climbs. The variable act.
The pivot (surge)Sustained high estradiol flips feedback from negative to positive; LH surges; ovulation follows about a day to a day and a half later.
Luteal phaseCorpus luteum forms, produces progesterone, matures the lining. The steadier act, but not perfectly fixed.
The forkNo pregnancy: luteolysis, hormones fall, lining sheds (menstruation). Pregnancy: hCG rescues the corpus luteum.

The Follicular Phase

  • FSH rise recruits antral follicles in overlapping waves (usually 2-3).
  • Inhibin B suppresses FSH, pruning the cohort to one dominant follicle.
  • Two-cell system: theca cells (LH) make androgens, granulosa cells (FSH) convert them to estradiol.
  • This phase absorbs most of the swing in total cycle length.

The Pivot

  • Threshold: estradiol above roughly 200 pg/mL, sustained for about 50 hours.
  • Kisspeptin neurons act as the switch from negative to positive feedback.
  • Ovulation lags the LH surge by about 24-36 hours.
  • A positive ovulation test detects the surge, not egg release itself.

The Luteal Phase

  • Corpus luteum needs continued LH to survive; produces progesterone from two cooperating cell types.
  • Progesterone builds a secretory endometrium and raises basal body temperature slightly.
  • No pregnancy: luteolysis after ~14 days. Pregnancy: hCG rescue until the placenta takes over (~7-10 weeks).

The Asymmetry, Quantified

  • Bull et al. (2019): follicular phase stretched up to 66% with longer cycles; luteal phase moved only ~5%.
  • Henry et al. (2024): median within-person variability ~5.2 days (follicular) vs. ~3.0 days (luteal).
  • Even healthy, screened cycles show real variability; steadier does not mean rigid.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of the cycle, not a license to diagnose or treat. A very long, absent, or newly irregular cycle, especially alongside heavy training and reduced eating, warrants evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
