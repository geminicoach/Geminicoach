Chapter 7: Reason Through a Real Pattern

Use the energy availability and RED-S framework to explain a real pattern, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Naomi, 23
Naomi is training for her first half-distance triathlon. Over the past four months she has steadily added swim, bike, and run volume, a little more each week, without changing what she eats. Nothing about her plate looks unusual, and her weight has not moved. But her long-run pace has been stuck for five weeks, she has come down with two colds in the past two months (more than she remembers catching in the two years before that), a dull ache in her shin that started a month ago still has not resolved, and she has not had a period in three cycles. She tells a training partner, "I'm not restricting anything, I eat whenever I'm hungry, so I don't think this is about food. I just think I need to push through it." She wants to know what is going on.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain what energy availability is, and how Naomi could be eating "plenty" by her own account while still running a shortfall in the energy left over for her body's core functions.
  2. Identify each piece of Naomi's pattern (stalled pace, recurring illness, unresolved shin ache, missed periods) and explain, mechanistically, how they can trace back to one upstream cause rather than four unrelated coincidences.
  3. Explain why the fact that her weight has not changed does not rule out low energy availability.
  4. State clearly what you, as an educated observer, should tell Naomi to do next, and why that is a referral rather than a training or eating adjustment you coach her through yourself.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing Naomi or prescribing a fix. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Assessment, diagnosis, and any change to training or eating belong to a qualified clinician, ideally as part of a multidisciplinary team.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Defines energy availability correctly (intake minus exercise expenditure, relative to fat-free mass) and distinguishes it from raw intake.
  • Explains why weight stability does not rule out low energy availability.
  • Connects the four signs (pace, illness, shin ache, missed periods) to one upstream cause via the RED-S framework, naming at least two mechanisms (for example, GnRH pulse suppression and the estrogen-bone link).
  • Does not diagnose Naomi, and explicitly recommends referral to a qualified clinician rather than a training or nutrition fix.
  • Communicates in clear, non-alarming, plain language appropriate for a non-specialist.
