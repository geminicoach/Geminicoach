The Scenario

Scenario · Marisol, 26
Marisol is an amateur rower training for a spring regatta. She is not using any hormonal contraceptive method. Over the past five months she has increased her training volume, taken on a second part-time job, and noticed her appetite has not really kept pace with the extra training. Her period, which had been regular for years, stopped five months ago. She feels tired some days but otherwise functional, and nothing else about her health has obviously changed. She tells a learner in this course: "I looked it up and it sounds like this happens to a lot of athletes when they train hard. I think I just need to relax about it. Should I just eat a bit more and see if it comes back on its own?"

Your Tasks

  1. Determine whether Marisol's situation fits the medically induced bucket, and explain why or why not.
  2. Explain why a functional or hypothalamic pattern is a plausible fit given what Marisol describes, without declaring that it is the cause.
  3. Explain, using the "diagnosis of exclusion" idea from the chapter, why nobody without clinical training, including you, can confirm that this is a functional pattern rather than something pathological.
  4. Write the recognize-and-refer response you would actually give Marisol, including what you will not say or suggest.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating recognition, not diagnosis. A responsible response identifies that this pattern is not a clearly expected induced change, names why a functional pattern is plausible without declaring it, and refers Marisol to a qualified clinician. It does not diagnose, name a likely cause with confidence, or recommend a specific fix such as eating more or training less.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Correctly identifies that this is not a clearly expected medically induced change, since Marisol uses no hormonal method.
  • Names the functional or hypothalamic pattern as plausible given training load and intake, without declaring it as the cause.
  • Explains that functional hypothalamic amenorrhea is a diagnosis of exclusion, requiring labs and possibly imaging to rule out pathological causes first.
  • Explicitly avoids diagnosing, naming a definitive cause, or recommending a fix such as eating more or training less.
  • Clearly refers Marisol to a qualified clinician, citing the persistence and duration of her amenorrhea as the reason evaluation is warranted.
