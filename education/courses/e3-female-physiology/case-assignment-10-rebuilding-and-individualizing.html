Chapter 10: Reason Through a Real Pattern

Use the pressure-canister and connective-tissue framework to explain a real pattern, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

The Scenario

Scenario · Devika, 31
Devika is eight months out from the vaginal delivery of her second child. She lifted weights for years before her pregnancies and thought little about her midline until now. During a plank or a heavy lift, she notices a visible ridge doming down the center of her stomach. A friend at her gym told her to "just close the gap" with daily crunches and a waist trainer. Devika also mentions, almost as an aside, that she sometimes leaks a little urine when she coughs hard, which she has been treating as "just normal after having a baby" and has not brought up with anyone else. She asks you, "Is the doming something I can just train my way out of, and is the leaking actually something to worry about?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what is likely happening at Devika's midline, and why "closing the gap" with crunches misunderstands the tissue involved.
  2. Explain why her doming under load is meaningful information about tissue readiness, not just a cosmetic issue.
  3. Address the leaking symptom specifically: is this something to normalize, monitor, or refer, and why?
  4. Describe what a responsible response to Devika would include, and where it must stop.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not designing Devika a rehabilitation plan or clearing her for any activity. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose or prescribe. Assessment of the abdominal wall and pelvic floor, and any return-to-load decision, belong to an obstetric provider or a pelvic-floor physical therapist.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the linea alba and collagen remodeling mechanism, and why gap width alone is an incomplete measure.
  • Explains why doming under load signals that current demand exceeds current tissue readiness (the pressure-canister model).
  • Correctly flags the leaking symptom as warranting referral to an OB provider or pelvic-floor physical therapist, not normalization.
  • Does not prescribe a rehab plan, exercise protocol, or timeline, and favors individualized clinical assessment over a calendar-based clearance.
  • Communicates with appropriate care and without alarm.
