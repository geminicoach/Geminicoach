Chapter 3: Read a Cycle-Tracking Flag

Use the follicular-luteal asymmetry from the chapter to evaluate whether an app's "irregular" warning is telling the truth.

The Scenario

Scenario · Solveig, 31
Solveig is a competitive rower who has been charting her cycle with a phone app for the past four months. Her cycles have run 26, 34, 24, and 30 days, and the app has flagged every single one with a red "irregular" warning, comparing each to a fixed 28-day standard. She has no other symptoms, her training and eating have been steady and adequate throughout, and she feels well. The warnings have made her anxious, and she asks whether something is wrong with her body.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain mechanistically why cycle-length variation of this kind is expected, focusing on which phase, follicular or luteal, is responsible and why.
  2. Explain why a fixed app threshold for "irregular" can be misleading given what the chapter's data show about normal variability.
  3. Decide whether Solveig's pattern, on its own, is something to reassure or something that would need a closer look, and explain what additional information, not present here, would change that judgment.
  4. Give one general-education response addressing her anxiety about the app's flags, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical evaluation begins.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding of cycle physiology, not diagnosing Solveig. A responsible response explains the mechanism honestly, including the real limits of app-based flags, and does not tell her whether her cycles are "fine" as a medical verdict. Ongoing changes or an absent cycle belong with a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Correctly attributes variability mainly to the follicular phase, explaining why the luteal phase is steadier but not perfectly fixed.
  • Explains why a fixed-day app threshold misapplies population data to one individual's cycle.
  • Reasons correctly that this variability alone is not itself alarming, while naming what would warrant evaluation.
  • Gives a responsible, education-only response that neither diagnoses nor over-reassures.
