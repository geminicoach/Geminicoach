The Scenario

Scenario · Devon, 30
Devon used a copper intrauterine device for four years and had regular, if somewhat heavier, monthly bleeding the whole time. Eight months ago Devon had it replaced with a hormonal intrauterine device. Since then, bleeding has become very light and, for the last three months, has stopped altogether. Devon feels physically normal otherwise: no pain, no other new symptoms, energy and mood unchanged. Devon messages a learner in this course: "With the copper one I bled every month like clockwork. Now nothing. Does that mean my body has stopped cycling? Should I go back to the copper one?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what the hormonal intrauterine device does at the endometrium, and why that is different from a whole-axis effect.
  2. Explain what an absent bleed on this device does and does not tell you about whether Devon is still ovulating.
  3. Contrast this mechanism with the copper device Devon used previously, using the four-question framework from the chapter.
  4. State clearly what you will and will not address regarding Devon's question about switching back, and identify anything that would warrant professional evaluation.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding of mechanism, not recommending a method. A responsible response explains what is happening physiologically, explicitly declines to advise switching, starting, or stopping any method, and identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation. Method selection and candidacy belong to a person and their clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains that the hormonal IUD acts locally on the endometrium (glandular atrophy, thinning) with only minimal systemic ovarian suppression.
  • States correctly that ovulation and the axis can continue upstream even when bleeding is minimal or absent.
  • Contrasts this with the copper device, which leaves the axis, ovulation, and bleeding untouched.
  • Explicitly declines to recommend switching back, continuing, or choosing any method.
  • Identifies what if anything would warrant professional evaluation (for example, a new symptom such as pain), and does not diagnose.
