Chapter 5 : The 19-Nortestosterones and Progestins

One missing carbon opens a second receptor, and that second receptor explains what aromatization alone cannot.

The Missing Carbon

The change19-nortestosterone (nandrolone) is testosterone with the angular methyl group at carbon 19 removed. Nothing else about the four-ring frame changes.
Why it mattersThe C19 methyl normally helps block testosterone from fitting the progesterone receptor (PR). Remove it and the molecule can bind PR as well as AR.
The consequenceEvery compound in this chapter is a dual-receptor ligand: a real androgen and a real progestin, from one shared molecule.

Three Compounds

  • Nandrolone: unmodified 19-nor backbone; converted by 5-alpha-reductase into a weaker metabolite (DHN), the reverse of testosterone to DHT.
  • Trenbolone: adds further ring double bonds; cannot aromatize at all; still carries progestogenic activity of its own; never approved for human use.
  • Trestolone (MENT): adds a 7-alpha-methyl group that blocks 5-alpha-reductase entirely; investigational only.

The Prolactin Pathway

  • Dopamine normally suppresses pituitary prolactin release (a tonic brake, not an occasional signal).
  • A 19-nor compound binds PR in the hypothalamus, reducing dopaminergic tone.
  • With less dopamine braking it, prolactin rises from the pituitary lactotrophs.
  • Elevated prolactin directly affects libido and arousal, and can act on breast tissue, independent of testosterone or estrogen.

Why This Breaks the "It's Just Estrogen" Model

  • Nandrolone aromatizes weakly; trenbolone does not aromatize at all. Both remain strongly linked to gynecomastia and mood-related symptoms.
  • A low aromatization rate says nothing about progestogenic or prolactin risk. They are separate mechanisms that can produce the same visible outcome.
  • "Deca dick": low libido during nandrolone use despite normal-looking testosterone and estradiol labs, explained by prolactin elevation via PR agonism, not a testosterone or estrogen problem.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the mechanisms work, not a license to diagnose or treat. Nipple discharge, persistent breast changes, or other alarm signs are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
