Chapter 7 : The 17-Alpha-Alkylated Orals

One carbon-17 modification lets a steroid survive being swallowed, and the same modification is the source of a shared hepatic and lipid risk signature.

The Mechanism

  • 17-alpha-alkylation: a methyl or ethyl group attached at carbon 17, next to the hydroxyl group the liver normally oxidizes to inactivate a steroid.
  • The added group creates steric hindrance, physically blocking hepatic enzymes from disassembling the molecule.
  • Result: the steroid survives first-pass hepatic metabolism and reaches circulation largely intact, which is the only reason it can be a swallowed tablet at all.
  • The same shielded structure forces hepatocytes to process the molecule directly and repeatedly, dose after dose. Oral delivery and hepatic burden are mechanically linked, not separate variables.

The Five-Name Roster

DianabolTestosterone-derived, aromatizable, added A-ring double bond.
AnadrolC2 hydroxymethylene addition; originally an anemia treatment.
WinstrolDHT-derived, pyrazole ring, does not aromatize.
SuperdrolDoubly methylated at C17 and C2; intensified resistance to clearance.
TurinabolC4 chlorine blocks aromatization.

All five differ in androgenic reputation. All five share the identical carbon-17 modification and, with it, the same hepatic and lipid risk signature.

Two Hepatic Pathways

  • Cholestasis: bile export disrupted, bile acids and bilirubin back up → jaundice, pruritus (itching), dark urine.
  • Oxidative stress: reactive oxygen species damage hepatocyte membranes → elevated ALT and AST.
  • Both are dose-related and largely reversible after discontinuation, but "often reversible" is not "without risk."
  • Rare, serious outcomes (case-report evidence): peliosis hepatis (blood-filled liver cavities) and hepatic adenoma (benign tumor with malignant-transformation risk).

The Parallel Lipid Signature

  • HDL-C suppressed, LDL-C elevated: the least favorable shift on both sides of the lipid ledger at once.
  • Hurley et al. (1984): roughly 55% HDL-C drop, 61% LDL-C rise, 280% rise in LDL:HDL ratio with androgen use in athletes.
  • Mechanistically linked to the hepatic pathway: the liver regulates lipoprotein metabolism, including hepatic lipase, the same organ under strain from processing the alkylated molecule.
  • Triglyceride behavior is less consistently documented than the HDL/LDL shift.

The Class-Level Lesson

  • "Class" is a biochemical claim, not a loose grouping: inheriting the 17-alpha-alkyl group means inheriting its hepatic and lipid risk signature, regardless of brand name or androgenic reputation.
  • A "milder" reputation almost always describes androgenic side effects or water retention, not hepatic or lipid risk.
  • The predictive question for any unfamiliar oral compound: is it 17-alpha-alkylated. If yes, expect this signature.
  • Supportive supplements (milk thistle, TUDCA) have biological plausibility but do not change the molecule's carbon-17 structure or eliminate the risk.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of a shared mechanism, not a license to diagnose or treat. Jaundice, itching, dark urine, persistent symptoms, or abnormal bloodwork are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
