The Scenario

Scenario · Sana, 27
Sana, a learner in this course, was told by a friend that his libido had been noticeably flattened for months into a period of nandrolone use, even though his most recent labs showed testosterone-equivalent levels holding steady and estradiol comfortably within range. Forum consensus kept blaming either "low T" or "high estrogen," the two explanations that seem to cover every side effect anyone reports, but neither one matches his actual numbers. Sana wants to know what a normal-looking hormone panel could possibly be missing.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain the structural change that defines the 19-nortestosterone class and how it opens a second hormone receptor that testosterone itself barely engages.
  2. Explain the prolactin pathway: how activating that second receptor in the hypothalamus can raise prolactin, step by step.
  3. Explain how elevated prolactin affects libido and arousal directly, independent of testosterone or estrogen levels.
  4. Explain why a standard testosterone-equivalent and estradiol panel could look completely normal while this mechanism is active, and state where general education ends and where ordering or interpreting further testing becomes a clinician's job.
