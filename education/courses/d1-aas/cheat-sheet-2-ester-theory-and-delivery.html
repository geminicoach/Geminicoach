Chapter 2 : Ester Theory and Delivery

One hormone, several delivery timers. The ester changes when testosterone arrives, never what it does once it gets there.

The Core Model

What an ester isA bond between a carboxylic acid and testosterone's C17 hydroxyl, releasing water. Same reaction that builds dietary fat.
What it changesAbsorption and clearance (pharmacokinetics), not receptor binding (pharmacology).
What it does not changeOnce hydrolyzed, free testosterone is chemically identical, no matter which ester delivered it.
LipophilicityLonger fatty-acid chain = more lipophilic = slower release from the depot.

The Ester Family

  • Propionate : short (3-carbon), fast, sharp release curve.
  • Enanthate : medium (7-carbon), close to cypionate despite different reputations.
  • Cypionate : medium (ring-containing acid), similar timing to enanthate.
  • Undecanoate : long (11-carbon), slow, flat release curve.
  • Blends (e.g. Sustanon) : several esters in one oil solution, combined curve.

Depot to Bloodstream

  • Depot : oil pool forms in muscle at the injection site.
  • Partitioning : ester molecules leave the oil for aqueous tissue, rate depends on lipophilicity.
  • Esterase : enzyme that hydrolyzes the ester bond, releasing free testosterone.
  • An intact ester is close to inert at AR; the tail sterically blocks binding.

Half-Life, Two Meanings

  • Free T half-life : tens of minutes, fixed, does not depend on ester.
  • Apparent (ester-limited) half-life : how long the depot takes to empty, varies by ester.
  • Release rate is not potency. Potency depends on receptor binding, unaffected by esterification.

Routes: Oil Depot, Suspension, Oral

  • Aqueous suspension : no oil, no long ester, fast onset, short curve.
  • Plain oral T : mostly destroyed by first-pass liver metabolism.
  • Oral testosterone undecanoate : lipophilic enough to enter the lymphatic system with dietary fat, partly bypassing the liver.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of pharmacokinetics, not a license to design an injection schedule. Understanding release timing does not tell any one person what preparation, if any, is appropriate for them. That belongs with a licensed physician.
