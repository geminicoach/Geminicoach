Chapter 11 : Thyroid Hormones (T3/T4)

One axis, one prohormone, one receptor expressed in nearly every tissue. Turning the dial up does not know how to spare muscle or the heart.

The HPT Axis, Top to Bottom

HypothalamusReleases TRH (thyrotropin-releasing hormone).
Anterior pituitaryReleases TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) in response to TRH.
Thyroid glandTSH drives iodine uptake and hormone release: mostly T4 (~85%), a smaller direct share of T3 (~15%).
FeedbackRising T3/T4, gland-made or exogenous, suppress TRH and TSH. The loop only reads the level, not the source.

T4 Is a Prohormone

  • T4 (thyroxine): the gland's main output, a stable, comparatively inert circulating reservoir.
  • T3 (triiodothyronine): the far more potent form that does most of the actual receptor work.
  • D1 / D2 deiodinases: convert T4 to active T3, tissue by tissue (liver/kidney; brain, pituitary, muscle).
  • D3 deiodinase: inactivates T4 and T3, a local brake protecting sensitive tissue.

The Receptor Switch

  • Thyroid hormone receptor (TR): nuclear, already bound to DNA at thyroid hormone response elements, paired with RXR.
  • No T3: corepressors bound, gene actively held off, not just neutral.
  • T3 binds: corepressors released, coactivators recruited, gene flips to actively transcribed.
  • Sets basal metabolic rate (BMR) across essentially every tissue expressing the receptor, not fat alone.

The Compounds

  • Levothyroxine: synthetic T4. Standard treatment for diagnosed hypothyroidism.
  • Liothyronine: synthetic T3. Faster onset, shorter half-life; narrower approved clinical use.
  • Both are real, approved medications for a genuine deficiency, not a fat-loss protocol studied in healthy people.

What Pushing the Dial Costs, Tissue by Tissue

MuscleRaised system-wide energy demand shifts protein turnover toward net breakdown (thyrotoxic myopathy); muscle becomes a drawn-down fuel and amino-acid source.
HeartTachycardia, increased contractility, higher myocardial oxygen demand; sustained exposure raises atrial fibrillation risk.
BoneRemodeling speeds up; resorption tends to outpace formation, net bone mineral density loss with sustained exposure.
The axis itselfSustained suppression of TRH/TSH; recovery is generally expected but not automatic, instantaneous, or guaranteed complete.
Where education ends   This builds axis-level mechanism literacy, not a license to diagnose, treat, or provide a dose or taper. Sustained elevated resting heart rate, unexplained muscle weakness, or suspected axis suppression are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
