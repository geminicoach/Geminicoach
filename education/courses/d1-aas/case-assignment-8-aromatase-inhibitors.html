The Scenario

Scenario · Rafael, 36
Rafael has been reading that gynecomastia and water retention are the real dangers of unmanaged estrogen, and that an aromatase inhibitor pushed as hard as a person can comfortably tolerate is therefore always the safer choice, since "estrogen is the thing you're trying to avoid." He wants to know whether lower estradiol is simply better, full stop, or whether that framing is missing something the chapter might have covered.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanistically, what an aromatase inhibitor actually does (upstream enzyme suppression, not receptor blockade), and why reversible and irreversible AI chemistry is not itself a safety ranking.
  2. Correct Rafael's "lower is always safer" framing using the U-shaped curve: name at least two documented consequences of estradiol suppressed too far, with the mechanism behind each.
  3. Name honestly where the strongest human evidence for those low-estradiol harms comes from, and why that population differs from typical unsupervised use.
  4. State clearly where this explanation ends: it is not a suppression target or a recommendation, and note what would warrant a qualified clinician's involvement.
