Chapter 1 : The Parent Molecule

One steroid nucleus, one receptor, two readings: anabolic in muscle, androgenic in skin, hair, and prostate.

Testosterone's Structure

Steroid nucleusFour fused rings (three six-carbon, one five-carbon), derived from cholesterol. Shared by cortisol, estradiol, progesterone, and every AAS.
C3 ketone, C4-C5 bondPart of what makes testosterone testosterone, not just any steroid.
C17 hydroxylThe single site where esters attach (covered in Chapter 2).
LipophilicFat soluble, crosses the cell membrane directly, no surface receptor needed.

How the Signal Works

  • Diffusion : testosterone crosses the membrane directly into the cytoplasm.
  • AR : androgen receptor, bound to HSP90 chaperones at rest.
  • Binding + dimerization : ligand binds, chaperones release, two AR molecules pair up.
  • Nucleus + ARE : the dimer binds an androgen response element and acts as a transcription factor.
  • Timing : gene to mRNA to protein, days to weeks, not an instant switch.

Anabolic vs. Androgenic

  • Anabolic : tissue building. Nitrogen retention, muscle protein synthesis, bone density.
  • Androgenic : masculinizing. Body hair, voice deepening, acne, prostate growth, scalp recession.
  • Both come from the same receptor, read differently by different tissue.
  • 5-alpha-reductase : amplifies the androgenic signal (converts T to DHT) in skin, hair, prostate.
  • No AAS achieves a complete anabolic/androgenic split in a living human.

Where the Rating Numbers Come From

  • Hershberger assay (1953) : castrated rats, dosed 7 to 10 days.
  • Levator ani muscle weight = "anabolic" proxy.
  • Prostate + seminal vesicle weight = "androgenic" proxy.
  • Testosterone propionate standardized to 100:100.
  • Never validated as a predictor of a specific human's experience.

What Makes a Molecule "Steroidal"

  • A structural definition: built on the four-ring frame, not a marketing category.
  • An AAS activates AR and produces both anabolic and androgenic effects, to some degree.
  • Common modifications: esterification (Ch. 2), 17-alpha alkylation, 5-alpha-reductase resistance, 19-nor structure, aromatization resistance (Ch. 3).
  • Every modification still signals through the same one receptor.
Where education ends   This builds biochemical literacy, not a license to diagnose, treat, or design a regimen. Understanding mechanism does not tell any one person what is appropriate for them. Decisions about using or monitoring any hormone belong with a licensed physician.
