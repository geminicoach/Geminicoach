Chapter 7 : Pressing and the Shoulder

The most mobile joint in the body, because it is the least bony-stable joint in the body. Everything below is that trade playing out.

The Trade: Mobility For Stability

Glenohumeral jointBall (humeral head) about three times larger than the shallow glenoid socket. Buys the greatest range of motion (ROM) of any joint in the body, at the cost of little bony restraint.
Hip joint, for contrastDeep acetabulum wraps most of the femoral head. Trades range of motion (ROM) for built-in bony security; rarely dislocates without major trauma.
What holds it togetherNot bone. A labrum deepens the socket somewhat, but through most of a press, active muscular coordination, the scapula and the rotator cuff, does the bulk of the stabilizing.

Scapulohumeral Rhythm

  • Scapula : shoulder blade; must upwardly rotate, tilt posteriorly, and externally rotate as the arm rises, so the socket keeps tracking underneath the humeral head.
  • Classic ratio : roughly two degrees of glenohumeral motion for every one degree of scapulothoracic motion, but the real ratio shifts across the range and under load.
  • Scapular dyskinesis : altered scapular motion; a potential impairment to shoulder function, not a self-diagnosis.

Two Moment Arms, Not One

  • Internal moment arm (the deltoid's own leverage) : short and mostly compressive at the bottom of a press; lengthens and becomes mostly rotational near shoulder height.
  • External moment arm : the perpendicular distance from the shoulder to the bar's line of gravity. Grows wherever the bar drifts forward of the joint.
  • The sticking point : where the external moment arm is temporarily longest. A geometry problem, not an effort problem; electromyography (EMG) shows muscle activity stays high through the stall.

The Rotator Cuff Force Couple

  • Four muscles: supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis. Job: keep the humeral head centered on the glenoid, not to move the arm through large ranges.
  • Concavity compression : the cuff presses the ball into the socket, resisting the deltoid's tendency to ride the head up and forward.
  • Force couple : deltoid supplies the big rotating pull; cuff supplies the smaller centering pull. Neither works well without the other, on every rep.

Grip, Bar Path, and the Overhead Question

  • Wider grip : shorter range, more chest and deltoid leverage, more total load, but more lateral force at the shoulder.
  • Narrower grip : longer range, more triceps involvement, more balanced or medial force.
  • A full overhead press asks for range of motion (ROM), scapular control, and rotator cuff capacity all at once. A restriction anywhere in that chain can push the bar path forward and lengthen the external moment arm right where the press is already hardest.
  • Whether, and how, a given learner should press overhead is an individual mechanical question, not a universal rule.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of shoulder mechanics, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Pain, a pinching or catching sensation, or a sense of instability during pressing are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician, never something to push through. Work within your role, whatever it is.
