Part A · The Hinge as a Long Moment Arm

The deadlift is described in the chapter as "the hip's solo performance." Answer in full sentences.

  1. Define the hip hinge pattern and explain what distinguishes it from a squat, referencing knee angle and how much the torso travels.
  2. Explain, mechanically, why a hinge produces a longer external moment arm at the hip than a squat moving a comparable load.
  3. Explain why the moment arm at the hip is smallest at the top of a deadlift and grows as the torso inclines forward toward the floor.

Part B · Keeping the Load Close

Bar-to-body distance is described as the largest lever a lifter directly controls.

  1. Using the definition of moment arm, explain why a barbell drifting a few centimeters away from the shins raises torque at the hip and the lumbar spine at the same time.
  2. A bar drifts from 5 centimeters to 12 centimeters in front of a lumbar segment. Explain what happens to the moment at that segment, and why the increase is more than double for that small a change in distance.
  3. Explain how a hex bar changes the demand on the hip, the lumbar spine, and the knee compared with a straight bar, and why that shift happens.

Part C · What "Neutral Spine" Means Mechanically

The chapter argues the spine is a stack of segments, not a single rigid lever.

  1. Explain, in your own words, why treating the trunk as one rigid beam is a useful simplification for calculating hip torque but an incomplete picture of what happens inside the spine itself.
  2. Distinguish compression from shear as two separate ways the lumbar spine experiences load, and explain why a posture that reduces one can raise the other.
  3. Explain what bracing and a rise in intra-abdominal pressure actually do to a lift, and what they do not do. Be specific about the difference between changing a moment arm and changing how load is transmitted through the stack.

Part D · Bar Path and Hip Height

  1. Explain how torso inclination and hip height together set the length of the moment arm at the hip and at the lumbar spine at any point during the pull.
  2. The chapter explains that the horizontal offset of the trunk scales with the sine of the torso's inclination from vertical. Explain why a small change in torso angle near horizontal produces a much larger change in torque than the same size angle change near vertical.
  3. Explain why a heavy hinge often feels hardest at the position of greatest torso inclination while the bar is still near the floor, rather than at the very top or the very bottom of the range.

Where education ends
This worksheet builds mechanical literacy, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how load moves through the hip and the spine, and treat anything that looks clinical (pain during hinging, an uncontrollable loss of spinal position, or symptoms that track with a specific load) as signs that warrant professional evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role: a coach explains mechanics and refers out, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
