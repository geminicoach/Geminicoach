Chapter 4: Reason Through a Real Sticking Point

Take the torque-and-moment-arm framework from the chapter and use it to explain a real training question, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Odalys, 33
Consider Odalys, who has been bench pressing consistently for about two years. On nearly every heavy set, the bar slows down and almost stalls a few inches off the chest, then finishes the rest of the press, including lockout, without much trouble. This pattern has been the same for months and has not gotten worse or changed in character. Odalys assumes this means the triceps are the weak link and has been adding extra isolation work aimed at "fixing" them, without much change in how the sticking point feels. Odalys asks: "Is this a triceps weakness I need to train harder, or is something else going on?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what is happening to the bar's moment arm through the range of the press, and why the demand on the pressing muscles is not constant from chest to lockout.
  2. Distinguish the internal torque the muscles are producing from the external torque the bar is imposing, and use that distinction to evaluate whether "the triceps are weak" is the most accurate explanation for the stall.
  3. Include a numeric moment-arm or torque illustration (you can estimate reasonable numbers) that shows why the ratio of demand to capacity is worst right around the sticking region.
  4. Give one practical, general-education response Odalys could receive, and state clearly where that mechanical explanation ends and a need for professional evaluation would begin.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies that the bar's external moment arm changes through the press and explains why demand peaks where that moment arm is longest relative to the muscles' leverage.
  • Distinguishes internal torque (muscle force acting through a small, largely fixed internal moment arm) from external torque (the load acting through a changing external moment arm).
  • Shows a numeric moment-arm or torque illustration, using the relationship torque equals force times moment arm, that supports the explanation.
  • Frames a longstanding, unchanged sticking point as a mechanical feature of the lift rather than proof of a weak muscle.
  • Offers a practical, general-education response and clearly states where general education ends and professional evaluation begins, without diagnosing.
