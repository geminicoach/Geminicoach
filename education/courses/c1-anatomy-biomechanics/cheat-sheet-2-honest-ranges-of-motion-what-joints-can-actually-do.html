Chapter 2 : Honest Ranges of Motion

One joint, one honest range: shaped by bone, tuned by task, and never quite the same from person to person.

The Four Joint Shapes

HipDeep ball-and-socket; trades range for stability. Bone can contact bone in flexion combined with adduction and internal rotation.
ShoulderShallow ball-and-socket; trades stability for range. The capsule and ligaments, not bone, do most of the work of stopping motion.
Knee / elbowHinge-like, mostly single-plane. Flexion end-range is often set by soft tissue meeting soft tissue.
Spine (intervertebral)Small gliding motions at each level sum across the column; the lumbar spine readily contributes motion a squat "borrows" from the hip.

Bony vs Soft-Tissue End-Range

  • Bony (hard): abrupt, immediate, does not change with stretching; bone meets bone, or close to it.
  • Soft-tissue (springy): gradual, springy give; capsule, ligament, and muscle drawing taut.
  • The same joint can be hard in one combined direction and soft in another, only a few degrees away.

Active vs Passive Range

  • Active: what a person's own muscles can move them through, unassisted.
  • Passive: what an external force (a partner, gravity, a strap) can move a joint through.
  • Active is normally smaller than passive; the gap explains why a lift can feel "blocked" while tissue still has room.
  • A single measured number never explains its own cause.

What Makes Ranges Individual

  • Acetabular depth and orientation vary from hip to hip.
  • Femoral neck angle and anteversion (forward twist) vary too.
  • These differences are set by skeletal shape, not by effort or stretching history.
  • A deeper socket or lower neck angle can mean an earlier, firm, painless bony stop.

Reading a Joint Honestly

  1. Active edge or passive edge? Strength/coordination ran out, or did tissue or bone actually stop the motion?
  2. If structural: soft (springy, gradual) or hard (abrupt, immediate)?
  3. Is this the joint's own arc, or is a neighboring segment quietly contributing or capping the motion?
  4. Is pain the limiter? If so, stop the mechanical questions and consider a referral.

What "Normal" Really Means

  • Reference ranges are population midpoints, built by averaging samples.
  • Even narrow, healthy samples show real spread between individuals.
  • A "normal" number is a starting point for observation, not a verdict on one specific joint.
  • The honest range is what this joint, on this task, in this body, actually permits.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how honest ranges of motion work, not a license to diagnose or treat. Pain, a sudden change in range, or catching and locking are signs that warrant professional evaluation. Work within your role, whatever it is.
