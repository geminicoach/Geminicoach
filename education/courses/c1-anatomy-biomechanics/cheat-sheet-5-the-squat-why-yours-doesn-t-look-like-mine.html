Chapter 5 : The Squat, Why Yours Doesn't Look Like Mine

One balance rule, three joints, and a torque bill that always has to be paid by somebody.

The One Rule Everything Else Follows From

Mid-foot constraintThe barbell's vertical line of gravity must stay over the mid-foot for the lift to stay balanced. Torso angle is downstream of this; bar path is the actual, non-negotiable rule.
Moment armThe horizontal distance between a joint's axis and the bar's line of gravity. External torque = bar load × moment arm.
Why the hole is hardestHip, knee, and ankle moment arms all grow through the descent and peak near the bottom, so torque peaks there too, even though the bar's weight never changes.
Torque is relocated, not deletedRestricting one joint's motion (e.g., knee travel) does not remove load from the body. It shifts that load to another joint, usually the hip and lower back.

Bar Position and Trunk Lean

  • High-bar: more upright torso, more knee flexion, more knee-dominant torque.
  • Low-bar: more forward lean, more hip flexion, more hip-dominant torque.
  • Lower bar placement forces more forward lean simply to keep that lower point stacked over the mid-foot.
  • Neither style is "more correct." They are two solutions to the same balance problem.

What the Knee-Barrier Study Showed

  • Blocking forward knee travel cut knee torque by roughly a fifth.
  • The same restriction raised hip torque roughly tenfold (Fry, Smith & Schilling, 2003).
  • Lesson: a cue that targets one joint in isolation ignores where the avoided load actually goes.

Why Segment Length Changes the Squat

  • Long femur relative to torso: hips travel back further, more forward lean needed.
  • Limited ankle dorsiflexion: caps forward knee travel, pushes hips back and torso down for a separate reason.
  • Hip socket orientation: varies by individual, cannot be trained into a different shape, changes which stance and depth feel open versus blocked.
  • Strength and flexibility often predict joint torques more strongly than segment-length ratios alone (Kim et al., 2021).

Forward Lean: Mechanics, Not Error

  • Forward lean is frequently the calculable result of bar height plus anthropometry, not a technical fault.
  • Coaching torso angle alone, without reference to bar path, mistakes a downstream effect for the actual rule.
  • The useful check: does the bar travel in a tight vertical line over the mid-foot, not what angle the torso happens to hold.
  • Correct form is a family of mechanically valid solutions, not one template copied onto every body.
Where education ends   This builds mechanical understanding of how load moves through the joints, not a license to diagnose or treat. New or worsening pain, joint instability, or other alarm signs are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
