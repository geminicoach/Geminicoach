Chapter 10: Reason Through an Unfamiliar Lift

Take the five-move framework from the chapter and use it to read a lift you have never been taught, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

The Scenario

Scenario · Sedona
Consider Sedona, who has completed this course and now spends time at a community gym helping members who ask for feedback. One evening, an experienced calisthenics athlete named Perri performs a movement Sedona has never watched up close: a Zercher squat, bar cradled in the crooks of the elbows and held tight against the ribs, with a stance narrower than any squat Sedona has coached and a torso that stays almost perfectly vertical throughout the descent. Someone nearby, watching, asks Sedona whether Perri is "doing it wrong" because it looks nothing like a back squat. Later in the same set, Perri mentions, almost in passing, that their forearms have started to ache a little right at the elbow crease by the last few reps. It settles within a few minutes and has been showing up consistently for a few weeks. Sedona wants to give a grounded answer to both questions: what does the geometry actually predict here, and does the forearm ache change anything about that answer?

Your Tasks

  1. Run the five-move framework on Perri's Zercher squat: identify the pattern, locate the gravity line, predict where peak demand concentrates, and name the muscles best positioned to meet it.
  2. Explain why Perri's narrow stance and near-vertical torso could be a mechanically coherent solution to holding the bar at the elbow crease, rather than a deviation from "real" squatting.
  3. Decide whether the repeated, mild, quickly-settling ache near the elbow crease reads as a mechanically expected variation or as a sign that warrants professional evaluation, and explain what additional information would move your judgment one way or the other.
  4. Give one practical, general-education response to the onlooker's question, and state clearly where general education ends and professional evaluation begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating mechanical understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Correctly runs the five moves: names the pattern as a squat and explains the bar's gravity line sitting close to the trunk at the elbow crease.
  • Explains how the elbow-crease bar position shortens the external moment arm relative to a back squat and mechanically accounts for the more upright torso.
  • Identifies the local, static holding demand at the elbow and forearm as distinct from the squat's hip and knee moments.
  • Reasons about the ache using its markers (reproducible, mild, quickly resolving, stable over weeks) and states what would change the call (worsening, sharp onset, numbness).
  • Gives a practical, general-education response and clearly states where general education ends and professional evaluation begins, without diagnosing.
