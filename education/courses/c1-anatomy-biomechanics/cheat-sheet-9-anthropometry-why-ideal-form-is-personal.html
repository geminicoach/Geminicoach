Chapter 9 : Anthropometry, Why Ideal Form Is Personal

One idea: proportions set the lever arms, and the lever arms set the form.

The One Idea Everything Else Follows From

AnthropometryThe measurement of segment lengths, breadths, and their ratios. It is an input to every torque equation, not an aesthetic category.
Moment armThe horizontal distance between a joint's axis and a load's line of gravity. External torque equals load multiplied by that distance.
The throughlineProportion sets the moment arms within a joint's hard limits. Set the moment arms and you have, in large part, predicted the form that minimizes demand.
Fault vs. adaptationA fault increases demand and buys nothing back. An adaptation looks unusual but is the cheapest available solution to a real geometric constraint.

The Squat: Femur-to-Torso Ratio

  • Long femur relative to torso: knees or hips must travel further from the bar, so forward trunk lean is the cheapest path to depth.
  • A wider, foot-rotated stance buys back range of motion for a long-femur learner (Demers et al., 2018; Park & Lim, 2021).
  • Trunk lean and tibia lean have opposite effects on the knee moment; judging either alone misreads which joint is under strain (Straub & Powers, 2024).
  • Stance interacts with the skeleton in it; there is no context-free correct stance (Schoenfeld, 2010).

The Deadlift: Spinal Moment Arm

  • Long torso: folding conventionally builds a large spinal moment arm, so sumo, which drops the hips between the legs, tends to shrink it (Cholewa et al., 2019).
  • Short torso: conventional already keeps the spinal moment arm manageable without the wide stance.
  • Hip moments did not differ significantly between sumo and conventional; sumo changes ankle and knee moments and total work, not hip demand alone (Escamilla et al., 2000).
  • Longer arms shorten the bar path and let the trunk start more upright (Lockie et al., 2018).

Pressing and Pulling: Arm-to-Torso Ratio

  • Long arms: longer bench bar path and larger shoulder and elbow moment arms; a wider grip shortens the path and reins the moment arm back in.
  • Short arms, thick torso: naturally short bar path, a pressing advantage that has nothing to do with effort.
  • The same long arms that cost a presser help a puller: a shorter deadlift bar path and a more upright start.
  • Grip width is a lever a learner can adjust once they know which moment arm they are managing.

Reading a Body, Responsibly

  • A cue that helps one set of proportions can increase demand for another; ask "right for whom," not "right or wrong."
  • Two very different causes, a long femur or limited ankle dorsiflexion, can produce the same visible forward lean. A photograph cannot tell them apart.
  • Variation is normal and expected. A pattern that reliably provokes discomfort is a sign that warrants professional evaluation, not proof that a learner's proportions are wrong.
Where education ends   This builds mechanical understanding of how proportion shapes technique, not a license to diagnose or treat. New or persistent pain, joint instability, or other alarm signs are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified professional. Work within your role, whatever it is.
