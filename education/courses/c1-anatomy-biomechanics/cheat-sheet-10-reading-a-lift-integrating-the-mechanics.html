Chapter 10 : Reading a Lift, Integrating the Mechanics

Five moves, one framework: pattern, gravity line, moment arm, muscle, proportion, run on anything with a bar attached to it.

The Five-Move Read

1. PatternClassify the movement (squat, hinge, press, pull) before measuring anything. This is the coarse filter that tells you which joints are the protagonists.
2. Gravity lineDrop a plumb line from the load's center of mass and note where it falls relative to each joint axis at the phase you are watching.
3. Moment arm peakTorque = force × the horizontal distance from that line to a joint. Peak demand, and the likely sticking point, sit wherever that distance is currently longest, not at a fixed spot.
4. Muscle matchAsk which muscles cross that joint with a favorable internal moment arm and a line of pull aligned to resist the torque you just found.
5. Proportion checkHold the prediction up against this body's honest ranges of motion and anthropometry. A read that ignores proportions predicts a lift no real person can perform.

Peak Demand Migrates

  • Hip thrust: hip extensor demand is highest early and decreases toward lockout as the femur approaches horizontal (Brazil et al., 2021).
  • Squat: trunk inclination and tibia inclination pull the knee moment in opposite directions; read either alone and you can reach the wrong conclusion (Straub & Powers, 2024).
  • Press: the sticking region is a geometric event, not a fixed point on the bar path. Grip width and segment length relocate it (Evangelista et al., 2025).
  • A deep squat can load hip and knee together near the bottom rather than trading demand cleanly between them.

What Predicts an Unusual-Looking Lift

  • Ankle dorsiflexion range of motion (ROM) and movement control predicted lumbopelvic flexion at squat depth; femur-to-tibia ratio did not (Berglund et al., 2024).
  • Relative strength and joint flexibility predicted torque distribution more strongly than segment-length ratio alone (Kim et al., 2021).
  • Stance width and trunk inclination together relocate demand between the sagittal and frontal planes at the hip (Hanen et al., 2025).
  • Folk attributions of unusual postures to bone length rarely survive contact with the data. Check flexibility and control first.

Variation or Warrants Evaluation

  • A feature is not concerning because it differs from a textbook photograph. It is concerning when it carries a marker outside the mechanical story.
  • Markers that shift a call toward professional evaluation: pain that tracks the movement, sudden loss of control, numbness or a give-way sensation, worsening one-sided asymmetry, a change cueing cannot resolve.
  • Screening tools can inform a read (reasonable reliability reported for the Functional Movement Screen) but cannot replace your judgment (Bonazza et al., 2017).

Transfer: Reading a Lift You Have Never Seen

  • Torque does not care whether you have personally coached this exercise before.
  • Running the same five moves, not memorizing more named exercises, is what makes the method reliable on something new.
  • The net joint moment is the same underlying quantity whether a laboratory measures it with instruments or you estimate it by eye. Your estimate is coarser, not a different kind of thing (Winter, 2009).
Where education ends   This builds the ability to read where demand concentrates in a lift and why, not a license to diagnose or treat. Pain that tracks a movement, sudden loss of control, numbness, worsening one-sided asymmetry, or other alarm signs are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
