Chapter 2: Reason Through a Real Limitation

Take the model of honest ranges of motion and use it to reason through a real limitation, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Bexley, 34
Consider Bexley, who has been attending the same twice-weekly movement class for about eight months. During the overhead reaching drill, Bexley's arm consistently stops well short of where the rest of the group ends up: elbow bent, ribs flaring forward, needing to lean back just to get the weight overhead at all. For the last two months, Bexley has followed a shoulder mobility routine most mornings, diligently, and the top position has not changed by more than a degree or two. There is no pain, no pinching, and no catching, just a firm, springy stop that arrives at almost exactly the same point every time. Bexley wants to know: "Am I just not stretching enough, or is something actually stopping me?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, using the chapter's model of end-ranges, what could physically be stopping Bexley's overhead reach, and describe how you would begin to tell whether it is closer to a bony or a soft-tissue limit.
  2. Explain why two months of consistent stretching producing almost no change is itself meaningful information, and what it suggests, or does not suggest, about the limiting structure.
  3. Identify at least one neighboring structure that could be contributing to, or masking, the true shoulder range, and explain how that changes what "the honest range" means for Bexley's shoulder specifically.
  4. Decide whether Bexley's situation reads as an ordinary mobility limit or as something that warrants professional evaluation, state clearly where general education ends, and give one practical, general-education next step.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language reasoning, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies anything that shows signs that warrant professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies the description (firm, springy, gradual) as more consistent with a soft-tissue limit than an abrupt bony one.
  • Recognizes that two months of unchanged stretching is itself informative, rather than simply recommending more of the same.
  • Identifies a neighboring structure (the shoulder blade, thoracic spine, or rib cage) that could be capping or masking the true glenohumeral range.
  • Explains that the honest range depends on the whole task, not a single fixed glenohumeral number.
  • Correctly frames the case as general education given the absence of pain, pinching, or catching, states where education ends, and does not diagnose.
