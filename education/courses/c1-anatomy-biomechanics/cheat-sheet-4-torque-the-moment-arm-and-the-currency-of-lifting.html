Chapter 4 : Torque, the Moment Arm, and the Currency of Lifting

Every hard part of a lift is a story about perpendicular distance. Learn to see it and you can read any lift.

The Core Equation

torque (τ) = force (F) × moment arm (d)

Force in newtons (N), moment arm in meters (m), torque in newton-meters (N·m). The moment arm is the perpendicular distance from the joint axis to the force's line of action, not the distance to the point where the force is applied. A force whose line of action passes straight through the axis has a moment arm of zero and produces zero torque, no matter how large it is.

Internal vs. External Torque

  • External torque : load's weight × external moment arm (horizontal distance from joint to the load's vertical line of gravity). This is the demand.
  • Internal torque : muscle tension × internal moment arm (perpendicular distance from joint to the muscle's line of pull). This is the supply.
  • Internal moment arms run in single centimeters; external moment arms run in tens of centimeters. That mismatch means muscles routinely produce forces several times the load itself.
  • A learner can change stance, grip, and bar path (external moment arm). Internal moment arms are set by skeletal geometry and do not change set to set.

Why Moment Arms Change Mid-Lift

  • Gravity always pulls straight down; the external moment arm is the horizontal distance from joint to load.
  • A limb hanging straight down has a near-zero external moment arm and feels near-weightless.
  • A limb at horizontal has its longest external moment arm; torque, and felt effort, peak there.
  • Internal moment arms also shift with joint angle, usually more gently, often peaking mid-range.

Strength Curves & Sticking Points

  • A strength curve plots a muscle group's maximal torque against joint angle; no muscle produces constant torque across its full range.
  • A sticking point is wherever the ratio of demand (external torque) to capacity (the strength curve) is worst.
  • A longstanding sticking point in a familiar lift is a mechanical feature, not automatic proof of a weak muscle.
  • Chains, bands, and cam pulleys reshape the demand curve to better match the strength curve.

Reading a Real Lift

  • "Keep the bar close" : a cue to minimize the external moment arm at the hip and lumbar spine, directly reducing torque demand there.
  • A wider, more upright stance shortens the moment arm at one joint while lengthening it at another; total demand does not vanish, it relocates.
  • A conventional deadlift's hardest inches are near the floor, where torso incline and bar position make both hip and lumbar external moment arms longest; both shrink fast once the bar clears the knees.
Where education ends   Mechanics explains why a lift feels the way it feels. It does not diagnose. A sticking point that has always been part of a lift's shape is a mechanical feature; a sensation that is new, sharp, localized, or different from a person's usual pattern is a sign that warrants evaluation by a qualified clinician, not a leverage problem to reason through. Work within your role, whatever it is.
