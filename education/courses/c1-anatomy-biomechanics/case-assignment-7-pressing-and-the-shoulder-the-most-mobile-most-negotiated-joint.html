Chapter 7: Reason Through a Real Sticking Point

Take the moment arm story from this chapter and use it to explain a real training question, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Tavish
Consider Tavish, who has pressed overhead consistently for about two years. On every heavy set, the bar stalls at the same point, just above forehead height, and it takes real, deliberate grinding to power through that patch. Once it clears, the bar speeds right back up on the way to lockout. There is no pain, no pinching, and nothing catches; it is just that one grinding patch, every single time. Tavish's training partner says the sticking point means the deltoid is "weak" and suggests dropping the overhead press for a few weeks of isolation work before going back to it heavy. Tavish is not convinced that is the right read on the problem and wants to understand what is actually happening mechanically before changing anything.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, why the bar slows at that specific point using the concept of the external moment arm, and describe what should happen to the moment arm once the bar clears that point.
  2. Evaluate the training partner's claim that the sticking point means Tavish's deltoid is "weak." Use what the electromyography (EMG) evidence described in the chapter actually shows about muscle activity during a sticking region.
  3. Identify what about Tavish's bar path could plausibly be contributing to a sticking region located that high in the lift, and suggest one concrete adjustment worth testing.
  4. State clearly what in this situation is ordinary mechanics to reason through, and what, if anything, would be a sign that warrants professional evaluation instead.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating mechanical understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing Tavish's shoulder. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything would warrant professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the sticking point as an external moment arm (geometry) problem, not a strength problem.
  • Correctly evaluates the "weak deltoid" claim against the electromyography (EMG) evidence that muscle activity stays high, even climbs, through the stall.
  • Identifies forward bar-path drift as a plausible mechanical contributor and proposes a specific, testable adjustment.
  • Correctly classifies this scenario (no pain, no pinching) as ordinary mechanics, not an automatic referral.
  • Names general signs that would warrant professional evaluation, without diagnosing Tavish.
