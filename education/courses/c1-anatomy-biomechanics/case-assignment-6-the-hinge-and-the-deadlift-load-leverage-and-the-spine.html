Take the moment-arm logic from the chapter and use it to explain a real pulling pattern, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Greer, 33
Consider Greer, who has been pulling conventional deadlifts for about a year, steadily adding weight. A training partner reviewing slow-motion video pointed out that Greer's lower back rounds slightly through the last few centimeters off the floor, but only on the heaviest single of a session, never on the warm-up sets. There is no pain, no numbness, and no loss of control mid-rep; it simply looks and feels different from the rest of the set. Greer was told to "just brace harder" to keep the back flat, tried it for a few weeks, and the same slight rounding still shows up occasionally on the heaviest pulls. Greer wants to know: is the rounding itself something to worry about, does bracing harder actually fix it, and should the bar path or setup change instead?

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, why Greer's lumbar spine is more likely to flex slightly on the heaviest, lowest-off-the-floor pulls than on lighter warm-up sets, using the moment arm and sine relationship from the chapter.
  2. Explain what bracing and a rise in intra-abdominal pressure can and cannot do about the rounding Greer describes.
  3. Decide whether the rounding, given the details in the scenario, reads as an expected mechanical pattern under heavy load or as something that needs professional evaluation, and explain how you know from the scenario itself.
  4. Give one practical, general-education suggestion related to bar path or setup, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating mechanical understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains why torque and the tendency toward lumbar flexion rise on the heaviest, most horizontal reps, using the moment arm and sine relationship.
  • Explains that bracing and intra-abdominal pressure change how load is transmitted through the spinal stack, not the horizontal distance between the bar and the spine.
  • Frames occasional, pain-free, load-dependent lumbar flexion as consistent with a known biomechanical pattern rather than automatically dangerous, without diagnosing Greer.
  • Offers a practical, general-education suggestion within scope, such as checking bar path and keeping the bar close rather than relying on bracing alone.
  • Identifies that pain, numbness, or a loss of controllable spinal position would warrant professional evaluation.
