Chapter 5: Reason Through a Real Squat

Take the moment-arm logic from the chapter and use it to explain a real movement pattern, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Ferran, 29
Consider Ferran, who has been back-squatting for about two years with steadily increasing loads. Someone reviewing his training video pointed out that his torso pitches forward noticeably at depth, his hips travel well behind his heels, and his knees barely reach past his toes at the bottom. He was told this "looked like a hip hinge with extra steps" and was advised to keep his chest more upright and stop his knees earlier on the way down. He tried both changes for several weeks. His bar path looked about the same on video, but he began noticing a dull ache low in his back by the end of his heavier sets, something he had not felt before. Ferran wants to know whether his original squat was actually a problem, and what his new discomfort means.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, why Ferran's original squat, with a forward-leaning torso and hips traveling back, could be a coherent solution to his own build rather than a technical error. Use the bar-over-mid-foot principle in your explanation.
  2. Explain what likely happened, mechanically, when Ferran was coached to keep his shins more vertical and his chest more upright. Where did the torque that used to sit at his knee most plausibly go?
  3. Decide what Ferran's new lower-back discomfort tells you and, just as importantly, what it does not tell you. Distinguish between explaining a movement pattern and evaluating a person's pain.
  4. Give one practical, general-education suggestion for how Ferran could check his own squat going forward, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating mechanical understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the mid-foot balance constraint and how it links bar position to trunk lean.
  • Connects Ferran's likely proportions (long femur relative to torso, and/or limited ankle dorsiflexion) to why his hips travel back and his torso leans forward.
  • Explains that restricting knee travel relocates torque rather than removing it, shifting demand toward the hip and lower back (Fry et al., 2003).
  • Distinguishes torso angle (anthropometry-dependent) from bar path over the mid-foot (the real, non-negotiable constraint).
  • Offers a practical, general-education suggestion within scope, such as checking the bar path rather than forcing a fixed torso angle.
  • Identifies that new or persistent discomfort warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
