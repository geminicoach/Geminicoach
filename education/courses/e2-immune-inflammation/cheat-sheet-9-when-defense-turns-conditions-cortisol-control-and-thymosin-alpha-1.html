Chapter 9 : When Defense Turns

Four ways balance fails, and two ways signaling can be nudged. Recognition, never diagnosis.

The Four Failure Modes

AutoimmunityToo much response, wrong target (self). Tolerance breakdown. Examples: rheumatoid arthritis, Hashimoto thyroiditis, type 1 diabetes, lupus.
Allergy / atopyToo much response, harmless target. Immunoglobulin E (IgE) sensitization then mast-cell elicitation. Can escalate to anaphylaxis.
Chronic low-grade inflammationRight idea, never resolves. Persistent triggers or deficient resolution; clinically silent, marker-detected.
ImmunodeficiencyToo little response. Infections too frequent, severe, persistent, or unusual. Primary (genetic) or secondary (acquired).

Tolerance & Autoimmunity

  • Central tolerance : autoreactive lymphocytes deleted/edited in thymus (T cells) or bone marrow (B cells).
  • Peripheral tolerance : regulatory T cells (Tregs) and checkpoints restrain what slips through.
  • Molecular mimicry : a microbial protein resembles self, provoking cross-reaction.
  • Meta-patterns : symmetric, morning stiffness improving with use, relapsing-remitting, family history.

Allergy: Two Phases

  • Sensitization : T-helper 2 (Th2) response, IgE class-switch, IgE loads onto mast cells via FcεRI. Silent.
  • Elicitation : re-exposure crosslinks IgE, mast cell degranulates, histamine released. Minutes.
  • Atopic march : eczema, rhinitis, food allergy, asthma; barrier defects let allergens in.
  • Hygiene hypothesis : early microbial diversity calibrates Th1/Th2/Treg balance.

Immunodeficiency

  • Primary immunodeficiency (PID) : genetic defect (severe combined immunodeficiency, or SCID; antibody deficiency; phagocyte defect; complement deficiency).
  • Secondary : acquired (viral infection, malnutrition, immunosuppressive drugs, splenectomy).
  • Signature : infections too frequent, too severe, too persistent, or too unusual.

Modulating the Signal (Mechanism Only)

  • Glucocorticoid receptor (GR) : represses nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) and activator protein 1 (AP-1) (transrepression); activates other genes (transactivation). Broad power, broad cost (insulin resistance, osteoporosis, muscle atrophy).
  • Thymosin alpha-1 : 28-amino-acid thymus-derived peptide; proposed to act via Toll-like receptors (TLR3/4/9/7) to support T-cell maturation. Broadly tolerated in trials; condition-specific efficacy remains preliminary; regulatory status is jurisdiction-dependent.
  • No doses, protocols, or sourcing are described here or anywhere in this course. Any decision about using either belongs to a clinician and patient together.
Where education ends   Recognizing a pattern is not diagnosing a condition. Every sign in this chapter is a prompt to seek professional evaluation, never a basis for self-diagnosis or self-treatment. Glucocorticoids and thymosin alpha-1 are described mechanistically only; no dose, protocol, or self-management guidance is given.
