Chapter 7 : The Athlete's Paradox

Exercise immunology is a masterclass in grading evidence honestly. This debate is not fully settled either way.

Two Timescales, Never Confuse Them

Acute boutWhat happens during and in the hours after one session. Neutrophils and natural killer (NK) cells surge into blood; some immune functions dip for roughly 3 to 24 hours after a prolonged hard bout.
Chronic trainingWhat happens to a body that trains regularly for months or years. Associated with lower resting inflammation and, epidemiologically, fewer reported respiratory infections than a sedentary lifestyle.

The J-Curve (Nieman, 1994)

  • Infection risk plotted against exercise workload traces a J shape.
  • Left arm (well supported) : moderate activity, lower risk than sedentary.
  • Right arm (contested) : very heavy exercise, proposed higher risk. Rests largely on self-reported symptoms and is confounded by sleep, fueling, and stress.

Open-Window Hypothesis: Three Pillars

  • Increased self-reported infections after heavy exertion. Reframing: infection data are thin; symptoms may be non-infectious airway irritation.
  • Falling salivary immunoglobulin A (IgA) concentration. Reframing: confounded by saliva flow rate, not secretion rate.
  • Sharp fall in circulating lymphocytes after exercise. Reframing: redistribution to peripheral tissues (heightened surveillance), not destruction.

This Debate Is Unsettled

  • Campbell and Turner (2018) argue the open-window evidence is weaker than long assumed.
  • Simpson et al. (2020) present a structured debate in which experts on both sides still disagree about whether exercise itself, or its confounders, drives illness in athletes.
  • Vaccination studies during the supposed "window" often show enhanced, not blunted, antibody responses.
  • Treat "exercise definitely suppresses immunity for 72 hours" and "the open window is fully debunked" as both overreaching the evidence.

What Both Camps Recommend

  • Progress training load gradually, avoid abrupt spikes.
  • Fuel around hard sessions (carbohydrate intake blunts the cortisol and catecholamine response).
  • Protect sleep, especially around competition.
  • Practice ordinary hygiene in high-density athlete environments.
  • Taper before major competition.
Where education ends   This builds literacy in exercise immunology and evidence-grading, not a license to diagnose overtraining syndrome or prescribe an individualized training plan. Persistent fatigue or unusual illness patterns that do not resolve with rest warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician or sports medicine professional.
