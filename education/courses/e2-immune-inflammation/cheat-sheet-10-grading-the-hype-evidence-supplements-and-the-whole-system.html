Chapter 10 : Grading the Hype

Mechanism is a hypothesis, not proof. Grade every claim, and tie every dose to the study that produced it.

The Evidence Hierarchy (weakest to strongest)

1. Mechanism/cell cultureReal for building causal stories; doses in a dish often exceed what a person can absorb.
2. Animal modelsAdds complexity but a poor stand-in for human metabolism and immune history.
3. Case reports/seriesGood for spotting rare harms; no comparison group, so cannot show benefit.
4. Cohort / case-controlLarge and cheap, but vulnerable to confounding (healthy-user bias).
5. Randomized controlled trial (RCT)Randomization balances confounders; blinding and placebo control bias.
6. Systematic review / meta-analysisPools RCTs for a precise combined estimate; only as good as the trials feeding it.

Five Grading Questions

  • 1. Measured : mechanism/surrogate or clinical outcome?
  • 2. Design : where on the hierarchy?
  • 3. Size/consistency : sample size, heterogeneity (I²)?
  • 4. Population/baseline : deficient vs. replete; general vs. extreme subgroup?
  • 5. Funding/conclusion : who paid, does the abstract match the data?

Statistics That Matter

  • Significance ≠ size : a tiny, trivial effect can still be "significant" with a huge sample.
  • Relative vs. absolute risk : "cuts risk in half" can mean 0.2% to 0.1%.
  • Number needed to treat (NNT) : how many must be treated for one to benefit.
  • Publication bias : positive results publish more readily than null ones.

The Cases, Graded (dose tied to study)

  • Vitamin D : Strong, but conditional, benefit concentrated in the deficient with daily/weekly dosing (Martineau et al., 2017). Confirm dose and target level with a clinician.
  • Vitamin C : Preliminary-to-none for general prophylaxis at ≥0.2 g/day; Contested-leaning-supported in physically extreme subgroups (Hemilä & Chalker, 2013). Confirm with a clinician.
  • Zinc : Contested, real duration effect but 95% heterogeneity and adverse events (Science et al., 2012). Confirm with a clinician.
  • Omega-3 : Strong in rheumatoid arthritis at >2 g/day EPA+DHA; Contested/Preliminary elsewhere (Calder, 2013). Confirm with a clinician (bleeding risk, drug interactions).
  • Antioxidant megadose : Below Preliminary, evidence points the other way; impairs training adaptation (Merry & Ristow, 2016).
  • Astragalus : Preliminary, no qualifying RCT evidence exists (Crawford et al., 2022).

Whole-System Map

  • Innate/barrier · Cytokine/resolution · Redox/hormesis · Stress/gut: four nodes any claim should be projected onto.
  • A stronger response is not always a better one: dysregulation and autoimmunity are diseases of doing too much (Chapter 9).
  • Sleep, exercise, and diet act on the whole map through many pathways at once; supplements, at their honest best, correct one specific deficiency in one pathway.
Where education ends   This builds evidence literacy, not a personal supplement plan. Every dose named here describes what a specific study used, never a recommendation. Confirm any supplement, dose, or regimen with a qualified clinician, especially if pregnant, immunocompromised, on medication, or managing a diagnosed condition.
