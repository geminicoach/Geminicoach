Chapter 5 : Rust and Repair

The same molecules that corrode us are also the ones that make us stronger. It is a dose story.

The Core Definition

Oxidative stressAn imbalance between oxidants and antioxidants in favor of the oxidants, disrupting redox signaling and control, or causing molecular damage (Sies, Berndt & Jones, 2017).
Reactive oxygen species (ROS)Oxygen-containing molecules more reactive than ordinary oxygen, for example superoxide, hydrogen peroxide, and the hydroxyl radical. Produced as a byproduct of the electron transport chain and deliberately by dedicated enzymes.
Oxidative eustressLow-level, physiological ROS signaling that regulates metabolism, growth, and defense.
Oxidative distressROS overwhelming the system, causing indiscriminate damage to lipids, proteins, and DNA.
Reductive stressToo much reducing (antioxidant) power, which silences the ROS signals the cell actually needs.

Endogenous Antioxidants

  • Superoxide dismutase (SOD) : converts superoxide into hydrogen peroxide. Catalytic.
  • Catalase : breaks hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen. Catalytic.
  • Glutathione peroxidase : neutralizes peroxides using glutathione. Catalytic.
  • Nrf2 : master transcription factor that switches on genes for antioxidant enzymes.
  • Thioredoxin / peroxiredoxin systems : parallel network that scavenges peroxide and relays redox signals.

Dietary Antioxidants

  • Vitamin C, vitamin E, and plant polyphenols are stoichiometric: each molecule is used up after one reaction, unlike catalytic enzymes.
  • Useful as part of an ordinary varied diet.
  • Megadosing does not simply "add more protection." It can blunt the ROS signal the body needs for adaptation.

Hormesis

  • A biphasic dose-response: too little stressor, no adaptation. A moderate dose, adaptive over-compensation, a stronger system. Too much, damage outruns repair.
  • Mitohormesis : exercise-induced ROS act as the trigger for beneficial adaptations, including new mitochondria and upregulated antioxidant defenses.
  • Ristow and colleagues found that high-dose antioxidant supplementation around training blunted some of these training adaptations.

Why Grading the Evidence Matters

  • ROS are hard to measure directly. Researchers usually measure downstream footprints (for example F2-isoprostanes, 8-OHdG).
  • Ask which biomarker was used, in which tissue, and whether a lab change actually translated into a health outcome.
  • Oxidative stress and inflammation reinforce each other in a feedback loop, which is why chronic low-grade inflammation is so corrosive.
Where education ends   This builds literacy in redox biology, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe supplement doses. Anyone with a diagnosed condition, medication, or specific dosing question should consult a qualified clinician or registered dietitian. Work within your role, whatever it is.
