Chapter 6 : The Weight We Carry

A hormone built to calm inflammation can, under chronic strain, end up feeding it.

The HPA Axis Cascade

HypothalamusReleases corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) in response to a perceived stressor.
Pituitary glandCRH triggers release of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) into the bloodstream.
Adrenal cortexACTH stimulates synthesis and release of cortisol, the final effector hormone.
Negative feedbackCortisol binds receptors in the hypothalamus, pituitary, and hippocampus to shut down further CRH and ACTH release once the threat passes.

Core Concepts

  • Allostasis : stability through change; regulatory outputs like cortisol and blood pressure vary on purpose to keep vital variables stable.
  • Allostatic load (McEwen) : cumulative wear and tear from repeated activation, or a response that fails to shut off.
  • Allostatic overload : the point where load exceeds capacity to adapt and the protective systems begin to injure.

Cortisol's Two Jobs

  • Metabolic mobilizer : raises blood glucose, breaks down protein and fat for fuel.
  • Immunomodulator : in the short term, suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IL-6) through the glucocorticoid receptor (GR).
  • GR resistance : chronic overexposure can make immune cells "harder of hearing" to cortisol, weakening its anti-inflammatory brake.

McEwen's Four Wear Patterns

  • Repeated hits : frequent stressors, each a normal response, without enough recovery time between them.
  • Failure to habituate : full-size cortisol surges keep firing even to a familiar, repeated stressor.
  • Failure to shut off : cortisol stays elevated after the stressor ends because the feedback brake is weak.
  • Inadequate response : cortisol fails to rise enough, so its restraint on inflammation is lost.

Measuring the Weight

  • Allostatic load index: a composite across neuroendocrine, immune/inflammatory, metabolic, and cardiovascular biomarkers.
  • A single cortisol sample is noisy. Diurnal slope (several samples across a day) and hair cortisol are more informative over time.
  • "Adrenal fatigue" is not a recognized clinical diagnosis.
Where education ends   This builds literacy in stress physiology, not a license to diagnose or treat. Persistent fatigue, mood changes, or a suspected hormonal issue are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
