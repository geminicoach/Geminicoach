Chapter 7: Reason Through a Contested Claim

Take the exercise immunology evidence from the chapter and grade a real claim honestly, without overstating either side.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Marcus, 29
Marcus is a dedicated amateur runner who just finished his fourth marathon. Like the last three times, he came down with a sore throat and runny nose about two days after the race. He tells his training group: "This is just science. My immune system gets suppressed for about 72 hours after a hard race, the open window is real, so honestly maybe I should stop racing marathons altogether if I want to stay healthy." A teammate pushes back, saying she read an article claiming the open window has been "totally debunked" and that Marcus should just train as hard as he wants. Marcus asks you: "Who is right?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain the J-curve model and identify which arm (moderate exercise or heavy exercise) is well supported by the evidence and which arm is contested.
  2. Walk through at least two of the three original pillars behind the open-window hypothesis, and explain how each has been reframed or questioned by more recent evidence.
  3. Directly address Marcus's and his teammate's claims. Is either one fully correct? State clearly how settled or unsettled the "hard exercise suppresses immunity for a fixed window" claim actually is, based on the evidence.
  4. Name at least one confounder that could explain Marcus's pattern of post-race illness, and give one or two practical, general-education suggestions that do not depend on resolving the debate either way.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and honest evidence-grading, not diagnosing Marcus or prescribing his training plan. This topic is genuinely debated among researchers. A responsible response holds that uncertainty honestly rather than declaring either side simply correct, and identifies that persistent or unusual illness patterns warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician or sports medicine professional.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the J-curve and correctly identifies that the moderate-exercise arm is well supported while the heavy-exercise arm is contested.
  • Walks through at least two of the three open-window pillars and their modern reframing.
  • Explicitly treats the "72-hour suppression" claim as an open, actively debated question, not as settled fact in either direction. This is the most heavily weighted item.
  • Names at least one plausible confounder (sleep, energy availability, stress, crowding) as an alternative explanation.
  • Offers practical, general-education guidance without overstating certainty, and does not diagnose or prescribe an individualized plan.
