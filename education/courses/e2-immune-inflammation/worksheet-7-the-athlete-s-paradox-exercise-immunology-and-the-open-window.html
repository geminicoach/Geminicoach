Part D · Trace a Scenario

A recreational marathon runner reports getting a cold within a few days after almost every big race, and has read online that this proves the open-window hypothesis is simply true.

  1. Name at least two confounders (other than "exercise suppressed my immune system") that could plausibly explain this pattern.
  2. What would a careful, evidence-graded response tell this runner about how settled versus contested the "hard exercise causes infection" claim actually is?

Part E · Reason About a Bottleneck

  1. The left arm of the J-curve (moderate exercise lowers infection risk relative to sedentary living) and the right arm (heavy exercise raises infection risk) are not equally well supported. Explain the asymmetry, and name one reason the right arm's evidence is weaker.
  2. List at least three practices that both the "yes, arduous exercise raises risk" camp and the "no, it is confounders" camp in the field's debate would still recommend to an athlete, and explain why both camps can agree on practice while disagreeing on mechanism.

Where education ends
This worksheet builds literacy in exercise immunology and in grading contested evidence, not a license to diagnose overtraining syndrome or prescribe individualized training plans. Persistent fatigue, unexplained illness, or performance decline that does not resolve with rest are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician or sports medicine professional. Work within your role, and resist the temptation to present a genuinely contested claim as settled fact in either direction.
