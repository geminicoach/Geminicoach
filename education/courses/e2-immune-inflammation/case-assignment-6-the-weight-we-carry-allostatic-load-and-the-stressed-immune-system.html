The Scenario

Scenario · Priya, 47
Priya has spent the last seven months as the primary caregiver for a parent recovering from a stroke, on top of a full-time job. She has had three minor colds in that period, more than she remembers having in years, and describes feeling "constantly rundown" and low in mood, though nothing has been diagnosed. A well-meaning friend told her this is "adrenal fatigue" and that she should buy a home cortisol test kit. Priya asks: "Is my stress actually doing something to my immune system, or is that just an excuse?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanistically, how a prolonged, unresolved stressor like Priya's caregiving situation could plausibly relate to more frequent minor illness (focus on allostatic load and the HPA axis).
  2. Explain how chronic stress can turn cortisol's normal anti-inflammatory effect into something that fails to restrain inflammation, naming at least one mechanism from the chapter.
  3. Evaluate the friend's "adrenal fatigue" claim and the home cortisol test kit suggestion. Is this a well-supported clinical diagnosis, and what does a single cortisol reading actually tell you?
  4. Give one practical, general-education suggestion, and state clearly where general education ends and where Priya's persistent symptoms warrant a qualified clinician.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. "Adrenal fatigue" is not a recognized clinical diagnosis. Persistent fatigue, mood changes, or suspected hormonal issues belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains allostatic load and allostasis, and connects it to Priya's prolonged, unresolved stressor.
  • Explains that chronic stress can weaken cortisol's anti-inflammatory brake, naming a mechanism such as glucocorticoid receptor resistance or a flattened diurnal rhythm.
  • Distinguishes this from acute stress, which mobilizes rather than dysregulates immunity.
  • Offers a practical, general-education suggestion within scope (for example, protecting sleep or building recovery time).
  • Correctly notes that "adrenal fatigue" is not an established diagnosis and that a single cortisol reading is a noisy, limited measure, and does not diagnose Priya.
