Chapter 8 : Sleep, Gut, and Plate

Three everyday levers, sleep, the gut microbiome, and nutrition, all converging on the same NF-κB-linked circuitry.

Sleep as an Active Immune State

Slow-wave sleep (SWS)Early-night, low-cortisol window; favors T-cell trafficking to lymph nodes, IL-12/APC-T-helper interaction, and consolidation of immune memory.
Sleep & vaccinesSleep after immunization strengthens and prolongs antibody response; sleep restriction blunts it (Spiegel et al., 2002).
NK cellsNatural killer cell activity drops within a day of partial sleep loss, reversing with recovery sleep (Irwin et al., 1996).
Sleep loss as stressorRaises evening cortisol and catecholamines; activates nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB); raises interleukin-6 (IL-6), tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α), and C-reactive protein (CRP): low-grade chronic inflammation (Irwin, 2019).
Circadian clocksImmune cells keep their own clocks (BMAL1, CLOCK); misalignment (shift work, jet lag) carries inflammatory risk distinct from short sleep alone.

The Gut & the Barrier

  • GALT : gut-associated lymphoid tissue trains tolerance (regulatory T cells, or Tregs) and targeted defense (T-helper 17 cells, or Th17).
  • Barrier layers : mucus, antimicrobial peptides, secretory immunoglobulin A (IgA), tight junctions, lamina propria.
  • Dysbiosis : lower diversity, fewer butyrate producers, pathobiont overgrowth; from antibiotics, low fiber, stress, infection.
  • LPS / TLR4 : leaked lipopolysaccharide (LPS) engages Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), driving NF-κB-linked systemic inflammation (metabolic endotoxemia).

Microbial Metabolites

  • SCFAs / butyrate : short-chain fatty acids from fermented fiber (microbiota-accessible carbohydrates, or MACs); fuel colonocytes, seal the barrier.
  • GPR43 / GPR109A : SCFA receptors that dampen inflammatory activation.
  • HDAC inhibition : butyrate inhibits histone deacetylase (HDAC) enzymes, opening the Foxp3 gene and promoting Treg differentiation.
  • Fermented foods : raised diversity and lowered 19 inflammatory markers in a 17-week randomized controlled trial (RCT) (Wastyk et al., 2021).

Nutrition, Graded Honestly

  • Protein/amino acids : fuel clonal expansion; deficiency is a leading cause of secondary immunodeficiency.
  • Zinc : cofactor for T-cell maturation and neutrophil killing; deficiency impairs immunity broadly.
  • Vitamin D : VDR on immune cells; induces cathelicidin; supports Treg differentiation.
  • Vitamin C : concentrated in neutrophils; supports chemotaxis, killing, and collagen/barrier integrity.
  • Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) : precursors for specialized pro-resolving mediators that actively end inflammation.

Where the Levers Meet

  • Sleep loss → cortisol/catecholamine shift → NF-κB.
  • Leaky gut → LPS → TLR4 → NF-κB.
  • Low fiber/omega-3 → starved resolution and redox programs that would otherwise switch NF-κB off.
  • Deficiency impairs immunity, but megadoses are not automatically better; any dose should be confirmed with a clinician.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of sleep, gut, and nutrition as immune modulators, not a license to diagnose, prescribe, or self-manage a supplement regimen. Persistent digestive symptoms, unexplained weight loss, blood, or recurrent unusual infections warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Any specific supplement dose is a conversation for a clinician, not a self-directed decision.
