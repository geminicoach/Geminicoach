Part C · Reason About Acute Versus Chronic Stress

  1. Under normal, acute conditions, is cortisol pro-inflammatory or anti-inflammatory? Name the two gene-regulation mechanisms (transactivation and transrepression) by which activated glucocorticoid receptor (GR) suppresses inflammatory genes.
  2. Explain how chronic, unresolved stress can turn that same anti-inflammatory hormone into a contributor to low-grade inflammation. Name at least one mechanism (for example, GR resistance or a shift in receptor balance).
  3. McEwen described several ways the stress-response system wears out. Name and briefly describe two of his four patterns (repeated hits, failure to habituate, failure to shut off, inadequate response).

Part D · Trace a Scenario

A person spends eight months as the primary caregiver for a family member with a serious illness, sleeping poorly and rarely getting a full day off.

  1. Using McEwen's patterns, which type of allostatic load wear is this scenario most likely to illustrate, and why?
  2. Describe what you would expect to happen to this person's cortisol rhythm over the eight months (for example, to the diurnal slope), and to their inflammatory markers.
  3. Why might this person notice they get sick more often, even though cortisol is normally an anti-inflammatory hormone?

Part E · Reason About a Bottleneck

  1. Two people report equal subjective "stress." One shows a sharp cortisol spike that returns to baseline within an hour; the other shows a modest spike that stays elevated all afternoon. Using McEwen's framework, which person is likely accumulating more allostatic load, and why does the shape of the curve matter more than the peak?
  2. Why can a single blood or saliva cortisol sample be a misleading way to assess someone's chronic stress state?

Where education ends
This worksheet builds literacy in stress physiology, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how the system works, and treat persistent fatigue, mood changes, or a suspected hormonal imbalance as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
