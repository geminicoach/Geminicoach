Chapter 1 : The Wall and the First Responders

A layered fortress: barriers stop most invaders, and fast, non-specific first responders handle the rest.

The Three Barrier Types

PhysicalSkin (stratum corneum, continuously shed), mucosal epithelia sealed by tight junctions, mucus and the mucociliary escalator in the airway.
ChemicalStomach acid (pH about 1.5 to 2), lysozyme in tears and saliva, skin's acidic fatty-acid film, antimicrobial peptides such as defensins and cathelicidins.
MicrobialThe commensal microbiota occupies attachment sites and nutrients, crowding out pathogens through competitive exclusion.

Cellular First Responders

  • Neutrophil : first and most numerous phagocyte on scene; short-lived.
  • Macrophage : longer-lived phagocyte; engulfs debris and signals other cells.
  • Dendritic cell : captures antigen and carries it to the lymph node, starting the handoff to adaptive immunity.
  • Natural killer (NK) cell : kills abnormal or infected host cells on sight, no prior exposure needed.

Recognition Without Memory

  • Pattern recognition receptor (PRR) : germline-encoded receptor, fixed at birth, same in every cell of a type.
  • Pathogen-associated molecular pattern (PAMP) : a shape shared by whole classes of microbes, for example bacterial lipopolysaccharide or flagellin.
  • Damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) : a signal from damaged self, released when a cell dies violently; explains why sterile injuries still inflame.
  • Complement system : blood proteins that punch holes in microbial membranes and tag microbes for destruction.

Defining Traits of Innate Immunity

  • Fast : responds within minutes to hours of a breach.
  • Non-specific : recognizes broad categories of threat, not individual pathogens.
  • No memory : the tenth exposure looks the same as the first.
  • Sets the stage for, and helps switch on, the adaptive immune system in Chapter 2.

Anatomy of a Breach

  • Barrier fails (splinter, cut, inhaled virus).
  • PRRs detect PAMPs or DAMPs at the site.
  • Local blood vessels widen and become leaky.
  • Neutrophils, then macrophages, are recruited to the site.
  • Dendritic cells carry antigen onward to start the adaptive response.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the body's first line of defense works, not a license to diagnose or treat. Spreading redness, red streaking, fever, pus, or worsening pain are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
