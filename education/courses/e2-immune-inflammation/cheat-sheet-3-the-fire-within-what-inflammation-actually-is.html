Chapter 3 : The Fire Within

Five classic signs, one master switch, and a family of cytokines that turn a local injury into a body-wide signal.

The Five Classic Signs

Rubor (redness)Increased blood flow to the area from local vasodilation.
Calor (warmth)Increased blood flow carrying core body heat to the surface.
Tumor (swelling)Increased vascular permeability lets protein-rich fluid leak into tissue.
Dolor (pain)Mediator-sensitized nerve endings, direct nociceptor activation, and mechanical pressure from swelling.
Functio laesa (loss of function)An emergent, partly adaptive cost that limits use of the injured area while it heals.

The Master Switch

  • Nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) : the transcription factor that switches on genes for TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, and adhesion molecules.
  • Both pattern recognition receptors and cytokine receptors converge on releasing NF-κB so it can enter the nucleus and act.

The Three Core Cytokines

  • TNF-α : fast, forceful, activates blood vessel walls.
  • IL-1 : innate immunity's signature messenger; matured by the inflammasome; an endogenous pyrogen (causes fever).
  • IL-6 : the systemic broadcaster; drives the liver's acute-phase response.

Reading the Fire from Blood

  • C-reactive protein (CRP) : a liver-made, IL-6-driven acute-phase protein.
  • Sensitive but non-specific: reports the magnitude of inflammation, not its location or cause.
  • A predictive and monitoring marker, not a stand-alone diagnosis.

Vascular and Cellular Events

  • Small blood vessels widen (vasodilation) and become leaky (increased permeability).
  • These two changes account for three of the five classic signs.
  • Leukocytes are recruited to the site via activated endothelial adhesion molecules.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how inflammation works, not a license to diagnose or treat. An unexplained elevated inflammatory marker, a joint that is red, hot, and painful for days without injury, or a fever that persists are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
