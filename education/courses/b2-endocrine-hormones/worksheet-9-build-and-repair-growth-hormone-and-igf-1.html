Part B · The Two-Step Relay

Growth hormone does much of its building without touching the target tissue directly.

  1. Describe the command-to-effector relay in order: where growth hormone goes, what it tells the liver to make, and what that downstream hormone does at the tissues.
  2. Contrast the tempo of the two hormones: growth hormone is bursty and short-lived, IGF-1 is steady and long-lived. Explain why IGF-1 is often the more useful thing to measure in a clinical setting.

Part C · Pulsatile Release and Sleep

Sleep is the headline lever. Reason from the mechanism, not from a slogan.

  1. The largest, most reproducible growth hormone pulse of the day is tied to a specific event, not to the clock on the wall. Name the event, and explain what Takahashi and colleagues observed in 1968 when a subject's sleep onset was delayed.
  2. Name three things that blunt the first-cycle night pulse, and give the mechanism for each in a phrase (why alcohol, why a large late high-sugar meal, why short or late sleep).
  3. The lifespan study tracked slow-wave sleep and growth hormone together across ages 16 to 83. Explain the direction of the arrow the authors were careful about: which is the lever and which is downstream, and why that ordering matters for advice.

Part D · Correct the Marketing

A friend forwards you an ad for an oral "HGH anti-aging" capsule stacked with arginine, marketed as a "GH releaser" that "reverses aging and rebuilds muscle," bundled with a home kit that measures one morning blood value. Take it apart using the chapter.

  1. Name the bait-and-switch at the heart of the pitch: what true, narrow physiological fact does it start from, and what much larger claim does it quietly swap in? Explain why the second does not follow from the first.
  2. Explain, from the measurement problem, why the bundled home kit's single blood value cannot honestly show that anything "raised your growth hormone."
  3. The ad borrows its credibility from a famous 1990 study and from the word "anti-aging." Summarize what the well-controlled follow-up evidence actually found about growth hormone in healthy older adults, including the trade-off between the small body-composition change and the adverse effects.
  4. Write two or three sentences you could actually say to that friend: correct the claim, explain the real lever they already own, and do not tip into diagnosing or prescribing anything.

Part E · Reason About the Whole System

Use the mechanisms, not the hype.

  1. Reduced growth hormone signaling extends lifespan in several mouse strains, and a Laron population showed strikingly low rates of cancer and diabetes. What does this counterintuitive finding tell you about treating "more growth hormone" as obviously good?
  2. Exercise also produces a real growth hormone pulse, yet the chapter says to protect sleep first if you can only protect one. Given that the exercise pulse still routes much of its benefit through IGF-1, explain in your own words why sleep wins the tiebreaker.

Where education ends
This worksheet builds endocrine literacy, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Growth hormone deficiency and acromegaly are real conditions, but they are diagnosed with dynamic testing and imaging by qualified clinicians, never from a chapter, a checklist, a wearable, or a home kit. This course never endorses the use of growth hormone, peptides, or "releaser" supplements. Treat the signs named here as context, not a screening tool, and treat anything that looks clinical as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
