Chapter 10 : Crossed Wires

The endocrine system is a web, not a stack of independent pipes. Most cross-talk changes sensitivity or availability, not raw levels.

Three Routes of Cross-Talk

ProductionHormone A changes how much of hormone B is made or released. The least common route in this chapter.
AvailabilityHormone A changes how much of hormone B is free to act, usually via a binding protein. Total unchanged, usable amount moves.
SensitivityHormone A changes how strongly the target tissue responds to hormone B, at the receptor or downstream. The workhorse of the chapter.
  • Rule of thumb: the level can look normal while the effect changes. Sensitivity cross-talk never shows up if you only measure levels.

The Documented Edges

  • Cortisol → insulin : lowers insulin sensitivity in muscle, liver, and fat; pancreas compensates with more insulin (Beaupere et al., 2021).
  • Thyroid → adrenergic : raises tissue responsiveness to adrenaline-type signals; sets metabolic tempo (Mullur et al., 2014).
  • Insulin → SHBG : suppresses the liver's SHBG, shifting the free fractions of testosterone and estradiol (Wallace et al., 2013).
  • Ghrelin → GH : stimulates growth-hormone release.

Leptin: The Master Coupler

  • A fuel-gauge signal from fat tissue, in proportion to stored energy, not a simple hunger hormone.
  • Acts permissively on the reproductive, thyroid, growth, and adrenal axes: grants permission, does not give orders.
  • When energy availability drops, leptin falls and several axes quiet together (Khan et al., 2012; Mantzoros et al., 2024).
  • Replacing leptin during a fast reverses the reproductive-axis drop, proving cause, not coincidence (Chou et al., 2011).

SHBG & the Free Fraction

  • Total testosterone counts every molecule, most of it bound and inactive.
  • Only the free (plus loosely albumin-bound) portion reaches receptors.
  • Insulin lowers SHBG, tilting the split toward free, active hormone.
  • SHBG tracks insulin sensitivity better than total testosterone does.

Reading Coupled Symptoms

  • Several outputs drifting the same way at the same time point upstream to a shared input.
  • When multiple outputs move together, suspect one shared cause before assuming several independent failures.
  • Short sleep lowers leptin (~15.5%) and raises ghrelin (~14.9%), independent of BMI (Taheri et al., 2004).
  • Stress, sleep, and energy availability are inputs to shared hubs, not narrow single-system levers.
Where education ends   This builds systems literacy, the ability to reason about knock-on effects, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe. A persistent, coupled, or worsening pattern (a changed cycle, ongoing fatigue, low drive, poor recovery, or other alarm signs) warrants evaluation by a qualified clinician working with measurements over time. Conditions are diagnosed by a clinician, never through this course. Work within your role, whatever it is.
