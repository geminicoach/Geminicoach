Chapter 4 : The Metabolic Thermostat

One dial sets the tempo of the whole engine: three floors, a storage form activated in the tissues, read with two numbers.

The Three Floors (HPT Axis)

1. HypothalamusReleases TRH over a short private line (portal system) so a low concentration still arrives loud and clear at the pituitary.
2. PituitaryReleases TSH into the general blood as a broadcast signal; only the thyroid is built to listen. TSH is the pituitary's opinion of the gland.
3. Thyroid glandButterfly gland at the windpipe; when TSH knocks it makes hormone, mostly the storage form T4. Needs dietary iodine to build it.
FeedbackT3 and T4 feed back negatively on both upper floors, holding a stable, individual setpoint. The TSH-to-hormone curve is steep, so TSH is a sensitive early gauge.

T4, T3 & Conversion

  • T4 (thyroxine) : mostly storage/transport form; four iodines; circulates ~100x T3.
  • T3 (triiodothyronine) : the active form; three iodines; enters cells and changes gene expression.
  • Deiodinase D1, D2 : tissue enzymes that remove one iodine to activate T4 into T3, on site.
  • D3 : inactivates hormone into inert reverse T3.
  • Selenium : built into the deiodinases (selenoproteins); a real reason nutrition touches thyroid.

One Dial, Many Systems

  • Thyroid hormone is an amine that acts like a steroid: T3 binds nuclear receptors (TRα, TRβ) and changes which genes are read, so effects build over days and weeks.
  • Basal metabolic rate : sets each cell's idle speed.
  • Heat production : more internal heat, so temperature tracks thyroid status.
  • Adrenergic sensitivity : potentiates adrenaline, linking thyroid to heart rate.

Too Slow vs Too Fast

  • Hypothyroid : fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, slowed everything. Leading cause where iodine is sufficient: Hashimoto's.
  • Hyperthyroid : anxiety, palpitations, weight loss, heat intolerance, tremor, loose stools. Leading cause: Graves' disease.
  • Near mirror images. Overactive carries red flags (heart strain); a racing or irregular heartbeat warrants evaluation.

Read Two Numbers

  • High TSH, low output : gland struggling = primary hypothyroidism.
  • Low TSH, low output : upper floor failing = central hypothyroidism.
  • Low TSH, high output : gland overproducing = primary hyperthyroidism.
  • Conversion is a tunable dial: under-eating and illness lower T3 while TSH and T4 stay put. A lab range is a population band, not a personal target; timing, rhythm, and pregnancy change interpretation.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the axis works, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. You can read the two-number logic and know what TSH, T4, and T3 represent, but interpreting anyone's labs, naming a condition, or recommending a supplement or medication is clinical care. "Thyroid support" supplements and self-diagnosis are not benign shortcuts, and blind iodine can harm where intake is sufficient. Persistent, worsening, or function-interfering symptoms, or a racing or irregular heartbeat, warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
