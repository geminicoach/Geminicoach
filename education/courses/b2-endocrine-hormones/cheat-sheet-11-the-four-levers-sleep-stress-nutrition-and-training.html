Chapter 11 : The Four Levers

Four inputs move most of everyday hormone status: sleep, stress, nutrition, training. Three are free, all four are mechanisms you already know.

The Four Levers

1. SleepBroadest reach: drives the slow-wave GH pulse, lowers leptin / raises ghrelin when short, degrades insulin sensitivity, raises evening cortisol, drops testosterone about 10-15% after one week of short sleep.
2. StressAcute cortisol mobilizes fuel and is adaptive; chronic HPA over-activation suppresses the reproductive, growth, and thyroid axes. The variable that flips it is duration and recovery.
3. NutritionRaw material (cholesterol backbone; enough total energy) and signal (protein incretins/mTOR, fat type/PPAR, fiber and glucose). Think sources, balance, quantity.
4. TrainingDurable payoff is chronic, not the acute spike: better insulin sensitivity, body composition, upregulated androgen receptors, a graceful cortisol-androgen balance.

Sleep : One Behavior, Five Readouts

  • GH pulse : rides the first slow-wave stage; front-loaded, dose-responsive to depth.
  • Appetite : short sleep lowers leptin, raises ghrelin; a false energy alarm.
  • Insulin : degraded without adequate beta-cell compensation, even in the young and healthy.
  • Cortisol : evening end of the curve flattens and elevates, working against deep sleep.
  • Testosterone : down 10-15% after one week of short sleep in healthy young men.

Stress : Acute vs Chronic

  • Cascade : CRH to ACTH to cortisol, with negative feedback that turns it off.
  • Acute : fuel mobilization, sharper focus, clean return to baseline. Adaptive.
  • Chronic : dysregulated rhythm, cross-axis suppression, metabolic strain. Allostatic load.
  • "Adrenal fatigue" : not how the axis fails; glands do not run out like a battery.

Nutrition : Two Jobs

  • Raw material : cholesterol is the backbone of every steroid hormone; under-eating (low energy availability) furloughs the reproductive axis.
  • Signal : protein drives incretins (GLP-1, GIP) and mTOR; fat type shifts PPAR signaling; fiber slows glucose.
  • Dose matters : any claim that never mentions quantity is not really about mechanism.
  • Retire : "clean vs dirty." Use sources, balance, quantity.

Training : Two Timescales

  • Acute : real but modest testosterone / GH spike for 15-30 min; not the proven driver of growth.
  • Chronic : androgen-receptor upregulation, insulin sensitivity, body composition, recovery balance.
  • Trained differ : same levers, personal baseline; variation is in how much, not whether biology applies.
  • Recovery : the half of training where adaptation happens; loops back to sleep and stress.

One Machine, Not Four

  • Insulin sensitivity is moved by all four levers at once, so no single hack replaces the fundamentals.
  • The systems are wired in loops, not a row; a nudge to one propagates to the others.
  • Improvements compound through the same connections that let problems compound. That is what "consistency" means biologically.

Two Ways to Get It Wrong

  • Fatalism : "hormones are destiny." Treats a dynamic system with inputs as a sealed box.
  • Hack-chasing : one supplement overrides the fundamentals. It cannot; they are the substrate.
  • The marketing tell : a real acute effect stretched into a chronic promise, or a deficiency fix sold to non-deficient people.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of the modifiable inputs to a healthy system, not a license to diagnose or treat. Persistent insomnia or suspected sleep apnea, clinical anxiety or depression, disordered eating or low energy availability, disrupted cycles, unexplained weight change, or stalled progress that persists or worsens are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Supplements and lifestyle protocols are not treatments; conditions are diagnosed by clinicians with tests. Work within your role, whatever it is.
