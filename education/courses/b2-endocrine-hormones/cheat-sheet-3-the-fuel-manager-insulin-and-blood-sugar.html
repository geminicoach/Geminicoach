Chapter 3 : The Fuel Manager

One tight glucose budget, two opposing hormones, and one misunderstood word: resistance.

The Core Loop

SetpointBlood glucose is defended in a narrow 4 to 6 mM band, about a teaspoon and a half of sugar total. Too low starves the brain fast; too high grinds down vessels and nerves over years.
InsulinMade by beta cells; the "store it and use it" signal of the fed state. Lowers glucose.
GlucagonMade by alpha cells next door; the "release it" signal of the fasted state. Raises glucose (glycogenolysis, then gluconeogenesis).
The ratioThe liver reads the insulin-to-glucagon ratio, not either hormone alone. High ratio = store; low ratio = release. It asks "which signal is winning."

How Insulin Works

  • Peptide hormone; docks on the surface insulin receptor.
  • Fires IRS, then the PI3K-AKT pathway.
  • AKT translocates GLUT4 to the surface of muscle and fat, so glucose floods in.
  • Storage tissues gate their doors behind insulin; brain and liver keep theirs open (GLUT1/2/3).
  • Also drives glycogen storage and tells the liver to stop dumping new glucose.

Where the Fuel Goes

  • Glycogen : finite, fast tank. Liver (~100 g, public, exports glucose); muscle (~300 to 500 g, private, feeds only itself).
  • Fat : large, slow, dense reserve; used when tanks are full and demand is low.
  • Destination depends on tank fullness and demand, not on food being "clean" or "dirty."
  • "Carbs make you fat" is a slogan; carbs mostly spare dietary fat rather than transmute into fat.

Sensitivity vs Resistance

  • Sensitivity : cells respond fully to a quiet insulin signal.
  • Resistance : the signal has to shout; the pancreas secretes more to do the same work.
  • Resistance is not silence and not low insulin. It is usually high insulin and deaf cells.
  • Defect lives mostly downstream of the receptor: ectopic lipid and diacylglycerol jam the IRS relay.
  • Muscle resistance may be the earliest defect, present years before glucose ever rises.

The Four Levers

  • Movement : contraction recruits GLUT4 via AMPK, no insulin needed; a post-exercise sensitivity boost lasts hours.
  • Muscle mass : the largest glucose sink; a bigger, used tank.
  • Sleep : short sleep measurably drops insulin sensitivity, even in the healthy.
  • Meal composition : sources and balance (fiber, protein, unsaturated fat), not virtue and sin.
  • Overnutrition is the through-line behind resistance.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of the loop, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Diabetes and prediabetes are diagnosed by clinicians using validated tests interpreted over time, never by a home device or a single lab value. Classic high-glucose signs (excessive thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, blurred vision, persistent fatigue) warrant prompt evaluation by a qualified clinician. Teach the mechanism, point to the clinician, and work within your role, whatever it is.
