Chapter 9 : Build and Repair

One command hormone (GH), one downstream effector (IGF-1), and one reliable lever you already own: deep sleep.

The Axis at a Glance

SomatotrophsAnterior-pituitary cells that release growth hormone (GH) into the blood in short bursts.
GHRHHypothalamic accelerator: tells the pituitary to release GH.
SomatostatinHypothalamic brake: tells the pituitary to hold back.
GH pulseGH is cleared in minutes, so it must shout in bursts, not trickle. A single random draw tells you almost nothing.
IGF-1 feedbackThe downstream effector loops back to brain and pituitary to dampen further GH release. The axis says "enough" as reliably as "go."

The Two-Step Relay

  • GH : bursty, short-lived command from the pituitary.
  • Liver : main factory; GH tells it to make IGF-1.
  • IGF-1 : steady, long-lived effector that does much of the building and repair, including most protein synthesis.
  • Foreman analogy: GH issues the order, IGF-1 is the crew that swings the hammer. No crew, little result.
  • Laron syndrome : GH high, receptor broken, IGF-1 low, stature very short. The command was never the point; the effector was.

Sleep: The Headline Lever

  • The largest, most reproducible daily pulse fires shortly after sleep onset, tied to the first slow-wave episode.
  • In adult men roughly 70% of sleep-related GH pulses coincide with deep sleep.
  • The pulse tracks the descent into deep sleep, not the clock. Delay sleep, delay the pulse.
  • Sleep is the lever; the hormone is downstream. Better slow-wave sleep tracks with more GH.

What Blunts the Night Pulse

  • Short / late sleep : cuts into the slow-wave-rich first third of the night.
  • Alcohol : speeds sleep onset but suppresses slow-wave sleep and fragments the back half.
  • Large late high-sugar meal : the insulin and glucose spike blunts the GH response.
  • Fix requires protecting the first cycle, not a supplement.

What GH Genuinely Does

  • Lipolytic : releases stored fat for fuel.
  • Anti-insulin on glucose : preserves blood sugar; excess reduces insulin sensitivity.
  • Anabolic on protein : builds and retains lean tissue, mostly via IGF-1.
  • Supports bone, tendon, and connective-tissue turnover. Overview-level background machinery, not a standalone "build button."

The Reputation vs the Evidence

  • The 1990 study that launched "anti-aging GH" was tiny (12 men) and measured body composition, not health.
  • Controlled trials in healthy elderly: small body-composition change, no proven strength or function gain, real adverse effects (swelling, joint pain, glucose trouble). Not recommended for anti-aging.
  • Reduced GH signaling extends lifespan in mice; a Laron population showed low cancer and diabetes. More GH is not simply "younger."

Spot the Bait-and-Switch

  • True, narrow fact: intravenous arginine can stimulate GH in a clinical test.
  • Unsupported swap: "so this capsule rebuilds your body."
  • Oral amino-acid "releasers" show weak, inconsistent effects, and can even blunt the exercise pulse.
  • A pulsatile, minutes-lived hormone cannot be honestly captured by one blood value. Ask three questions: healthy people or deficient? a real outcome or just a hormone level? who benefits if you believe it?

Exercise: The Secondary Lever

  • Resistance training reliably triggers a GH pulse; endurance needs intensity above the lactate threshold for about ten minutes.
  • Much of the training benefit still routes through IGF-1, not GH directly.
  • Training and sleep reinforce each other. If you can only protect one, protect sleep: it holds the largest, most consistent pulse.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the system works, not a license to diagnose or treat. Growth hormone deficiency and acromegaly are real conditions, but they are diagnosed with dynamic testing and imaging by qualified clinicians, never from a chapter, a checklist, a wearable, or a home kit. This course never endorses the use of growth hormone, peptides, or "releaser" supplements. Treat the signs named here as context, not a screening tool, and hand anything clinical to the people trained and licensed to answer it. Work within your role, whatever it is.
