Chapter 8 : Hunger's Two Voices

Two hormones, two timescales, one decision-maker: ghrelin says "now," leptin says "how much we have," and the hypothalamus blends them into felt hunger.

The Two Voices

GhrelinMade mainly in the stomach lining. The only known appetite-raising (orexigenic) circulating hormone. Rises 1 to 2 hours before habitual meals (the pre-prandial surge), peaks near mealtime, falls when nutrients arrive. Answers "should I start a meal now?" on a scale of hours.
LeptinMade by fat cells (adipocytes) in proportion to fat mass. Signals "how much fuel is in storage." High when stores are ample, low in deficit. A slow, smoothed signal on a scale of weeks. Defends much harder against loss than against gain.

Where They Land

  • Hypothalamus : integrates both signals; sits by a leaky part of the blood-brain barrier so blood-borne hormones reach it directly.
  • Arcuate nucleus : the integration hub.
  • NPY / AgRP neurons : drive appetite up.
  • POMC / CART neurons : drive appetite down.
  • Leptin and insulin inhibit the "eat" neurons and stimulate the "stop" neurons; ghrelin does the reverse.

What Shuts Ghrelin Off

  • Nutrients arriving in the small intestine, not just stomach stretch.
  • Protein and carbohydrate suppress it more durably than fat.
  • A mostly-fat meal can leave hunger returning sooner.
  • Practical target: roughly 25 to 30 g protein per meal for a meaningful effect on appetite (Leidy et al., 2015).
  • Ghrelin is partly trained by habit: regular meal timing tames it.

Leptin Resistance

  • In obesity leptin is high (hyperleptinemia) but the brain's response is muffled: loud but unheard.
  • Mirrors insulin resistance: more hormone, less listening, a louder signal needed for the old effect.
  • Cellular brakes: SOCS-3 and Tyr985 dampen LRb / STAT3 signaling; barrier transport also drops.
  • Cause vs effect is unsettled: reliably observed, not proven to be the sole cause (Myers et al., 2010).

The Setpoint & Dieting

  • Same architecture as insulin / glucagon: sensor plus opposing pair plus defended setpoint.
  • Defense is asymmetric: harder against loss than gain.
  • A year after diet-induced loss, leptin was still low, ghrelin still high, hunger still elevated (Sumithran et al., 2011).
  • That is the biology behind weight regain, not lost motivation.

Sleep, Stress & the Lean Case

  • Short sleep lowers leptin, raises ghrelin and hunger (Spiegel et al., 2004; Taheri et al., 2004, n=1,024).
  • Stress pulls the same levers and travels with poor sleep.
  • Low energy availability drops leptin, which throttles thyroid and reproductive function. Less fat is not always better.

Myths, Corrected

  • "Leptin supplement" : the problem is resistance, not deficiency; injected leptin barely helps ordinary obesity (Heymsfield et al., 1999); oral leptin is a protein and gets digested.
  • Leptin works only where it is truly absent (congenital deficiency; Farooqi et al., 1999).
  • "Fix your hormones to lose weight" : a reversed arrow. The hormonal picture is largely downstream of the state.
  • Honest levers: sleep, meal rhythm, protein, stress.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how appetite is regulated, not a license to diagnose or treat. Eating disorders and metabolic conditions are diagnosed and treated by clinicians. Relentless hunger that overwhelms daily life, a pattern of restriction, bingeing or purging, distress around eating that will not lift, unexplained weight change, or disrupted cycles are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
