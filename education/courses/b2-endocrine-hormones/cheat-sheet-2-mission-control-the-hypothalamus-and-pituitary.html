Chapter 2 : Mission Control

One template runs the whole endocrine system: brain to pituitary to gland, with the output looping back to quiet its own command.

The Generic Axis (the keystone)

1. HypothalamusThe general. Reads stress, temperature, light, hunger; sends a small releasing hormone down the portal system.
2. Anterior pituitaryThe lieutenant. Takes dictation and amplifies it, releasing a larger tropic hormone to the target gland.
3. Target glandThe factory. Receives the order and pours out the final hormone in even larger quantity.
4. Final hormoneThe real product. Does the job in your tissues, then loops back up to quiet the chain (negative feedback).

The Three Axes

  • HPA (stress) : CRH → ACTH → adrenal cortex → cortisol.
  • HPT (thyroid) : TRH → TSH → thyroid → thyroid hormone.
  • HPG (reproduction) : GnRH → LH / FSH → gonads → sex steroids.
  • Same three boxes, same feedback arrow, different labels.

Two Pituitaries in One

  • Posterior : displaced brain; nerve endings release oxytocin and vasopressin directly.
  • Anterior : a true gland, controlled remotely through the hypophyseal portal blood system.
  • Portal system : a private, tiny blood supply; a whisper arrives as a loud, concentrated order.

Accelerators & Brakes

  • Releasing hormones : CRH, TRH, GnRH, GHRH; the "go" signals.
  • Inhibiting hormones : somatostatin (brakes growth hormone); dopamine (brakes prolactin).
  • Prolactin defaults to "on"; cut the stalk and it rises because the brake is gone.
  • Direction of control beats memorizing names.

Which Floor Is Broken?

  • One number is a symptom; two numbers are a location.
  • Low output + HIGH upstream : the gland (primary problem).
  • Low output + LOW upstream : the pituitary or hypothalamus (secondary or tertiary).
  • Axes pulse on daily and hourly rhythms, so a lone reading can mislead.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the command chain works, not a license to diagnose or treat. The tidy two-reading pattern is far messier in real life, where medications, timing, illness, and lab variation blur the picture. Persistent or severe symptoms, a clear change from someone's usual pattern, or a real result to interpret warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
