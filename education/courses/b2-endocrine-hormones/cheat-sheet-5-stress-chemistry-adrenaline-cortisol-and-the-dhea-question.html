Chapter 5 : Stress Chemistry

One pair of glands, three products: a fast amine, a slow steroid, and an upstream precursor. Fast, slow, upstream.

Two Axes, Two Speeds

SAM axis (fast)Nerve to adrenal medulla to adrenaline (epinephrine) straight into the blood. Skips the pituitary. Acts in seconds.
HPA axis (slow)Hypothalamus (CRH) to pituitary (ACTH) to adrenal cortex to cortisol. Full Chapter 2 template. Minutes to hours.
FeedbackThe HPA axis is a closed loop with negative feedback, so it is self-correcting, like a thermostat, not a reservoir that empties.

Adrenaline : the fast amine

  • What : a catecholamine built from tyrosine (PNMT makes it).
  • Why fast : binds surface receptors; flips switches already installed. No gene changes.
  • Does : raises heart rate, opens airways, mobilizes fuel (glycogenolysis + free fatty acids).
  • Link : a counter-regulator to insulin, "spend, do not store."

Cortisol : the slow steroid

  • What : a glucocorticoid, built from cholesterol in the cortex.
  • Why slow : enters the cell, changes gene transcription; effect builds and lingers.
  • Rhythm : peaks near waking, bottoms out late evening (driven by the SCN clock).
  • Does : gluconeogenesis, tempers inflammation, supports cognition and blood pressure. Not a toxin.

Cortisol x Insulin

  • Cortisol raises glucose and blunts insulin signaling (nudges toward insulin resistance).
  • Chronically high insulin can push the HPA axis toward "functional hypercortisolism."
  • Together they favor visceral fat and metabolic syndrome. Direction is elevated / dysregulated, not deficient.

DHEA : the precursor

  • What : most abundant steroid in the body; made in the zona reticularis.
  • Role : upstream raw material, weak on its own; converted downstream to testosterone and estradiol.
  • Intracrinology : much conversion happens locally in tissues, so a blood level is supply, not the finished product.
  • Adrenopause : falls ~1-2%/yr to ~10-20% of peak in old age, while cortisol stays relatively preserved. Reallocation, not failure.

Load, Not "Fatigue"

  • Allostatic load : cumulative wear from a stress response that stays switched on and never resets. Real and evidence-based.
  • "Adrenal fatigue" : failed a systematic review; not recognized by any endocrinology society. Not a diagnosis.
  • Real disease : Addison's disease / adrenal insufficiency (too little cortisol) and Cushing's syndrome (too much) are clinician-diagnosed.
  • Testing trap : late-night salivary cortisol screens suspected Cushing's under a clinician; a four-point wellness "curve" is not a diagnosis and captures mostly rhythm noise.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the stress axis works, not a license to diagnose or treat. "Adrenal fatigue" is not a medical diagnosis; real adrenal disease (Addison's disease, Cushing's syndrome) is diagnosed by clinicians with specific endocrine testing such as an ACTH stimulation test, never through a wellness saliva panel. A DHEA capsule is a hormone precursor, not a benign vitamin. Persistent fatigue, unexplained weight change, low blood pressure, salt craving, or darkening skin warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
