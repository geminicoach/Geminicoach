Part C · Why You Cannot Move One Hormone in Isolation

  1. The chapter argues the endocrine system is "a web, not a stack of independent pipes." Explain, in your own words, why that structure means you cannot change one hormone without tugging others. Use one concrete edge from the chapter to make the point.
  2. Someone reads that they should "just raise testosterone." Explain why the number they actually care about (the free, active fraction) is governed by a metabolic signal, and why targeting the sex hormone directly misses the plot.
  3. Give one reason the body is built this way on purpose. What coordination problem does cross-talk solve that a set of sealed, independent pipes could not?

Part D · Trace the Stress Ripple

A person goes through a prolonged stretch of high, sustained stress chemistry. Trace the ripple through the web. At each step, name the mechanism, not just the direction.

  1. Cortisol stays elevated. What does it do to insulin sensitivity, and in which three tissues?
  2. How does the pancreas respond to that shift, and why is it a smart short-term move but a costly long-term one?
  3. Higher insulin now reaches the sex-hormone system. Through which liver protein, and what happens to the free fractions of testosterone and estradiol?
  4. Stress often arrives alongside poor sleep. Using the leptin-and-ghrelin finding from the chapter, explain how short sleep pushes appetite signals in an appetite-promoting direction.
  5. Pull it together: name three systems that have now moved from a single upstream input, and state the rule of thumb the chapter gives for reading several outputs that drift together.

Part E · The Master Coupler

Leptin is the node that touches the most edges. Reason about what happens when energy availability drops far enough.

  1. Explain what it means to say leptin acts permissively on several axes, rather than driving them. What is the difference between granting permission and giving an order?
  2. When leptin falls sharply, which axes quiet down, and why do they move together rather than one at a time?
  3. The chapter describes an experiment where replacement leptin was given during a fast. What did that design let researchers separate, and what does it tell you about cause versus coincidence?

Where education ends
This worksheet builds systems literacy, the ability to reason about knock-on effects, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how the wires connect, and treat any persistent, coupled, or significant pattern (a changed cycle, ongoing fatigue, low drive, poor recovery, or other alarm signs) as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician who works with measurements over time. There is no prescribing guidance in this course. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
