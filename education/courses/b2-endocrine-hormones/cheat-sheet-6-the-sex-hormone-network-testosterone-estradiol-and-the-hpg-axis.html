Chapter 6 : The Sex Hormone Network

One axis, three tiers, one assembly line: testosterone and estradiol live in every body, differing in ratio, not presence.

The HPG Axis (three tiers)

Tier 1 · HypothalamusReleases GnRH in pulses, set by upstream kisspeptin (KNDy) neurons. Pulse frequency and amplitude shape the whole axis; a flat, continuous signal suppresses it.
Tier 2 · PituitaryResponds to GnRH pulses by releasing the gonadotropins LH and FSH into the blood.
Tier 3 · GonadsMake the final hormones: testosterone, estradiol, and progesterone.
FeedbackThe final hormones feed back on the hypothalamus and pituitary by negative feedback, holding levels stable.

The Biosynthesis Tree

  • Cholesterol : the raw material every steroid is built from.
  • CYP17A1 : routes a cell toward the sex-steroid branch.
  • Aromatase (CYP19A1) : converts testosterone into estradiol.
  • 5-alpha-reductase : converts testosterone into DHT (more potent androgen).
  • DHEA : adrenal precursor feeding tissue-level (intracrine) synthesis; declines with age.

Estradiol, in Every Body

  • Estradiol (E2) : the primary, most potent estrogen; the same molecule in both sexes.
  • Essential in males for bone, brain, metabolism, mood, and fertility.
  • Made from testosterone by aromatase, so it tracks with testosterone.
  • The difference between sexes is concentration and ratio, not presence.

Why They Act Slowly

  • As steroids, these act mainly through the genomic nuclear-receptor pathway.
  • That means effects build slowly and fade slowly, the powerful long-timescale mode.
  • Any promise of a fast hormonal fix is fighting the mechanism.

When the Axis Drifts

  • Low testosterone (men) : diagnosed only with symptoms plus reliably low morning labs, not symptoms alone.
  • PCOS : sits at the crossroads of the reproductive axis and metabolism; insulin resistance often contributes.
  • Menopause transition : the ovarian phase of the axis winds down over time.
  • Low-output axis : very low energy availability or very high load can slow the KNDy clock and suppress the axis.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the axis works, not a license to diagnose, prescribe, or adjust therapy. Low testosterone, PCOS, and the menopause transition are diagnosed by clinicians with labs and clinical judgment, and their therapies (testosterone therapy, PCOS medications, hormone therapy) are prescribed and monitored by clinicians. A period that stops without explanation, or any question about starting, stopping, or changing hormone therapy, warrants a qualified clinician. This course never directs anyone to begin or end hormone therapy. Work within your role, whatever it is.
