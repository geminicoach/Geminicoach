Part F · Scope of Practice

Knowing the line between general education and clinical care is a core competency, not a formality, and it matters more here than almost anywhere else in this course. Hormone conditions such as low testosterone, PCOS, and the menopause transition are diagnosed by clinicians using labs and clinical judgment, and their therapies (testosterone therapy, medications for PCOS, hormone therapy for menopause) are prescribed and monitored by clinicians. This course explains the mechanism; it never diagnoses, and it never directs anyone to start or stop hormone therapy. Where education ends
This worksheet builds hormone literacy, not a license to diagnose, prescribe, or adjust therapy, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how the axis works, and treat anything that looks clinical (a period that stops without explanation, symptoms severe enough to disrupt daily life, or a question about starting, stopping, or changing hormone therapy) as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician. This course never directs anyone to begin or end hormone therapy. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose or self-prescribe.
