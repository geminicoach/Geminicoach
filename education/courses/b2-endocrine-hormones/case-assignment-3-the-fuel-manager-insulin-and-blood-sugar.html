Case Assignment · B2 Endocrine System

Chapter 3: Reason Through a Real Worry

Take the fuel-management loop and use it to explain a real worry, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Rosa, 44
Consider Rosa, who works a desk job, sits most of the day, and by her own description "does not eat that badly." Over the last few years she has gradually put on weight around the middle, feels a slump most afternoons, and gets hungry again soon after lunch even when lunch was large. At a routine check-up her doctor said her fasting glucose was "on the higher side of normal" and that they would keep an eye on it. Rosa went looking online, found a wall of contradictory advice, and came away convinced her problem is "too much insulin" and that the fix is to fear carbohydrates and eat almost none. She has not been given any diagnosis. She is trying to reverse-engineer her own biology from headlines, and she asks you: "Do I have prediabetes? Should I cut out all carbs?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what Rosa's cluster of signs suggests using the concepts from the chapter (compensated insulin resistance, the insulin-to-glucagon loop, and why a "normal" glucose can hide high insulin).
  2. Identify the error in Rosa's "insulin is the enemy, so eliminate carbohydrate" reasoning, and explain what the real problem is (deaf cells, not too much signal) and what the durable levers actually are.
  3. Address Rosa's direct question about whether she has prediabetes. State clearly why a single "higher side of normal" number is not a diagnosis, and whose job that determination is.
  4. Give one or two general-education suggestions within scope, and mark plainly where general education ends and clinical care begins for Rosa.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, keeps the levers as general health guidance, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diabetes and prediabetes are diagnosed by a qualified clinician using validated tests interpreted over time, never by a single lab value, a home device, or this course. Work within your role, whatever it is.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the compensated-resistance mechanism and why a "normal" glucose can hide high insulin.
  • Identifies and corrects the "insulin is the enemy" / eliminate-carbohydrate error (the problem is deaf cells, not too much signal).
  • Names the levers that restore sensitivity (movement, muscle, sleep, meal composition) as general health guidance, with at least one mechanism.
  • Frames a single borderline lab value as not a diagnosis and routes the diagnosis to a clinician.
  • Stays role-neutral, does not diagnose, and identifies what warrants professional evaluation.
