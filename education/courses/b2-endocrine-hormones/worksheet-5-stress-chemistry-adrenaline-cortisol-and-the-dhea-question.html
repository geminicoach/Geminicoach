Part C · Cortisol and Its Daily Rhythm

  2. Describe the normal 24-hour cortisol rhythm: roughly when it peaks, when it bottoms out, and what drives it. Why is a high 8 a.m. reading not automatically a sign of stress?

Part D · DHEA, the Precursor

  3. DHEA declines steadily with age (adrenopause) while cortisol stays relatively preserved. Explain why this reflects the gland reallocating its product mix rather than "wearing out."

Where education ends
This worksheet builds stress-physiology literacy, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. You can explain how the two axes work, read the cortisol rhythm, and recognize why "adrenal fatigue" is not a diagnosis, which lets you follow a conversation, ask a sharper question, and spot when something warrants medical evaluation. It does not let you interpret anyone's cortisol or DHEA results, name a condition, or recommend starting or stopping a supplement or hormone. Real adrenal disease, such as Addison's disease and Cushing's syndrome, is diagnosed and managed by qualified clinicians using specific endocrine testing (for example an ACTH stimulation test), never through this course or a wellness saliva panel. "Adrenal fatigue" is not a medical diagnosis. A DHEA capsule is a hormone precursor with downstream effects, not a benign vitamin. Treat persistent fatigue, unexplained weight change, low blood pressure, salt craving, or darkening skin as signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
