Chapter 3 : When You Eat: Signal Versus Noise

Timing is real, mostly small, and badly oversold. Fix the total and the protein first; the clock is trim, not foundation.

The Hierarchy of Impact (top to bottom)

1. Total energySurplus, deficit, or maintenance decides the direction you move. No timing trick overrides it.
2. Total proteinFat-free mass gains climb up to roughly 1.62 g/kg/day, then plateau (Morton et al., 2017). More past that buys nothing for hypertrophy.
3. DistributionSpreading protein across about four meals is a real, second-order refinement with 12-week outcome evidence.
4. Peri-workout timingThe anabolic window. Real mechanism, small and soft outcome for most people; matters most for fasted or twice-a-day training.
5. Supplement timingCreatine at 8 a.m. or 8 p.m. For body composition, this is a decorative vase. Bottom for a reason.

Mechanism vs Outcome

  • Mechanism : a real biological process (insulin moves nutrients; training sensitizes muscle).
  • Outcome : what happens to a person over weeks and months.
  • A spike you can measure in hours is not a difference you can see in a year. The body is buffered and forgiving.
  • Most bad advice lives in the gap: a real mechanism inflated into an outcome it cannot support.

The Anabolic Window

  • Muscle stays sensitized to protein for at least 24 hours after training (Kerksick et al., 2017), not 30 minutes.
  • A pre-workout meal is still releasing amino acids, so post-workout urgency is low for fed lifters.
  • The window tightens in one case: fasted training. Then get 20 to 40 g protein in reasonably soon.
  • Barn door, not a keyhole (Aragon & Schoenfeld, 2013).

Per-Meal Ceiling & Distribution

  • Myofibrillar synthesis rises to about 20 g whey, then flattens (Witard et al., 2014). Surplus is oxidized.
  • Practical target: ~0.4 g/kg per meal, at least four meals, to clear 1.6 g/kg/day (Schoenfeld & Aragon, 2018). Soft shoulder, not a hard wall.
  • Areta (2013): 20 g every 3 h beat both tiny and giant doses (mechanism).
  • Yasuda (2020): even distribution beat dinner-skewed over 12 weeks at equal totals (outcome).

Carbohydrate: The Real Clock

  • Glycogen must be physically rebuilt; the clock matters only on a short turnaround.
  • Under ~4 h to the next hard bout: refeed fast, ~1.2 g/kg/h, high-glycemic (Kerksick et al., 2017).
  • A full day to recover: total carbohydrate refills the tank; the early window buys speed, not size (Parkin et al., 1997).
  • Built for two-a-days and tournaments, not for "my next workout is tomorrow."

Signal vs Noise

  • Meal frequency : no independent effect on body composition once total is fixed; an appetite tool, not a metabolic lever (Schoenfeld et al., 2015).
  • Breakfast : the correlation-causation trap. Trials show it adds calories and does not cause weight loss (Sievert et al., 2019).
  • Pre-sleep protein : slow casein raises overnight synthesis; added muscle over 12 weeks, partly by extending distribution (Res 2012; Snijders 2015).
  • Chrononutrition : eating earlier improved metabolic markers independent of weight in a small trial (Sutton et al., 2018). Promising frontier, not a settled rule.

Spend Attention From the Top Down

  • Fix the total. Hit the protein (about 1.6 g/kg/day). Spread it across roughly four meals.
  • Eat around training in a way that feels good; for most people the biggest honest payoff of timing is comfort, sleep, and adherence.
  • Only then, if chasing the last one percent, worry about the window. Most people invert this and polish the trim while the foundation cracks.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how timing and total intake work, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe. A managed medical condition (such as diabetes), signs of disordered eating, unexplained weight loss, or distress around food are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified professional. Work within your role, whatever it is.
