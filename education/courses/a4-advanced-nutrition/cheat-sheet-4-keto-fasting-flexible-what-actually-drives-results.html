Chapter 4 : Keto, Fasting, Flexible: What Actually Drives Results

Three diet tribes, one shared mechanism. A diet is a delivery system for an energy balance and a protein intake; adherence quietly decides the rest.

The Three Layers Inside Every Diet

1. RulesWhat the box tells you to do: no carbs, no eating before noon, track your macros. Where the branding lives.
2. MechanismWhat the rules are supposed to trigger: ketosis, autophagy, a fasted fat-burning state. Where the drama lives.
3. OutcomeWhat you actually care about: fat lost, muscle kept, energy, health, a body you can maintain. Follow the chain here every time.
  • The branding lives on layer one and makes promises about layer three by way of loud claims about layer two. Ask whether the mechanism genuinely moves the outcome, or is just an interesting footnote the marketing inflated.

The Shared Mechanism

  • A diet is a delivery system for two things: an energy balance and a protein intake.
  • Energy balance : a sustained deficit produces fat loss. No diet escapes this accounting.
  • Protein : protects muscle in a deficit and drives fullness. The second non-negotiable.
  • Match calories and protein, and keto, fasting, and flexible converge. Feeling different is not working differently.

Intermittent Fasting

  • An umbrella for eating windows: time-restricted eating, alternate-day, 5:2. Common feature is structural, not metabolic.
  • The "metabolic switch" is real fuel-timing, not fuel-loss; over 24 hours the books still close on energy balance.
  • Autophagy : real biology, unestablished human dose; not a proven fat-loss lever for skipping breakfast.
  • Cioffi et al. (2018): 11 RCTs, weighted mean difference -0.61 kg, p 0.87. No meaningful advantage over continuous restriction.

Ketogenic Diets

  • The first-week whoosh is glycogen and water (about 3 g water per 1 g glycogen), not fat. It rushes back when carbs return.
  • Can blunt the appetite rise that comes with dieting (Gibson et al., 2014), but the effect is small in absolute terms.
  • Helps mainly people who do not love carb-rich foods; a preference tool, not metabolic magic.
  • Real, replicated performance cost : impaired economy and a 2.3% slower 10,000 m in elite race walkers, no rebound after restoring carbs (Burke et al., 2020).

Low-Carb vs Low-Fat: DIETFITS

  • Gardner et al. (2018): 609 adults randomized to healthy low-fat or healthy low-carb for 12 months, both well supported.
  • Weight change -5.3 kg (low-fat) vs -6.0 kg (low-carb). Between-group difference 0.7 kg (CI -0.2 to 1.6). A wash.
  • Neither a "low-carb genotype" nor baseline insulin secretion predicted who did better.
  • The carb wars, settled by a large, fair, year-long trial.

Flexible Dieting

  • A framework, not a diet: hit your targets with any foods. No food is "clean" or "dirty," only more or less useful in context.
  • A meta-framework that can contain keto or fasting; its cost is that it asks you to track, at least for a while.
  • Conlin et al. (2021): flexible vs rigid at matched macros; equal fat loss, better post-diet fat-free mass and psychology.
  • Flexible control links to lower BMI, less bingeing, better mood; rigid control to the opposite (Westenhoefer 1999; Stewart 2002).

Adherence: The Deciding Variable

  • Once calories and protein are matched, fat-loss differences between diets shrink toward nothing.
  • Alhassan et al. (2008), A TO Z study: adherence predicted 12-month weight loss better than which diet group you were in.
  • The diet you follow beats the "better" diet you abandon, every time.
  • "Is keto better than fasting" is the wrong question. Ask: better for what, for whom, and can they stick to it.

The Four Levers That Actually Separate Diets

  • Food preference : the plan that bans what you love will lose eventually.
  • Satiety : how full the approach keeps you on the calories it allows.
  • Social and schedule fit : a fixed eating window assumes a fixed day; rotating shifts and real life break rigid plans.
  • Sustainability : the structure you can repeat long enough to matter is the one that works. Fit and consistency, not the name on the plan, decide where you land.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how diets deliver an energy balance and protein, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe. A managed medical condition (such as diabetes), signs of disordered eating, unexplained weight loss, or distress around food are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified professional. Work within your role, whatever it is.
