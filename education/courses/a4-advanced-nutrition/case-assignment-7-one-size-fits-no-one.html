Chapter 7: Calibrate to the Person, Not the Marketing

Take the method of individualization and use it to separate the real story from the sales pitch, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Sil, 41
Consider Sil, who lifts three times a week and lost about eighteen pounds two years ago on a moderate, higher-protein deficit. Nine of those pounds have crept back, and Sil is frustrated. A coworker raved about a DNA-diet kit and a continuous glucose monitor, so Sil bought both. The kit flagged an FTO "risk" variant and an MTHFR variant, and recommended a low-carbohydrate plan plus a stack of methylated supplements. The monitor shows blood sugar "spiking" after oatmeal, bananas, and rice, foods Sil has eaten happily for years. Sil arrives convinced that genetics explain the regain, that carbohydrates now have to go, and that the MTHFR result needs treating. A recent physical, including fasting glucose and HbA1c, came back entirely normal. Sil asks: "So my body is just different now, right? Should I cut the carbs and get on the supplements?"

Your Tasks

  1. Walk Sil's situation down the leverage list from the chapter and explain what most likely drove the regain. Say why that answer sits at the top of the variation hierarchy (body size, intake, activity) rather than in Sil's DNA.
  2. Interpret the DNA-diet results responsibly. Explain what the FTO and MTHFR findings do and do not justify, using the evidence, and why normal bloodwork matters to that reading.
  3. Address the glucose monitor. Explain why Sil's post-meal rises can be genuinely individual and still not be a problem, and why cutting the oatmeal, banana, and rice could do more harm than good here.
  4. Give Sil a practical plan built on principles first, then measured feedback, then real life. Then state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins, including what you can and cannot say about the medical test results and the supplements.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing or prescribing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Interpreting medical or genetic test results, treating a suspected condition, and starting or dosing any supplement or medication belong to a qualified clinician, never to this course or to a coaching role.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Locates the regain at the top of the leverage list: over two years, intake crept past expenditure and the deficit became maintenance and then a small surplus. The plan stopped being run, not working.
  • Reads the FTO result correctly (associated with weight but does not predict which diet works; small effect counteracted by ordinary diet and activity) and the MTHFR result correctly (modest effect, largely managed by adequate dietary folate), so neither justifies low-carb or a boutique supplement stack on normal bloodwork.
  • Handles the monitor honestly: post-meal responses are individual, but a normal rise on normal bloodwork is a healthy pancreas working, and flattening curves has little evidence of benefit for a metabolically healthy person; cutting enjoyed staples would attack adherence.
  • Offers a practical, in-scope plan: a modest protein-adequate deficit from foods Sil will sustain, the three weekly lifting sessions protected, individualized through measured feedback and Sil's real life rather than a genome report or a wearable.
  • Stays in scope: frames the problem as physiology and behavior, refers interpretation of medical test results and any medication or supplement decision to a clinician, and does not diagnose or prescribe.
