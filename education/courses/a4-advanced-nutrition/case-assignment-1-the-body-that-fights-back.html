Chapter 1: Reason Through a Stubborn Stall

Take the adaptive-system model of metabolism and use it to explain a real complaint, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Omar, 39
Consider Omar, who set out to get lean fast. He cut hard, dropping to roughly 1500 calories a day, and added an hour of intense cardio most mornings. The first six weeks were thrilling: nearly 7 kilograms gone. Then it stalled. By week twelve the scale had not moved in eighteen days despite the same low intake and the same brutal cardio. He is cold all the time, his hands are restless, he wakes up ravenous, and his lifts in the gym have collapsed. He is convinced his metabolism is broken. His instinct is to cut calories further and add a second cardio session, and he asks whether that is the right move.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what is happening to Omar's expenditure using the four components of TDEE and the idea of adaptive thermogenesis (name which signals are moving and in which direction).
  2. Account for why the depth and duration of his approach, a steep deficit plus heavy cardio plus low protein sustained for three months, would provoke an especially large defense.
  3. Before accepting "broken metabolism," rule out the impostors: say what you would check and over what window, rather than trusting an eighteen-day flat patch.
  4. Recommend a strategy and explain why it runs opposite to his instinct. State clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Signs of disordered eating, missing periods, or other medical symptoms, and any medication or hormonal questions, belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the stall mechanically through the four TDEE components (RMR beyond prediction, NEAT collapse, TEF shrinking with intake, more efficient movement) and names adaptive thermogenesis.
  • Connects the depth and duration of the deficit to a larger defense, driven by a sharper leptin decline and lower sympathetic and thyroid output.
  • Rules out the three impostors (water and glycogen, untracked intake creep, reduced NEAT) and reads the four-week trend before diagnosing true adaptation.
  • Proposes a sound strategy: ease the alarm with a diet break toward maintenance, then a moderate deficit, adequate protein, and resistance training, rather than cutting harder.
  • Identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
