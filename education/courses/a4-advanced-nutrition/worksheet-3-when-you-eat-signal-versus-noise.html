Part A · Mechanism Is Not Outcome

This is the discipline the whole chapter turns on. Answer each in one or two sentences.

  1. In your own words, define a mechanism and define an outcome, and explain why a spike you can measure in a four-hour window is not the same as a difference you can see on the body a year later.
  2. Insulin is called the most abused mechanism in the field. Explain how a real fact about insulin (it moves glucose and amino acids into cells) gets inflated into a timing claim the evidence does not support.
  3. Name one nutrition claim you have personally heard that confuses a mechanism with an outcome, and say what evidence would be needed to know whether the outcome is real.

Part C · The Anabolic Window, Fed Versus Fasted

The window is real but wide. Show that you understand why by reasoning about amino acids in the blood, not about a clock.

  1. The position stand concluded muscle stays sensitized to protein for at least 24 hours after training (Kerksick et al., 2017). Explain why the widely sold "30-minute window" is off by roughly a factor of forty-eight, and what that means for a person who eats a normal meal an hour after lifting.
  2. Explain why a pre-workout meal changes how much a post-workout meal matters. Use the idea of amino acids already being on station in the bloodstream.
  3. Name the one circumstance where the window genuinely tightens, and give the practical adjustment (a dose and a rough timing) for that person.

Part D · The Per-Meal Ceiling and Distribution

This is the one timing lever with outcome evidence behind it. Do the reasoning, and do the arithmetic.

  1. Witard et al. (2014) found myofibrillar protein synthesis rose with dose up to about 20 grams of whey and then flattened, with 40 grams adding essentially nothing extra. Explain the "per-meal ceiling" (muscle-full effect) and where the surplus amino acids went.
  2. Schoenfeld and Aragon (2018) argue the strict "20 to 25 grams and no more" reading is too rigid. Give two reasons a mixed meal in a real person can push more protein to use than isolated whey did in that study.
  3. Distinguish the Areta (2013) study from the Yasuda (2020) study: which measured a mechanism and which measured an outcome, and why does having both matter for trusting the distribution advice?
  4. Design a day for an 80 kg person using the roughly 0.4 g/kg per meal target across four meals. Give the grams per meal and the daily total, and confirm it clears the 1.6 g/kg/day the outcome literature points to.

Where education ends
This worksheet builds nutrition literacy, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how timing, distribution, and total intake work, and treat anything that looks clinical (a managed medical condition such as diabetes, signs of disordered eating, unexplained weight loss, or distress around food) as warranting evaluation by a qualified professional. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
