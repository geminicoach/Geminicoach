Part C · What the Controlled Evidence Actually Shows

Match each study to the conclusion it supports, then explain what that conclusion means for someone choosing a diet.

  1. Two meta-analyses (Cioffi et al., 2018; Harris et al., 2017) compared intermittent energy restriction against continuous restriction. What did they find when total energy intake was comparable, and what does it tell you about the claim that fasting "boosts metabolism"?
  2. The DIETFITS trial (Gardner et al., 2018) randomized 609 adults to healthy low-fat or healthy low-carbohydrate for a year. Give the between-group weight difference, explain what happened when they tested the genotype and insulin-secretion "escape hatches," and say why the huge spread within each group is the most important detail.
  3. Burke et al. (2020) studied elite race walkers on a ketogenic low-carbohydrate diet. What performance cost did they measure, and why does the absence of a rebound after restoring carbohydrate matter for the "fat-adapt then reintroduce carbs" claim?

Part E · Two Caveats People Skip

  1. Mamerow et al. (2014) held daily protein constant and only changed how it was spread across the day. What did an even distribution do to 24-hour muscle protein synthesis versus a skewed pattern, and why does that create a specific trade-off for someone using a short fasting window while training to keep muscle?

Where education ends
This worksheet builds nutrition literacy and the ability to see through marketing claims, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe a diet for anyone with a medical condition. Explain how the mechanisms work and match structures to preferences and schedules in general terms. Treat anything clinical (disordered-eating patterns, diabetes or other conditions managed with medication, pregnancy, or a person asking you to manage a diagnosed illness) as warranting a qualified clinician or registered dietitian. Work within your role: a coach educates and refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
