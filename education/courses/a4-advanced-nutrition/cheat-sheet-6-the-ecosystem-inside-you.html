Chapter 6 : The Ecosystem Inside You

A metabolic organ you feed every day: ferment fiber into SCFAs, grade inflammation honestly, and separate earned claims from marketing.

The Central Mechanism: Fermentation

WhereThe colon: slow-moving, less acidic, fed by what the small intestine could not absorb. Almost all fermentation happens here.
FuelFermentable fiber and resistant starch that reach the colon undigested.
OutputShort-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), the payoff of the whole chapter.

The Three SCFAs

  • Butyrate : preferred fuel for colon-lining cells; the gut wall eats what the microbes make.
  • Propionate : travels to the liver; involved in glucose metabolism.
  • Acetate : most abundant; circulates more widely.
  • Together they influence barrier integrity, glucose handling, immune signaling, and appetite.

What the Ecosystem Does

  • Ferments fiber your own enzymes cannot touch.
  • Trains the immune system (roughly 70% of immune tissue is near the gut).
  • Crowds out potential pathogens; makes some vitamin K and B vitamins.
  • No single ideal microbiome : grade function (diversity, SCFA capacity, stable barrier), not a list of "good bugs."

Fiber Is a Category

  • Inulin : highly fermentable; onions, garlic, chicory, asparagus.
  • Resistant starch : feeds butyrate producers; cooked-and-cooled potatoes, legumes, green bananas.
  • Beta-glucan : viscous; oats and barley; affects cholesterol and blood sugar.
  • Wheat bran : poorly fermented; adds bulk, speeds transit.
  • Effect depends on fiber type, dose, and your baseline. Variety beats any "miracle fiber."

Feed It Well (High Confidence)

  • Aim for roughly 30 plant species a week (veg, fruit, grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, herbs, spices).
  • Add fiber gradually: bloating is normal fermentation, usually a ramp-rate problem that settles in 1 to 2 weeks.
  • Consider fermented foods (yogurt, kefir, kraut, kimchi).
  • Only about 5% of people meet the fiber target; the "gap" is real and partly a perception gap.
  • Community shifts in days but tracks your habitual pattern and drifts back: consistency beats a one-week reset.

Inflammation, Precisely

  • Acute : normal, healthy, time-limited defense. Do not want to eliminate it.
  • Chronic low-grade : smoldering, associated with type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.
  • Causality is unresolved and reliable biomarkers are lacking (Minihane et al., 2015).
  • It travels in a crowd: visceral fat, poor sleep, stress, inactivity, low-plant diet. "Associated with" is not "causes."

Gut Barrier & "Leaky Gut"

  • Single cell layer, tight junctions, mucus; keeps gut contents out of the blood.
  • LPS leakage → metabolic endotoxemia is a plausible, actively studied path to low-grade inflammation.
  • Permeability is real and measurable, and increased in IBD, celiac, and some metabolic conditions.
  • The "a supplement seals it in a week" narrative runs far ahead of the evidence.

Gut, Appetite & Everyday

  • Gut-brain axis: gastric distension, GLP-1, PYY, CCK, ghrelin, and the vagus nerve report on the meal.
  • SCFAs may nudge GLP-1 and PYY; high-fiber whole foods support fullness and steadier energy.
  • Regularity tracks fiber, fluid, and transit; stress and sleep change all of it (two-way axis).

Grading the Products

  • Probiotics : strain-specific and mostly transient; a few defined wins (e.g., antibiotic-associated diarrhea), oversold elsewhere.
  • Test kits : score you against an "ideal" that does not exist; advice converges on eat more fiber and plants (free).
  • FMT : strongly supported for recurrent C. difficile; investigational elsewhere; real infection risk; regulated medicine.
  • Same intervention can be well-supported for one use and unsupported for the dozen it is marketed for.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the ecosystem works, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe. Persistent diarrhea, blood in the stool, unexplained weight loss, or a suspected inflammatory bowel or celiac condition warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Probiotics, test kits, and fecal transplants are not prescribed or endorsed through this course. Work within your role, whatever it is.
