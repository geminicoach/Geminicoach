Chapter 2 : Deficits, Surpluses, and the Math of Change

Pick a rate of change first, then back into the calories. Speed and preservation pull against each other; the size of the imbalance is the dial.

The Central Trade-off

The dialThe size of your energy imbalance sets where you land between speed of change and preservation of muscle and sanity.
Bill 1 · Lean massA steeper deficit pulls more fuel from muscle. The deeper the hole, the more of your loss is tissue you wanted to keep.
Bill 2 · AdaptationA bigger deficit provokes a stronger defensive response: more hormonal pushback, fatigue, and unconscious drop in daily movement.
Bill 3 · AdherenceHunger, irritability, and food preoccupation are predictable outputs of a steep cut, and the top reason diets fail.

The Math That Lies

  • 3,500 rule : a static model. Assumes expenditure stays fixed, so 500 kcal/day predicts a pound a week forever. It does not.
  • As you shrink you cost less to run: resting metabolism, movement cost, and digestion all fall, plus adaptive thermogenesis on top.
  • The paper deficit narrows on its own as expenditure drifts down to meet intake.
  • So real fat loss is a curve, not a line: fast early, decelerating, settling at a plateau. The plateau is predicted, not a failure.

The Dynamic Model (Hall 2011)

  • Response is slow: half-time of roughly one year toward the new steady weight.
  • Better rule of thumb: about 10 kcal per pound lost comes off daily maintenance. Lose 20 lb, lose ~200 kcal/day of the deficit.
  • More body fat means a larger response and a longer road to steady state.
  • A deficit is a starting hypothesis, not a contract. Run a feedback loop.

Rate First, Calories Second

  • Scale the deficit as a percent of bodyweight per week, which fits the person automatically.
  • Higher body fat (>~25% M / 32% F): around 1.0%/week.
  • Moderate body fat: about 0.5 to 0.75%/week. The general sweet spot.
  • Lean, chasing the last bit: roughly 0.5%/week or less. Patience stops being optional.
  • Garthe (2011): same total loss, but 0.7%/week kept muscle (+2.1% lean) while 1.4%/week did not.

Protein: The Load-Bearing Wall

  • 0.8 g/kg is a floor for not being deficient, not a target for keeping muscle.
  • General dieters: aim 1.6 to 2.0 g/kg of bodyweight. Lean and deep in a cut: 2.3 to 3.1 g/kg of fat-free mass (Helms 2014).
  • Gains from resistance training plateau near 1.6 g/kg (Morton 2017); Refalo (2025) shows a linear benefit in a deficit.
  • Longland (2016): in a 40% deficit, 2.4 g/kg + hard training gained 1.2 kg lean while losing fat; 1.2 g/kg did not.
  • Protein + lifting are one preservation system. Set protein first among the macros.

Surplus & Recomposition

  • Building muscle usually needs a modest surplus (~5 to 15%, often 200 to 350 kcal). A big surplus becomes fat, not faster muscle.
  • Recomp (lose fat + gain muscle at once) is real, not just for beginners (Barakat 2020), enabled by training + high protein.
  • Four dials: training age, body-fat level, detraining status, protein adequacy.
  • Beginner / returning / higher body fat + protein = recomp expected. Lean and advanced = pick one direction.
  • Murphy & Koehler (2021): a ~500 kcal deficit roughly blunts new muscle gain. Below it recomp has room; above it, preserve.

Realistic Rates

  • Fat is lost far faster than muscle is built. Two different clocks.
  • Fat loss: 0.5 to 1.0% of bodyweight/week. Faster buys speed with lean mass.
  • Muscle: beginner ~1 to 1.5% of bodyweight/month; halves in years 2 to 3; halves again when advanced.
  • Judge a gaining phase in months, by measurements and strength logs, not the mirror.

Structuring Time

  • Diet break : after ~6 to 12 weeks, eat at maintenance for 1 to 2 weeks to ease adaptation and restore adherence. Not backsliding.
  • Refeed : a shorter, carb-focused day or two, aimed at sustainability and training quality, not a metabolic cheat code.
  • MATADOR (Byrne 2018) : same total deficit in blocks lost more fat and blunted the drop in resting expenditure.
  • Reverse dieting : raise calories in small steps to find true maintenance and avoid a sharp rebound. It recovers expenditure, it does not inflate it.

Assemble the Skeleton of a Plan

  • 1. Name the goal honestly (fat loss, muscle gain, or recomp) using the four dials.
  • 2. Choose a rate, not a calorie number: 0.5 to 1.0%/week to cut, a modest 5 to 15% surplus to gain. Let the rate set the calories.
  • 3. Set protein first (~1.6 to 2.0 g/kg), then fill fat and carbs. Keep the lifting volume up.
  • 4. Run a weekly-average feedback loop and structure time with breaks. The deficit is a hypothesis you adjust as the body adapts.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how energy balance and the math of change work, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe. Very low intake with signs of under-fuelling, loss of menstrual periods, hair loss, disordered-eating patterns, a managed medical condition, or unexplained weight change are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified professional. Work within your role, whatever it is.
