Chapter 7 : One Size Fits No One

Keep the sound general rule, adjust the setting, and always name the mechanism that justifies the move.

The Average Hides the Person

  • A reported result is a mean: the center of a distribution no single person has to occupy.
  • Fed identical meals, healthy people vary enormously: about 103% for triglyceride, 68% glucose, 59% insulin (Berry et al., 2020).
  • "This works" is a bet with decent odds, not a promise. A "non-response" is often just the tail of the spread.
  • The average tells you where to begin; the person tells you where to go.

Where Variation Lives (In Order)

  • Body size / composition : biggest, most boring; scale doses per kg.
  • Activity / training history : reshapes fuel handling; swings expenditure.
  • Age & sex : real, mechanism-backed shifts.
  • Gut microbiome : strong for blood fats and sugar.
  • Genetics : usually a modest, single-digit slice.
  • Health & medications : can outweigh any diet tweak.
  • Food environment / preferences : the gate every plan must pass.

Trained vs General Population

  • Timing sits low in the hierarchy; total protein and energy balance rule for most.
  • Payoff of tight timing rises for the trained, and when sessions are under about 4 to 8 hours apart (Kerksick et al., 2017).
  • Mori (2014): immediate post-exercise feeding raised nitrogen balance in trained men, not untrained; training builds a body that responds to the finer dials.
  • Chase timing only after the big rocks are in the jar.

Aging: Anabolic Resistance

  • Sarcopenia is muscle as the organ of independence, not a cosmetic loss.
  • Anabolic resistance : a blunted response to amino acids and load, a dimmed dial, not a broken wire (Breen & Phillips, 2011).
  • Push protein toward 1.2 to 1.5 g/kg/day, real dose per meal (Deutz et al., 2014).
  • Protein plus resistance training, together, beats either alone (Rogeri et al., 2021).

Female-Specific, Without Mystification

  • Protein toward the upper range, roughly 1.8 to 2.2 g/kg/day, higher in the luteal phase (progesterone is catabolic) (Sims et al., 2023).
  • Watch iron, calcium, vitamin D; peri/postmenopause raises the stakes on protein and lifting (Dolph et al., 2021).
  • Answer is informed flexibility, not a rigid day-by-day template.

Energy Availability & REDs

  • EA = (intake minus exercise energy) / kg fat-free mass; target near 45 kcal/kg FFM/day.
  • Low EA runs a spectrum from adaptable to problematic, not a binary switch (Mountjoy et al., 2023).
  • Costs are systemic: metabolism, menstruation, bone, immunity, glycogen.
  • A lost or disrupted cycle is a warning light, never a trophy. The fix is more food, not more discipline.

Building Plant-Based Diets

  • Parity is achievable; the one variable to manage on purpose is protein (Hernandez-Lougedo et al., 2023).
  • Plant proteins score lower (PDCAAS / DIAAS) but are not inadequate; soy and even rice protein match animal sources when dosed (Hertzler et al., 2020).
  • Three levers: raise total dose, pair complementary proteins across the day, use quality/fortified sources.
  • Plan gaps: B12 (non-negotiable), iron, calcium, vitamin D, omega-3, zinc, iodine.

DNA-Diet Kits: Read the Hype

  • Most traits are polygenic; a few tested variants cannot write your diet.
  • FTO : linked to weight, but does not predict which diet works; ordinary diet and activity counteract it (Livingstone et al., 2016).
  • MTHFR : modest effect, managed by adequate folate (Jin et al., 2018).
  • CYP1A2 : caffeine response is learned faster by paying attention than by genotyping (Guest et al., 2021).

Personalizing by Glucose: Strong Case, Real Limits

The findingPost-meal glucose responses to identical foods are highly individual and can be predicted and improved with person-specific models (Zeevi et al., 2015; reproduced by Mendes-Soares et al., 2019). Glycemic index is a population average, often near useless for one person.
The limitsThe measured outcome was the glucose curve, not weight, health, or how anyone felt. Strong evidence applies to people with a real glucose problem (prediabetes, type 2 diabetes), not to metabolically healthy CGM users. A normal rise after a normal meal is the system working.
The methodPrinciples first (energy balance, adequate protein, whole food, training), then measured feedback (weight trend, strength, energy, hunger), then the person's real life (environment, preferences, culture). Variation bends the defaults; it does not delete them.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how to individualize sound general rules, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe. A disrupted or absent menstrual cycle, a suspected medical condition, the interpretation of genetic or metabolic test results, and any medication or supplement decision belong to a qualified clinician, not to this course. Work within your role, whatever it is.
