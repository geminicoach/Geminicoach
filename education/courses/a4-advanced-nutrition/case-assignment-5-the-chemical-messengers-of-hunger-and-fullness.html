Chapter 5: Read the Signals, Not the Willpower

Take the appetite-hormone system and use it to explain a real struggle, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Leon, 43
Consider Leon, who has been trying to lose about twenty pounds for the better part of a year. He is not lazy and he is not uninformed. For the first couple of weeks of any diet he eats well and feels fine. Then, every time, it falls apart the same way: around week three the hunger turns constant and gnawing, he thinks about food during meetings, and the salads that satisfied him at first leave him rummaging the pantry an hour later. A few nights a week he ends up at the counter finishing the kids' leftover snacks long after he is full. He sleeps about six hours a night, less when work is heavy. He has decided the problem is that he simply lacks willpower, and he is close to quitting. He has also heard about GLP-1 medications and asks, "Should I just get on one of those, and what dose would I need?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, why Leon reliably comes apart around week three. Use leptin, the arcuate nucleus (AgRP/NPY versus POMC), and adaptive thermogenesis.
  2. Account for the role of his sleep, and for why his particular meals and his late-night grazing make the problem worse. Bring in ghrelin, the satiety peptides, and the hedonic reward system.
  3. Give Leon a set of practical, input-level suggestions that work with his signaling rather than against it, and say why each one helps.
  4. Address his medication question responsibly. State clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins, and what you can and cannot say about starting or dosing a GLP-1 medication.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing or prescribing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Whether a person should start a GLP-1 or any other weight-loss medication, and at what dose, is a decision between them and a physician; it is outside the scope of this course and of a coaching role.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains that a sustained deficit lowers leptin, and that the arcuate nucleus tips toward the AgRP/NPY hunger neurons and away from the POMC satiety neurons (adaptive thermogenesis).
  • Identifies short sleep as an independent driver that lowers leptin and raises ghrelin, stacking a second appetite onto the first.
  • Explains why low-protein, low-volume meals barely recruit the satiety committee (CCK, PYY, GLP-1, stomach stretch), and why late-night grazing is the hedonic reward system overriding a full stomach.
  • Offers practical, input-level suggestions within scope: protect sleep, build meals around protein and high-volume whole foods, run a moderate deficit with maintenance breaks, manage the night-time environment.
  • Reframes the struggle as physiology rather than willpower, refers the medication decision to a physician, and does not diagnose or prescribe.
