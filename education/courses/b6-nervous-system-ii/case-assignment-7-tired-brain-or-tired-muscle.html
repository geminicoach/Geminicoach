The Scenario

Scenario · Zaina, 33
Consider Zaina, who is training for an obstacle-course race while working a stretch of extended hospital shifts. On Tuesday, her overhead press dropped noticeably on her final working sets. Her warm-ups and earlier submaximal sets had felt completely normal, and her shoulders were not sore, pumped, or burning, just unresponsive at the top end. That day followed two consecutive double shifts and a short night of sleep. On Friday, her squat volume sets felt "dead" from the very first set. Her legs were visibly shaking and pumped, and the soreness matched a genuinely high-rep leg session she had done two days earlier. Rest between sets did not bring the force back. Zaina wants to know whether she should back off everything for a full week, or whether these are two unrelated, ordinary bad days.

Your Tasks

  1. Using the markers from the chapter, classify Tuesday's session and Friday's session separately as central-leaning, peripheral-leaning, or mixed, and justify each call with specific evidence from the scenario.
  2. Explain why the two classifications call for different recovery responses, and describe what each response should actually involve.
  3. Decide whether this week's pattern, two sessions, several days apart, each with its own plausible explanation, meets the bar for nonfunctional overreaching (NFOR) described in the chapter, or whether it is still within ordinary central and peripheral fatigue. Justify your answer using the duration-and-pattern reasoning from the chapter.
  4. Give one practical, general-education response for Zaina, and state clearly what would need to appear before this situation should be handed to a qualified professional rather than managed with general training adjustments.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language reasoning, not diagnosing Zaina or anyone else. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified professional.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Classifies Tuesday's session as central-leaning, citing the normal warm-ups, absence of local muscle burn, and the sleep and workload context.
  • Classifies Friday's session as peripheral-leaning, citing the visible shaking, pump, and the match to a genuinely high-rep prior session.
  • Explains that central fatigue calls for a state-based response (sleep, reduced cognitive and arousal load) while peripheral fatigue calls for a substrate-and-time response.
  • Reasons from duration and pattern, not the size of a single bad session, to judge whether this meets the bar for nonfunctional overreaching (NFOR).
  • Offers a practical, general-education suggestion and identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, without diagnosing.
