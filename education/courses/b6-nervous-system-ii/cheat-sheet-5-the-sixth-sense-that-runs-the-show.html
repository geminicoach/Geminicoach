Chapter 5 : The Sixth Sense That Runs the Show

Proprioception runs movement below awareness: two sensors, one fast reflex loop, and a rebound trick built on both.

The Two Proprioceptors

Muscle spindleSits parallel to muscle fibers; reports length and, especially, the velocity of stretch. Tuned moment to moment by its own gamma motor neurons. Signal carried by fast Ia afferents (length also by slower group II).
Golgi tendon organ (GTO)Sits in series at the muscle-tendon junction; reports tension, the actual force passing through the tendon, across the whole working range, not just near failure. Signal carried by Ib afferents.
Joint position senseThe fused sense of a limb's position without looking, built from spindles, tendon organs, plus joint, ligament, and skin receptors. Tested by reproducing a target angle from memory; the error is measurable and can lag behind strength after injury.

Reflex Arc & Reciprocal Inhibition

  • Stretch reflex : tendon tap stretches spindles, signal crosses one synapse, muscle contracts. About 25 to 50 ms, faster than conscious reaction (about 150 to 200 ms).
  • Reciprocal inhibition : the same signal that excites the stretched muscle also inhibits its opposite, so movement is clean, not a tug of war.
  • Longer-latency response : a second, slower burst (about 50 to 100 ms) that loops through more circuitry and can be shaped by context.

Feedforward vs. Feedback

  • Feedforward : a motor command issued from a plan, before sensory confirmation; fast but blind to surprises.
  • Feedback : a correction issued after sensing an error; accurate but slower.
  • Internal forward model : a prediction of a limb's state, compared against incoming proprioceptive feedback, that corrects a movement mid-flight, before it finishes.

Below Awareness, on Purpose

  • Most proprioceptive computation stays out of awareness because awareness is slow and serial.
  • Practice does not remove control; it moves correction to faster, subconscious loops.
  • Proprioceptive training is real and measurable, but gains are specific to the conditions trained.

The Stretch-Shortening Cycle (SSC)

  • Rapid stretch immediately before shortening; two engines drive the rebound.
  • Elastic energy : tendon stores energy on the stretch, if the reversal is fast enough to return it.
  • Reflex burst : the stretch reflex fires at the reversal, adding force that is not consciously commanded.
  • Sweet spot: moderate depth, fast reversal, high stiffness. Too deep and slow bleeds the spring; too shallow never loads it.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how proprioception and reflex control work, not a license to diagnose or treat. Persistent unsteadiness after a cleared injury, numbness, tingling, locking, or swelling are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
