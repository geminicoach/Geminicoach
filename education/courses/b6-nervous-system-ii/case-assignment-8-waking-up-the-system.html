Chapter 8: Reason Through a Real Warmup

Take the fatigue-versus-potentiation model and use it to diagnose a real warmup, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Oscar, 34
Consider Oscar, who plays in a recreational basketball league and wants his first few possessions of each scrimmage to feel sharp instead of sluggish. His routine looks like this: ten minutes of easy jogging to feel warm, then a single heavy back squat in the gym down the hall from the court, followed by roughly fifteen minutes chatting with a training partner while the equipment gets racked and the rest of the group finishes warming up, and then straight onto the court for the opening possession, without ever rehearsing a jump, a cut, or a defensive slide at anything close to game speed. Oscar reports that his first live possession always feels slow and heavy-legged, and he assumed the heavy squat simply had not "kicked in" yet by the time he got on the court.

Your Tasks

  1. Using the two-job model from the chapter (general raise versus specific preparation), explain what Oscar's routine got right and what it left out entirely.
  2. Using the potentiation-versus-fatigue window concept, explain what the model predicts happens to potentiation and to fatigue across the roughly fifteen-minute gap between Oscar's heavy squat and his first live possession, and why that gap likely worked against him.
  3. Identify the missing RAMP component in Oscar's routine, and explain, independent of timing, why skipping it would cause sluggishness even if the timing had been perfect.
  4. Propose a redesigned sequence, described in general terms and without prescribing specific loads or personal numbers, that would place a potentiating effort's window on Oscar's first live possession. Then state clearly where general education stops and individualized coaching or clinical guidance begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language reasoning, not prescribing a program. A responsible response explains the mechanism, proposes a general redesign, and does not diagnose Oscar's knee, prescribe specific loads, or clear him for near-maximal lifting. Program design and clearance to load belong to a qualified coach or clinician working with him directly.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies that the general raise (jogging) addressed temperature and conduction, but movement-specific rehearsal was never done.
  • Explains, using the decay logic of the two curves, why waiting roughly fifteen minutes after the heavy squat likely closed the potentiation window before the first live possession.
  • Names the missing RAMP component (movement-specific rehearsal, or activate and mobilize) and explains that coordination and reflexive timing are pattern-specific, not restored by temperature alone.
  • Proposes a general redesign that adds rehearsal and tightens the timing, without prescribing specific numeric loads.
  • States clearly where general education stops and individualized coaching or clinical guidance begins, and does not diagnose Oscar's knee.
