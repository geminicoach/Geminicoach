Chapter 1: Reason Through a Real Plateau

Take the recruitment ladder and use it to explain a real training pattern, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Kofi, 33
Consider Kofi, who has spent the last several months training almost entirely with moderate loads, around 60 to 70 percent of a comfortable working maximum, for high-volume sets. Kofi has been diligent, and it has paid off in a specific way: rep-max numbers on those moderate sets have climbed steadily, and eight-rep personal bests keep falling. But when Kofi occasionally attempts a true one-rep maximal lift, the number barely moves, and the attempt feels unfamiliar and uncoordinated in a way the steady rep-max progress never predicted. Kofi wonders: "I am working hard and I can see the progress on paper. Why doesn't my actual max lift number move?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, which motor units Kofi's moderate-load, high-volume program is training thoroughly, and which units the program is not reaching, using the recruitment ladder from the chapter.
  2. Using Henneman's size principle, explain why simply adding more volume at the same 60 to 70 percent intensity cannot, by itself, recruit the units that a true maximal single actually requires.
  3. Distinguish between tissue-level hypertrophy and nervous-system-level recruitment efficiency, and explain why Kofi could genuinely be progressing at one without progressing much at the other.
  4. Give one practical, general-education observation about training across the full intensity range, and state clearly where general education ends and individualized program design or professional evaluation begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not prescribing an individualized program or diagnosing anything. A responsible response explains the mechanism, offers a general observation, and identifies what, if anything, calls for individualized guidance or professional evaluation. Designing a specific program for a specific person, and evaluating pain or injury, belong to individualized coaching direction or a qualified clinician, not to a chapter like this one.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies which motor units Kofi's moderate-load, high-volume program reaches and trains well (small and fast fatigue-resistant units).
  • Explains, via the size principle, why adding volume at the same intensity cannot recruit the untouched high-threshold, fast fatigable units.
  • Distinguishes tissue-level hypertrophy from nervous-system-level recruitment efficiency, and explains why the two do not automatically track together.
  • Offers a practical, general-education observation about training across the intensity range, without prescribing an individualized program.
  • Correctly frames the limit of general education here, naming what would call for individualized or professional guidance, and does not diagnose or prescribe.
