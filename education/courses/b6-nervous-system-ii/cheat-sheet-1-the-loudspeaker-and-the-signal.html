Chapter 1 : The Loudspeaker and the Signal

The muscle is the loudspeaker. The motor unit (MU), one motor neuron plus every fiber it commands, is the signal.

The Motor Unit, Defined

Motor neuronA nerve cell whose cell body sits in the spinal cord and whose axon reaches out to muscle fibers.
Motor unit (MU)One motor neuron plus every muscle fiber that neuron innervates. The functional unit of force, not the whole muscle.
Innervation ratioFibers per neuron. Few fibers (a handful) buys fine, gradable control. Many fibers (hundreds or thousands) buys coarse, powerful force.
All-or-nothingWhen a motor neuron fires, every fiber it controls contracts together. There is no partial contraction of one unit.

Henneman's Size Principle

  • The rule : recruitment order is fixed, small motor neurons first, large ones last.
  • The mechanism : small neurons have higher input resistance, so the same synaptic drive produces a bigger voltage swing and an earlier threshold.
  • Not by choice : order is set by the pool's electrical architecture, and it resists override by simple intention in ordinary lifting.
  • Confirmed : in cat reflex studies, voluntary human contraction, and high-density electromyography (EMG).

Three Flavors of Engine

  • Slow (S) : few fibers, low force, tireless. Pairs with Type I fibers.
  • Fast fatigue-resistant (FR) : moderate fibers and force, sustains real effort a good while. Pairs with Type IIa fibers.
  • Fast fatigable (FF) : many fibers, high force, fast, tires quickly. Pairs with Type IIx fibers.
  • Force capacity and fatigue resistance trade off across all three, by design, not by flaw.

Two Levers for More Force

  • Recruitment : switching on additional, previously silent motor units. Does most of the work at low to moderate force.
  • Rate coding : firing already-active units faster. Does more of the work once most available units are already on, near maximal effort.
  • Light tasks stay parked in small, tireless units all day. Maximal efforts must reach the big, fatigable units, and cannot last long once they do.

Why "Train Motor Units, Not Muscles"

  • Intensity is a recruitment statement: how far up the ladder an effort climbs.
  • Volume at a low intensity cannot substitute for intensity when the goal needs high-threshold units.
  • Endurance and peak force live at opposite ends of the same ladder; one stimulus cannot maximize both.
  • Hypertrophy (tissue size) and recruitment efficiency (nervous-system command) are different adaptations and do not automatically track together.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of the motor unit and the recruitment ladder, not a license to diagnose, treat, or individually prescribe training. Sharp or persistent pain, sudden weakness, numbness, or an asymmetry that will not resolve with normal training variation are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
