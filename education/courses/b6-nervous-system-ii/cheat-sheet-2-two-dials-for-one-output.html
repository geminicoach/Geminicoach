Chapter 2 : Two Dials for One Output

One muscle, two control dials: recruitment (how many motor units) and rate coding (how fast they fire).

The Two Dials

RecruitmentHow many motor units (MU) are switched on. Each unit is all-or-nothing; there is no partial contraction of a single unit. Units join in a fixed order, smallest and most fatigue-resistant first (the size principle).
Rate codingHow fast each already-active motor unit's neuron is firing. Faster firing stacks twitches on top of each other (summation) until they fuse into a smooth, higher plateau of force called tetanus.

The Ramp, Not the Staircase

  • Recruitment alone would produce a staircase: a jump in force each time a new unit joins, then a flat pause.
  • Rate coding fills the gaps between those steps, turning the staircase into a smooth ramp.
  • "Recruited" tells you a unit is participating. It does not tell you how hard that unit is working.

Division of Labor

  • Low-to-mid force: recruitment and rate coding rise together.
  • Near maximal force: the muscle nears its recruitment ceiling (almost everything it will recruit is already recruited), so further force comes almost entirely from rate coding.
  • Where the ceiling sits differs by muscle: small hand and forearm muscles tend to finish recruiting earlier than large limb muscles.

Rate Coding Reserve

  • The gap between a motor unit's current firing rate and its maximal firing rate.
  • Untrained or unpracticed maximal efforts often leave real force unspent inside this gap.
  • Closing this gap, not adding muscle tissue, explains a large share of early strength gains.

Rate of Force Development (RFD) and Explosive Strength

  • RFD: how quickly force climbs from zero once a contraction begins.
  • Driven mainly by how fast motor units are recruited and how high their initial firing rate reaches in the first tens of milliseconds.
  • Often more decisive than peak force for sprinting, jumping, and quick changes of direction, since the task ends before peak force could ever be reached.
  • Peak strength and explosive strength are related but separate skills; a program can build one without the other.

What Trains Each Dial

  • Rate coding: maximal-intent effort, whether heavy near-maximal loads or explosive light-to-moderate loads moved with speed. The common thread is a demand on timing, not on muscle mass.
  • Recruitment: genuinely heavy loads, or lighter loads taken to real proximity to failure. Both routes eventually reach the largest motor units.
  • A program that only chases fatigue and volume can raise the recruitment ceiling while leaving rate coding underdeveloped.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the nervous system grades force, not a license to design a training program or evaluate an injury. Pain, a movement that should be assessed in person, or a question about a specific person's specific situation are signs that warrant input from a qualified coach or clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
