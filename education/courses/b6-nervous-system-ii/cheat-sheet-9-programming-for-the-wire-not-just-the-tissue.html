Chapter 9 : Programming for the Wire, Not Just the Tissue

Strength is a skill expressed through tissue. Assemble recruitment, skill, fatigue, and priming into one training logic.

The Capstone Model, Four Levers

RecruitmentDeveloped by intensity, broadly loads at or above 85 percent of a one-repetition maximum (1RM). Sets the ceiling of which motor units are even in play.
Rate codingDeveloped by intent, deliberate maximal effort to accelerate a load, even a light one. Sets the quality of drive at any load.
Skill / coordinationBuilt by matching practice to the learner's stage, variability early, specificity late, and correct exercise order within a session.
Central fatigueManaged by budgeting the central account separately from the peripheral one, and by deloads and tapers that clear it on schedule.
PrimingA short, low-volume, high-intent session, timed one to two days out, that briefly potentiates recruitment and rate coding without leaving residual fatigue.

Neural Drive: Intensity vs. Intent

  • Intensity : sets the recruitment ceiling; high-threshold motor units only switch on near-maximal loads.
  • Intent : sets rate coding; a submaximal load moved with genuine maximal intent still trains discharge rate.
  • Heavy grinding sets cost more central fatigue per rep than fast, submaximal, high-intent work. A mature program uses both, at different points.
  • Protect proximity to maximal intent over proximity to failure; a grinding set buries the central nervous system (CNS) and degrades the quality it was meant to train.

Specificity & Exercise Order

  • Variability early in a cycle builds a broad, transferable pattern; specificity late rehearses the exact test context.
  • Match practice to the learner's stage: cognitive needs frequent, low-load, high-feedback reps; autonomous needs specificity and load.
  • Order a session by neural demand, not muscle group: the movement that most needs recruitment, rate coding, and coordination goes first, while the wire is freshest.

Central vs. Peripheral Fatigue

  • Peripheral : impairment at the muscle itself. Soreness is a peripheral signal.
  • Central : the central nervous system (CNS) failing to fully activate muscle that is otherwise ready. Soreness tells you almost nothing about it.
  • A learner can be muscularly fresh and neurally flat at the same time; never schedule the week's heaviest neural session off a soreness check alone.
  • High-skill work needs a fresh central account to be productive at all; coordination degrades early under central fatigue.

Deload, Taper, and Priming

  • Deload : restores readiness within a cycle; a planned dip that clears fatigue so the next block lands on rested tissue and a rested wire.
  • Taper : a peaking tool aimed at a date. Hold intensity, cut volume by roughly 60 to 90 percent, gradually, not in one step.
  • Priming : the fine-tuning knob at the very end of a taper. Low volume, high intent, precise timing, one to two days out.
  • Cut intensity instead of volume on a taper and you detrain the recruitment the cycle spent months building.

Velocity as a Readout

  • Velocity-based training (VBT) uses the speed a load moves as a direct, session-by-session readout of neural readiness, where soreness gives none.
  • Slower speed on a fixed submaximal load, with no soreness and no load change, signals accumulating central fatigue.
  • Practical uses: load-velocity profiling to estimate a current 1RM, session tracking as an early-warning system, and velocity-loss thresholds to cap fatigue within a set.

Honest Limits

  • The exact anatomical site of many neural adaptations, spinal, supraspinal, or an interaction of the two, is still genuinely contested; you do not need that debate resolved to program well.
  • The percentages in this chapter are population averages, not guarantees. Find a specific learner's optimum by tracking real output, not by looking a single number up in a table.
  • Programming for the wire still has to respect joints, connective tissue, sleep, and life outside training. It is not an argument for maximal intent on every set.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the nervous system and the muscular tissue are trained together, not a license to diagnose, treat, or replace a qualified coach or clinician. A sudden neurological event, an unexplained one-sided loss of function, or a persistent injury are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
