Chapter 4: Reason Through a Real Learning Curve

Take the stage model of motor learning and use it to explain a real learner's progress, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Lourdes, eight months into pottery wheel throwing
Consider Lourdes, who took up pottery wheel throwing eight months ago. For the first several weeks, every bowl came out lopsided, she could not explain why a piece collapsed under her hands, and she needed her teacher to point out nearly every mistake before she could fix it. Now, eight months in, her bowls come out symmetrical almost every time. She can feel when the clay has drifted off center and correct it mid-throw, though she still has to consciously think through each step of the process. Lately her teacher has started giving her a single external cue per throw, something like "let the clay guide your hands," instead of the running list of corrections she used to get, and Lourdes has noticed her work actually improved once the constant correction stopped. She is about to start learning a new skill, a language, and wonders whether the same pattern applies, how she should plan her practice for it, and why her teacher's approach changed the way it did.

Your Tasks

  1. Identify what stage of motor learning Lourdes's pottery skill is in right now, using the chapter's three-question field test, and justify your answer with specific details from the scenario.
  2. Using the guidance hypothesis, explain why her teacher's shift to a single, sparse, external cue likely helped Lourdes rather than holding her back.
  3. Design a practice plan for the new skill Lourdes is about to start, the language. Specify how blocked versus random practice, and massed versus distributed practice, should be used differently in her first week compared with a few months in.
  4. Explain, in plain language a beginner could follow, why automaticity will eventually free Lourdes's attention in her new skill, and describe one concrete thing she will be able to do once that happens that she cannot do now.

Where education ends
