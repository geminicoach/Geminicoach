Chapter 6: Reason Through a Real Complaint

Take the mechanics of coordination, the kinetic chain, and movement economy and use them to explain a real complaint, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Freddie, 33
Consider Freddie, who plays in an adult recreational tennis league twice a week. Frustrated that their serve had plateaued, Freddie spent the off-season adding weight to seated shoulder presses and triceps extensions on machines, following a training article that claimed serve speed comes down to arm strength. By the start of the new season, Freddie's one-rep numbers on those machines were up substantially. Serve speed did not improve. Over the past few weeks of full-intensity practice, Freddie has also noticed a dull ache building in the shoulder that was not there before, present mainly after long sessions and gradually getting a little more noticeable, though it has never been sudden or sharp and does not limit reaching overhead in daily life. Freddie wonders: "I got stronger. Why didn't my serve get faster, and should I be worried about my shoulder?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, why isolated shoulder and triceps strength gains would not be expected to translate into serve speed, using proximal-to-distal kinetic-chain sequencing and muscle synergies.
  2. Using anticipatory postural adjustments and the ensemble roles of agonist, antagonist, synergist, and stabilizer, explain what a seated, trunk-supported isolation exercise fails to train that an actual serve requires.
  3. Decide whether Freddie's plateau reads as an ordinary, explainable coordination gap or as something that needs closer assessment, and explain how you know, citing the mechanisms involved.
  4. Give one practical, general-education suggestion for what Freddie's training would need to resemble to actually build serve speed, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins, given the new shoulder ache.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains that proximal-to-distal kinetic-chain sequencing and muscle synergies, not isolated prime-mover strength, determine serve speed, and that a single-joint machine movement trains no sequencing between segments.
  • Explains that a seated, trunk-supported isolation exercise removes the need for the ensemble (agonist, antagonist, synergist, stabilizer) to coordinate, and removes the need for anticipatory postural adjustments, so both go untrained.
  • Reasons that Freddie's plateau reads as a plausible, ordinary coordination or task-specificity gap rather than a strength ceiling, and explains the reasoning.
  • Gives a practical, general-education suggestion for training that resembles the real serve's speed, posture, and sequencing.
  • Identifies that the new, gradually worsening shoulder ache warrants professional evaluation before continuing the program, and does not diagnose.
