Chapter 6 : The Orchestra, Not the Soloist

Coordination, not raw strength, is the ceiling on skillful movement: an ensemble of roles, a sequence, and a budget for effort.

The Ensemble: Four Roles, One Movement

AgonistThe prime mover, most directly responsible for producing the movement being watched.
AntagonistSits on the opposite side of the joint; must relax on cue (reciprocal inhibition) rather than fight the agonist.
SynergistAssists the agonist by adding force or by canceling out an unwanted side effect at another joint.
StabilizerHolds a nearby segment, often the trunk or shoulder blade, rigid so the prime mover has something firm to pull against.
  • The same muscle can hold different roles in different movements, or at different instants within the same movement. The question is not "how strong," it is "doing its assigned job, at its assigned moment, in this movement."

Synergies & Motor Programs

  • Degrees-of-freedom problem : with hundreds of muscles and near-infinite ways to move, the nervous system cannot plausibly command each muscle one at a time (Bernstein).
  • Muscle synergy : a coordinated, reusable package of muscle activation, selected and timed as a unit rather than muscle by muscle.
  • Generalized motor program (GMP) : Schmidt's stored structure for a class of movements; relative timing stays invariant while speed, size, and the specific muscles used are adjustable parameters.
  • Coordination is task-specific: a pattern trained in one movement does not automatically transfer to a different one, even with the same muscles.

The Kinetic Chain

  • Proximal-to-distal sequencing : large segments near the center move first, smaller segments farther out move last, like cracking a whip from the handle.
  • Each segment accelerates, peaks, then decelerates, handing its energy and angular momentum to the next segment.
  • Firing everything at once wastes the handoff; power is a timing problem more than a force problem.
  • Owens, Coyles, and Khaiyat (2024): in injured throwers, elbow extension and shoulder flexion, normally coupled at the final handoff, come apart in time, and ball speed correlates with arm-joint velocity instead of the whole chain.

Anticipatory Postural Adjustments (APAs)

  • Postural muscles (calf, back) fire before the prime mover (shoulder) so stability arrives with the movement, not after it.
  • Feedforward : planned in advance, based on a predicted disturbance. Feedback : a reactive correction, always at least a little late by definition.
  • APAs are learnable and context-dependent; they degrade first under fatigue or rushing, and go untrained on a fully supported machine.

Movement Economy & Co-contraction

  • Co-contraction : agonist and antagonist pulling against each other at the same time, spending energy and stiffening the joint.
  • Hogan (1984): co-contraction increases joint stiffness, useful when the task is unstable, unfamiliar, or precision matters over speed.
  • Armstrong, Deluzio, and Scott (2025): a little co-contraction can speed up corrective reflex responses; it is not simply an error.
  • Brueckner, Kiss, and Muehlbauer (2018): skill tracks cleaner timing and less co-contraction, not higher raw muscle activity. Skill is subtraction as much as addition.
  • Wasteful co-contraction is the kind still present once a task is familiar, stable, and well learned.

Four Questions for Watching a Movement

  • What is the firing sequence, proximal to distal or scrambled?
  • Is a stable base set in time, before the prime mover fires?
  • How much effort is being wasted on unnecessary co-contraction?
  • Is this coordination specific to the task that actually matters, or to a proxy that only resembles it?
Where education ends   This builds understanding of coordination, sequencing, and movement economy, not a license to diagnose or treat. Sudden or sharp pain, worsening soreness, or loss of range of motion are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
