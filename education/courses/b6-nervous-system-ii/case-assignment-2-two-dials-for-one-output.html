Case Assignment · B6 Nervous System II

Chapter 2: Reason Through a Real Plateau

Take the two-dial model of force and use it to explain a real training plateau, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Sable, 29
Consider Sable, who has followed a straightforward strength program for eight months: back squat and deadlift, moderate-to-heavy loads, controlled tempo, steady load increases week after week. The numbers on both lifts have climbed reliably the whole time. Three months ago, Sable also started training for a recreational obstacle course race that demands explosive box jumps and fast sprint starts between obstacles. Despite the squat and deadlift still climbing, Sable's jump height and ten-meter sprint time have barely moved. Sable assumed that a bigger squat number would automatically carry over to a higher jump and a faster start, since in Sable's mind "getting stronger" was one thing, and more of it should show up everywhere force is needed. Sable wants to know: "Am I doing something wrong, or is my body just not built for explosive stuff?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, using recruitment and rate coding, why Sable's squat and deadlift numbers keep rising while jump height and sprint start have barely changed.
  2. Explain what rate coding reserve is, and why a controlled-tempo heavy squat program might build a large reserve without ever teaching the nervous system to spend it quickly.
  3. Define rate of force development (RFD) in plain language, and explain why it, rather than peak force, is likely the more decisive quality for Sable's jump and sprint goals.
  4. Recommend, in general-education terms, what category of training could target the piece Sable's program has been missing, and state clearly where general education ends and where a qualified coach or clinician would need to take over.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not designing Sable's next training block or evaluating any injury. A responsible response explains the mechanism, offers a general-education direction, and identifies where professional coaching or clinical judgment needs to take over. Program design and injury evaluation belong to a qualified coach or clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains recruitment (how many motor units are active) and rate coding (how fast each fires) as the two dials, and applies both to Sable's rising squat.
  • Explains why controlled-tempo heavy squatting builds the recruitment ceiling well but gives the nervous system unlimited time to reach a high firing rate, so it does little to train the speed of reaching that rate.
  • Defines rate of force development (RFD) accurately and ties it to rapid recruitment and high initial firing rate rather than eventual peak force.
  • Names rate coding reserve as a plausible reason strength can rise with little new muscle, and connects it to Sable's situation.
  • Offers a general-education training direction (for example, explosive-intent or contrast work added to the existing base) without prescribing a specific program.
  • States clearly where general education ends and where a coach's or clinician's judgment needs to begin, without diagnosing anything.
