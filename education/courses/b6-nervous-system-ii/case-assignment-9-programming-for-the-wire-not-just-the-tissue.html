Chapter 9: Reason Through a Real Complaint

Take the whole course, recruitment, skill, fatigue, and priming, and use it to reason through a real programming problem, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 30 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Marguerite, 34
Consider Marguerite, who is five weeks out from a strength competition. For the last three weeks her top single has stalled, and on her most recent heavy session it actually felt slower coming off the floor than it did a month ago, even though the weight on the bar did not change. Nothing hurts. She has no soreness at all going into most sessions, and every recovery check she runs, sleep, appetite, mood, comes back normal. Her weekly plan puts her heaviest, most maximal lifting on Monday and her technical practice, the session where she drills bar path and positioning, on Tuesday, because that felt like the natural order to her. Marguerite is unsure whether to add more volume to break the plateau, back off entirely, or change something about how her week is arranged, and she wants to know how to use her final five weeks before the competition.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, whether Marguerite's stalled lift and slowing bar speed, given the complete absence of soreness, points to a tissue (peripheral) problem or a wire (central) problem, and say how you know.
  2. Explain what the order of her week, heavy max-intent lifting on Monday directly followed by technical practice on Tuesday, is likely doing to her technical session, and why that would happen even though her tissue feels completely fine.
  3. Give a general-education outline for her final five weeks: what her weekly sequencing should change, and what a proper taper should do with intensity and volume as the competition approaches.
  4. State clearly where general education ends and where Marguerite would need a qualified coach or clinician, and do not diagnose.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing or prescribing a competition plan. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Program design for a real competitor and diagnosis of any injury belong to a qualified coach or clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Correctly identifies the pattern as central fatigue rather than peripheral (tissue) fatigue, citing the absent soreness and the slowing bar speed as evidence.
  • Explains that sequencing, a max-intent day placed directly before a technical day, degrades the technical session through a depleted central account, even with tissue that feels fresh.
  • Correctly describes a taper as holding intensity while cutting volume by roughly 60 to 90 percent, applied gradually, and distinguishes it from a deload.
  • Positions a short, low-volume priming session appropriately near the end, layered on top of the taper rather than replacing it.
  • Identifies what, if anything, warrants evaluation by a qualified coach or clinician, and does not diagnose.
