Chapter 3: Reason Through a Real Situation

Take the neural model of strength and use it to evaluate a real claim, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Emeka
Consider Emeka, who is six weeks into a structured strength program. In week one, Emeka could complete a set of leg presses at a modest load for a handful of clean repetitions. By week six, that load has climbed by roughly 25 percent, the movement looks noticeably more controlled on video, and Emeka feels more confident under load. A tape measurement of Emeka's thigh, taken at the start of the program and again in week six, shows almost no change. A friend, meaning well, tells Emeka that this is not real strength, just "neural tricks" that will disappear the moment training stops, and that only visible muscle growth counts as genuine progress. Separately, Emeka mentions a mild, dull ache on the front of one knee that has been present for about ten days, has not been improving, and shows up specifically when pressing through a deep bend in that knee.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what has most likely changed in Emeka's nervous system over these six weeks. Use at least two of the four neural contributors from the chapter and connect each one specifically to what Emeka is experiencing.
  2. Evaluate the friend's claim that Emeka's gain "is not real" and "will disappear." Treat these as two separate claims, and use specific evidence from the chapter, such as electromyography (EMG) findings, the gap between voluntary and electrically stimulated force, or cross-education research, to support or refute each part.
  3. Decide whether Emeka's knee ache should be reasoned about the same way as the strength gain, that is, as "probably just neural, nothing to worry about." Explain your answer using the distinction between how quickly the nervous system adapts and how quickly tendons and connective tissue adapt.
  4. Write the general-education response you would give Emeka about the ache. Be explicit about where general education ends and where the situation calls for a qualified clinician.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies at least two of the four neural contributors, fuller recruitment, higher firing rate, reduced antagonist co-contraction, or improved intermuscular coordination, and applies them specifically to Emeka's situation.
  • Evaluates the friend's claim point by point, addressing whether the gain is "not real" and whether it "will disappear" as two distinct questions, each supported with specific chapter evidence.
  • Distinguishes the timeline of neural adaptation from the slower timeline of tendon and connective tissue adaptation, and applies that distinction to the knee ache.
  • Offers a general-education response to the ache that does not diagnose and clearly states where general education ends and clinical care begins.
  • Written in clear, complete sentences a non-specialist could follow, with no one-word answers.
