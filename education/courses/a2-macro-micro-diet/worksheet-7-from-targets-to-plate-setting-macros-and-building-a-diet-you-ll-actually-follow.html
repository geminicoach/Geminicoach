Part D · Translate Targets into a Plate

Numbers on a screen do not feed anyone. Build a followable structure from three ideas: protein anchors, satiety levers, and built-in room for enjoyed foods.

  1. A daily target of about 165 g of protein is spread across three meals. Give a protein anchor for each meal (name a food and a rough gram figure), and explain why spreading protein across meals may use it better than saving it for one large dinner.
  2. A person can only manage two eating occasions because of a long commute and a late shift. What does the meal-frequency evidence let you tell them about meal count, and what is the one thing each of those two meals must still contain?
  3. Explain how fiber and fat work as satiety levers, and why a deficit built on higher-volume, lower-energy-density foods feels less like deprivation. Use the idea of energy density in your answer.
  4. Why should a well-built plate deliberately reserve a slice of calories for foods chosen purely for enjoyment? Explain why this is a load-bearing design feature and not a weakness in the plan.

Part E · Adherence Beats Composition

Use the "engineer adherence" logic from the chapter.

  1. Two landmark trials found that macro ratio barely predicted who lost weight, while sticking to the plan did. In your own words, state what a person actually assembling a diet should take from that, and what it means the goal of the design is.
  2. Distinguish rigid control from flexible control, giving one concrete behaviour that marks each. Then explain why "no clean foods, no dirty foods" follows directly from the flexibility evidence.
  3. A plan prescribes 1,450 kcal, six exact meals every three hours, named foods weighed to the gram, and a rule that nothing off the list is allowed. Name two specific ways this plan was designed to fail for a shift-working parent, and describe the rebuilt version of each.

Where education ends
This worksheet builds nutrition literacy and diet-design reasoning, not a license to diagnose, prescribe, or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how the framework works, and treat anything that looks clinical (a diagnosed disease, medication and lab management, a possible eating disorder, or other alarm signs) as warranting evaluation by a qualified professional. Work within your role, whatever it is: refer when something exceeds general education, and never self-diagnose.
