Chapter 5 : The Vitamin Spectrum

Thirteen vitamins, one organizing split, and a single dose axis with harm at both ends.

The Split That Organizes Everything

Fat-soluble (4)A, D, E, K. Need dietary fat to absorb (bile, micelles, chylomicrons, lymph). Stored in liver and fat, so they deplete slowly. Excess accumulates, so toxicity is a real risk.
Water-soluble (9)C and the eight Bs. Absorb easily in the gut's watery contents. Not banked in quantity, so intake must be consistent and deficiency develops faster. Surplus washes out, so toxicity from food is essentially a non-issue.

What Vitamins Do

  • Carry zero calories; they are not fuel.
  • Most act as coenzymes: helper tools enzymes need to run a reaction.
  • B vitamins help release energy already in food; they do not add it.
  • A (vision, gene regulation), D (acts like a hormone, calcium and bone), K (blood clotting) are regulators and enablers, not classic coenzymes.
  • Needed in mg or mcg, not the grams of macros. Small scale is why both deficiency and excess are easy to reach.

The Dose-Response Spectrum

  • Deficiency (left): too little, function breaks over time.
  • Sufficiency (middle): the healthy window; modest surplus handled fine.
  • Toxicity (right): so much the vitamin itself harms.
  • "More" is not a direction, it is a position on a curve that turns down at both ends.
  • RDA: a floor with cushion (meets ~97 to 98% of people). UL: the guardrail at the top. The gap is the width of the window.

Timelines: The Clock Runs Differently

  • Water-soluble: fast in, fast empty. Gaps show in weeks to months (scurvy is the classic).
  • Fat-soluble: stores buffer them, so gaps take months to years and are well advanced by the time they show.
  • B12: the exception. Water-soluble but banked in the liver, so a shortfall can take years to surface, then hits the nerves.

Signs: Patterns, Never Diagnoses

  • C: bleeding gums, bruising, poor wound healing.
  • A: night-vision loss, dry eyes, more infections.
  • D: bone pain and weakness (deficiency); hypercalcemia, confusion, nausea (toxicity).
  • B12 / folate: large-cell anemia; B12 adds numbness, tingling, balance and cognitive changes.
  • B6: megadose supplements can cause nerve damage (the water-soluble exception).
  • These overlap with countless conditions; confirming needs a clinician and usually a blood test.

Read the Pattern for Gaps

  • Omitting a whole food group risks the micronutrients that group supplies.
  • Vegan: B12 (and often D). B12 supplementation is a structural requirement.
  • Very low-fat: A, D, E, K, through the absorption mechanism.
  • Low produce: vitamin C and folate.
  • Supplements do not automatically close these gaps. Fix the food structure first.

Food-First and When Supplements Fit

  • Whole foods deliver vitamins with the fat and matrix that aid absorption, and make overdose nearly impossible.
  • Supplement when food cannot reasonably close the gap: B12 for strict vegans, folic acid for anyone who could become pregnant, vitamin D where sun is limited (ideally test-guided), confirmed deficiencies.
  • Megadosing a non-deficient person: flat upside, real downside (fat-soluble toxicity; B6 nerve damage; folate masking B12).
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the spectrum works and how to steer food choices, not a license to diagnose or treat. Supplements are tools with a working range, not treatments. Confirming a deficiency, interpreting blood work, dosing therapeutically, or anything touching pregnancy, medication interactions, or malabsorption warrants a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
