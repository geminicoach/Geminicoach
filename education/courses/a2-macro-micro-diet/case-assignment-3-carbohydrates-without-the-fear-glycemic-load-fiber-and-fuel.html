Chapter 3: Walk Someone Back From the Cliff

Use glycemic load, fiber, and fuel to explain a real spiral in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Marco, 34
Consider Marco, who arrives convinced he has "a sugar problem." Over the past six months, after a run of online videos about glycemic index, he has cut fruit, carrots, oats, rice, and bread. His days now run on chicken, eggs, and nuts, with almost no carbohydrate-rich plants. He reports being tired by mid-afternoon, constipated, and irritable around his morning workouts, and, despite all the restriction, no leaner than when he started. When you estimate it, his fiber intake is around 10 grams a day. His stated goal is steady energy and gradual fat loss. He asks: "Is sugar wrecking me, and what should I cut next?"

Your Tasks

  1. Separate glycemic index from glycemic load for him, and use that distinction to explain why the high-index foods he cut (watermelon, carrots, fruit) were not the threat he thought.
  2. Explain how the fiber gap accounts for most of his symptom list: the mid-afternoon crash, the constipation, and the lost satiety. Name the daily fiber range he is missing.
  3. Address the morning-workout irritability. Decide whether this is a carbohydrate-timing problem or a total-intake problem, and say how you know.
  4. Give the first change you would teach (addition or subtraction?), walk the priority hierarchy out loud, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, frames sugar with structure rather than shame, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. A diabetes diagnosis, medication or insulin dosing, and alarm signs belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Separates glycemic index (speed at a fixed 50 grams) from glycemic load (index scaled by the real portion) and applies it to the foods Marco cut.
  • Identifies the fiber gap as the driver of the crash, constipation, and lost satiety, and names the 25 to 29 gram range.
  • Frames the workout issue as low total daily carbohydrate, not a timing or "anabolic window" problem.
  • Corrects by addition rather than subtraction, ramped gradually with water, and states the priority hierarchy (total intake, then fiber and whole sources, then glycemic and timing trivia).
  • Frames sugar with structure not shame, identifies what warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
