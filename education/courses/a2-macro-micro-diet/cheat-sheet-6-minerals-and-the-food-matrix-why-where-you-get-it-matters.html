Chapter 6 : Minerals and the Food Matrix

The label reports total content. What you absorb depends on the source, the meal, and the preparation.

The Mineral Cast

MajorNeeded in larger daily amounts: calcium, magnesium, potassium, sodium, phosphorus, chloride. Structure, fluid balance, nerve and muscle signaling.
TraceNeeded in tiny amounts: iron, zinc, iodine, selenium, copper. "Trace" is the dose, not the importance.
Hard to getMinerals are charged and reactive, so they bind to other food components. That is why absorption, not intake, is the real number.

Two Numbers Not on the Label

  • Bioaccessibility : how much digestion releases from the food.
  • Bioavailability : how much you actually absorb and use.
  • The label lists only total content, neither of these.
  • Food matrix : what a nutrient is packaged with changes how much you get. A pill and a plate behave differently.

Enhancers & Inhibitors

  • Vitamin C : strong enhancer of non-heme iron.
  • Meat factor : animal-tissue peptides boost non-heme iron in the same meal.
  • Phytates : in grains and legumes, bind minerals (the plant's vault).
  • Polyphenols : tea and coffee block non-heme iron; keep them off iron meals.
  • Calcium : a big dose competes with iron in the same meal.

Heme vs Non-Heme Iron

  • Heme (animal) : stable 25 to 30 percent absorbed; resists inhibitors.
  • Non-heme (plant) : swings from roughly 2 to 20 percent, set by the meal.
  • Same milligrams, very different delivery.
  • Plant-based eaters absorb less by default, so they engineer meals on purpose. The gut also up-regulates absorption when stores fall.

Preparation & Patterns

  • Soaking / sprouting / fermenting : cut phytate a lot (up to about 85.6 percent in maize), raising mineral bioavailability.
  • Fiber can bind minerals, but the fix is pattern and preparation, not stripping out whole plant foods.
  • Zinc-copper seesaw : high-dose zinc can drive copper deficiency.
  • Think whole-diet, not spreadsheet.

Risk, and Supplements

  • Highest iron risk : menstruating women, pregnancy, endurance athletes, plant-based (compounds).
  • Iodine : risk is geographic; iodized salt keeps it rare.
  • Supplements help a specific, identified shortfall; they harm as blanket insurance (megadosing, narrow safe window for iron and selenium).
  • Keep iron pills away from children.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how absorption works, not a license to diagnose or treat. Suspected deficiency, persistent fatigue or performance loss, pregnancy, or a decision to supplement iron or other minerals at dose are signs to seek blood work and evaluation from a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
