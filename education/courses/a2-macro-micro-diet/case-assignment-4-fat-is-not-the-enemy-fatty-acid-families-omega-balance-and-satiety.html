Chapter 4: Reason Through a Stalled Cut

Take the fatty-acid families, the omega balance, and the fat floor and use them to explain a real complaint, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Sana, 34
Consider Sana, who decides to lean out and gets disciplined about it. She hits her protein every day, keeps her carbohydrate up to fuel training, and treats fat as the easiest place to cut calories because she read online that fat is "just energy." She drops added oils, switches to fat-free dressings, trims every visible bit of fat off her chicken, and skips the egg yolks. On paper it is a clean, high-protein, moderate-carb deficit, and for six weeks it works. Then around week eight her energy and drive fall off, her mood goes flat, her skin turns dry and her joints feel creaky, and her salads "taste like cardboard." She has started fantasizing about food at night. Her calories and protein are still exactly on target, so she is convinced she is doing everything right. She asks: "Is something wrong with me, or with the diet?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what is happening to Sana using the chapter, and say clearly why this is a problem of diet composition rather than a problem of the calorie deficit itself.
  2. Take at least three of her symptoms (low drive and flat mood, dry skin and creaky joints, the "cardboard" vegetables, the nighttime food thoughts) and map each one to a specific job fat was doing.
  3. Decide whether this reads as predictable physiology or something to worry about, and say how you know.
  4. Give a practical, general-education fix that keeps her goal intact, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis, medication, and the management of any lipid or hormonal condition belong to a qualified clinician or registered dietitian.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Frames the crash as a composition problem below the fat floor, not a fault of the calorie deficit.
  • Maps low drive and flat mood to fat as substrate for hormone production (steroidogenesis).
  • Maps dry skin, creaky joints, and dull-tasting vegetables to essential fats, omega-3, and fat-soluble vitamin absorption.
  • Explains the satiety and satisfaction failure that fuels cravings and diet abandonment.
  • Offers a redistribution fix that keeps the deficit and protein, restores the fat floor, and adds omega-3 rather than cutting omega-6.
  • Identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
