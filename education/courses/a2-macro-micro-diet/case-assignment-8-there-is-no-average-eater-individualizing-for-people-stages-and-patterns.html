The Scenario

Scenario · Anya, 69
Consider Anya, who is 69 and has eaten a fully plant-based diet for the last five years. She walks most days and does a little gardening. She describes her meals as "plenty of lentils, rice, vegetables, and fruit" and says she feels she is "eating healthy." Over the past two years she has noticed she tires more easily and that her grip has weakened, opening a jar now takes real effort. She takes no supplements. She has no diagnosed illness. She wonders whether her diet needs adjusting or whether this is "just age."

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, from mechanism, why Anya is a stack of two individualization forces (an older adult and a fully plant-based eater), and which lever each force pulls first. Make clear that individualizing means reordering known levers, not inventing new rules.
  2. Set a starting protein target for her, giving both a total (in g/kg) and a distribution plan, and justify it using anabolic resistance and the leucine threshold.
  3. Name the plant-based pressure points in her current pattern and give a concrete, "how not what" strategy for each, including the one nutrient a varied diet alone cannot fix.
  4. State clearly where general education ends: what in Anya's picture warrants a qualified clinician, and what you would not attempt to diagnose, interpret, or treat.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating mechanistic reasoning and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response individualizes food for a healthy person, identifies what warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose or interpret labs. Diagnosed disease, clinical pregnancy, disordered eating, and lab interpretation belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies the two stacked forces (older adult and plant-based) and frames individualization as reordering known levers, not inventing rules.
  • Older adult: explains anabolic resistance and the raised per-meal leucine threshold; sets total protein near 1.0 to 1.5 g/kg AND emphasizes distribution across meals.
  • Names the plant-based pressure points with correct fixes: B12 mandatory (supplement or fortified), iron as a bioavailability project (vitamin C pairing, soaking/sprouting), omega-3 as algae-based EPA/DHA.
  • Addresses protein quality: lower digestibility means aim a little higher and build variety / complementary proteins across the day.
  • Identifies the off-ramps, refers for possible deficiency work-up and lab interpretation, and does not diagnose.
