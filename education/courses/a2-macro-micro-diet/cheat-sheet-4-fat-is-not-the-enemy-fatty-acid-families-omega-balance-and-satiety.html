Chapter 4 : Fat Is Not the Enemy

Fat has real jobs. Sort it by shape, balance the omegas, and keep it above a floor.

The Three Families, by Shape

SaturatedNo double bonds, straight chains that pack tight, so often solid at room temperature and chemically stable. Butter, steak fat, coconut oil. Its single gram count is a weak health predictor; source and pattern matter more.
MonounsaturatedOne double bond, one kink, so liquid at room temperature but firms in the fridge. Olive oil, avocado, most nuts. The friendliest family and a sensible default cooking and dressing fat.
PolyunsaturatedTwo or more double bonds, liquid even when cold, and the fastest to go rancid. Seed oils (omega-6) and fish, flax, chia (omega-3). Contains the two essential fats.

The Essential Fats

  • ALA : omega-3, essential; from flax, chia, walnuts.
  • Linoleic acid : omega-6, essential; from seed oils.
  • EPA and DHA : long-chain omega-3; best from fatty fish because human conversion from ALA is inefficient (single-digit percent).
  • Arachidonic acid : made from omega-6; feeds a different set of signals. Both lines are needed.

The Omega Ratio

  • Evolutionary pattern near 1:1; modern Western diets near 15:1 omega-6 to omega-3.
  • Reasonable target: 4:1 or lower.
  • Both fats individually track with lower mortality; the risk is the lopsided ratio, meaning too little omega-3.
  • Fix by adding omega-3, not by torching omega-6.

Three Jobs Fat Cannot Skip

  • Membranes : builds cell walls; the brain is roughly 60% fat, rich in DHA.
  • Absorption : vitamins A, D, E, K and carotenoids need fat in the same meal. A fat-free salad delivers almost none of them.
  • Hormones : cholesterol from dietary fat is the substrate for steroid hormones; very low fat can lower testosterone in men.

Trans Fat: the Real Exception

  • Industrial partially hydrogenated oil raises LDL and lowers HDL, a uniquely bad combination.
  • Linked to higher coronary and all-cause mortality; the one fat term to minimize on sight.
  • Scan the ingredient list, not just the "0 g" front claim.
  • Natural ruminant trans fat in dairy and meat looks different in the data.

Satiety and the Fat Floor

  • Per calorie, fat is the least filling macro (protein > carb > fat), but it drives meal satisfaction.
  • Fat fills you up per gram, not per calorie, so form matters: nuts and avocado over poured oil.
  • Floor: roughly 20 to 35% of calories, rarely under about 0.5 g per kg.
  • Below the floor: hormones, vitamin absorption, mood, and adherence all pay a tax.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how fats and omega balance work, not a license to diagnose, treat, or manage medication. A diagnosed lipid disorder, medication decisions, a strong family history, or persistent symptoms are signs that warrant a qualified clinician or registered dietitian. Work within your role, whatever it is.
