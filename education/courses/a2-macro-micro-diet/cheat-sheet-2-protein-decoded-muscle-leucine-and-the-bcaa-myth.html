Chapter 2 : Protein, Decoded

One switch (muscle protein synthesis), one trigger (leucine), one rule (clear the threshold several times a day).

The Core Mechanism

Net balanceMuscle change = synthesis (MPS) minus breakdown (MPB), summed over days and weeks. Fasted = slight negative; a protein meal swings it positive for a few hours.
No storageThere is no amino acid tank. Fall short and the body breaks down its own muscle for materials, which is why dose and timing matter.
The thresholdA meal needs enough leucine, fast enough, to push MPS near its max. Past that, extra protein in one meal is oxidized, not built.
TrainingResistance work makes the muscle more responsive to the protein that follows for many hours. Load and feed are partners, not substitutes.

The Per-Meal Dose

  • Young adults : ~20 g quality protein maximally stimulates MPS; 40 g does not build more (Moore 2009; Witard 2014).
  • Older adults : anabolic resistance raises the bar to ~30 g protein / ~2.8 g leucine per meal (Layman 2024).
  • Distribution : ~20 g every 3 hours beat both big boluses and tiny doses over 12 h (Areta 2013).
  • Rule of thumb : a complete protein source at 3 to 4 meals a day.

Why Leucine

  • Leucine = the doorbell : it signals (mTORC1) that protein has arrived, start building.
  • Other amino acids = the materials : needed to actually finish the build.
  • 9 essential amino acids (EAAs) must come from food; 3 of them are BCAAs (leucine, isoleucine, valine).
  • Leucine content, not just gram count, decides if a meal crosses the threshold.

The BCAA Myth, Reasoned

  • True: leucine triggers MPS, and BCAA tubs contain leucine.
  • False jump: a new protein needs all 9 EAAs; BCAAs supply only 3.
  • Alone, they force the cell to break down muscle for the missing 6 bricks: a wash at best (Wolfe 2017).
  • Verdict: redundant once total protein is adequate. Not a scam, just food you already ate.

Protein Quality

  • Completeness : all 9 EAAs in the right proportions.
  • Digestibility : how much you actually absorb.
  • DIAAS > PDCAAS : ileal, uncapped, more accurate (Marinangeli & House 2017).
  • Plants: often lower in leucine and one EAA (grains low in lysine, legumes low in methionine). Variety across the day covers the gaps (van Vliet 2015).

Targets & Second Job

  • Daily target : ~1.6 to 2.2 g/kg/day for active or body-composition goals; 0.8 g/kg is just a floor.
  • Gains in lean mass level off near ~1.6 g/kg/day (Morton 2018).
  • Dieter's anchor : highest thermic effect and strongest satiety; protects lean mass in a deficit (Halton & Hu 2004; Leidy 2015).
  • Get total right first; distribution is the finishing move for older adults and trained lifters.

Threshold-Clearing Doses on a Plate

120 to 150 g meat/fish~30 to 40 g protein, clears the threshold on its own.
Dairy / eggsA cup of Greek yogurt or cottage cheese, 3 to 4 whole eggs, a scoop of whey, or a large glass of milk plus a small source.
Plant (larger portions)1.5 cups cooked lentils, a block of firm tofu, tempeh, or a couple cups of beans, often paired with a grain across the day. Soy is complete.
Do not anchor onA few nuts, a spoon of peanut butter, or a slice of bread. They contribute but quietly miss the threshold.
