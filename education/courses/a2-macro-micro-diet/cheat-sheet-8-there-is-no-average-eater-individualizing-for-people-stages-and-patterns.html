Chapter 8 : There Is No Average Eater

One framework, bent to a real person: learn the baseline cold, then layer named deviations for training, life stage, and pattern.

Baseline, Then Deviations

BaselineThe population default (e.g., the 0.8 g/kg protein RDA) is a deficiency floor, not an optimization target. Learn it first; you cannot bend a rule you never learned.
Force 1 : TrainingRaises the size of the targets. More hard work in the week means more protein and carbohydrate, and more attention to total energy.
Force 2 : Life stageChanges which targets are high-stakes: growth, older adulthood, pregnancy / lactation.
Force 3 : PatternChanges the route, not the goal: omnivore, vegetarian, vegan, and cultural cuisine shift the bioavailability math.
Force 4 : AdherencePreference, schedule, culture, and friction decide whether any plan happens. A plan not followed is the wrong plan.

Trained Needs

  • Protein : 1.2 to 2.0 g/kg/day for athletes; ~1.4 to 1.8 for hard-but-not-elite training.
  • Carbohydrate : scales to load, ~3 to 5 g/kg (light) up to 8 to 12 g/kg (heavy daily training).
  • Energy availability : foundational. A deep deficit diverts protein to fuel and stalls adaptation.
  • Fat floor : ~0.5 to 0.8 g/kg; do not crush it for carbs (essential fats, hormones).
  • Hydration : scales with volume and heat; the cheapest performance fix.

Life Stages

  • Growth : net tissue builders; food-first, never restrict, refer clinical concerns.
  • Older adult : anabolic resistance raises the per-meal leucine threshold. Total protein 1.0 to 1.5 g/kg AND spread across meals. Watch vitamin D, calcium, appetite.
  • Pregnancy / lactation : broad rise in needs; iron, B12, vitamin D, calcium, iodine, omega-3s monitored. Co-managed by a clinical team; support, do not write, the plan.

Plant-Based Pressure Points

  • Vitamin B12 : mandatory supplement or fortified food. A hard requirement, not a preference.
  • Iron & zinc : non-heme, less absorbed; pair with vitamin C, soak / sprout / ferment to cut phytate.
  • Omega-3 : plants give ALA; conversion is limited, so use algae-based EPA/DHA.

Protein Quality (Not Just Grams)

  • DIAAS / PDCAAS : score how much protein survives digestion and covers essential amino acids.
  • Single plant proteins score lower (legumes low in methionine, grains low in lysine).
  • Fixes: aim a little higher, build variety, combine complementary proteins across the day.

Fasting, Culture & Adherence

  • Ramadan / time-restricted : keep protein total and quality; hydration and carbohydrate slip first; train near a feeding window.
  • Cultural fit : move the lever using foods already in the kitchen, not an imported template.
  • Consistency : a "B-plus" plan run for a year beats an "A-plus" plan abandoned in three weeks.

The Lane and the Off-Ramps

  • In the lane : food-first individualization for healthy people, adjusting protein, carbs, bioavailability, and fit to the real life.
  • Off-ramp : diagnosed disease needing medical nutrition therapy (diabetes, kidney, celiac, IBD), clinical pregnancy, suspected disordered eating, and lab interpretation. Refer early.
Where education ends   This builds the judgment to individualize a general framework, not a license to diagnose, treat, or interpret labs. Diagnosed conditions, clinical pregnancy, disordered eating, and lab values are signs to route to a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
