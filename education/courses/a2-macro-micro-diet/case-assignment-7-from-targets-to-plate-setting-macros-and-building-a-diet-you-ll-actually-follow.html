Chapter 7: Rebuild a Plan for a Real Life

Take the whole workflow, numbers to plate, and use it to diagnose why a plan collapsed and to design one a real person could actually follow.

The Scenario

Scenario · Jonah, 42
Consider Jonah, who works rotating twelve-hour shifts and wants to lose about fifteen pounds. His previous plan, handed to him on a spreadsheet, set 1,450 calories, six exact meals spaced every three hours, specific named foods weighed to the gram, and a firm rule that nothing off the list ever touches his plate. For three weeks he was perfect. Then a night shift wrecked the meal timing, he missed two of the six meals, decided the day was ruined, and ate freely to make up for it. The pattern set in: rigid for a stretch, then a collapse, then guilt, then a rigid restart. Six weeks later he had lost almost nothing and felt like a failure. Jonah asks: "Do I just need more discipline, or was something wrong with the plan?"

Your Tasks

  1. Diagnose the plan. Using the chapter's framework, explain the specific ways this plan was engineered to fail for someone on a rotating shift, covering the size of the deficit, the six-meal timing rule, and the "nothing off the list" rule.
  2. Rebuild the numbers. Size a defensible goal adjustment and set macros in priority order as ranges (protein first in grams per kilogram, a fat floor, carbohydrate as the remainder). Show enough reasoning that someone could follow your logic.
  3. Rebuild the structure. Choose a meal frequency and tracking method that survive a chaotic week, justify each choice from the evidence, and say what every eating occasion must still contain.
  4. Answer Jonah's question directly, name adherence as the decisive variable, and state clearly where general nutrition education ends and where a qualified professional takes over.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating diet-design reasoning and plain-language communication, not diagnosing, prescribing, or treating. A responsible response reasons within general nutrition education, identifies anything that warrants professional evaluation (a diagnosed disease, medication or lab management, or a possible eating disorder), and refers it. Diagnosis, treatment, and medical nutrition therapy belong to a qualified professional.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Sizes a moderate, sustainable deficit tied to real maintenance rather than the deep 1,450-calorie cut.
  • Sets macros in priority order as ranges: protein first in grams per kilogram, fat to a floor, carbohydrate as the remainder.
  • Fits meal frequency to the shift with a protein anchor at each occasion, noting that frequency itself does not drive results while protein amount does.
  • Names adherence (flexible over rigid control) as the decisive variable and builds in slack and room for enjoyed foods.
  • Stays within general-education scope, refers anything clinical, and does not diagnose or prescribe.
