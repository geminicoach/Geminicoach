Chapter 1: When the Math Keeps Lying

Take the master variable and use it to explain, in plain language, why a diligent person's numbers are not adding up, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Raj, 38
Consider Raj, who wants to lose fat and likes to do things properly. He found a calorie calculator online, entered his stats, and it told him his maintenance was 2,600 kcal. He set himself a clean 500 kcal deficit at 2,100 kcal, started logging everything in an app, and waited for the promised half-kilogram a week. Three weeks in, his weight had barely moved, and the daily number bounced more than a kilogram in either direction, so he could not even tell what was happening. He works a desk job, has been sleeping poorly through a busy stretch, and is diligent about his log. "I am eating 2,100 calories," he says. "The math says I should be losing. Either the math is broken or I am."

Your Tasks

  1. Explain why the 2,600 kcal figure from the calculator is a starting hypothesis rather than a verified fact, and name the input most likely to have inflated it for someone with Raj's day.
  2. Account for why his logged 2,100 kcal might not be the deficit he thinks it is, using what the chapter says about self-reported intake.
  3. Explain why his scale bounces more than a kilogram day to day, and describe how he should read the signal instead of reacting to any single morning.
  4. Recommend a responsible next step, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation (for example a history of disordered eating or unexplained symptoms), and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies the calculator output as a population estimate and starting hypothesis, not a verified fact, and names the activity factor / low NEAT as the likely inflation.
  • Explains that honest, unconscious under-logging shrinks the real deficit, so the true gap is smaller than 500 kcal.
  • Separates daily scale noise (water, glycogen, gut contents, sleep, stress) from the underlying trend, and names the 7-day rolling average read over two to four weeks.
  • Recommends verifying real maintenance first (steady intake, trend reading) rather than cutting harder, and checks hunger, energy, and performance alongside the scale.
  • Stays within general education, identifies what warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
