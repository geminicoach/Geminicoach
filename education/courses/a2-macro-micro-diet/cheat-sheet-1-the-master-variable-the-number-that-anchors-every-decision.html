Chapter 1 : The Master Variable

Energy is the trunk of the tree. Establish the budget first, then spend it. Every macro decision sits downstream of the total.

TDEE : The Four Envelopes

RMR (~60-70%)Resting metabolic rate: the cost of staying alive at rest. Driven mostly by fat-free mass (muscle and organs). Usually the largest slice.
TEF (~5-15%)Thermic effect of food: the cost of digesting and processing meals. Highest for protein (about 20-30%), lowest for fat.
EAT (varies)Exercise activity thermogenesis: deliberate, structured training. Often a small slice for non-athletes.
NEAT (varies most)Non-exercise activity thermogenesis: walking, standing, fidgeting, chores. The most variable component, differing by up to 2,000 kcal/day between similar people.

Estimating Maintenance

Men: RMR = (10 x wt) + (6.25 x ht) - (5 x age) + 5
Women: RMR = (10 x wt) + (6.25 x ht) - (5 x age) - 161
TDEE = RMR x activity factor
  • Mifflin-St Jeor : best available RMR predictor; wt in kg, ht in cm, age in years.
  • Activity factor : about 1.2 (sedentary) to 1.9 (very heavy). The sloppiest input, because it is a behavioral guess.
  • The output is a hypothesis, not a fact. Individual error plus factor error can put it 300-500 kcal off.

Setting the Goal

  • Deficit : intake below maintenance, to lose mass.
  • Surplus : intake above maintenance, to gain mass.
  • Maintenance : intake at the number, to hold. A real goal, not just a baseline.
  • About 7,700 kcal per kg of body fat. Frame the cut as a percentage: about 15-25% below for fat loss, only 5-15% above for muscle gain.
  • Bigger is not better, only faster. The best deficit is the largest one a person can sustain without hunger, energy, sleep, or adherence falling apart.

A Dynamic System

  • Calories in and out are linked, not independent dials. Change one and the body adjusts the other.
  • Adaptation : cut intake and RMR, NEAT, and TEF all fall, so a 500 kcal deficit can quietly become 250.
  • Constrained model : total expenditure may plateau at high activity rather than rising forever (Pontzer et al., 2016).
  • Sleep and stress : short sleep raises appetite and lowers movement. Fixing sleep is an energy-balance lever, not a soft add-on.

Trend, Not Noise

  • A single day can swing 1-2 kg on water, glycogen, gut contents, sodium, and hormones. That is not fat.
  • 7-day rolling average : smooths the bounce so the real trend appears. Read it over 2-4 weeks.
  • Watch three signals alongside the scale: hunger, energy, performance.
  • The scale trend is the source of truth; the equation and the food log are only estimates.

Where Estimates Go Wrong

  • Activity factor : the biggest error; people overestimate how active they are.
  • Body composition : the equation cannot see fat-free mass differences at the same weight.
  • Under-reporting : self-reported intake runs low, often by a lot, and it is the rule, not the exception. Verify against the trend.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the energy budget works, not a license to diagnose or treat. Unexplained weight change, a history of disordered eating, or other alarm signs are reasons to involve a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
