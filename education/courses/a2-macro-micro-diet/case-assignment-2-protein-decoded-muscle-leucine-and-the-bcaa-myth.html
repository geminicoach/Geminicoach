Chapter 2: Reason Through the Tub

Take the leucine-threshold mechanism and use it to explain a real, frustrating plateau in plain language, within the bounds of general nutrition education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Ellie, 34
Consider Ellie, who lifts weights four mornings a week and is frustrated that her strength and muscle have stalled for months. She weighs about 68 kilograms. She sips a fruit-punch BCAA tub during every session because an ad promised it would "feed the muscle." When her actual eating is laid out, it looks like this: breakfast is coffee and a piece of fruit, lunch is a salad with a small amount of chicken (maybe 20 grams of protein), she trains before dinner on a near-empty stomach, and dinner is large, around 65 grams of protein and usually the only serious protein of her day. Her daily total lands near 95 grams. She has no diagnosed medical condition. She wonders: "Do I need a better supplement, or am I just not built to gain muscle?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what is happening across Ellie's day using net balance (synthesis minus breakdown) and the per-meal MPS threshold. Why does one big dinner ring the doorbell only once?
  2. Evaluate her daily total in grams per kilogram, and explain why both the amount and the distribution are working against her.
  3. Reason out what the BCAA tub is and is not doing during a fasted session, using the brick model (three of nine essential amino acids). State the honest verdict.
  4. Give a concrete, general-education fix built from the chapter's mechanism, and state clearly where general nutrition education ends and professional or clinical care begins.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing or prescribing a diet for a medical condition. A responsible response explains the mechanism, points to professional evaluation where warranted (for example, kidney disease, an eating disorder, or unexplained muscle loss), and does not diagnose. Medical nutrition therapy belongs to a registered dietitian or physician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains net balance (synthesis minus breakdown) and why the per-meal MPS threshold means one big dinner is mostly wasted past the first 30 to 40 grams.
  • Identifies that Ellie's total protein is too low (near 1.3 to 1.4 g/kg) AND badly distributed, using rough numbers.
  • Reasons the BCAA myth from the brick model: three of nine essential amino acids, so on an empty stomach the cell must break down existing muscle to supply the rest; redundant once total protein is adequate.
  • Gives a concrete, mechanism-based fix: raise total toward ~1.6 to 2.0 g/kg, add complete leucine-rich protein at breakfast and lunch, eat a real meal around training, shrink the dinner overshoot, retire the tub.
  • Stays within general-education scope, refers clinical or medical questions to a qualified professional, and does not diagnose or prescribe.
