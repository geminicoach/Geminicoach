Chapter 3 : Carbohydrates Without the Fear

One fuel, many packages: speed vs quantity, fiber first, and timing only when the tank runs low.

The Two Numbers, Kept Apart

Glycemic index (GI)Speed: how fast and high a fixed 50 g of a food's carbohydrate raises glucose. Low ≤55, medium 56-69, high ≥70. Ignores portion.
Glycemic load (GL)Speed × portion: GL = (GI × grams available carb per serving) ÷ 100. Low ≤10, medium 11-19, high ≥20.
WatermelonGI ~76 but only ~6 g carb per 120 g serving, so GL ~5. Scary index, trivial load. Both true; they answer different questions.

What Blunts a Glucose Curve

  • Soluble fiber : gels in the gut, slows glucose to a drip.
  • Fat : slows gastric emptying, delivers carb gradually.
  • Protein : slows emptying, primes insulin and gut hormones.
  • Intact structure : starch locked in cell walls digests slowly.
  • Fix a "spiky" meal by adding to it, not banning the carb.

Fiber : Two Kinds, Two Jobs

  • Soluble : gels; oats, beans, apples, psyllium, barley. Slows emptying, boosts satiety, softens the curve.
  • Insoluble : bulks; wheat bran, skins, seeds. Speeds transit, supports regularity.
  • Most plants carry both. Do not track the ratio; eat varied plants.
  • Aim for 25-29 g/day; ramp gradually with water.

Fermentation & the Gut

  • Bacteria ferment some fibers into short-chain fatty acids (acetate, propionate, butyrate).
  • Butyrate fuels the colon lining; feeding your bacteria feeds the gut wall.
  • Variety feeds a diverse microbiome; supplements do not.
  • Early gas or bloating is adaptation, not damage.

Fuel : Two Glycogen Tanks

  • Liver ~80-120 g : feeds blood glucose between meals and overnight.
  • Muscle ~300-500 g : selfish; fuels only its own contractions.
  • ~90-120 min of hard endurance work before muscle stores run low.
  • A lifting session or a jog uses a slice; normal eating refills it.

When Timing Actually Matters

  • Carb availability : are stores plus intake enough for the work?
  • Matters for long high-intensity or repeated same-day efforts (30-60 g carb/hr past 60 min; fast refuel between bouts).
  • Rounding error for a walk or one lifting session; the tank stays full.
  • For most people, total daily intake beats the clock.

Refined vs Whole

  • Whole grain = bran + germ + endosperm; refining keeps mostly the starch.
  • Milling shatters cell walls, so exposed starch digests fast: taller curve, same grams.
  • "Whole grain" on a label but pulverized to fine flour loses much of the edge; structure does the work.
  • Favor a default (intact over stripped), not a ban.

The Priority Hierarchy

  • 1. Total daily intake matched to goals and activity.
  • 2. Fiber & whole sources : strongest outcome evidence in the chapter.
  • 3. GI & timing trivia : a distant third for most people.
  • Quality (the form carb arrives in) predicts outcomes better than raw quantity.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how carbohydrate works, not a license to diagnose or treat. A diabetes diagnosis, medication or insulin dosing, unexplained weight loss, constant thirst, or other alarm signs warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Frame sugar with structure, not shame, and work within your role, whatever it is.
