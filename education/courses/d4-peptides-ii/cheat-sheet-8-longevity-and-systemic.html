Chapter 8 : Longevity and Systemic Miscellany

Four compounds, same confident marketing tone, four very different rungs on the evidence ladder.

Epithalon

  • What: synthetic tetrapeptide (AEDG), from an earlier pineal extract, epithalamin.
  • Claim: activates telomerase, extends lifespan, regulates pineal-melatonin axis.
  • Evidence: cell-culture and rodent data, mostly one research group, no controlled human trial.
  • Caution: telomerase activation has theoretical relevance to cancer biology.

Humanin

  • What: peptide encoded in mitochondrial DNA (a mitochondrial-derived peptide).
  • Claim: broad cytoprotection, especially neurons and cardiac tissue.
  • Mechanism: blunts pro-apoptotic signaling (binds BAX); secreted form activates JAK2/STAT3 survival signaling.
  • Evidence: overwhelmingly preclinical; human data is correlational, not interventional.

ARA-290 (Cibinetide)

  • What: engineered from erythropoietin, targets the innate repair receptor, not the classical EPO receptor.
  • Claim: nerve protection and repair, without erythropoietic (blood-boosting) effects.
  • Evidence: two completed phase 2 trials in sarcoidosis-associated small fiber neuropathy.
  • Status: strongest human evidence in this chapter, still investigational, not approved.

VIP (Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide)

  • What: naturally occurring neuropeptide, acts on VPAC1/VPAC2 receptors.
  • Claim: immune and inflammatory modulation, shifting T cells toward a regulatory state.
  • Evidence: one small, open-label, 20-patient, 4-week study, inhaled, in sarcoidosis, no placebo arm.
  • Note: strong decades-old mechanism, thin clinical testing.
