The Scenario

Scenario · Naledi, 27
Naledi works in marketing and is currently taking a prescribed SSRI for generalized anxiety. During a busy stretch at work, she came across forum posts pairing "semax for focus" during crunch periods with "selank for anxiety" before big presentations, and separately saw delta sleep-inducing peptide (DSIP) mentioned for sleep. She has not tried any of these compounds. Before she brings anything up with her prescriber, she wants to understand what each one is actually proposed to do, mechanistically, and how strong the evidence behind each claim really is.

Your Tasks

  1. State the intended use and mechanism for semax (focus) and selank (anxiety) separately and precisely, using the chapter's language.
  2. Grade the evidence for semax's focus claim and selank's anxiety claim, distinguishing rodent or preclinical data from confirmed human findings, and noting each compound's regulatory history in Russia versus its evidence outside Russia.
  3. Identify the specific, genuine consideration in Naledi's situation, her existing SSRI prescription, and explain why it matters given what the chapter says about interaction data for these compounds, without recommending she start or stop anything.
  4. Give a responsible response and state clearly where general education ends and where a conversation with her prescriber is warranted.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language evaluation of a claim, not diagnosing Naledi or telling her what to take. A responsible response explains the mechanisms and the evidence honestly, names what if anything would warrant professional input, and does not diagnose. Decisions about combining any compound with a prescribed medication belong with a licensed prescriber.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Correctly states semax's intended use and mechanism (ACTH(4-10) fragment, BDNF/NGF, dopaminergic).
  • Correctly states selank's intended use and mechanism (tuftsin fragment, GABAergic tone, autonomic balance, enkephalin).
  • Grades the evidence honestly for both, separating rodent data and Russian regulatory history from independent human confirmation.
  • Identifies the unstudied interaction risk with her SSRI as the genuine reason to involve her prescriber, without making it the whole answer.
  • Contains no dosing, cycle, stack, or protocol recommendation.
