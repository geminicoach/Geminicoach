Chapter 7 : The Melanocortin Switchboard

One precursor hormone, five receptors, and two headline compounds that share biology but not evidence.

The Five Melanocortin Receptors

MC1RSkin melanocytes; pigmentation switch (eumelanin vs pheomelanin) plus local anti-inflammatory tone.
MC2RAdrenal cortex; the ACTH receptor, drives HPA axis cortisol output.
MC3RHypothalamus and peripheral immune tissue; energy balance and inflammatory tone.
MC4RHypothalamus; appetite and satiety plus central sexual arousal circuits.
MC5RExocrine glands (sebaceous, lacrimal); secretory gland output.

Bremelanotide (PT-141, Vyleesi)

  • Mechanism: central MC4R/MC3R agonist in hypothalamic circuits.
  • Approved use: acquired, generalized HSDD in premenopausal women.
  • Evidence: RECONNECT phase 3 RCTs plus a year-long open-label extension.
  • Labeled risk: transient blood pressure rise, nausea, flushing, headache.

Melanotan II

  • Mechanism: non-selective MC1R, MC3R, MC4R agonist.
  • Marketed for: tanning (MC1R) and libido (MC3R/MC4R overlap).
  • Evidence: no completed controlled human trial for either use; manufacturer promotion and anecdote.
  • Documented harm: case report of new pigmented lesion and confirmed melanoma following use.

KPV: The Counterexample

  • A three-amino-acid fragment of alpha-MSH (Lys-Pro-Val).
  • Anti-inflammatory in skin and gut, not a classic MC1R agonist.
  • Does not produce pigmentation or arousal effects.
  • Shared ancestry with alpha-MSH is not shared mechanism.

Why Receptor Selectivity Is Hard

  • ACTH and the alpha-MSH family share a four-amino-acid core recognized by every melanocortin receptor.
  • That shared core makes a truly single-receptor melanocortin drug mechanically difficult to build.
  • Bremelanotide concentrates mostly on MC4R/MC3R; melanotan II spreads across MC1R, MC3R, and MC4R.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of receptor biology and evidence grading, not a license to diagnose, prescribe, or recommend a compound. A new or changing pigmented lesion warrants evaluation by a qualified dermatologist. Work within your role, whatever it is.
