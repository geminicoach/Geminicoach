Case Assignment · D4 Peptides II

Chapter 7: Two Tabs, One Receptor Family

Use the melanocortin system to explain why two chemically related peptides occupy such different evidence and regulatory tiers.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Priya, 33
Priya is a learner working through this course who came across two peptide-vendor pages in the same week. One described bremelanotide, referencing an FDA approval and a prescription pathway. The other described melanotan II, sold with no prescription required, promising a tan plus a libido boost, and using nearly identical language about "activating melanocortin receptors." Priya also noticed a small, darkened spot on her forearm that has looked slightly different over the past few weeks; she has never used either compound but wants to understand what a changing mole actually means in this context before she reads any further. She wants to know whether the two products actually belong in the same category, and what the evidence says about each.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanistically, what bremelanotide and melanotan II share at the receptor level, and where their receptor activity diverges.
  2. Contrast the evidence behind each compound's marketed use: what supports bremelanotide's approval, and what melanotan II's claims actually rest on.
  3. Explain the mechanism connecting systemic MC1R activation to pigmented-lesion and melanoma risk, and why that risk is specific to melanotan II rather than bremelanotide.
  4. Say what, if anything, in this scenario would warrant a referral to a clinician, and be clear about where general education ends.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism and evidence grade, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose a skin finding or recommend a compound. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the shared receptor overlap (MC3R/MC4R) without treating it as shared safety or approval status.
  • Contrasts bremelanotide's RCT-supported evidence (the RECONNECT phase 3 trials) with melanotan II's case-report-only evidence.
  • Explains the mechanism linking systemic MC1R activation to pigmented-lesion and melanoma risk.
  • Keeps the discussion evidence-grade and mechanism-focused; does not recommend or endorse either compound.
  • Identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation (a changing mole), and does not diagnose.
