Chapter 9 : The Body's Messengers

Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) is a demand signal, not a supply gauge. Total testosterone is a sum of bound and free hormone. Both are read against age, timing, and life stage, never a single flat line.

The Thyroid Axis, In Order

HypothalamusReleases thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH), prompting the pituitary.
PituitaryReleases thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), a request for more thyroid hormone. Rises when the thyroid falls short, falls when the thyroid has more than met the request.
Thyroid glandReleases free thyroxine (free T4) and a smaller amount of free triiodothyronine (free T3) in response to TSH.
FeedbackOnce free T4 and free T3 are sufficient, TRH and TSH throttle back. A thermostat, not a one-way dial.

Reading TSH Correctly

  • High TSH : brain asking loudly; thyroid underactive (hypothyroid pattern).
  • Low TSH : brain gone quiet; thyroid overactive (hyperthyroid pattern).
  • Population range: roughly 0.4 to 4.0, sometimes 4.5. Built from statistical percentiles, not a proven health/disease line.
  • Commonly cited functional target for a healthy young adult: roughly 1 to 2. Evidence-graded, still contested, never a diagnostic line or a self-treat target.
  • A borderline TSH near the floor (like 0.7 in a 22-year-old) clears the population range but still warrants a look at free T4 and free T3 before treating it as fine.

Free T4, Free T3, and TPO Antibodies

  • Free T4 : unbound, active reservoir hormone; the correct second test when TSH raises a question.
  • Free T3 : more biologically potent; made partly by the thyroid, partly by tissue conversion of T4. Useful when symptoms and TSH/free T4 do not match.
  • TPO antibodies : thyroid peroxidase (TPO) is an enzyme inside thyroid cells; antibodies against it are a hallmark of autoimmune (Hashimoto) thyroiditis, even when TSH and free T4 still look normal.

Age and Pregnancy Reset the Range

  • TSH drifts upward with healthy aging, without a matching fall in free T4. A number that prompts a look at 25 can be unremarkable at 75.
  • Never paste a young-adult functional target (roughly 1 to 2) onto an older adult's chart.
  • Pregnancy: rising pregnancy hormones (via human chorionic gonadotropin) suppress TSH, especially in the first trimester.
  • Trimester-specific ranges apply, with a lower TSH ceiling (commonly cited around 4.0) in early pregnancy, returning toward the standard non-pregnant range by the third trimester.

Testosterone: What the Number Is Built From

Total testosteroneEvery molecule in blood, bound or free. The default number most panels report.
Sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG)Carrier protein, mostly liver-made, that locks away roughly 44% of circulating testosterone. Rises with age, overactive thyroid, liver disease; falls with obesity, insulin resistance, underactive thyroid.
Free testosteroneThe unbound, biologically active fraction, roughly 1 to 2% of the total. Often calculated from total, SHBG, and albumin rather than measured directly.
Diurnal rhythmPeaks in the early morning; can fall 20 to 25% by afternoon in young men. Rhythm flattens with age. Morning draws are the standard for evaluation.

Why the Same Total Can Mislead

  • Rising SHBG can hide a falling free testosterone behind a stable or rising total.
  • Falling SHBG (common with obesity) can make a modest total look more worrying on paper than the bioavailable reality.
  • An afternoon draw in a young man can read 20 to 25% below the true morning value, for no reason other than the clock.
  • A low-looking value plus a mistimed draw calls for a repeat morning draw, ideally with SHBG, before any conclusion.

Age Bands for Testosterone

  • Healthy young men (about 19 to 39): roughly 264 to 916 ng/dL (2.5th to 97.5th percentile), already a wide band.
  • Average decline: roughly 1 to 2% per year through mid and later adulthood, a gradual drift, not a cliff.
  • A value that is unremarkable at 65 can be a meaningful decline at 25, and the reverse mismatch is just as common a misreading.
  • A genuine evaluation requires consistent, correctly measured, correctly timed testosterone plus compatible symptoms, weighed by a clinician, never one number against one universal cutoff.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of two feedback systems, not a license to diagnose or treat. A discordant panel, a new neck swelling, a resting heart rate that will not settle, unexplained weight change, or persistent symptoms alongside a borderline result are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is, and never chase a functional target or a supplement on your own.
