Chapter 2: Reason Through a Real Report

Take the tools of position, life stage, and personal baseline and use them to reason through a real set of results, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Iris, 45
Consider Iris, who is 45 and just received her annual bloodwork. Her hemoglobin reads 12.1 g/dL against a printed range of 12.0 to 15.5 g/dL, and her fasting glucose reads 97 mg/dL against a printed range of 70 to 99 mg/dL. Neither value is flagged on the report; both print in plain text. Iris has been strength training consistently for the past three years and has lost fifteen pounds over that time. Bloodwork from three years ago, before she started training, showed her hemoglobin at 13.4 g/dL and her fasting glucose at 88 mg/dL. Iris looks at this year's unflagged report and asks: "Is something wrong? Should I be worried?"

Your Tasks

  1. Using position-within-range logic, explain roughly where each of Iris's two values sits inside its printed interval, and why an unflagged report does not fully answer whether either value is unremarkable.
  2. Explain what, if anything, about Iris's life stage and training history might mean the standard adult interval is not the most precise comparison for her, drawing on the chapter's discussion of life-stage banding.
  3. Use Iris's three-year trend to reason about whether either value looks like a meaningful personal change or an ordinary continuation, drawing on the chapter's discussion of personal baseline.
  4. Give Iris a general-education response that explains the mechanisms involved, does not diagnose, and states clearly where general education ends and professional evaluation begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician, regardless of what role you hold when you read a report.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies the approximate zone of both the hemoglobin value (near the floor) and the glucose value (near the ceiling), rather than treating an unflagged report as fully reassuring.
  • Explains that Iris's age and training history are life-stage and activity context the standard adult interval was not built specifically around.
  • Uses the three-year trend, hemoglobin down and glucose up, as more informative than either single value read alone, in the spirit of the chapter's personal baseline discussion.
  • Offers a practical, general-education response within scope that explains mechanism rather than assigning a cause.
  • Identifies what, if anything, warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
