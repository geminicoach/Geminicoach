Chapter 7: Reason Through a Real Iron Panel

Take the ferritin, transferrin saturation, and C-reactive protein model and use it to explain a real pair of results, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Esther, 29
Consider Esther, who runs competitively and menstruates regularly. Her first iron panel, drawn during a stretch of unusual fatigue, showed ferritin at 22 micrograms per liter, transferrin saturation (TSAT) at 14 percent, and an elevated total iron-binding capacity (TIBC), a textbook picture of low iron stores. She started an iron supplement and adjusted her diet. Eight weeks later, about ten days after spraining her ankle on a trail run, she had the panel redrawn. This time ferritin came back at 58 micrograms per liter, comfortably inside the laboratory's normal range, but her transferrin saturation (TSAT) was still only 16 percent, and no C-reactive protein (CRP) was ordered with either draw. Her hemoglobin has stayed within normal limits on both panels. Esther reads the second report as good news and assumes the supplement worked, though she still feels flat on long runs and asks: "My ferritin looks normal now. Am I actually fixed?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain what ferritin's two roles, storage marker and acute-phase reactant, mean for how the second panel should be read, and explain specifically why the recent ankle injury and the missing C-reactive protein (CRP) result matter here.
  2. Use the transferrin saturation (TSAT) values from both panels, together with the elevated total iron-binding capacity (TIBC) noted on the first panel, to decide whether Esther's iron status looks genuinely resolved, and explain your reasoning.
  3. Connect this case to the iron deficiency without anemia progression and to the microcytic, hypochromic pattern from the complete blood count (CBC) chapter. Explain why Esther's normal hemoglobin does not settle the question either way.
  4. Considering that Esther is a menstruating endurance athlete, explain why a "normal" ferritin threshold might not be reassuring for her specifically, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.
