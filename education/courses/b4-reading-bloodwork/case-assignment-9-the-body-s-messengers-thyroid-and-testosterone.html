The Scenario

Scenario · Colby, 26
Consider Colby, who has spent the last few months feeling persistently low on energy and mentally foggy despite a fairly steady sleep schedule. Colby ordered a direct-to-consumer panel that included thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) and total testosterone. The report came back with green checkmarks next to both: TSH at 0.6, inside the lab's printed range of roughly 0.4 to 4.5, and total testosterone inside its printed range as well, drawn at 5:30 in the evening after a long workday. No free thyroxine (free T4), free triiodothyronine (free T3), or sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) was included in the panel. While reading around online, Colby found a forum thread describing a TSH "under 1" as borderline and is now considering ordering an over-the-counter thyroid support supplement before the next round of testing. Colby wants to know whether the report is actually telling the whole story, and whether the supplement is a reasonable next step.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain what Colby's TSH of 0.6 does and does not tell us, using the demand-signal model from the chapter, and state what should be checked next before drawing any conclusion.
  2. Explain why the testosterone result, drawn at 5:30 p.m., cannot be trusted as Colby's true baseline, and describe what should happen before treating it as one.
  3. Evaluate Colby's plan to start an over-the-counter thyroid supplement in response to the "borderline" language seen online. Explain why this falls outside general education and what a responsible response says instead.
  4. State clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins in Colby's case, and say what, if anything, would count as a sign warranting medical evaluation here.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis, treatment, and any supplement or hormone decision belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Reads TSH 0.6 as a demand signal near the floor of the range, not a verdict; correctly notes that a low TSH points toward the overactive side of the thyroid axis.
  • Names free T4, and free T3 where the picture stays unclear, as the correct next test rather than accepting the green checkmark at face value.
  • Explains the diurnal testosterone rhythm and why a 5:30 p.m. draw cannot stand in for a morning baseline; recommends a repeat morning draw, ideally with SHBG.
  • Identifies self-directed thyroid supplementation as outside general education, names the risk of pushing a stable thyroid axis off balance, and redirects to a qualified clinician.
  • States plainly where general education ends and clinical care begins, and does not diagnose Colby with any thyroid or testosterone condition.
