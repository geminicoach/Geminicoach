The Scenario

Scenario · Maren, 44
Consider Maren, who works a standard daytime schedule at a logistics company and runs three or four times a week, including a longer run most Sunday mornings. Her annual panel arrived in her patient portal two days after one of those long Sunday runs, drawn at 7:30 a.m. after an overnight fast. Three values are flagged: her aspartate aminotransferase (AST) is high at 58 U/L, her ferritin is low at 19 ng/mL, and her thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) is high at 4.3 mIU/L. Sitting quietly nearby, unflagged: her C-reactive protein (CRP) is normal at 0.4 mg/L, her gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT) and bilirubin are both normal, her mean corpuscular volume (MCV) is mid-range at 89 fL, and her transferrin saturation is low-normal at 14 percent. Maren read only the three red lines, assumed something was wrong with her liver, her iron, and her thyroid all at once, and is asking: "Is my whole body falling apart, or am I missing something?"

Your Tasks

  1. Read Maren's panel as a whole. For each of the three flagged values, run the four moves from the chapter (position, age band and life stage, reference interval versus decision limit, and confounders) and explain what each move tells you.
  2. Apply the relevant cross-check to each flagged value, using the quiet, unflagged values in the scenario, and explain whether each flag should be downgraded, upgraded, or left as is.
  3. For at least one finding, propose a plausible benign explanation and a legitimate concern that a clinician should still weigh.
  4. State clearly which findings, if any, warrant a conversation with a clinician, phrase them as specific questions Maren could bring to that conversation, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating the ability to read a full panel and communicate it responsibly, not diagnosing. A responsible response runs the four moves, applies the relevant cross-checks, proposes benign explanations alongside legitimate concerns, and identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, phrased as questions rather than a verdict. Interpretation is literacy; diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Reads the panel as a whole, a constellation, rather than reacting to any single flagged line in isolation.
  • Correctly runs the four moves (position, age band, reference interval versus decision limit, confounders) on the flagged values.
  • Correctly applies at least one named cross-check (ferritin against CRP, AST against recent exercise and GGT/bilirubin, or a comparable pairing) to explain or contextualize a finding.
  • Proposes a plausible benign explanation and a legitimate concern for at least one out-of-range finding.
  • Identifies specifically which findings warrant a clinician conversation, phrased as questions, not a self-issued diagnosis or a recommended treatment.
