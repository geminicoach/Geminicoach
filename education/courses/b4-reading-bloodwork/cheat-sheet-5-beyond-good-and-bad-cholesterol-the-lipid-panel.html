Chapter 5 : Beyond Good and Bad Cholesterol

Lipoproteins are transport particles, not a two-team scoreboard. The fuller picture reads better than any single number.

The Core Particles

Low-density lipoprotein (LDL)Cholesterol-dense outbound particle, labeled with apolipoprotein B (apoB); can lodge in the artery wall. Measures cholesterol mass, not particle count.
High-density lipoprotein (HDL)Smallest, densest particle, labeled with apolipoprotein A1 (apoA1); runs the return route (reverse cholesterol transport) back to the liver.
Very low-density lipoprotein (VLDL)Largest, most triglyceride-rich particle, made by the liver; also apoB-labeled and can contribute to arterial risk.
TriglyceridesThree fatty acid chains on a glycerol backbone; the dominant cargo of VLDL and, after a meal, chylomicrons. The most behaviorally responsive marker of the three.
Total cholesterol (TC)The sum of cholesterol carried across every particle class.

Seeing the Fuller Fleet

  • Non-HDL cholesterol : total cholesterol minus HDL cholesterol; automatically captures LDL, VLDL, IDL, and lipoprotein(a) together.
  • Apolipoprotein B (apoB) : counts atherogenic particles directly, one apoB molecule per particle, regardless of cholesterol mass.
  • Discordance : when two people share an LDL cholesterol reading but carry meaningfully different particle counts.
  • TC/HDL and TG/HDL ratios : quick, informal pattern-recognition tools, not diagnostic tests on their own.

Fasting vs Non-Fasting

  • Fasting is not routinely required for most of a lipid profile.
  • Total cholesterol and HDL cholesterol barely shift after eating; non-HDL cholesterol shifts even less.
  • Triglycerides rise modestly after a meal, the one marker fasting status meaningfully affects.
  • Fasting still matters if triglycerides run high (roughly 400 mg/dL or more) or when the Friedewald calculation is used.
  • Compare fasting to fasting, and non-fasting to non-fasting, for a genuine trend.

What Actually Moves

  • Triglycerides : most responsive, often within days. Driven up by refined carbohydrate, added sugar, and alcohol; lowered by visceral fat loss.
  • HDL cholesterol : modest, dose-dependent response to sustained aerobic training. Drugs built to raise HDL alone largely failed to cut cardiovascular events.
  • LDL cholesterol : most stubborn to diet; responds moderately to sustained saturated fat reduction, most reliably to medication when clinically appropriate.

Cutoffs Are Decisions, Not Grades

  • Desirable, borderline, and high are outcome-based decision limits from outcomes research, not population percentiles.
  • What counts as reasonable shifts with age and life stage; cumulative years of LDL exposure appear to matter, not just the level at one moment.
  • "In range" is not the same claim as "optimal."
  • Researchers still debate optimal targets in older adults and which marker, LDL, non-HDL, or apoB, should anchor treatment.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the lipid panel works, not a license to diagnose or treat. Triglycerides near or above 500 milligrams per deciliter, or an LDL cholesterol in the 190s alongside a family history of early heart disease, are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
