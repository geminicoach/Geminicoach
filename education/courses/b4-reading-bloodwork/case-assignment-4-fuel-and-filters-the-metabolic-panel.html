Chapter 4: Reason Through a Real Panel

Take the fuel-and-filters framework from the chapter and use it to sort a real, mostly-in-range panel into what can wait, what should resolve on its own, and what deserves a real conversation.

The Scenario

Scenario · Renata, 42
Consider Renata, who trains for long-distance cycling on weekends and just received her annual metabolic panel. Her fasting glucose read 93 mg/dL and her hemoglobin A1c read 5.5 percent, both comfortably normal. Her fasting insulin, however, was flagged high-normal, and looking back at her last two annual panels, that number has been climbing steadily each year even though her glucose and A1c have not moved. Her sodium came back at 133 mmol/L, drawn about ninety minutes after a long, hot Saturday ride during which she had been drinking heavily at every rest stop to "stay ahead of dehydration." And her creatinine was mildly elevated, nudging her estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) into a flagged range, drawn two days after a demanding block of hill-climb training. Renata wants to know which of these three results, if any, actually deserves a real conversation with her doctor.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain what is happening with Renata's sodium result mechanically, and say whether it is a value that should resolve on its own with rest and normal hydration, or one that should be treated as an urgent concern. Explain your reasoning.
  2. Explain what is happening with Renata's creatinine and eGFR flag. Name the two confounders at work, and describe what retest strategy would separate a training artifact from a genuine kidney problem.
  3. Explain why Renata's rising fasting insulin, even with normal glucose and HbA1c, deserves genuine follow-through rather than dismissal. Describe what a good next step, and a better question to bring to a clinician, would look like.
  4. Across all three results, explain in your own words the difference between a value that is "context-driven" and one that "warrants medical evaluation," and state clearly where general education ends and clinical judgment begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism behind each result, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the sodium result as hydration-driven, not a salt-intake problem, and expects it to resolve with rest and normal hydration.
  • Identifies both confounders behind the creatinine and eGFR flag, muscle mass and recent hard training, and proposes a rested, well-hydrated retest, or a cystatin C-based estimate, to separate artifact from a real signal.
  • Frames the rising fasting insulin trend, despite normal glucose and HbA1c, as the earliest and most consequential signal on the panel, and offers a specific, useful next step rather than panic or dismissal.
  • Clearly distinguishes context-driven values from persistent or trending values that warrant medical evaluation.
  • States plainly where general education ends and clinical judgment begins, and does not diagnose.
