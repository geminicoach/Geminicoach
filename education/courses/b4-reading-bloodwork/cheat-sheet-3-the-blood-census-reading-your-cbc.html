Chapter 3 : The Blood Census

One tube of blood, three cell populations, read as a system, in context, never as a list of flags.

The Core CBC Lines

Hemoglobin (Hb)The oxygen-carrying protein inside red cells. A concentration, so hydration and plasma volume move it. Read first, against the right reference.
Hematocrit (Hct)The percentage of blood volume that is red cells. Tracks with hemoglobin, roughly three times the Hb value, since it is also a concentration.
RBC countThe literal headcount of red cells per volume. Less informative alone than the indices built from it.
MCVMean corpuscular volume: the average red cell size. The master sorting tool for a low hemoglobin.
RDWRed cell distribution width: how variable cell sizes are. Can flag a developing or mixed deficiency before MCV shifts.
WBC + differentialWhite blood cell count and its five-part breakdown. The differential, not the total alone, carries the real story.
PlateletsCell fragments that drive clotting. Most elevated counts are reactive, not a primary marrow problem.

Microcytic vs Macrocytic

  • Microcytic (MCV below ~80 fL): small cells, think iron deficiency, order ferritin.
  • Normocytic (MCV ~80 to 100 fL): normal-sized cells, a broad middle bucket (blood loss, chronic disease, kidney causes, early deficiency).
  • Macrocytic (MCV above ~100 fL): large cells, think vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, order levels and a smear.
  • A high RDW alongside a normal average MCV can hide a mixed or early deficiency.

Age & Sex Baselines

  • Newborns run high (fetal hemoglobin), then dip to a normal physiologic nadir in infancy.
  • Puberty splits the range by sex: rising testosterone lifts the male baseline, menstruation pulls the female baseline down.
  • Pregnancy dilutes hemoglobin through expected plasma volume expansion, a physiologic drop, not automatically iron deficiency.
  • Older adults have a slightly lower healthy floor, but a low value still warrants evaluation rather than automatic dismissal.

Reading in Context: The Athlete Pattern

  • Endurance training expands plasma volume, a real cardiovascular adaptation.
  • Because hemoglobin and hematocrit are concentrations, that extra plasma dilutes the reading: dilutional pseudoanemia.
  • Typically resolves within roughly three to five days of reduced training load.
  • True iron deficiency can look similar on hemoglobin alone; ferritin is the real tie-breaker, alongside MCV and RDW.

White Cells & Platelets

  • Neutrophils: first responders to bacterial infection. Lymphocytes: viral defense and memory. Monocytes: cleanup crew. Eosinophils: allergic and parasitic response. Basophils: inflammatory signaling.
  • A modest, transient rise in white cells after illness or hard exercise is usually reactive, not concerning.
  • Most elevated platelet counts are reactive to infection, injury, or inflammation and settle once the trigger resolves.
  • Persistence without an obvious trigger, or disturbance across several lines at once, is what warrants professional evaluation.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how a complete blood count (CBC) works, not a license to diagnose or treat. A persistently abnormal line, several disturbed lines at once, or a number accompanied by symptoms are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
