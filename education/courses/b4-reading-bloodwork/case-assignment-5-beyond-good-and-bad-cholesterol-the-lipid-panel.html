Chapter 5: Reason Through a Real Lipid Panel

Take the lipid panel model and use it to explain a real set of results, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Miles, 47
Consider Miles, who has two fasting lipid panels drawn three years apart at the same clinic. Three years ago: LDL cholesterol 116 milligrams per deciliter, HDL cholesterol 49 milligrams per deciliter, triglycerides 90 milligrams per deciliter. This year: LDL cholesterol 119 milligrams per deciliter, essentially unchanged, HDL cholesterol 40 milligrams per deciliter, down meaningfully, and triglycerides 205 milligrams per deciliter, more than double. The note attached to this year's report read only: "LDL stable, HDL down slightly, watch your diet." Miles has been busier at work, has been eating out more, and has cut back his usual weekend runs, though he has not changed anything else he can think of. He wants to know: "My so-called bad number barely moved. Is anything actually different?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain what a single-number reading of Miles's LDL cholesterol would say, and then explain why that reading is incomplete. Calculate or describe what likely happened to his non-HDL cholesterol and his triglyceride to HDL ratio across the three years, and explain why those numbers matter more here than LDL cholesterol alone.
  2. Identify which marker moved the most and explain why that marker, specifically, is the one most sensitive to everyday behavior such as diet, alcohol, and aerobic activity.
  3. Propose one or two plausible, ordinary explanations for the shift, framed as hypotheses worth raising with a clinician, not as a diagnosis you are making yourself.
  4. Decide whether Miles's results belong among the signs that warrant medical evaluation right now, or whether this is a genuinely useful education moment instead, and explain how you know. State clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains why LDL cholesterol alone can look unchanged while non-HDL cholesterol and the triglyceride to HDL ratio shift meaningfully.
  • Correctly identifies triglycerides as the most behaviorally responsive marker and connects the change to plausible carbohydrate, alcohol, or activity shifts.
  • Frames the case as a genuinely useful education moment, not alarm, while still naming a general-education next step (for example, raising it with a clinician).
  • Does not diagnose a specific condition and does not prescribe a treatment.
  • Identifies what, if anything, would count as a sign warranting professional evaluation, and states where general education ends.
