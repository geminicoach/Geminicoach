Chapter 7 : Iron and the Inflammation Trap

Ferritin does double duty as a storage marker and an inflammation marker. Read it next to C-reactive protein (CRP) and transferrin saturation (TSAT), never alone.

The Core Markers

FerritinProtein that stores iron in the liver, spleen, and bone marrow, and also an acute-phase reactant that rises during inflammation regardless of true iron stores.
C-reactive protein (CRP)General inflammation marker with no role in iron storage or transport; the lens that tells you whether ferritin can be trusted at face value.
Transferrin saturation (TSAT)Percentage of transferrin's iron-carrying capacity that is actually loaded with iron; serum iron divided by total iron-binding capacity (TIBC), times 100. Under 20 percent signals trouble.
Total iron-binding capacity (TIBC)The total capacity of the transferrin pool to carry iron; rises when the body is genuinely short on iron, as more transferrin is manufactured to scavenge what little iron is available.
HepcidinLiver-made hormone, the master switch of iron regulation. Inflammation raises it; it degrades ferroportin, trapping iron in storage and starving the bone marrow.

Three Patterns, Triangulated

  • Absolute deficiency : ferritin low, TIBC high, TSAT low. Every marker agrees.
  • Anemia of inflammation : ferritin normal or high, TIBC low or unremarkable, TSAT low. Hepcidin has locked stores shut.
  • Combined picture : ferritin dragged into a normal-looking zone by inflammation while stores are genuinely low; TSAT stays low. The hardest pattern to read from ferritin alone.

Reading CRP Correctly

  • Check CRP before trusting ferritin, not after.
  • CRP normal: ferritin can be read against a life-stage-appropriate threshold.
  • CRP elevated: distrust a normal or high ferritin; lean on transferrin saturation (TSAT) instead.
  • Inflammation inflates ferritin by roughly 50 percent on average (Thurnham et al., 2010).
  • World Health Organization (WHO): use a 70 µg/L threshold, not the usual 15 µg/L, when CRP or alpha-1-acid glycoprotein (AGP) is elevated.

Iron Deficiency Without Anemia

  • Depletion progresses in order: stores fall first (ferritin), transport fails next (TSAT), hemoglobin drops last.
  • A person can be measurably iron deficient, with real costs to energy and exercise capacity, well before a complete blood count (CBC) shows anything abnormal.
  • Normal hemoglobin does not rule out depleted stores or impaired transport.

Microcytic Anemia, Tied to the CBC

  • Iron deficiency is the leading cause of a low mean corpuscular volume (MCV) and low mean corpuscular hemoglobin (MCH), the microcytic, hypochromic pattern.
  • Mechanism: without enough iron, the bone marrow keeps making red blood cells on schedule, but under-fills them.
  • Anemia of inflammation can mimic this pattern even when stores are not truly empty, since hepcidin starves the marrow of iron regardless.
  • CRP plus TSAT separate true deficiency from anemia of inflammation when the complete blood count (CBC) pattern alone cannot.

Age, Sex, and Life Stage

  • Menstruating women : chronic monthly loss; a low-normal ferritin is worth investigating, not filing away.
  • Pregnancy : iron demand rises sharply; mild inflammation later in pregnancy complicates the reading.
  • Endurance athletes : elevated iron demand meets training-induced inflammation; a normal-looking ferritin cannot be trusted at face value.
  • Older adults and chronic disease : more common low-grade inflammation makes ferritin more often falsely reassuring.

Reading Protocol

  • 1. Check CRP first, before glancing at the ferritin value.
  • 2. CRP unremarkable: read ferritin against a life-stage-appropriate threshold, not just the lab's printed lower bound.
  • 3. CRP elevated: distrust the ferritin, weight your read toward transferrin saturation (TSAT).
  • 4. Cross-check the complete blood count (CBC): does a microcytic, hypochromic pattern agree with the story the iron panel tells?
  • 5. Frame, do not diagnose. A concerning pattern is a sign that warrants medical evaluation.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how ferritin, transferrin saturation (TSAT), total iron-binding capacity (TIBC), and C-reactive protein (CRP) fit together, not a license to diagnose or treat. A transferrin saturation (TSAT) that stays under 20 percent, a ferritin that will not budge, or an unexplained iron deficiency with no obvious source are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
