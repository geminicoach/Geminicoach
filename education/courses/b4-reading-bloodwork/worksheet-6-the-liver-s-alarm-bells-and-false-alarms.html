Part C · The Muscle Confound

AST is far less discreet than ALT about where it comes from, and that wide distribution is the reason this chapter exists.

  1. Explain, in two or three sentences, why AST's wide tissue distribution (liver, skeletal muscle, heart muscle, red blood cells, kidneys, and brain) makes it a much less reliable liver-specific signal than ALT.
  2. Describe the finding from Pettersson et al. (2008) in your own words: what happened to AST, ALT, and the other muscle markers in previously untrained men after a single hard weightlifting session, and roughly how long did the elevation last?
  3. In that same study, bilirubin, gamma-glutamyl transferase, and alkaline phosphatase all stayed within normal range even while AST and ALT rose. Explain why that detail matters for telling a muscle source apart from a liver source.

Part D · Pre-Draw Timing: the 48 to 72 Hour Window

A blood test is a snapshot, and that snapshot quietly includes the aftershocks of whatever the body did in the days immediately before it.

  1. Explain why the two to three days before a blood draw are part of the measurement itself, rather than background noise a lab result can safely ignore.
  2. List two pieces of practical timing advice from the chapter: one for getting a clean baseline reading, and one for what to do if a hard or unfamiliar workout could not be avoided before a draw.
  3. Explain the direction of the bias described in the chapter: does recent hard training tend to push the AST-to-ALT ratio up or down, and why does knowing that direction matter when you are weighing a borderline result?

Where education ends
This worksheet builds literacy in reading a liver panel, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how ALT, AST, and the AST-to-ALT ratio behave, and treat jaundice, dark urine, pain in the upper right abdomen, persistent or symptomatic elevation, or other signs that warrant medical evaluation as belonging to a qualified clinician. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
