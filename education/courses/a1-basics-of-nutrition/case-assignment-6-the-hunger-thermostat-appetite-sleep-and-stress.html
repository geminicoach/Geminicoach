Chapter 6: Reason Through a Real Complaint

Take the hunger thermostat and use it to explain a real complaint, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Nora, 36
Consider Nora, who started a demanding new role four months ago and has been sleeping in broken stretches of about five hours a night while a stressful workload drags on. She has not changed what she buys at the grocery store and would tell you, honestly, that she eats "about the same as always." But she has gained weight, and she notices a specific new pattern: late in the evening she is pulled toward cookies and ice cream she used to walk past, and on weekends she finds herself moving less and grazing more. She has decided the problem is that she has "lost her willpower." There is no purging, no severe restriction, and no loss of control around food that frightens her. She asks: "Why is this happening, and how do I get my discipline back?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what chronic short sleep is doing to Nora's ghrelin and leptin, and why her body behaves as if it were partly starved even though her actual energy stores have not fallen.
  2. Account for why the cravings are specifically for sweet, dense food rather than an extra helping of dinner, and describe how reduced NEAT completes the "double hit" that lets her weight drift up on the same shopping list.
  3. Address her framing directly: explain why "get my discipline back" is the wrong tool for this problem, using the idea of appetite as a regulated, self-defending system.
  4. Give two or three practical, general-education suggestions that change the inputs rather than demanding more restraint, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants qualified evaluation, and does not diagnose. Signs of disordered eating, such as recurring loss of control around food, purging, or severe restriction, warrant evaluation by a qualified professional. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains that chronic short sleep lowers leptin and raises ghrelin, producing a false "under-fueled" state.
  • Explains that chronic stress and cortisol bias the reward system toward dense, palatable food (hedonic appetite), which is why the cravings are for sweets rather than more dinner.
  • Names the NEAT double hit: the same fatigue that raises intake lowers spontaneous movement, so calories drift up on an unchanged shopping list.
  • Frames appetite as a regulated, self-defending system and explains why willpower alone is an unfair, losing fight.
  • Offers practical, in-scope input changes: protect sleep first, build meals around protein, fiber, and volume, and engineer the food environment.
  • Identifies that disordered-eating signs warrant qualified evaluation, and does not diagnose.
