Chapter 8: Reason Through a Real Complaint

Use the final chapter, labels, plates, myths, and habits, to make sense of a familiar pattern, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 30 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Tessa, 41
Consider Tessa, who has lost the same fifteen pounds at least six times. Every January, or before an event, she commits hard: a strict plan she found online, no bread, no sugar, tiny portions, and a punishing workout schedule. For three or four weeks it works and she feels in control. Then a stressful stretch hits, she misses a day, decides she has "blown it," and abandons the whole thing. The weight returns, usually with a little extra. She leaned heavily on products labeled "diet" and "low-fat," yet often felt hungry within the hour and concluded she simply lacked discipline. She weighs herself every morning and judges the day by the number. She knows a lot about nutrition from years of these cycles. What she has never had is an approach that survives an ordinary bad week. She asks: "Why can't I make this stick? Do I just have no willpower?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, using the chapter, why Tessa's approach is set up to collapse, and why "no willpower" is the wrong diagnosis. Name the delivery-system and biology problems, not a character flaw.
  2. Apply label literacy: explain how reading the back of the box (serving size, added sugars, fiber) would reveal why her "diet" foods left her hungry within the hour, and connect it to the mechanisms of Chapters 1 and 5.
  3. Redesign the approach using the Half-Plate Rule anchored to a stable cue and the habit science (automaticity, one missed day, the multi-week trend). Name the mechanisms each move satisfies.
  4. State clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins for someone like Tessa, and what you would and would not offer.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing or prescribing. A responsible response explains the mechanisms, offers food-first, general-education moves, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose, prescribe a calorie target, or promise a weight outcome. Individualized diet plans, and any sign of disordered eating or a medical condition, belong to a qualified clinician or registered dietitian.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Reframes the repeated failure as a delivery-system and biology problem, not a willpower or character failing.
  • Applies label literacy: serving size, added sugars, and fiber explain the spike-crash hunger she misread as weakness (Chapters 1 and 5).
  • Applies the Half-Plate Rule anchored to a stable cue, and names the four mechanisms it satisfies (nutrient density, macro balance, fiber, satiety).
  • Applies the habit science: automaticity takes roughly two to three months with wide variation, one missed day does not break the curve, and progress is judged on a two-to-four-week trend, not a single day of scale noise.
  • Stays in scope: food-first, general education, no calorie prescription or diagnosis, and refers clinical or disordered-eating signals to a qualified professional.
