Chapter 6 : The Hunger Thermostat

Appetite is a regulated system, not a referendum on your character. Change the inputs, not just the output.

Three Words, Three Systems

HungerThe homeostatic drive to eat: the low-fuel alarm that builds as the stomach empties and circulating fuels fall.
AppetiteThe reward-driven wanting: desire for a specific food, heavily shaped by context, emotion, and cue.
SatietyThe signals that end a meal and keep you uninterested afterward: stretch, gut peptides, and the slow hormonal tally.

Ghrelin vs Leptin

  • Ghrelin : stomach; fast "time to eat" pulse that rises before meals and falls after. The only well-characterized hunger-raising hormone.
  • Leptin : fat tissue, in proportion to fat mass; slow "stores are adequate" statement.
  • Asymmetry : a strong low-fuel alarm, a weak high-fuel brake. Built to defend against starvation, not surplus.
  • Leptin resistance : in obesity leptin is high but unread, so the body acts under-fueled amid surplus.

The Gut's Meal-End Signals

  • CCK : upper small intestine, within minutes of fat and protein; early "stop" signal, works fast, fades fast.
  • GLP-1 & PYY : lower gut; slower, longer arc; reduce appetite and slow stomach emptying.
  • Eating slowly lets these land before the plate is empty, so you stop sooner on less.

Three Levers That Make a Meal Filling

  • Protein : the most satiety per calorie; strongest trigger for CCK and PYY, plus a high thermic cost.
  • Viscous fiber : gel-forming fiber from whole foods slows emptying and prolongs GLP-1 and PYY.
  • Volume / low energy density : the stomach counts amount, so water- and fiber-rich food fills it at a low calorie cost.

The Two Hijackers

  • Short sleep : two days at ~4 hours drops leptin ~18% and raises ghrelin and hunger (Spiegel 2004); the body reads a false energy emergency and craves dense, sweet food.
  • Chronic stress : cortisol biases the reward system toward palatable food (hedonic appetite). Acute stress does the opposite and suppresses appetite.
  • NEAT double hit : the same fatigue lowers spontaneous movement, so intake rises while expenditure falls.

Working With the Signals

  • Willpower is a terrible long-term plan, and not because anyone is weak: it fatigues across the day while the regulator pushes steadily.
  • Protect sleep first, it is invisible on a food log.
  • Build meals around protein and fiber; use volume.
  • Manage chronic stressors; engineer the food environment so the easy choice is the helpful choice.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the appetite regulator works, not a license to diagnose or treat. Signs of disordered eating, such as recurring loss of control around food, purging, severe restriction, or marked unexplained weight change, warrant evaluation by a qualified professional. Work within your role, whatever it is, and do not self-diagnose.
