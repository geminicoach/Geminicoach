Part A · Write the Master Equation

Energy balance is the spine of the whole chapter. Set it down in your own words.

  1. Write the energy balance equation in words, naming the three quantities it relates (the change in body energy stores, energy intake, and energy expenditure).

  3. The phrase that "does the damage," the chapter says, is energy expenditure. Explain why picturing expenditure as "the calories I burn at the gym" leads people astray.

Part C · Why BMR Is Large, and Why It Varies

  1. Johnstone and colleagues (2005) partitioned the between-person variation in BMR. Name the single factor that explained roughly 63 percent of it, and explain in a sentence why that makes a larger or more muscular person tend to have a higher BMR.
  2. Using that finding, explain what resistance training actually does for resting metabolism, and why the effect is gradual rather than a dramatic post-workout "afterburn."
  3. Someone says their friend "can eat anything because of a fast metabolism." Give the more accurate, physiology-based explanation the chapter offers, in two or three sentences.

Part D · The Thermic Effect of Food

Not all calories cost the same to process. Use Westerterp's (2004) hierarchy.

  1. Rank the macronutrients (plus alcohol) from most to least thermically expensive to process, and note the rough percentage of a protein-rich meal's calories that are spent simply handling it.
  2. A person swaps a 400-kilocalorie breakfast pastry for 400 kilocalories of eggs and Greek yogurt. TEF is higher for the second meal, but the chapter flags a second, larger reason the swap may help. Name it and explain which side of the energy balance equation it acts on.

Part E · NEAT and the "I Earned It" Trap

This is where "calories out" turns out to be far more than exercise. Reason carefully.

  1. By roughly how much can NEAT differ between two people of the same body weight, and why does the chapter call it "the great variable"? Give an example of the kind of movement that counts as NEAT.
  2. In Levine's 1999 overfeeding study, volunteers were fed 1,000 extra kilocalories a day. Explain what happened to their fat gain and the role NEAT played, and what that shows about whether intake and expenditure are truly independent.
  3. Estimate roughly how many minutes of brisk walking it would take to "burn off" a 480-kilocalorie muffin, then explain in your own words why "I worked out, so I earned this" so often fails as arithmetic.
  4. Pontzer and colleagues (2016) found that total daily expenditure plateaus at high activity levels rather than climbing without limit. Name this idea and explain the mechanism the body is thought to use to hold expenditure within a narrow band.

Where education ends
This worksheet builds nutrition literacy, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how the energy budget works, and treat anything that looks clinical (unexplained weight change, disordered eating, a diagnosed metabolic or thyroid condition, or medication questions) as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
