Chapter 8 : Putting It on the Plate

Three lifelong skills: read a label honestly, build a plate without arithmetic, and change a habit so it holds.

Read the Panel in This Order

1. Serving sizeThe lens and the trick. Every number is multiplied by it. Ask "how much will I actually eat?" and scale. A container often holds more than one serving.
2. CaloriesThe energy deposit, not the quality of the transaction. Good for comparing similar foods; silent on fullness or raw materials.
3. Macros (g)Fat 9, carb 4, protein 4 kcal/g. Grams times value roughly rebuilds the calorie line; a small gap is usually fiber or rounding.
4. Added sugarsOne of the most honest lines. Total sugars includes natural sugar; added isolates what was poured in. Natural arrives with fiber and water, added arrives alone.
5. Fiber & sodiumHigh fiber, moderate sodium reads as "minimally processed." Fiber slows digestion and feeds the microbiome; sodium hides in packaged food.
6. %DVFraction of a reference adult's daily need. Rule of thumb: 5% or less is low, 20% or more is high. Pattern recognition, not precise accounting.

Front of Pack = Marketing

  • "Natural" : no enforced nutritional meaning.
  • "Made with whole grains" : can be mostly refined flour; "made with" sets no minimum.
  • "High protein" : often also high added sugar or sodium.
  • "No added sugar" : may still be high in concentrated natural sugars.
  • Rule: let the front raise a question, let the back answer it.

The Half-Plate Rule

  • Half the plate: vegetables, fruit, or salad. That is the whole instruction.
  • Quarter: protein (beans, fish, eggs, poultry, tofu).
  • Quarter: intact carbohydrate (whole grains, starchy veg).
  • Geometry replaces arithmetic: no grams, no app, no willpower.
  • Flexible by design: 40% veg still beats 5%. It bans no food.

Four Mechanisms a Good Plate Satisfies

  • Nutrient density (Ch 5) : most micronutrients per calorie.
  • Macro balance (Ch 3) : steady energy, no spike-crash.
  • Fiber (Ch 7) : slows emptying, feeds microbiome.
  • Satiety (Ch 6) : protein, fiber, and volume send fullness signals.

Myths, Busted by Mechanism

  • "A calorie is a calorie" : source changes thermic effect, absorption, and appetite (Ch 2, 5).
  • "Carbs/fat/gluten are bad" : the quality within a class beats the class (Ch 3).
  • "Just more willpower" : appetite is a defended hormonal system (Ch 6).
  • "Out-exercise a bad diet" : exercise cost is modest and compensated (Ch 2).
  • "A supplement replaces a diet" : a pill cannot reproduce the food package (Ch 4, 5).

Habits That Last

  • Automaticity, not motivation, is the goal.
  • Median ~66 days to automatic, with wide variation; "21 days" is a myth.
  • One missed day does not break the curve.
  • Anchor it: "After I [daily event], I will [small behavior]."
  • Judge the two-to-four-week trend, not the daily scale.
  • Support the signals: sleep, satiety, food environment.
Where education ends   This builds label, plate, and habit literacy, not a license to diagnose, prescribe, or treat. Individualized calorie or weight-loss prescriptions, a suspected deficiency, symptoms during a restrictive regimen, and any sign of disordered eating warrant a qualified clinician or registered dietitian. Work within your role, whatever it is.
