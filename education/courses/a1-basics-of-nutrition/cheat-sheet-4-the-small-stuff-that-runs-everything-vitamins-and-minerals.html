Chapter 4 : The Small Stuff That Runs Everything

Needed in milligrams, indispensable in everything: the tools, spark plugs, and wiring of metabolism.

The Core Split : Solubility

Fat-solubleA, D, E, K. Ride along with dietary fat and bile; stored in liver and fat; you can go weeks without them, and you can overdose, because excess is stored, not excreted. Toxicity risk is real, almost always from supplements.
Water-solubleEight B vitamins and vitamin C. Absorbed directly, stored poorly, surplus flushed in urine. Acute toxicity rare, but the supply line must be replenished continually. B12 is the exception: the liver banks a multi-year reserve.

Vitamins vs Minerals

  • Minerals : chemical elements (iron, zinc, calcium, iodine, magnesium); come from rock and soil; not destroyed by cooking.
  • Vitamins : organic molecules built by living things; degraded by heat, light, or oxygen; body cannot make enough, so diet must supply them.
  • Both needed in milligrams to micrograms; both supply almost no energy.

The Big Idea : Cofactors

  • Most enzymes need a non-protein helper bolted in, the cofactor (a coenzyme if organic, a metal cofactor if a metal ion).
  • The enzyme is the power tool; the cofactor is the bit. No bit, no cut.
  • Remove a milligram-scale cofactor and a reaction processing thousands of calories simply stops.

Cofactors in the Energy Pathways

  • Niacin (B3) : becomes NAD⁺/NADH, electron carrier.
  • Riboflavin (B2) : becomes FAD/FADH₂.
  • Thiamine (B1) : pyruvate dehydrogenase, gate to acetyl-CoA.
  • Pantothenic acid (B5) : backbone of coenzyme A.
  • Magnesium : stabilizes and activates ATP.
  • Iron : cytochromes of the electron transport chain.

Five Nutrients Worth Knowing

  • Iron : oxygen transport; heme (meat) absorbs well, non-heme (plants) poorly; vitamin C boosts it, tea and phytates cut it.
  • Vitamin D : drives calcium absorption; mostly made in skin; few food sources.
  • Vitamin B12 : nerve myelin and DNA/red-cell building; animal foods only; large reserve fails slowly.
  • Calcium : bone bank plus muscle, nerve, clotting; a long-game nutrient.
  • Magnesium : hundreds of reactions; stripped out when grains are refined.

Flagship Deficiencies

ScurvyVitamin C; collagen collapses, gums bleed, wounds and old scars reopen.
RicketsVitamin D; bone will not harden, legs bow (osteomalacia in adults).
AnemiaIron; too little hemoglobin, oxygen not delivered, fatigue and pallor.
GoitreIodine; thyroid enlarges, T3/T4 fall, metabolism slows.

Enough, Not Megadoses

  • Benefit follows a curve: deficiency, then sufficiency (enzymes saturated, more buys nothing), then a danger zone of excess.
  • Water-soluble vitamins have wide margins; fat-soluble (vitamin A) and some minerals (selenium, iron) are much tighter.
  • The risk is almost never food; it is stacked supplements quietly summing past the upper limit.

Food First : Variety and the Matrix

  • Bioavailability is what you absorb, not what the label says went in.
  • Whole foods deliver nutrients embedded in a matrix of fiber, fat, and cofactors that work best together.
  • Variety closes gaps you would never think to target; supplements are targeted tools for known gaps, food is the foundation.
  • Triage theory (Ames): chronic shortfall spends the future to pay for today, invisible until it is not.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the nutrients work and which foods deliver them, not a license to diagnose, prescribe, or treat. A suspected deficiency is confirmed with lab testing, and correcting it, especially with supplements that carry real toxicity risk, is a qualified clinician's job. Steer food choices, and refer out for the rest. Work within your role, whatever it is.
