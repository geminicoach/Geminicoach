Chapter 7 : The Forgotten Nutrients

Three things that carry almost no calories and run almost everything: water, the electrolytes dissolved in it, and fiber.

Water: Five Jobs

Solvent & reactantMetabolism runs in water; it is the fluid substrates react in and a molecule consumed or made in hydrolysis and condensation.
TransportPlasma is mostly water, carrying nutrients to tissues and waste to the kidneys and lungs.
ThermoregulatorHigh heat capacity absorbs heat; evaporating sweat sheds it. Hard work in heat can cost over a liter of sweat an hour.
Lubricant & cushionSynovial fluid in joints, cerebrospinal fluid around the brain.
Tightly defendedTotal body water held within about 0.2% of body mass per day.

Water Balance

  • Osmoreceptors : sense blood solute concentration.
  • Baroreceptors : sense blood volume and pressure.
  • ADH (vasopressin) : tells kidneys to reabsorb water; also drives thirst. Dehydrated = small, dark urine.
  • Flow-through, not a bucket: extra water you do not need leaves within a couple of hours.

Sensible Hydration

  • Drink to thirst; a bit more in heat, altitude, hard exercise, illness.
  • "Eight glasses" has no rigorous basis; needs vary with size, diet, activity, climate.
  • Roughly 20 to 30% of intake comes from food; tea and coffee count net positive.
  • Check: pale straw urine is fine; deep amber means dry.
  • Hyponatremia : too much plain water too fast dilutes sodium; rare but serious.

Electrolytes: The Charge

  • Charged minerals in solution: Na, K, Cl, Ca, Mg.
  • Sodium-potassium pump keeps sodium high outside cells, potassium high inside.
  • That stored gradient is what nerves fire and muscles contract with.
  • Balance, not just intake: where sodium goes, water follows; sodium sets blood volume and pressure.

The Sodium Paradox

  • General population: intake is roughly double the ceiling, mostly from processed and restaurant food, raising cardiovascular risk via blood pressure.
  • Heavy sweaters mid-event: genuinely lose sodium and may need to replace it.
  • Potassium is under-eaten; more nudges sodium out and eases pressure. Aim is balance, not zero salt.
  • One move fixes both: more whole plant foods (up potassium, down processed sodium).

Fiber: Non-Digestible Carbohydrate, Three Jobs

1. Slows digestionViscous soluble fiber thickens gut contents, slows gastric emptying, flattens the blood-sugar spike, and sustains fullness (keeps satiety signals up, ghrelin down).
2. Feeds the gutFermentable fiber reaching the colon becomes short-chain fatty acids (acetate, propionate, butyrate). Butyrate fuels the colon lining. Diversity of fiber beats volume.
3. Marks food qualityProcessing's most reliable signature is stripped fiber. High-fiber foods are intact, slow, satiating, microbiota-friendly; refined ones are the opposite.

Soluble vs Insoluble

  • Soluble : forms a gel; steadies blood sugar, binds bile to lower LDL. Oats, beans, apples, citrus, psyllium.
  • Insoluble : holds structure, adds bulk, keeps you regular. Wheat bran, skins, whole-grain husk.
  • Whole foods carry both; a varied plate beats an isolated supplement.

How Much & How To Add

  • Target: about 25 g/day (women), about 38 g/day (men), roughly 14 g per 1,000 calories.
  • Over 90% of adults fall short; the gap tracks refined food.
  • Close it with whole grains, beans, whole fruit, vegetables, nuts and seeds, not a pill.
  • Ramp up slowly over weeks and drink water, or expect gas and bloating.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how water, electrolytes, and fiber work, not a license to diagnose or treat. Fainting, confusion, blood in the stool, unexplained weight loss, a persistent change in bowel habits, or a possible medication interaction are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. High-dose electrolyte pills are a medical decision, not a wellness habit. Work within your role, whatever it is.
