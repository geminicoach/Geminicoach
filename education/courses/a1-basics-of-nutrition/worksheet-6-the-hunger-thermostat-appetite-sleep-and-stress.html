Part D · The Two Hijackers: Sleep and Stress

The regulator does not run in a vacuum. Trace how each input moves the signals, in full sentences.

  1. Short sleep. In the Spiegel study, two days of roughly four hours of sleep moved leptin and ghrelin in specific directions. State both directions and explain why the body then behaves as if it were partly starved even though its actual stores have not changed.
  2. Chronic stress. Acute stress and chronic stress affect appetite differently. Distinguish the two, and explain how cortisol biases the reward system rather than simply raising hunger in the empty-stomach sense.
  3. The double hit. Explain how the same fatigue and stress that raise intake also lower expenditure through NEAT, and why that lets calories drift upward for someone whose food choices have not consciously changed.

Where education ends
This worksheet builds appetite literacy, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how the regulator works, and treat anything that looks clinical as warranting evaluation by a qualified professional. Signs of disordered eating, such as recurring loss of control around food, purging, severe restriction, a preoccupation that dominates daily life, or marked unexplained weight change, warrant qualified evaluation and are outside general education. Work within your role: refer when something reads as clinical, and do not self-diagnose.
