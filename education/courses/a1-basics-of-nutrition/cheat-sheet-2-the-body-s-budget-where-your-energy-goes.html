Chapter 2 : The Body's Budget

One master equation, four line items on the "calories out" side, and one big lever you can actually pull.

The Master Equation

  • Energy balance : change in body energy stores = energy intake − energy expenditure.
  • It is the first law of thermodynamics applied to a body, not an opinion. Surpluses are stored (mostly as fat), deficits are drawn down.
  • The catch: expenditure is not a fixed number you set at the gym. It is dynamic and it pushes back (Hall et al., 2012).

The Four Line Items (TDEE)

1. BMRCost of simply being alive: heart, lungs, brain, kidneys, ion pumps, protein turnover. Roughly 60 to 70 percent of TDEE, the largest slice by far.
2. TEFThermic effect of food: the tax on digesting and processing meals. Roughly 8 to 15 percent.
3. NEATNon-exercise activity thermogenesis: walking, standing, fidgeting, chores. The great variable, and the movable lever.
4. ExerciseDeliberate, planned training. Usually the smallest wedge, and zero on many days.

What Drives BMR

  • Lean mass : explained about 63 percent of BMR variance between people (Johnstone et al., 2005).
  • Fat mass (~6%) and age (~2%) matter far less; sex adds little once lean mass is counted.
  • More lean, active tissue = higher resting burn. Resistance training protects it, a slow effect, not "afterburn."

TEF by Macronutrient

  • Hierarchy: alcohol > protein > carbohydrate > fat (Westerterp, 2004).
  • Protein is expensive: ~20 to 30 percent of its calories are spent handling it.
  • Bigger story: protein's satiety. It fills you up and quietly lowers intake, the side that moves the needle.

NEAT: The Quiet Thermostat

  • Can differ by up to ~2,000 kcal/day between two same-weight people (Levine et al., 2006).
  • Overfeeding study: fed 1,000 extra kcal/day, fat gain varied tenfold; NEAT explained about two-thirds of the extra burn, predicting resistance to fat gain (r = 0.77) (Levine et al., 1999).
  • Intake and expenditure are coupled, not independent.

Why Expenditure Pushes Back

  • Constrained TDEE : at high activity, total burn plateaus rather than climbing forever (Pontzer et al., 2016).
  • Metabolic adaptation : eat less and BMR, NEAT, and efficiency all drift to defend your current state. That is a plateau, not a failure.
  • You do not out-train the fork: a single reward food often outweighs the workout.

Finding Real Maintenance & The Lever

  • Calculators give an educated average; verify by tracking intake and the bodyweight trend over two to four weeks.
  • The one big movable number is NEAT: stairs, walking, standing, pacing on calls, chores. Protect it with a moderate deficit, not a crash.
  • Keep protein and training to hold lean mass and protect BMR; judge progress over weeks, not days.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the energy budget works, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe. Unexplained weight change, disordered eating, or a diagnosed metabolic or thyroid condition are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
