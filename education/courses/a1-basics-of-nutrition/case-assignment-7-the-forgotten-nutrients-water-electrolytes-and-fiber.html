Chapter 7: Reason Through the Forgotten Nutrients

Take water, electrolytes, and fiber off the page and use them to explain a real cluster of complaints, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Wes, 34
Consider Wes, who took up running in the spring and by July is training four mornings a week in real heat. He drinks water constantly from a large bottle and is proud that he is "always hydrated," and on long runs he sips plain water often. Two things keep going wrong. His calves cramp hard in the back half of every long run, sometimes badly enough to make him stop. And away from running he is chronically constipated and bloated, and flat by mid-afternoon, despite what he thinks of as a light, healthy diet: coffee and a granola bar in the morning, a white-flour wrap or a quick pasta for lunch and dinner, not much in the way of beans, whole fruit, vegetables, or whole grains. He has started to dread the runs and assumes he is just not built for distance. He asks: "I drink more water than anyone I know. Why is my body falling apart?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, why the cramps are an electrolyte-and-fluid problem rather than a lack of water, using the relationship between sweat, sodium, and the fluid around working muscle.
  2. Account for the constipation, the bloating, and the mid-afternoon slump through the fiber lens: name the role of insoluble fiber, the role of soluble fiber, and what his refined diet is doing to his blood sugar.
  3. Recommend a coherent set of general-education adjustments, and explain why one change (more whole plant foods) helps potassium and fiber at the same time and why the fiber increase should be gradual.
  4. State clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins, and name what, if anything here, would warrant professional evaluation.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanisms, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis, prescriptions, and medication decisions belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the plain-water-plus-heavy-sweat mismatch: sodium concentration falls while total water stays up, disturbing fluid balance around muscle and driving cramps.
  • Links the refined, low-fiber diet to constipation and bloating (too little insoluble fiber to bulk and move) and to the blood-sugar spike-and-crash behind the afternoon slump (too little soluble fiber).
  • Identifies "more whole plant foods" as the single change that raises potassium and fiber together, and advises ramping fiber up gradually with adequate fluid.
  • Separates genuine physiology from the "always hydrated" marketing frame: drinking still more water is not the fix here.
  • Identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
