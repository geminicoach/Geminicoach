Chapter 6 : No Clean Switches

Three systems, always running at once. Only the proportional blend shifts as intensity and duration change.

The Intensity-Duration Spectrum (Measured Aerobic Share)

1 to 3 sec (heavy single)Overwhelmingly phosphagen; oxidative and glycolytic contribute only a small slice, never zero.
100 m (~10 to 11 sec)Roughly 20 to 25% aerobic on average; phosphagen still the largest single contributor.
200 m (~20 to 25 sec)Roughly 29% aerobic; glycolysis near its peak.
400 m (~45 to 60 sec)Roughly 43% aerobic.
800 m (~2 min)Roughly 66% aerobic, already majority-aerobic by energy supplied.
1500 m (~3.5 to 4 min)Roughly 84% aerobic.
5 km and beyondHigh 80s percent aerobic or more; anaerobic systems cover surges and the finishing kick only.

The Gear-Switch Myth

  • Old model: phosphagen, then glycolysis, then aerobic, in clean sequence.
  • Reality: all three run from the first instant, each with its own kinetics (ramp speed).
  • A peak is not an on-off state; systems overlap continuously.
  • Aerobic and anaerobic contributions cross over at roughly 75 seconds of all-out effort, not two to three minutes.

Why They Must Overlap

  • ATP demand can jump roughly a thousandfold within one stride.
  • Oxidative output rises with a time constant of about 20 to 30 seconds; it cannot jump instantly.
  • The shortfall while it ramps up is the oxygen deficit, covered by the phosphagen and glycolytic systems.

Fiber Types and Recruitment

  • Type I (slow-twitch): mitochondria-rich, oxidative, fatigue-resistant, recruited first.
  • Type II (fast-twitch): built for force and speed, glycolytic/phosphagen-biased, recruited as demand rises.
  • Recruitment follows the size principle: small motor units first, large ones as force demand climbs.
  • No fiber type is purely one system; every fiber has some of each.

Measuring the Blend

  • Indirect calorimetry measures oxidative contribution only (oxygen and carbon dioxide).
  • Accumulated oxygen deficit and lactate/phosphocreatine methods estimate the anaerobic share, with different assumptions.
  • Methods sometimes disagree, especially for short, explosive efforts, because there is no true seam to measure across.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the three energy systems blend, not a license to diagnose or treat, or to design an individualized training program. Chest pain, irregular heartbeat, or unusual dizziness during exercise are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
