The Scenario

Scenario · Devon, a gym-goer designing his own interval workouts
Devon read an old fitness article that describes energy systems like gears in a car: "the first ten seconds are pure phosphagen, then you shift into glycolysis for about two minutes, then your aerobic system finally kicks in after that." Based on this, he is designing his interval program around strict rest periods, assuming that any work interval under two minutes is "basically anaerobic" and that his aerobic fitness is irrelevant to those efforts. He asks: "Is this gear model basically right? Should I keep building my intervals around it?"

Your Tasks

  1. Evaluate the gear-switch claim against what the chapter describes as the blended, kinetics-based model. Say clearly whether it is accurate.
  2. Explain why all three systems ramp up at different speeds rather than switching on in sequence, and what the "oxygen deficit" has to do with it.
  3. Cite at least one specific measured contribution figure from the chapter (for example, from a 200, 400, or 800 meter race) that challenges Devon's "under two minutes is basically anaerobic" assumption.
  4. Give one reasonable, general-education implication for how Devon might think differently about his aerobic fitness and his intervals, and state clearly where general education ends and individualized program design begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not designing an individualized training program or diagnosing a medical condition. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Individualized program design and diagnosis belong to a qualified coach or clinician working within their credential.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Correctly rejects the gear-switch model in favor of a continuous, simultaneous blend.
  • Explains kinetics (staggered ramps) and the oxygen deficit concept.
  • Cites at least one accurate measured contribution figure showing substantial aerobic contribution well before two minutes.
  • Offers a reasonable, general-education implication for training or pacing within scope.
  • Notes what if anything would need professional or coaching expertise, and does not overstate certainty.
