Chapter 2: Explain the Rest Interval

Use the phosphagen system to justify a training decision, and know where the coaching conversation has to stop.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Marcus, 29, amateur powerlifter
Marcus is preparing for his first competition and does not understand why his coach insists on three to five minutes of rest between heavy single-rep attempts near his maximum. "I feel recovered after ninety seconds," he says. He has also started taking a creatine supplement because a training partner said it "gives your muscles more ATP directly," and he wants to know if that is accurate. Marcus mentions, in passing, that he has a family history of kidney issues and wonders if the supplement is safe for him.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanistically, what happens to Marcus's phosphocreatine and ATP during a heavy single-rep attempt, naming the reaction involved.
  2. Explain why a three to five minute rest interval is recommended, using what the chapter describes about the two-phase resynthesis of phosphocreatine.
  3. Correct or confirm the claim that creatine supplementation "gives your muscles more ATP directly." Be precise about what creatine actually does.
  4. Address Marcus's kidney-history question appropriately, stating clearly where general education ends and a conversation with his physician needs to begin.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating mechanistic understanding of the phosphagen system, not prescribing a supplement plan or clearing someone medically. A responsible response explains the physiology, identifies what warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose or approve supplement use for someone with a relevant medical history. That decision belongs to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains phosphocreatine depletion and the creatine kinase reaction that rebuilds ATP during a heavy lift.
  • Explains rest interval design based on the two-phase phosphocreatine resynthesis kinetics (fast phase near two to three minutes, slower additional phase) and its dependence on oxygen during rest.
  • Correctly explains that creatine supplementation raises stored phosphocreatine, not literal ATP itself.
  • Notes the phosphagen system is anaerobic and produces no lactate, distinguishing the "gassed" feeling from acid buildup.
  • Identifies the scope boundary: individualized supplement safety with a pre-existing condition belongs to a qualified clinician, and does not offer a prescriptive recommendation.
