Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Marcus, 37, first-time marathoner
Marcus finished his first marathon but describes the last six kilometers as brutal. His legs went "dead and heavy" and would not respond, and separately he felt "foggy, irritable, and a little unsteady," and had trouble doing simple math to check his pace. He had one small carbohydrate gel around the halfway point and nothing after that. He asks: "Did I just run out of oxygen? My coach said I should have breathed differently."

Your Tasks

  1. Correct the "ran out of oxygen" claim. Explain, mechanically, what actually limited Marcus late in the race, using muscle glycogen and liver glycogen specifically.
  2. Account for why his symptoms came in two different flavors, heavy legs versus mental fog, rather than one single feeling.
  3. Explain why carbohydrate specifically, and not just "more fuel in general," was the missing piece, given how much stored fat he still had available.
  4. Give one practical, general-education fueling suggestion for his next race, and state clearly where general education ends and individualized coaching or clinical care begins.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not prescribing an individualized race-fueling plan or diagnosing a medical condition. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Individualized fueling plans and diagnosis belong to a qualified coach or clinician working within their credential.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Distinguishes muscle glycogen (local, powers contraction) from liver glycogen (defends blood glucose).
  • Explains hitting the wall as glycogen depletion, correctly linking each pool to its own symptom.
  • Frames the problem as fuel availability, not oxygen deprivation.
  • Offers a practical, general-education fueling suggestion within scope.
  • Identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
