The Scenario

Scenario · Alex, 38
Alex is training for a half marathon and, almost without fail, hits a wall of heavy legs and foggy thinking around mile 10 of every long run, even though earlier miles feel fine. At the same time, Alex has decided to add two extra high-intensity interval sessions per week and cut daily calories substantially, hoping to "recomp," meaning lose fat and build visible muscle at the same time before race day. A training partner also told Alex that doing extra ab exercises every morning will "burn off the belly fat" faster. Alex asks you: "Why do I hit the wall, will this plan actually work, and is my partner right about the ab exercises?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain why Alex hits the wall around mile 10, using the three-system blend and glycogen depletion.
  2. Explain what training adaptation would push that wall further out, naming the mitochondrial mechanism involved.
  3. Evaluate Alex's recomposition plan (large calorie deficit plus added high-intensity training) using the two-way relationship between body composition and metabolism. Is it realistic as designed, and what tension should Alex expect?
  4. Correct the ab-exercise claim, identifying whether it is a magnitude error or a location error, and why.
  5. State when this situation would move from general education into needing a professional's evaluation, without diagnosing anything yourself.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating integrated understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing or prescribing a training plan. A responsible response explains the mechanisms, gives an honest assessment of the plan's tensions, and identifies what if anything (persistent excessive fatigue, marked performance decline, or menstrual changes) warrants evaluation by a qualified clinician or sports dietitian.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains hitting the wall via the three-system blend and glycogen depletion.
  • Explains the mitochondrial and crossover adaptation that would extend time to the wall.
  • Evaluates the recomposition plan using the composition-metabolism two-way street, with an honest, realistic assessment.
  • Correctly identifies and corrects the ab-exercise myth with proper mechanism-versus-location reasoning.
  • Identifies when professional evaluation is warranted, and does not diagnose.
