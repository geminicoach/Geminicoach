The Scenario

Scenario · Dana, 27
Dana has been eating very little for about ten weeks, hoping to lose weight quickly before an event. Her weight loss has stalled over the past three weeks despite sticking to the plan. She also mentions, almost in passing, that she feels cold all the time now, her hair seems to be thinning, her energy has crashed, and her periods have stopped. A coworker told her the stall means she has "wrecked her metabolism forever" and should eat even less to force it to respond. Dana asks: "Did I break my metabolism, and should I cut calories further?"

Your Tasks

  1. Using honest total daily energy expenditure accounting, explain why weight loss can stall even while someone stays in a calorie deficit.
  2. Address the coworker's "wrecked forever" claim using what the chapter says about adaptive thermogenesis: how large is it, and is it permanent?
  3. Identify which of Dana's symptoms go beyond ordinary adaptive thermogenesis and explain why they warrant professional evaluation rather than more restriction.
  4. Give a responsible, general-education response, and state clearly where education ends and clinical care begins.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Cold intolerance, hair thinning, and missed menstrual cycles alongside restricted eating can reflect thyroid dysfunction, relative energy deficiency, or disordered eating, and evaluating and treating those is the job of a qualified clinician, not a course.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains honest TDEE accounting (NEAT drop, resting-rate drift, measurement error) as a driver of the stall.
  • Correctly characterizes adaptive thermogenesis as real but modest and reversible, retiring the "wrecked forever" claim.
  • Identifies the red flags (missed periods, hair thinning, cold intolerance) as warranting professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
  • Gives a responsible, non-restrictive general-education suggestion and states the education/clinical boundary clearly.
