Chapter 5 : Choosing the Fuel

A small, fast tank (carbohydrate) and a vast, slow tank (fat). Intensity decides which one the body leans on.

The Fuel Pools

Muscle glycogenStored inside the fibers that burn it. Private, local, fast. Fuels contraction of that muscle only; roughly 300 to 400+ g total.
Liver glycogenSmall store (roughly 80 to 100 g). Defends blood glucose for the whole body, especially the brain, which needs circulating glucose.
Intramuscular triglycerideFat droplets stored inside muscle fibers, close to the mitochondria that burn them.
Adipose triglycerideThe body's largest energy store by far, roughly 70,000 to 100,000+ kcal in a lean adult. Slow to mobilize and burn.

The Crossover Concept

  • Crossover point : intensity where fat's and carbohydrate's percentage shares are equal.
  • Below it, fat supplies the larger share; above it, carbohydrate does.
  • Fatmax : intensity where absolute fat burning (g/min) peaks, usually lower than the crossover point.
  • Neither fuel is ever fully off. This is a continuous shift in proportion, not a switch.
  • Endurance training shifts the crossover point to a higher intensity.

The Lactate Threshold

  • A zone bounded by two inflection points, not one single line.
  • "Anaerobic threshold" is a misnomer: the muscle is not out of oxygen.
  • Rising lactate reflects accelerated glycolysis (production) outpacing clearance, not suffocation.
  • Lactate is a recyclable fuel, shuttled to the heart and liver, not a waste product.
  • Delayed-onset muscle soreness comes from mechanical microdamage, not lactate.

Hitting the Wall

  • Caused by glycogen depletion, not simply running out of oxygen.
  • Falling muscle glycogen : heavy, unresponsive legs; forced slowdown.
  • Falling liver glycogen : low blood glucose; mental fog, irritability.
  • Ingesting carbohydrate during a long event spares both pools and delays the wall.

Why Rate Beats Size

  • Fat is huge but slow: only the oxidative pathway can burn it, and it is oxygen-limited.
  • Carbohydrate is small but fast: it can also run anaerobically for a rapid top-up.
  • Steep demand can only be met by carbohydrate, forcing the fuel choice as intensity rises.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of fuel selection during exercise, not a license to diagnose or treat, or to prescribe an individualized fueling plan. Confusion, unsteadiness, or fainting during exercise are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
