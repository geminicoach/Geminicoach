Chapter 8 : The Afterburn Audited

The afterburn is real but small. The honest daily energy budget is dominated by resting metabolism and ordinary movement, not one workout.

EPOC: Excess Post-Exercise Oxygen Consumption

Fast componentResolves within about an hour; phosphocreatine resynthesis, oxygen store refill, elevated breathing and heart work.
Slow componentCan linger for hours after very demanding sessions; driven by elevated temperature, catecholamines, cortisol, glycogen resynthesis, and tissue repair.
Honest magnitudeTypical afterburn: roughly 25 to 70 kilocalories, even after hard interval sessions. That is about 1 to 4 percent of a typical day's intake.
The "48-hour burn" claimTakes the faint slow component from extreme sessions and treats it as large and universal. It is neither.

The Four Parts of TDEE

  • Resting metabolic rate (RMR): ~60–70%. Driven mostly by lean tissue, especially liver, brain, heart, kidneys.
  • Thermic effect of food (TEF): ~10%. Protein costs the most to process; fat costs the least.
  • Exercise activity thermogenesis (EAT): deliberate workouts; usually a modest wedge of the whole pie.
  • Non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT): walking, standing, fidgeting, chores. Often the largest swing factor.

NEAT: The Quiet Giant

  • Can differ by up to roughly 2,000 kilocalories per day between two similarly sized people.
  • Falls quietly during sustained dieting, narrowing the intended deficit without any conscious choice to move less.
  • Shaped by occupation, climate, walkability, and individual neural regulation of spontaneous movement.

Measurement Carries Error

  • Basal metabolic rate (BMR): strictest conditions (fasted, waking, thermoneutral, no prior activity).
  • Resting metabolic rate (RMR): slightly less strict; runs a few percent higher than BMR.
  • Predictive equations (e.g., Mifflin-St Jeor) are accurate within 10% for only about 7 in 10 people.

"Starvation Mode," Corrected

  • Adaptive thermogenesis is real: a modest drop in resting expenditure beyond what tissue loss alone predicts.
  • Measured size in normal-weight subjects: on the order of about 100 kilocalories per day.
  • It slows progress; it does not halt it, and it is not a permanent metabolic injury.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of honest energy accounting, not a license to diagnose or treat. Persistent fatigue, cold intolerance, hair changes, or missed menstrual cycles alongside restricted eating can reflect thyroid dysfunction, relative energy deficiency, or disordered eating, and warrant evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional. Work within your role, whatever it is.
