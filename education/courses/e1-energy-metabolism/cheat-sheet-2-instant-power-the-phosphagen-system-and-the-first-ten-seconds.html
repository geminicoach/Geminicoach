Chapter 2 : Instant Power

The phosphagen (ATP-PCr) system: highest power, smallest capacity, gone in about ten seconds.

The Creatine Kinase Reaction

Backup batteryCreatine phosphate (phosphocreatine), stored in muscle, cannot power contraction directly.
The reactionCreatine phosphate + adenosine diphosphate → (creatine kinase) → adenosine triphosphate + free creatine.
SpeedRuns in a single enzymatic step, the fastest of the three resynthesis pathways.
Oxygen needed?No. Fully anaerobic; produces no lactate and no carbon dioxide.

The Central Bargain

  • Power : the highest of any resynthesis system.
  • Capacity : the smallest; the tank empties in roughly ten seconds of maximal work.
  • All three systems are active from the first instant of effort; ten seconds marks when phosphagen dominance fades, not a hard cutoff.
  • Adenylate kinase (two adenosine diphosphate → one adenosine triphosphate + one adenosine monophosphate) offers a small extra backup as adenosine diphosphate accumulates.

Which Activities Live Here

  • Maximal single-effort lifts, jumps, throws, and sprint starts.
  • Anything requiring peak force or peak speed for a few seconds at most.
  • Beyond about ten seconds, glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation take over a growing share of ATP resynthesis.

Refilling the Tank

  • Phosphocreatine resynthesis after intense exercise has two phases: a fast phase, largely complete within about two to three minutes, and a slower phase taking several more minutes.
  • Resynthesis of phosphocreatine during rest depends on oxidative metabolism, which is why rest interval length matters for repeated maximal efforts.
  • This kinetics is the physiological basis for the three to five minute rest intervals used between heavy singles in strength training.

Creatine Supplementation

  • The body makes creatine from three amino acids and also obtains it from meat and fish.
  • Supplementation raises muscle phosphocreatine stores; it does not add ATP to muscle directly.
  • Among the best-supported ergogenic aids for short, repeated, high-intensity efforts.
  • Individualized safety questions (for example, with kidney conditions) belong to a qualified clinician.

Myths to Retire

  • The phosphagen system produces no lactic acid; the "gassed" feeling after a maximal effort is not acid buildup from this system.
  • Delayed onset muscle soreness after heavy lifting is caused by mechanical microdamage and repair, not by acid from the phosphagen system.
  • The ten-second figure is a fade point for dominance, not a switch that turns the system off.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the phosphagen system fuels brief, explosive effort, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe supplementation. Any question involving a pre-existing medical condition, medication, or organ health is a sign that warrants evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
