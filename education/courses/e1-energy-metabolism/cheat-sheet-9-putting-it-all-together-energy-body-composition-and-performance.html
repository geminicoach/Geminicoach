Chapter 9 : Putting It All Together

One integrated system: fuel systems blend, thresholds shift with training, mitochondria explain the shift, and honest accounting explains body composition change.

Three Systems, One Continuous Blend

Phosphagen systemCreatine phosphate, no oxygen needed. Instant power, exhausted in roughly 10 seconds. Dominates a 100 meter sprint.
Glycolytic systemBreaks down carbohydrate without oxygen. Large power, limited by rising acidity. Central to events of roughly 30 seconds to 2 minutes, such as an 800 meter race.
Oxidative systemUses oxygen in mitochondria to burn carbohydrate and fat. Lower peak power, far more total energy. Dominates anything beyond a few minutes, such as a marathon.

All three contribute at once; their share shifts continuously with duration and intensity, they do not switch on and off in turn.

Thresholds and Flexibility

  • Crossover point: intensity where carbohydrate overtakes fat as the dominant fuel. Training pushes it higher.
  • Lactate threshold: intensity where lactate production outruns clearance. Related to, but distinct from, the crossover.
  • Metabolic flexibility: the ability to switch fuel use appropriately to demand and availability.

Mitochondria: The Common Cause

  • Endurance training raises mitochondrial content and respiratory capacity (Holloszy & Coyle).
  • Trigger: AMPK and PGC-1 alpha, activated by repeated training bouts.
  • More mitochondria: more fat oxidation at a given pace, slower glycogen depletion, less lactate produced, higher VO2max.

Composition ↔ Metabolism

  • Lean mass (especially liver, brain, heart, kidneys, muscle) drives most of resting metabolic rate.
  • Energy balance over months shifts fat and lean mass, which then changes resting metabolic rate again: a loop, not a one-way arrow.

TDEE, Honestly Accounted

  • Measured by doubly labelled water in free-living people; even very active people cap out near 2 to 2.5 times RMR.
  • Compensation: added exercise is often partly offset by less spontaneous movement and a slight drop in resting rate.
  • As body mass falls during weight loss, TDEE drifts down too, so a fixed kcal-to-weight rule overstates long-term results.

Three Myths, Retired

  • Afterburn myth (magnitude error): EPOC is real but small; it is not a fat-loss engine.
  • Starvation mode myth (magnitude error): adaptive thermogenesis is real, modest (~100 kcal/day), and reversible, not permanent damage.
  • Spot reduction myth (location error): a worked muscle draws fat from the shared bloodstream, not preferentially from fat sitting on top of it.
Where education ends   This builds an integrated understanding of energy metabolism, not a license to diagnose or treat. Persistent unexplained changes in weight, energy, temperature tolerance, or menstrual function, and suspected relative energy deficiency in sport (RED-S), are clinical questions for a qualified healthcare professional. Work within your role, whatever it is.
