Chapter 8: Defend or Prune an Inherited Cabinet

Take the five-gate pipeline and run it on a real, messy cabinet, ending in a short justified plan or an honest "very little," within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Ruth, 58
Consider Ruth, who eats a reasonably varied omnivore diet, walks most days, and takes one prescription medication for blood pressure. Over the past two years she inherited a cabinet from podcasts and a well-meaning friend: a daily multivitamin, a greens powder, a broad-spectrum probiotic she cannot name a reason for, a "stress support" blend, fish oil, iron she started on her own because she "felt a bit tired," vitamin D, and a B12 tablet. She is not looking for a longer list. She asks a single, honest question: "Which of these actually earn a place, and which should come out?"

Your Tasks

  1. Run the five-gate pipeline forward on Ruth's situation. Explain what each gate is checking, and why starting from the bottles she already owns is the wrong direction.
  2. Sort her items into the low-risk baseline tier and the test-first targeted tier. Say which items need a measured number before anyone acts, and which of her current items she is taking without that number.
  3. Because Ruth is older and on a medication, explain why the final gate, screening the whole stack for risk and interactions, carries more weight here than it would for a healthy twenty-five-year-old.
  4. State Ruth's defensible outcome: the short list that earns a place, or the honest "very little, pending a test," with a one-sentence defense for anything you keep. Then state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating the reasoning and plain-language communication, not diagnosing, interpreting Ruth's labs, setting her doses, or clearing her interactions. A responsible response runs the gates, points anything clinical toward a qualified clinician, and does not prescribe. Interpreting a ferritin result, deciding an iron dose, and confirming that a supplement is safe alongside a prescription medication belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Runs the five gates forward from gap to bottle, not backward from the inherited items.
  • Identifies that most inherited items (multivitamin, greens, unindicated probiotic, "stress support," collagen-type add-ons) die at Gate 3 for lack of a goal-specific gap and goal-specific evidence.
  • Separates baseline from test-first, and flags iron and vitamin D as items that need a measured number; notes the self-started iron on "feeling tired" as the move the framework forbids.
  • Gives the interaction screen extra weight because Ruth is older and on medication, and assesses the stack as a system rather than item by item.
  • Ends with a defensible outcome (short list or honest "very little, pending testing"), and refers dose, lab interpretation, and interaction decisions to a clinician without diagnosing.
