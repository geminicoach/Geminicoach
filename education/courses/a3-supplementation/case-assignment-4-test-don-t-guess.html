Chapter 4: Reason Through a "Just In Case" Bottle

Take the "test, don't guess" discipline and use it to reason through a real supplement decision, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Jade, 27
Consider Jade, who trains for road races on a fairly high running volume and moved to a mostly plant-based diet about a year ago. For the past couple of months she has felt flat: sessions that used to feel easy now feel heavy, she is foggy by mid-afternoon, and her hands run cold. A teammate mentions iron, she remembers a podcast about athletes and low iron, and she buys a high-dose iron supplement and starts taking it daily. Three weeks in she feels a little better, decides it worked, and plans to keep going. She has never had a blood test. She asks you: "Sounds like I nailed it, right?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain why "I feel tired, so I'll take iron" is a guess rather than a decision, and what the honest version of that thought would be.
  2. Build Jade's risk profile from the details given, and explain why her real risk factors make testing more clearly warranted, not less, and why iron is not the only nutrient in play.
  3. Explain why a normal complete blood count would not settle the iron question and why a normal total serum B12 would not settle the B12 question, and name the more informative tests without interpreting any specific number.
  4. Identify which of the four blind-supplementation failure modes Jade has walked into, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care (ordering and reading labs, setting a dose) begins.

Your Response

0 words
Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing or prescribing. A responsible response explains the reasoning, points to testing where it would change the decision, and identifies what warrants professional evaluation. Ordering and interpreting lab tests and setting a dose from a result belong to a qualified clinician; a learner does not diagnose from labs.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Frames fatigue as a nonspecific one-bit signal, and testing rather than a bottle as the right next step.
  • Builds Jade's risk profile (menstruating, mostly plant-based, high training load) and explains why it makes testing warranted rather than optional.
  • Explains why a normal complete blood count and a normal total serum B12 can both mislead, and names the more informative tests (ferritin read with awareness of inflammation, transferrin saturation, functional B12 markers).
  • Identifies at least two blind-supplementation failure modes at play (overshooting iron, masking a possible B12 problem, false security from a three-week lift).
  • Stays within scope: does not diagnose, interpret a specific result, or prescribe a dose.
