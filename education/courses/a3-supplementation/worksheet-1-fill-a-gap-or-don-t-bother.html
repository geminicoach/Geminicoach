Part A · State the Gap Principle

The whole course hangs on one sentence. Reconstruct it, then take it apart.

  1. Write the gap principle in your own words: a supplement earns a place only if two conditions hold. Name both.
  2. The chapter says the gap principle "moves the burden of proof." In the backward model the default is "probably take it." In the gap model the default flips. Explain what the new default is and why that flip matters.

Part C · Food First and the Matrix Advantage

  1. The chapter insists food first is "an evidence position, not a slogan." Give the two evidence-based reasons whole foods and fortified foods usually close a gap more reliably than an isolated capsule.
  2. Explain the "matrix advantage" in your own words. Name at least two co-travelers a whole orange delivers that isolated vitamin C leaves out, and say why that can change how much of the nutrient the body actually uses.
  3. Isolating the presumed active component of fruits and vegetables has repeatedly failed to reproduce their benefit in trials. What does that finding imply about the confidence with which anyone can promise a capsule will replace a food?

Where education ends
This worksheet builds the ability to reason about supplements, not a license to diagnose, prescribe, or treat, in yourself or anyone else. A supplement is not a treatment. Explain how the decision pathway works, and treat anything that looks clinical (a suspected deficiency, alarm symptoms, a drug-nutrient interaction, or a request to correct a diagnosed condition) as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role: refer what belongs to a clinician, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose or self-prescribe.
