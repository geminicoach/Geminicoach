Part B · Run It Forward, Not Backward

  1. The chapter warns that the most common mistake is running the pipeline backward. In your own words, describe what "running it backward" looks like, and why it produces a fragile stack.
  2. A person tells you they already love a specific pre-workout blend and want to find a reason to keep taking it. Explain, using the gates, why starting from the bottle is the wrong move, and what the first question should be instead.
  3. Nearly half of supplement users report taking things simply to "improve or maintain" general health, with no specific gap in mind. Explain why the pipeline is designed to replace that motivation rather than serve it.

Part D · One Process, Different Answers

The proof that this is a process and not a product list is that the same gates produce different stacks for different people. Pick one of the two profiles below, run it through all five gates, and end with a short justified plan (which items earn a place, and why) or an honest "nothing."

Choose one profile (a) A busy general-population person: varied diet, some sun, not pregnant, not on a restrictive pattern, not elderly.   (b) A fully plant-based lifter who trains hard four days a week in a northern city.
  1. Which profile did you choose, and what real gaps (if any) does their context predict at Gate 1?
  2. For each candidate gap, does food close it conveniently, and if not, which food-only exception applies?
  3. State the defensible outcome: the short list that survives all five gates, or the honest "very little, possibly nothing," and say what drove the difference, the process or the person's context.

Part E · The One-Sentence Defense and the Prune

A defensible answer has a fixed shape: it names the gap, states why food did not close it, points to evidence for the actual goal, and ends with the condition that would remove the item.

  1. Write one full defensible sentence, in that shape, for a single item you would keep for someone you know (including yourself).
  2. Now steelman a cut: pick an item that many people take and argue, in your own words, why it should come out even though removal has no marketing behind it.
  3. The chapter frames review as three moves: re-test the gap, remove what no longer earns its place, and resist the pull to add. Explain why "the person who can confidently remove four items understands more than the person who added a fifth."

Where education ends
This worksheet builds the reasoning that generates a defensible stack, not a license to diagnose, order tests, or prescribe doses, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how the five gates work, and treat anything that looks clinical (a lab value to interpret, a dose to set, a possible supplement-drug interaction, or a symptom like unexplained fatigue or weight loss) as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose or self-prescribe.
