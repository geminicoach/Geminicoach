Chapter 5 : The Gut Frontier

Three products, one shelf, wildly uneven evidence. Rate each on its own merits and hold different confidence levels at once.

The Three Products, Strongest First

FiberBest-supported gut intervention, full stop. Dose-response links to lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, less heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, colorectal cancer (Reynolds 2019). Food first.
ProbioticsReal but narrow. Effects live at the strain-and-outcome level, not "probiotics." No class effect. A benefit for one strain does not transfer to another product.
EnzymesEssential for documented deficiency, unproven for everyone else. Most buyers do not have the problem the capsule solves.

Fiber and the Gap

  • The gap : most eat ~12 to 14 g/day against a 25 to 30 g-plus target; fewer than 5% hit it.
  • Soluble/fermentable : oats, legumes, apple; gels and ferments to short-chain fatty acids.
  • Insoluble : bran, skins, nuts; adds bulk, speeds transit.
  • Prebiotic : food for the microbes you already carry, not a dose of new ones.
  • Butyrate : the SCFA that fuels the colon lining (colonocytes).
  • Food beats powder: mortality data ride on whole-food fiber.

The Tolerance Ceiling

  • Fermentation makes gas. That is normal, not a malfunction.
  • Ramp too fast and you outrun your microbes: bloating, cramping. Usually a rate problem, not a fiber problem.
  • Rule: increase gradually over weeks, add water alongside.
  • 12 to 28 g over a month is comfortable; in two days it is not.

Probiotics: Read the Label

  • Three levels : genus, species, strain. Benefit lives at the strain.
  • Strain count : mostly theater. Ten random strains beat zero right ones only by accident.
  • CFU number : meaningless unless the strain has a proven effect at a known dose.
  • "Clinically studied" : usually means some strain, some outcome, some population, not this blend for your symptom.
  • Rational buy needs three things: named strain, evidenced outcome, matching dose.

Where Probiotics Are Real

  • Antibiotic-associated diarrhea : S. boulardii, L. rhamnosus GG; effect depends on strain, dose duration, and even which antibiotic (Szajewska 2026).
  • IBS : specific strains beat placebo for specific symptoms; no one product covers the syndrome (Xie 2023).
  • Colonization : most strains just pass through; the same product colonizes some guts and not others; stool tests miss the gut lining (Zmora 2018).

Enzymes: Narrow Doors

  • PERT : prescribed, dose-matched treatment for exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis, pancreatic cancer). Real medicine (Ianiro 2016).
  • Lactase : for lactose intolerance (the ~75% adult default), taken with dairy.
  • Pattern: a known deficiency, corrected by the specific missing enzyme, for a specific food.
  • General "digest better" blends in healthy people: evidence lacking (Roxas 2008).

Microbiome Tests and the Four Steps

  • No validated "optimal" gut to match you against; composition shifts week to week; stool misses the lining.
  • Same company tests, defines the "problem," and sells the fix. Precision theater, not medicine.
  • Step 1 : how strong and relevant is the best evidence?
  • Step 2 : at what level does the effect live?
  • Step 3 : do you actually have the condition it targets?
  • Step 4 : default to food and the cheaper, broader-benefit option. And refuse to moralize.
Where education ends   This builds evidence-reasoning skill, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe. A persistent change in digestion, bleeding, unexplained weight loss, or symptoms that worry you are reasons to see a qualified clinician, not reasons to buy a supplement. Match a product to a named problem or skip it, and work within your role, whatever it is.
