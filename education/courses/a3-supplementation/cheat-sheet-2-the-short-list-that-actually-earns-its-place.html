Chapter 2 : The Short List That Actually Earns Its Place

One rule (evidence belongs to supplement-plus-outcome), three gap-fillers, three performance tools.

The Operating Rule

  • Strength of evidence is a property of a supplement paired with a named outcome, never of the pill alone.
  • Finish the sentence every time: evidence for what outcome, in whom, and how strong.
  • "Backed by science" is a marketing phrase until it names the outcome. If it cannot, it is doing more selling than informing.
  • Food first: for the gap-fillers, diet often closes the gap and "associated with" is not "fixes."

The Short List at a Glance

Vitamin DGap-filler. Strong case for children, adults 75+, pregnancy, prediabetes; weak as a disease-prevention pill for healthy adults under 75. Repletion dose ~1000 to 2000 IU/day with food.
Omega-3Gap-filler. Strong for lowering triglycerides; contested, modest for broad heart outcomes. Use EPA/DHA from fish or algae, not ALA from flax.
MagnesiumGap-filler. Real, common shortfall (~half under-consume); weak-but-plausible for faster sleep onset in older adults; thinner for stress and energy.
CreatinePerformance. Best-evidenced ergogenic; 3 to 5 g/day monohydrate. Modest memory signal, larger in older/stressed people. Strong long-term safety.
CaffeinePerformance. Strongest for endurance and alertness; 3 to 6 mg/kg ~60 min before. Watch sleep and tolerance.
ProteinConvenience. A fast way to hit a daily target; benefit plateaus near 1.6 g/kg. Not a muscle potion.

Vitamin D: The Nuance

  • Status : serum 25(OH)D; below ~30 nmol/L is deficient, the gray zone above is contested.
  • Empiric dosing : children, adults 75+, pregnancy, prediabetes (Demay et al., 2024).
  • VITAL : raises blood levels but did not cut total cancer or major cardiovascular events in healthy adults (Manson et al., 2020).
  • Ceiling : fat-soluble and stored; chronic megadosing (well past 4000 IU) can raise calcium and harm. More is backwards.
  • Testing : not routine for healthy adults; earns its cost when the answer changes management.

Omega-3: Source & Dose

  • EPA + DHA : the active marine forms, from fish or algae; algae is bioequivalent (Bailey et al., 2025).
  • ALA : plant form (flax, walnuts); converts to EPA/DHA poorly, low single digits.
  • Triglycerides : strong, dose-dependent; often needs the multi-gram range.
  • Tradeoff : higher doses carry atrial-fibrillation and bleeding signals (Khan et al., 2021). More is not automatically safer.

Creatine: Use It Right

  • Mechanism : tops up phosphocreatine to rebuild ATP; lets you train slightly harder, which builds the muscle.
  • Dose : 3 to 5 g/day monohydrate. Loading (~20 g/day for 5 to 7 days) is faster, not better.
  • Form : plain monohydrate has the evidence; premium "absorption" blends do not beat it.
  • Safety : strong long-term data; kidney and cramp fears do not hold up in healthy people (Kreider et al., 2017).

Caffeine: The Tolerance Catch

  • Mechanism : blocks adenosine, lowering perceived effort; the work is not lighter, it just feels it.
  • Dose & timing : 3 to 6 mg/kg ~60 min before; more adds jitters, not performance (Guest et al., 2021).
  • Sleep : long half-life; keep it to the first half of the day to protect sleep.
  • Tolerance : daily heavy use blunts the effect. Strategic use beats constant use.

Magnesium & Protein: Honest Limits

  • Magnesium : judge it by whether sleep onset improves; well-absorbed forms (citrate, glycinate, malate) over oxide.
  • Associations : the long disease list is a reason to eat magnesium-rich food, not proof a pill prevents disease.
  • Protein : supports training when you fall short of your total; benefit plateaus near 1.6 g/kg (Morton et al., 2018).
  • Type : whey, casein, or plant blends all work; the daily total outweighs the choice.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of the evidence, not a license to diagnose, treat, or prescribe. Existing kidney, liver, or metabolic conditions, pregnancy, medication interactions, and symptoms of toxicity (nausea, excessive thirst, confusion at high vitamin D intakes) are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
