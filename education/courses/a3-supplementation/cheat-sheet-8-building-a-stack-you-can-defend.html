Chapter 8 : Building a Stack You Can Defend

The deliverable is a process, not a product: five gates that run in order and end in a short, justified plan or an honest "nothing."

The Five Gates (run in order, each can stop a candidate)

1. Real gapA genuine shortfall from diet, life stage, context, or a measured deficiency, not a wish to feel better. Decide here whether to test or predict.
2. Food firstFood is the default answer. "Food first, but not always food only": a bottle earns its place only when a specific exception makes food-only fail.
3. Evidence for the goalAdequate evidence for your goal, your population, at a realistic dose. Evidence for something else does not count. Most hyped items die here.
4. Quality and doseThird-party testing, the studied form, and a dose that matches the trials. Perfect evidence is useless if the product does not match it.
5. Screen the stackAssess the whole stack as a system for risk and interactions, especially as it grows and especially on medication.

Run It Forward

  • Forward = gap, then food, then evidence, then product, then interaction screen.
  • Backward = fall in love with a bottle, then hunt for a gap to justify it. This is the common mistake.
  • The first question is never "what's the best supplement?" It is "what is actually missing, and is a supplement the right tool?"

Two Tiers

  • Baseline (low-risk) : reasonable for many without a test, because the gap is common or the safety margin is large. Example: creatine, adequate protein.
  • Test-first (targeted) : needs a measured number before acting. Example: iron (excess is real harm), vitamin D, B12 in most people.
  • Fast triage: would a reasonable clinician be comfortable with this without a blood test? If no, and there is no number behind it, that is a problem.

Same Process, Different Answers

  • Plant-based eater : B12 and often algae omega-3 and more. A justified list.
  • Poor sleeper : mostly behavior; a small conditional add at most.
  • Older adult on meds : real gaps, heavy interaction screen.
  • Healthy general person : often near-zero, maybe vitamin D. Zero is a valid output.

The One-Sentence Defense

  • Fixed shape: name the gap, say why food did not close it, point to evidence for the actual goal, and end with the condition that would remove it.
  • The skeptical-colleague test: say the sentence out loud, bottle by bottle. If you reach or pad, the item goes.
  • "You don't need anything, and here is why" is a complete, defensible answer.

Review and Prune (the 3 R's)

  • Re-test the gap: a gap that has closed is no longer a reason.
  • Remove what no longer earns its place. The hard part is willingness to cut, not knowing what.
  • Resist the constant pull to add. Subtraction, not collecting, is the mark of competence.
Where education ends   This builds the reasoning that generates a defensible stack, not a license to diagnose, order or interpret tests, or set doses. Interpreting a lab value, dosing a test-first nutrient like iron, and clearing a supplement-drug interaction are clinical decisions that belong to a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
