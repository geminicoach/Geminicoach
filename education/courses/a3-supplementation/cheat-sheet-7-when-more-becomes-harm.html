Chapter 7 : When More Becomes Harm

A physiologically active compound has no inert "off" state. Potent enough to help means potent enough to harm at the wrong dose, combination, or body.

Three Beliefs This Chapter Retires

Harmless by defaultBenefit and risk are not on separate switches. If the upside collapses to near zero, the downside still stays real.
More is betterThe dose-response curve turns helpful into harmful as intake climbs. More is a direction, not always an improvement.
"Natural" is safeIsolated, concentrated, and high-dose is a different intervention from the same nutrient in whole food.

The Upper Limit (UL)

  • UL : highest daily intake judged unlikely to harm almost anyone; built from the no-harm level divided by an uncertainty factor.
  • A conservative ceiling with margin baked in, not a line where harm switches on.
  • Not a target. Every step toward it climbs the harm side of the curve, not the benefit side.
  • Three numbers per nutrient: what you need, what you already get, and the ceiling. Land between them.

Two Drains

  • Water-soluble (B-complex, C) : drain open, surplus largely excreted; forgiving but not infinite (B6 neuropathy, C and oxalate stones).
  • Fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) : drain welded shut, banked in fat and liver; chronic excess accumulates toward hypervitaminosis with no daily reset.
  • Fat-soluble toxicity comes from supplements and hidden stacking, almost never from food.

The U-Curve & Three Minerals

  • Iron : no exit route; surplus accumulates and drives oxidative damage in liver, heart, glands.
  • Zinc : does not poison directly; high dose induces copper loss, causing anemia and nerve damage.
  • Selenium : narrowest window; selenosis sits just above the requirement (hair and nail loss, garlic breath).
  • Narrow window : correct a measured shortfall to a target and stop. Do not load for insurance.

The Antioxidant Paradox

  • Beta-carotene pills raised lung cancer in smokers (ATBC 1994; Omenn 1996).
  • High-dose vitamin E raised prostate cancer (Klein / SELECT 2011).
  • High-dose C and E blunted training adaptations (Ristow 2009): the exercise stress is a signal, not just damage.
  • Food source, dose, and context decide the outcome, not the molecule alone.

Interactions : Two Types

  • Pharmacokinetic : changes how much drug is present (St. John's wort speeds clearance; calcium and iron bind drugs in the gut).
  • Pharmacodynamic : changes what a fixed amount does (vitamin K vs warfarin; vitamin E and fish oil add to bleeding risk).
  • Frequent offenders: St. John's wort, calcium, iron, ginkgo, vitamin E. Frequent victims: warfarin, insulin, digoxin (narrow therapeutic index).

Risk Surface Grows Fast

  • For n items, unique pairs = n(n-1)/2. 2 items : 1 pair. 5 : 10. 10 : 45.
  • The fifth product adds four new pairings; the tenth adds nine. Not linear, geometric.
  • A bigger stack is a bigger risk surface. Keep stacks deliberately small.

Where Risk Concentrates

  • Pregnancy : preformed vitamin A is a teratogen; lower ceiling, higher stakes.
  • Liver or kidney disease : impaired buffering and clearance; excess accumulates.
  • Polypharmacy : more drugs, more nodes in the web, often less organ reserve.
  • Hidden stacking across fortified products can clear a UL while every label looks fine.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of dose, accumulation, competition, and crosstalk, not a license to diagnose, prescribe, or manage a medication. A medication in the picture, a higher-risk situation (pregnancy, liver or kidney disease, polypharmacy), or an alarm sign such as unusual bleeding is a reason to bring the full list of everything taken, by name and dose, to a qualified clinician or pharmacist. For interactions, that disclosure is the control measure, not a formality. Work within your role, whatever it is.
