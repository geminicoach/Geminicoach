The Scenario

Scenario · Mel, 41
Consider Mel, who lifts weights three times a week, works long indoor days at a high latitude, and reads enough to be suspicious of obvious junk. Mel's cabinet reflects that: only supplements believed to be "backed by science." There is vitamin D, fish oil, magnesium for sleep, a tub of premium creatine marketed as "advanced absorption," caffeine pills taken whenever remembered including some afternoons, and three different protein powders. Mel feels vaguely virtuous about the collection and vaguely unsure whether any of it is doing anything, and asks: "What should I keep, what should I fix, and what is just expensive?"

Your Tasks

  1. Sort the three gap supplements (vitamin D, magnesium, fish oil). For each, name the outcome that would justify it, judge whether Mel's context makes it a real candidate, and say how Mel should know if it is working.
  2. Fix the performance shelf (creatine, caffeine). Say what to keep, what to change about form, dose, or timing, and why the change matters for someone who trains.
  3. Resolve the three protein powders, and explain what actually drives the result the powders are sold for.
  4. State clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins for Mel, and name anything that would send you to a qualified clinician rather than answering yourself.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing or prescribing. A responsible response explains the evidence and the gap logic, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation (existing conditions, toxicity symptoms, pregnancy, medication interactions), and does not diagnose or set a personal medical dose. Diagnosis, prescription, and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Applies the evidence-for-an-outcome rule: finishes the sentence with a named outcome, population, and rough strength.
  • Sorts the gap supplements correctly: strong vitamin D case for Mel's indoor, high-latitude context; magnesium judged honestly by sleep onset; fish oil likely redundant if oily fish is already eaten and the goal is only vague heart health.
  • Fixes the performance shelf: plain monohydrate at 3 to 5 g/day over the premium "advanced absorption" blend; caffeine pulled to the first half of the day and used around training; tolerance from daily heavy use addressed.
  • Resolves the protein powders as one logistics tool, with the daily total near 1.6 g/kg driving the result rather than the type or the brand.
  • Stays within general education, does not diagnose or prescribe, and flags what warrants a clinician.
