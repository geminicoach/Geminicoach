Cheat-Sheet · A3 Supplementation

Chapter 1 : Fill a Gap or Don't Bother

Start from the shortfall, not the sales pitch. No real gap, no purchase.

The Gap Principle

  • A supplement earns a place only if a real shortfall exists that it can plausibly close, and only if food cannot close that shortfall more reliably first.
  • It flips the default from "probably take it" to "probably skip it." The bottle is the fallback, not the starting point.
  • The backward pattern: hear about a product, buy it, then invent the reason. Reverse it.

Four Ways to Read a Claim

DeficiencyTrue clinical shortfall with consequences. Correcting it is treatment, not wellness.
InsufficiencyBelow a reference threshold but no overt disease. A population odds nudge, not a personal diagnosis.
ManufacturedAn invented gap with no defined requirement to be short of. Never justifies action.
OptimizedNormal status pitched as "leaving performance on the table." Usually has no anchored number.

Food First (An Evidence Position)

  • Whole foods deliver a matrix: fiber, protein, minerals, and co-travelers that shape absorption.
  • Fortified staples already close many population gaps quietly.
  • Isolated extracts often fail to reproduce whole-food benefits in trials.
  • Reach for the bottle only when food genuinely cannot close the gap.

Baseline vs. Targeted

  • Baseline : low-dose hedge against a common shortfall in your group (a vegan and B12). Modest by design.
  • Targeted : an adequate dose for a confirmed individual problem (iron for diagnosed anemia).
  • Different burdens of proof. Always know which decision you are making.

The Four Gates

  • 1. Gap? Deficiency, insufficiency, or manufactured. Manufactured stops here.
  • 2. Food? Can your real diet close it? If yes, food fix, not a purchase.
  • 3. Baseline or targeted? This sets the evidence bar.
  • 4. Evidence and dose? Right compound, adequate dose, under the ceiling. If unconfirmed: test and target.

Why "It Can't Hurt" Is Usually Wrong

  • Nutrients have upper limits; overlapping products can stack past them.
  • Every supplement carries cost in money and attention.
  • The phrase is the signature of having skipped the gap question.
  • Six inputs decide every answer: diet, sun, training load, life stage, medications, measured status.
Where education ends   This builds the ability to reason about supplements, not a license to diagnose, prescribe, or treat. A supplement is not a treatment. A suspected deficiency, alarm symptoms, or a drug-nutrient interaction (for example a long-term medication that lowers a nutrient's absorption) are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
