Part B · Read the Supplement Facts Panel

The panel is the only legally standardized place the product has to tell you what is inside. Read it in order.

  1. Why do you read the serving size first, before any ingredient amount? Explain what it scales and why a company might make a serving several capsules.
  2. A label reads "Vitamin C, 500 mg" with a serving size of three capsules and ninety capsules in the bottle. How much vitamin C is in one capsule, how many days does the bottle actually last, and how does that change the real price per dose?
  3. What does a percent Daily Value (%DV) actually tell you, and what does a blank %DV or a dagger symbol next to a botanical (for example "Ashwagandha root extract, 300 mg") mean for the dose question?

Part C · Proprietary Blends and the Descending-Order Tell

  1. A proprietary blend lists several ingredients under one total weight and hides the individual amounts. Explain, in your own words, why a hidden per-ingredient dose should be treated as an absent dose until proven otherwise.
  2. A label shows "Recovery Complex, 500 mg" listing five ingredients, and the marketing centers on the fifth one. What is the maximum possible amount of that fifth ingredient, what is the realistic amount given descending-order labeling, and what does that tell you about the marketing?
  3. Why is the word "blend," "matrix," or "complex" itself a documented risk flag, even before you read the ingredients?

Where education ends
This worksheet builds supplement literacy, not a license to diagnose, treat, or clear anyone for use. Explain how labels, doses, and claims work, and treat anything that looks clinical (a strong adverse reaction, a suspected undeclared drug, a medication interaction, or a medical condition behind the question) as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
