Chapter 4 : The Soreness Myth

Delayed onset muscle soreness is a coarse, easily manufactured signal, not a scoreboard for growth or readiness.

What DOMS Is

DefinitionTender, aching sensation and mechanical hyperalgesia in skeletal muscle after unaccustomed exercise, most often eccentric loading.
OnsetRoughly 8 to 24 hours after the triggering session.
PeakBetween 24 and 72 hours after the session.
ResolutionWithin about a week.
Main triggersEccentric loading (muscle lengthening under tension) and unaccustomed loading (unfamiliar to the tissue).

Four Persistent Myths

  • Not lactic acid : blood lactate clears within about an hour, long before soreness builds.
  • Not a growth readout : Damas et al. (2016), early high-soreness synthesis is mostly repair, not new tissue.
  • Not a workout grade : soreness tracks novelty, not training quality; beginners are sore at almost everything.
  • Not the same as readiness : a person can be sore and ready, or pain free and under-recovered.

The Repeated Bout Effect

  • One prior bout of eccentric exercise protects against damage and soreness from a similar bout later.
  • Mechanisms: added sarcomeres in series, altered inflammatory response, better force distribution through connective tissue (McHugh, 2003).
  • Adaptation itself suppresses the soreness signal, well before the training benefit stops accruing.
  • Fading soreness under a well-matched, progressive program usually means the protection is working, not that training has stalled.

The Mechanism Is Unsettled

  • Classic account: mechanical disruption, then inflammation, then sensitized nerve endings (nociceptors).
  • Mizumura and Taguchi (2016): hyperalgesia can occur without much measurable damage, via neurotrophic pathways (NGF, GDNF).
  • Creatine kinase (a damage marker) does not track soreness or growth cleanly, and varies enormously between people.
  • Multiple mechanisms likely operate together; no single tidy story explains it all.

Ordinary DOMS vs. Warning Sign

  • Ordinary DOMS : diffuse, bilateral, in the muscle belly, follows the expected timeline, eases with light movement.
  • Warning sign : sharp, joint-centered, appears during the movement, worsening swelling, marked one-sided pain, or lingers past about a week.
  • Rare but serious: severe swelling, dark or tea-colored urine, and pain far beyond what the exercise would justify can signal a medical emergency.
Where education ends   This builds an accurate model of ordinary training soreness, not a license to diagnose or treat. Sharp or joint-centered pain, worsening swelling, marked asymmetry, or pain lingering well past a week are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
