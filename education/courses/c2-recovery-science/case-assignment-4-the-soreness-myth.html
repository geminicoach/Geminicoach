The Scenario

Scenario · Naomi, 29
Consider Naomi, who switched to a new strength program three weeks ago. Her first two sessions left her unable to sit down comfortably for two days afterward, a soreness she found almost thrilling because it felt like proof the program was doing something. Her training loads have kept climbing steadily since then, exactly as the program prescribes, but by week three she is barely sore the morning after a session. She has started wondering whether her body has "gotten used to it" in a bad way, and whether she should swap in a few unfamiliar exercises to "wake the muscles back up" and feel that soreness again. She asks: "Did the program stop working, and should I switch things up to feel it again?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, why Naomi's soreness faded even though her training load kept climbing (use the repeated bout effect).
  2. Use the logic from Damas and colleagues (2016) to explain what her early, high-soreness weeks were probably building compared to what her current, low-soreness weeks are probably building.
  3. Decide whether Naomi should switch exercises to chase the soreness back, and justify your answer.
  4. Give one practical, general-education suggestion for how Naomi should actually track her progress, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies the repeated bout effect as the reason soreness faded even as load kept climbing.
  • Explains that early, high-soreness protein synthesis is mostly repair (Damas et al., 2016), and that synthesis tracking real hypertrophy shows up later, once damage markers attenuate.
  • Correctly advises against switching exercises to chase soreness, explaining that doing so resets repeated bout protection without adding real adaptation.
  • Offers a practical, general-education suggestion within scope (e.g., track load and reps over time, not soreness, as the real progress marker).
  • Identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
