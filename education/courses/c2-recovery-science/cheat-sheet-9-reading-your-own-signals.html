Chapter 9 : Reading Your Own Signals

No single signal is trustworthy alone. Read convergence against your own baseline, and act on the pattern, not the panic.

The Six Signals

Resting heart rateCheap and comparable day to day if measured consistently; low specificity, only tells you something shifted.
HRVThe noisiest single reading on the list; only useful as a rolling average against your own baseline.
Session / daily RPEQuantifies the training dose actually delivered; cheap, sensitive, catches rising internal cost.
Sleep qualityNot the same variable as sleep duration; rate it directly rather than inferring it from hours slept.
MoodFast and sensitive; weak alone, but a meaningful pattern across several consecutive days.
Body weightThe slowest signal; read a weekly average, since daily swings are mostly water.

Reading Principles

  • Subjective self-report often outperforms objective measures for sensitivity to training load.
  • Convergence : when several weak signals agree, meaning multiplies.
  • Baseline : read every signal against your own rolling history, never a population norm.

Push, Hold, Back Off

  • Push : signals sit at or above baseline; spend the readiness you have earned.
  • Hold : signals are mixed or mildly down; trim volume or intensity.
  • Back off : multiple signals converge below baseline across several days.

Grading Wearables Honestly

  • Raw sensor data (resting heart rate, HRV trend) is useful texture.
  • Proprietary composite readiness scores are undisclosed and thinly validated.
  • Treat the branded score as one weak, opaque signal, never the verdict.

The Boundary

  • Sharp or localized pain, illness, or persistent dysfunction is not a readiness input.
  • It is a cue to stop self-managing and seek professional evaluation.
  • Keep this flag loud and separate from the other six signals.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how to read your own readiness signals, not a license to diagnose or treat. Sharp or localized pain, illness, or dysfunction that does not resolve with normal recovery is a sign that warrants evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
