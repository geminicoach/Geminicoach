The Scenario

Scenario · Kian, 24
Consider Kian, a recreational climber who works rotating retail shifts. On workdays he goes to bed around eleven and wakes at quarter past four for an early shift, roughly five hours and fifteen minutes in bed. On his two days off he sleeps until about nine. He calls himself "a five-hour person" because he does not feel sleepy at his desk. His finger and pulling strength keep improving month over month, but a new climbing technique he has been drilling for weeks simply is not sticking from one session to the next. He has also had two minor colds in the past six weeks, more than usual for him. He is wondering whether he needs a different training split for technique days.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what Kian's early alarm is doing to his sleep architecture, and which stage of sleep he is disproportionately losing.
  2. Connect that specific stage to motor learning and skill consolidation, and explain why his strength keeps rising while his technique acquisition has stalled.
  3. Evaluate his claim that he is "a five-hour person" using the evidence on subjective versus objective decline.
  4. Give one practical, general-education suggestion, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains that an early alarm truncates the back end of the night and disproportionately cuts REM-rich late cycles rather than early slow-wave sleep.
  • Connects REM loss to motor learning and skill consolidation, explaining why strength can rise while technique acquisition stalls.
  • Uses Van Dongen et al. (2003) to challenge the "five-hour person" self-assessment, citing the subjective/objective disconnect and cumulative deficit.
  • Offers a practical, general-education suggestion within scope (protecting sleep timing, narrowing the weekday-weekend swing).
  • Identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
