Chapter 1: Reason Through a Real Plateau

Take the stimulus, recovery, adaptation loop and use it to explain a real training plateau, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Deshawn, 29
Consider Deshawn, a recreational lifter ten weeks into a new program. His numbers stalled around week seven, so three weeks ago he added a second daily session, reasoning that if one hard session drives adaptation, two must drive more. Since then his lifts have gotten lighter, not heavier, his legs feel flat most mornings, and his resting heart rate has been running a few beats higher than usual on several mornings this week. He has not missed a session and has not gotten hurt. There is no pain and no joint dysfunction. Deshawn wonders: "Should I push through this, or is something actually going wrong?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what is happening to Deshawn's training using the stimulus, recovery, adaptation loop (focus on what adding a second daily session did to his recovery window).
  2. Use the fitness-fatigue model to explain why his current, lighter numbers do not necessarily mean his underlying fitness has stopped improving.
  3. Decide whether this reads as ordinary functional overreaching that could still rebound, or a drift toward non-functional overreaching, and say how you know.
  4. Give one practical, general-education suggestion, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the loop and why stacking a second daily session before recovery completes interrupts adaptation rather than adding to it.
  • Applies the fitness-fatigue model to explain why his current low numbers do not necessarily mean his fitness has stopped rising.
  • Reasons about whether this looks like functional overreaching that could still resolve or a drift toward non-functional overreaching, and justifies the call.
  • Offers a practical, general-education suggestion within scope (e.g., back off, space stimulus, planned recovery).
  • Identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
