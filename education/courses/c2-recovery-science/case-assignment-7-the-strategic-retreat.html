Chapter 7: Reason Through a Stalled Block

Use the fitness-fatigue model and the deload logic from the chapter to reason through a real training plateau, in plain language.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Denise Okafor, 44
Denise is five weeks into an eight-week build block ahead of her first half marathon, with no rest week scheduled until week eight. For the first three weeks her easy runs felt easy and her long-run pace kept improving. Over the last two weeks, her easy runs at the same heart rate have started to feel noticeably harder, and her long-run pace has not improved despite two more weeks of training. She is not sore in any one spot, she is sleeping normally, and nothing in her life outside training has changed. This weekend's plan calls for a hard interval session. She is deciding whether to run it as written or scale it back, and she wants to understand what is actually happening before she decides.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, using the fitness-fatigue model, what is most likely happening to Denise's pace at a given heart rate over these five weeks.
  2. Decide whether Denise's situation looks like functional overreaching that will resolve on its own, or something drifting toward non-functional overreaching, and name the specific signal that would tell you which.
  3. Decide whether a deload is warranted right now, and if so, describe how you would shape it: what to cut, what to protect, and roughly how deep.
  4. Give one practical, general-education recommendation for this weekend's session, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding of training-load reasoning, not diagnosing or prescribing medical care. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies the fitness-fatigue mechanism behind the stalled pace: fitness still rising, fatigue rising faster and unrelieved, preparedness bending down.
  • Distinguishes functional from non-functional overreaching and names an autoregulatory signal (pace at fixed heart rate) rather than relying on feel alone.
  • Concludes a deload is warranted now and shapes it correctly: cut volume, protect some intensity, keep frequency, choose a depth that fits a routine block.
  • Gives a practical, general-education recommendation within scope, and states where general education ends and clinical care begins.
