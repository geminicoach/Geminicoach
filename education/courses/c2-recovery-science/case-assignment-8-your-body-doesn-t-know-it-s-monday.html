Use the total-load model from the chapter to reason through a training stall that has nothing to do with the training itself.

The Scenario

Scenario · Theo Lindqvist, 31
Theo is a software engineer and recreational powerlifter, eight weeks into a strength block that had been progressing steadily. His company then entered a three-week product launch crunch: sixty-hour work weeks, sleep down to five or six broken hours a night, and a constant low hum of pressure from his manager. He kept his training program exactly as written, reasoning that since the sets and loads on the page had not changed, his results should not change either. By the end of the crunch, his squat had stalled well below where it was tracking, he felt persistently drained between sessions, and he caught a cold that lingered for two weeks instead of his usual few days. He wants to know whether his program stopped working, given that he never actually changed it.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, using the shared regulatory systems described in the chapter, why Theo's work stress could affect his training outcomes even though his training itself never changed.
  2. Define total load in this context, and explain specifically why keeping training constant during the crunch overspent Theo's recovery capacity.
  3. Recommend how Theo should have adjusted his training during the three crunch weeks, and how he should resume afterward.
  4. State where this kind of adjustment (a training-load decision) ends and where a symptom would warrant professional evaluation instead.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding of total-load reasoning, not diagnosing or prescribing medical care. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the shared regulatory mechanism (allostatic load): the same systems (HPA axis, sympathetic, immune) respond to work stress and training stress alike.
  • Correctly frames total load as training plus life stress, and explains why unchanged training overspent capacity during the crunch.
  • Gives an appropriate practical adjustment: reduce training volume and intensity during the crunch weeks, protect sleep where possible, resume normal progression after.
  • Identifies that symptoms persisting well past the stressful period warrant professional evaluation, not just further programming changes.
