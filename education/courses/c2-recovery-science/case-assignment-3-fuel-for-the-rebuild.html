The Scenario

Scenario · Aisha, 27
Consider Aisha, ten weeks out from her first competitive fitness event. Three days a week she trains a fasted 6 a.m. conditioning session followed by a noon Olympic lifting session, roughly six hours apart. She follows a 16:8 eating pattern, so nothing crosses her lips between the morning session and her first meal at noon. To make her competition weight category, she has also trimmed her daily calories over the past three weeks while her training volume climbed. Lately she has felt lightheaded partway through her noon sessions, her lifts have gotten noticeably lighter, and she just realized her period, usually predictable, is now two weeks late. She wants to know if she simply needs a faster post-workout shake.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain whether a "faster" post-workout shake actually addresses her compressed six-hour training window, and why or why not.
  2. Explain the mechanism connecting her trimmed calories and rising training volume to energy availability, and how that could account for the lightheadedness and the lighter lifts.
  3. Identify which of her symptoms is a genuine signal that goes beyond what a nutrition framework alone can interpret, and explain why.
  4. Give one practical, general-education suggestion, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician or registered dietitian.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the compressed-window carbohydrate timing issue and why a "faster" shake alone is not the core fix given the fasting pattern and totals.
  • Explains the energy availability mechanism linking trimmed calories and rising volume to lightheadedness and reduced lift performance, referencing the big-rocks hierarchy.
  • Identifies the missed period as a genuine signal consistent with Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport that is outside what a nutrition framework alone can interpret.
  • Offers a practical, general-education, in-scope suggestion (e.g., a carbohydrate snack inside the compressed window, or general sequencing advice).
  • Clearly states where general education ends and clinical or dietitian care begins, and does not diagnose.
