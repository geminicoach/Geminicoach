Chapter 6 : Ice, Heat, and Honest Grades

Grade every modality on the same four questions: mechanism, evidence strength, perceived recovery versus real adaptation, and interference.

The Modality Report Card

Cold water immersionStrong evidence: clearly lowers soreness. Strong evidence: clearly blunts hypertrophy when used habitually after strength sessions (Roberts et al., 2015; Pinero et al., 2024). Mild interference for strength; negligible for endurance.
Heat / saunaWeak, unclear evidence as acute recovery. Moderate, promising evidence as a repeated stimulus for endurance heat acclimation when programmed deliberately (Ahokas et al., 2025).
MassageStrong evidence, best-in-class for lowering soreness and perceived fatigue; neutral on structural adaptation; no interference (Dupuy et al., 2018).
CompressionLarge but equivocal evidence; small positive on perceived soreness; neutral on adaptation and performance; harmless (Weakley et al., 2021).
Foam rollingMinor and partly negligible for recovery; better evidence as a pre-training warm-up tool; no interference (Wiewelhove et al., 2019).

The Four-Question Rubric

  • What does the modality actually do, mechanistically?
  • How strong is the evidence (meta-analysis vs. one small study)?
  • Does it move perceived recovery, real adaptation, or both?
  • Does it ever interfere with the adaptation training was meant to provoke?

Why Cold Water Can Backfire

  • Post-training inflammatory and cell-signaling activity is message traffic, not damage to erase.
  • Cooling tissue soon after training blunts satellite cell activation and mTOR signaling (Roberts et al., 2015).
  • Lowers myofibrillar protein synthesis even with adequate protein intake (Fuchs et al., 2019).
  • Interference is training-mode specific: strongest for hypertrophy, milder for strength, negligible for endurance.

Reasoning by Goal

  • What is the block building right now (hypertrophy, strength, endurance)?
  • How close is the next real event that will actually be judged?
  • What does the modality cost, given those two answers?
  • Off-season and building: protect the adaptive signal. Near a goal event: prioritize feeling and performing well.

The One Real Exception

  • Of the five modalities graded, only cold water immersion shows confirmed interference with adaptation, and only in specific conditions.
  • Massage, compression, and foam rolling carry no documented adaptive downside.
  • Everything in this chapter still ranks below the foundational levers: sleep and nutrition.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of what recovery modalities actually do, not a license to diagnose or treat. Pain that is sharp, joint-centered, or that persists beyond the ordinary soreness window is a signal that warrants evaluation by a qualified clinician, not an icing or heating protocol. Work within your role, whatever it is.
