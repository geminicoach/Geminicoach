Chapter 3 : Fuel for the Rebuild

Bricks and electricity for the rebuild: materials, fuel, fluid, and a solvent energy budget.

The Four Budgets

ProteinBuilding material for repair and remodeling. Total daily intake, 1.4-2.0 g/kg/day, is the dominant lever; whole foods work as well as powders.
CarbohydrateRefills glycogen spent during training. Provision speeds resynthesis; added protein does little once calories are matched.
HydrationThe medium and coolant that lets every other reaction run. Thirst-guided drinking covers most sessions.
Energy availabilityThe gate before everything: intake minus exercise expenditure. Chronically too low, and the body triages construction.

Protein, In Numbers

  • Daily total : 1.4-2.0 g/kg/day for most exercising individuals (Jäger et al., 2017).
  • Per-meal dose : roughly 0.25 g/kg, about 20-40 g, spaced every 3-4 hours.
  • Leucine threshold : the per-serving trigger, roughly 700-3,000 mg, that switches on synthesis.
  • Source matters less than total; whole foods carry a fuller nutrient matrix.

The Anabolic Window Myth

  • Pre-exercise feeding keeps amino acids elevated well past the workout; there is no cliff-edge window.
  • Once total daily protein is controlled for, timing's independent effect on hypertrophy is not significant (Schoenfeld et al., 2013).
  • Carbohydrate timing genuinely matters only when two hard sessions fall within about 8 hours of each other (Kerksick et al., 2017).

Hydration Rules of Thumb

  • Losses beyond about 2% of body mass carry a real performance cost (Casa et al., 2019).
  • Weigh before and after a representative session; a pound lost is close to a pint of fluid lost.
  • Thirst is a well-calibrated signal for most people in most situations; forced overdrinking has its own risk.

Big Rocks Before Small Rocks

  • 1. Be solvent: sufficient energy availability.
  • 2. Hit the totals: daily protein, daily carbohydrate, fluid and sodium.
  • 3. Tune timing only where the clock is the binding constraint.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of the recovery-nutrition budgets, not a license to diagnose or treat. Persistent under-fueling, preoccupation with food or body composition, lost menstrual function, or recurrent stress fractures are signs consistent with Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs) and warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician or sports dietitian.
