The Scenario

Scenario · Priya Chandran, 29
Priya is a recreational cyclist who wears a fitness tracker and checks her readiness score most mornings. After a friend's birthday dinner, wine, a late meal, and a shorter night than usual, her tracker showed a sharp overnight drop in heart rate variability and flashed a low readiness score. She nearly canceled the next morning's planned interval ride. Separately, over the past four consecutive mornings, she has noticed her resting heart rate sitting five to six beats above her usual baseline, her mood has felt flat, and her sleep quality ratings have been lower than normal, alongside a general, non-specific tiredness. On top of all this, she mentions a sharp, localized pain on the outside of her ankle near her Achilles tendon that started during a recent run and has not gone away despite two easy days.

Your Tasks

  1. Evaluate the single overnight HRV drop on its own. Is it meaningful on its own, and why or why not?
  2. Evaluate the four-day convergent trend in resting heart rate, mood, and sleep quality. What does this pattern indicate, and what action, push, hold, or back off, fits it best?
  3. Address the Achilles pain separately from the readiness signals. Where does this fall relative to what a monitoring dashboard is built to interpret, and what should Priya do about it?
  4. Bring it together into one practical recommendation for Priya's week, being explicit about which parts are a training-load decision and which part is not.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding of readiness-signal reasoning, not diagnosing or prescribing medical care. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies the single-morning HRV dip as low-value and noisy, and names a concrete source of noise (alcohol, late meal, disrupted sleep, unfamiliar conditions).
  • Reads the multi-day convergent trend in resting heart rate, mood, and sleep quality as a genuine signal, referencing convergence and baseline, and recommends hold or back off.
  • Separates the sharp, localized Achilles pain as outside monitoring scope and states it warrants professional evaluation, not a training-load adjustment.
  • Synthesizes into one clear, practical recommendation that distinguishes trend-reading from single-reading reactions.
