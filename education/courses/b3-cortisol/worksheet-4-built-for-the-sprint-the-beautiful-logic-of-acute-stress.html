Where education ends
This worksheet builds understanding of how the acute stress response is built, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Explaining that a fast adrenaline surge and a lingering cortisol wave are normal features of a near miss is useful general education. Judging whether a specific reaction in a specific body is a sign of a problem is a clinical judgment, and it belongs to a qualified clinician who can take a history and interpret it in context. Treat anything that looks clinical (chest pain, breathlessness, fainting, reactions without a trigger, or a fear that is interfering with daily life) as warranting evaluation. Work within your role: a non-clinician refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
