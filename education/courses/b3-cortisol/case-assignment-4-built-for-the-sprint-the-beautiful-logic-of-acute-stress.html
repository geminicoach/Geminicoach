Chapter 4: Reason Through a Real Reaction

Take the logic of acute stress and use it to explain a real reaction, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Tomas, 38
Consider Tomas, who was crossing a street when a cyclist he never saw swerved past him at speed. He jumped back onto the curb, missing a collision by inches. In the instant before he had even registered the cyclist, his heart was already hammering and his hands had flown up. For a good fifteen minutes afterward he felt jittery, his mouth was dry, and a low buzz of alertness would not settle, even though the danger was long gone. Days later he can still replay the moment with unsettling vividness. What alarms Tomas is not the near miss. It is his own reaction, which struck him as excessive, as if his body had overreacted to a threat that had already passed. He asks: "Is my stress response too big? Is something wrong with me?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what happened in Tomas's body using the two arms from the chapter. Name which arm made his heart pound before he consciously saw the cyclist, and name which arm was still climbing while the first faded.
  2. Account for why the jittery buzz lingered for fifteen minutes after the danger was gone, and why the memory remains so vivid. Tie each to a specific feature of the design.
  3. Decide whether the intensity and persistence Tomas describes read as normal physiology or as something to worry about, and say precisely how far your reasoning can go and where it must stop.
  4. Draft an honest, plain-language answer to his question, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical judgment begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, frames the intensity as a feature of the design rather than a fault, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not issue a verdict on this individual's health. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Correctly separates the two arms by messenger, route, and speed: the sympathetic-adrenal-medullary (SAM) system releases adrenaline within seconds via a fast neural route; the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis releases cortisol over minutes via a slower hormonal cascade.
  • Explains the lingering buzz using the staggered timing: the fast arm switching off while the slow arm is still ramping up.
  • Frames the intensity, persistence, and vivid memory as adaptive design (off-switch, negative feedback, stress-enhanced consolidation), not malfunction.
  • Stays within scope: explains the general mechanism without issuing a clinical verdict on Tomas specifically.
  • Identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
