Chapter 2: Read the Printout, Not the Panic

Take the wiring diagram of the stress axis and use it to make sense of a real lab result, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Bram, 38
Consider Bram, who has been feeling "wired and tired" for months and ordered an at-home saliva cortisol kit. He spat into four little tubes across one day and received a printout with a jagged line: higher in the morning, lower by night. A forum told him the line meant his adrenals were "burning out" and that he should buy a supplement to rebuild them. To check, Bram held his saliva numbers against a blood-test reference range he found online; they did not match, which convinced him something was broken. He asks: "Are my adrenals running dry? Is this proof?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, why cortisol cannot be "depleted" the way Bram fears, using the manufacturing model from the chapter (built on demand from cholesterol, not drawn from a stored reserve).
  2. Account for why comparing his saliva numbers to a blood reference range is a measurement error, using the free-versus-total cortisol distinction.
  3. Interpret the jagged line itself: what does a higher-in-the-morning, lower-at-night curve delivered in pulses tell you about whether his feedback loop is working?
  4. State clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins, including what, if anything, would actually warrant a qualified clinician's evaluation.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Bram's printout is a teaching object, not a verdict; diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician who can order the right test, in the right fluid, and read it in full clinical context.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains that cortisol is manufactured on demand from cholesterol, not stored, so there is no reserve to drain.
  • Distinguishes free cortisol (what saliva reflects) from total cortisol (what most blood assays report), and names the comparison as a units error.
  • Reads the jagged line as a normal diurnal rhythm plus hourly ultradian pulses, the signature of a working self-limiting loop.
  • Frames the result as normal physiology rather than a diagnosis, and stays within scope.
  • Identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose.
