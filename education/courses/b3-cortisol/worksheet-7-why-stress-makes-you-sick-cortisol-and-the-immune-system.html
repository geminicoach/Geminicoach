Part C · The Central Paradox

  1. In your own words, state the prescription-pad paradox: synthetic cortisol is prescribed to shut inflammation down, yet chronic stress, which floods the body with the same hormone, is associated with more inflammation. Why is the naive syllogism "cortisol suppresses immunity, so stress suppresses immunity" incomplete rather than simply wrong?
  2. Explain how glucocorticoid receptor resistance (GCR) resolves the paradox. Make clear how a person can have high circulating cortisol and rising inflammation at the same time, and name what has actually failed.
  3. The chapter says inflammation rises "not despite the high cortisol but, in a real sense, because the cells stopped answering it." Restate that idea using the on-switch and off-switch language, and say which switch has been unplugged.

Part E · Reason About the Timing

Use the "deploy then stand down" logic from the chapter.

  1. A learner feels sharp and healthy all through a punishing work crunch, then gets a head cold within days of the pressure lifting. Explain why the illness surfaces in the letdown rather than during the crunch itself. What was the acute machinery doing while she felt fine, and what had been quietly building?
  2. Wound healing depends on pro-inflammatory cytokines arriving at the cut, yet chronic stress is elsewhere linked to too much inflammation. Explain why this is not a contradiction, using the idea that dysregulation means losing the ability to put the right amount of inflammation in the right place at the right time.
