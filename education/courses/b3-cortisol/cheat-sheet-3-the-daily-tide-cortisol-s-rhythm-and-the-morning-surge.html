Chapter 3 : The Daily Tide

Cortisol is a tide, not a thermostat: one curve, a tenfold daily swing, and a number that means nothing without its hour.

The Diurnal Curve

Pre-dawn (~04:30)Cortisol already climbing hours before waking. The rise is anticipatory, not reactive: the body pays in advance for getting up.
Peak (~08:30)Highest point of the day. A high morning value is expected, not a warning.
DaytimeA long, gentle decline across the waking hours.
Nadir (~midnight)Broad, flat overnight trough, the lowest point. Healthy people should be low here.
SwingRoughly tenfold between morning peak and overnight trough. Healthy is a trajectory, not a value.

The Two Clocks

  • Suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) : master clock above the optic chiasm; light-entrained via melanopsin retinal cells.
  • Adrenal peripheral clock : local clock that gates how strongly the gland answers adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH).
  • Bimodal timekeeping : SCN schedules the axis; the adrenal clock tunes the response. Robust, but damaged when the two are forced out of phase.

The HPA Signal

  • SCN : sets the daily schedule.
  • CRH : corticotropin-releasing hormone, from the hypothalamus.
  • ACTH : adrenocorticotropic hormone, from the pituitary.
  • Cortisol : from the adrenal cortex; also helps synchronize clocks body-wide.

Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR)

  • A sharp extra surge of about 50 to 75% in the 30 minutes after waking, on top of the rising curve.
  • Sample at 0, 15, 30, 45 min; verify true waking time. Miss the timing and the marker dissolves.
  • AUCg : area under the curve to ground = total output. AUCi : area to increase = the dynamic rise only.
  • Dual-control model : part fixed circadian clock, part anticipatory forecast of the day's demands.

Why Timing Is Everything

  • A value without a timestamp is uninterpretable, not just less useful.
  • ~480 nmol/L at 8 a.m. and ~60 nmol/L at midnight can be the same healthy person.
  • Read the curve, not the number. One draw cannot show a shape.
  • Late-night salivary cortisol is useful only because it samples the trough.

Deviations to Recognize

  • Night-shift work : SCN and behavior out of phase; morning peak blunts, evening cortisol rises, disorder spreads into metabolism.
  • Flattened curve : a statement about shape, not height; the peak-to-trough difference shrinks. Invisible from a single draw.
  • The single-sample trap : judging a moving value against a fixed threshold "diagnoses" a healthy person as high in the morning and low at night.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the rhythm works, not a license to diagnose or treat. A high morning number is usually a healthy feature, not a defect. Interpreting a real person's results, including your own, is diagnosis and belongs to a qualified clinician who can order properly timed, repeated testing. Work within your role, whatever it is.
