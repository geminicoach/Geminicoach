Chapter 5: Read the Load, Not the Number

Use the keystone of the course to make sense of a scattered set of borderline numbers, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 30 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Greta, 52
Consider Greta, who has spent the better part of a decade in demanding work with light, broken sleep. At a routine check she is told her resting blood pressure has been "creeping up" over the past year, though still short of any threshold. She mentions feeling "wired but tired." She has since read that her morning cortisol rise looks blunted and her evening level higher than it should be, and that her waist measurement and fasting glucose have drifted upward. Every individual value has been described to her as "within range." Greta wants to understand the pattern: "If nothing is actually wrong with any single number, why does it feel like something is quietly adding up? And what, if anything, should I make of it?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what allostatic load is and why reading Greta's numbers together tells a different story than reading any one of them alone. Use the distinction between allostasis and homeostasis to ground it.
  2. Identify which of the four modes of allostatic overload best fit the picture she describes (the flattened diurnal slope, the blunted feedback, the rising resting pressure), and explain the mechanism behind at least two of them.
  3. Connect the pattern to the cardiovascular system: why is a resting blood pressure that "creeps up" one of the most legible readouts of load, and how does a flattened evening curve cost the vessels their nightly recovery window?
  4. State clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins here: what a learner can responsibly explain, what must not be self-scored or self-diagnosed, and what warrants a clinician.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible, role-neutral response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Interpreting or scoring allostatic load in a real person, and deciding what a creeping blood pressure means for them, belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Reads the scattered numbers together as multi-system allostatic load rather than as isolated values, grounded in allostasis versus homeostasis.
  • Names the correct failure modes (repeated hits and/or failure to shut off; blunted feedback showing as a flattened diurnal slope) with the mechanism behind them.
  • Connects the pattern to the cardiovascular ledger and explains the lost nightly recovery window from an elevated evening curve.
  • Frames "everything is individually fine" and "real cumulative wear is accumulating" as both true, avoiding both alarm and dismissal.
  • States scope of practice clearly: no self-scoring, no self-diagnosis, and refers the rising blood pressure and any red flags to a qualified clinician.
