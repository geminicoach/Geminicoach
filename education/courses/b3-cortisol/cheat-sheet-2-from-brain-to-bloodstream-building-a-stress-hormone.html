Chapter 2 : From Brain to Bloodstream

Three organs, one molecule of cholesterol, one feedback loop: how a stressor becomes cortisol, and then turns itself off.

The Cascade (HPA Axis)

1. HypothalamusParaventricular nucleus neurons sense a stressor and release corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) into the private pituitary portal vessels (sometimes with vasopressin, AVP, to potentiate it).
2. Ant. pituitaryCRH drives corticotrophs to cleave the precursor proopiomelanocortin (POMC) and release adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) into the general bloodstream. Short half-life (~10 min), so it arrives in pulses.
3. Adrenal cortexACTH reads out at the melanocortin-2 receptor (MC2R) on the zona fasciculata and drives synthesis of cortisol, built on demand from cholesterol.

Why a Chain, Not One Gland

  • Amplification : a whisper at the top becomes a shout at the bottom; each stage speaks in larger numbers.
  • Fine control : multiple junctions to dial up, damp, delay, or shut off.
  • Multiple inputs : emotion enters at the top, physical signals (blood pressure, glucose, inflammation) enter lower.

The Three Floors

  • Zona glomerulosa : lacks P450c17, makes aldosterone; run by renin-angiotensin and potassium, not ACTH.
  • Zona fasciculata : has P450c17, the true cortisol plant; the floor ACTH shouts at.
  • Zona reticularis : makes adrenal androgens DHEA and DHEA-S.
  • Same cholesterol, different enzymes, different product.

Cholesterol to Cortisol

  • All steroids start from cholesterol.
  • StAR protein ferries cholesterol into the mitochondrion; P450scc then makes pregnenolone. This is the rate-limiting first step and ACTH's main lever.
  • Downstream enzymes (P450c17, CYP21, CYP11B1) act like assembly stations; one missing station reroutes the whole line.
  • Cortisol is made to order, not stored, so glands cannot be "drained."

Negative Feedback (The Core Idea)

  • Cortisol suppresses CRH and ACTH: the output ends the input.
  • Fast brake : seconds, non-genomic; endocannabinoids quiet the hypothalamus.
  • Slow brake : hours, genomic; glucocorticoid receptor (GR) suppresses CRH and POMC genes.
  • Ultradian pulses : the loop's built-in delay makes cortisol oscillate hourly, not sit flat.
  • A healthy response is defined by reliable return to baseline, not by how high it climbs.

Free Versus Bound Cortisol

  • ~80 to 90% bound to cortisol-binding globulin (CBG), ~10 to 15% loosely on albumin, only ~5 to 10% free and active.
  • CBG is not passive: fever and inflammation loosen its grip and release active cortisol locally.
  • Blood assays usually measure total; saliva reflects free; do not compare them directly.
  • Estrogen raises CBG, so it can raise total cortisol without raising the free fraction.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the axis works, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. A jagged saliva curve is usually a normal rhythm, not proof of failure. Unexplained weight loss, darkening skin, dizziness on standing, persistent fatigue, or any result someone is genuinely worried about warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
