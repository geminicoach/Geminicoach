Cheat-Sheet · B3 Cortisol

Chapter 6 : Fuel, Fat, and Muscle

One job (mobilize fuel), performed in the wrong context (chronic, low demand, deaf to insulin) becomes the metabolic bill.

The One-Job Frame

Core jobCortisol mobilizes fuel: it gets energy substrate into the blood and keeps it there. Almost every metabolic effect is a variation on this one theme.
How it actsMainly a transcriptional regulator. It binds the glucocorticoid receptor (GR), enters the nucleus, and retunes hundreds of genes, so effects are slow, broad, and durable over hours to days.
Why it turns badNothing about the molecule changes. The context does: too long, when nothing is burning the fuel, in a body that has stopped listening to insulin.

Liver: New Glucose From Scratch

  • Gluconeogenesis : gluco (glucose) + neo (new) + genesis (creation). Building glucose from non-carbohydrate parts.
  • Raw materials : amino acids from muscle, glycerol from fat, lactate from working tissue.
  • PEPCK (phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase) : the on-ramp; commits carbon to the glucose route.
  • G6Pase (glucose-6-phosphatase) : the off-ramp; lets finished glucose leave the cell.
  • Cortisol controls both ends, so hepatic glucose output climbs and stays climbed.

Blood: Cortisol Versus Insulin

  • Insulin antagonism : cortisol opposes insulin, keeping glucose in circulation.
  • Two fronts: central (more liver output) and peripheral (less uptake at muscle and fat).
  • GLUT4 : the glucose doorway; cortisol blunts its move to the cell surface, so less glucose gets in.
  • Insulin resistance : same insulin doing less; the pancreas shouts louder (more insulin) to compensate.
  • Bathtub image: tap open wider at the liver, drains partly plugged at muscle and fat.

Fat: The Lipolysis Paradox

  • Acutely, cortisol is lipolytic : it breaks fat down.
  • Chronically, it drives visceral fat redistribution : central storage, not uniform gain.
  • The decisive companion is insulin : cortisol plus high insulin favors central storage.
  • 11-beta-HSD1 regenerates active cortisol inside fat and liver, so tissue exposure can be high even when blood cortisol reads normal.
  • "The cortisol level" is not one number in the blood.

Muscle: The Catabolic Cost

  • Catabolic : cortisol activates the ubiquitin-proteasome system via atrogin-1 and MuRF-1, feeding amino acids to the glucose factory.
  • Anti-anabolic : it suppresses IGF-I and upregulates myostatin, both through the PI3K/Akt/FoxO hub.
  • Preferentially wastes fast-twitch type II fibers, the same ones lost in age-related sarcopenia.
  • Fat up and muscle down is one hormonal environment, two faces of one process.

Refusing "Cortisol Detox"

  • Cortisol is not a toxin to flush; it is an essential hormone. "Detox" is a category error.
  • A kit cannot capture cortisol made locally by 11-beta-HSD1, and a capsule cannot block it there.
  • No single lever: visceral fat and insulin resistance emerge from cortisol dynamics interacting with sleep, energy balance, and activity.
  • Honest flip side: sleep, movement, and meal timing matter because they shift the diurnal curve, insulin sensitivity, and muscle mass, not because they "detox" anything.

The Epidemiology Nuance

  • Chronic stress and type 2 diabetes travel together partly through a flattened diurnal cortisol curve (loss of the healthy peak-and-trough) and partly through behavior (food, activity, sleep).
  • The association is comparable in strength to traditional risks like inactivity or adiposity, yet cortisol does not "make you fat" alone. It shifts a metabolic environment shaped by many interacting variables.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how cortisol shapes metabolism, not a license to diagnose or treat. Constructing a plausible mechanism is education; declaring which mechanism is operating in a particular person is diagnosis. Rapid central weight change, unexplained fatigue, rising thirst, or muscle loss you cannot account for are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
