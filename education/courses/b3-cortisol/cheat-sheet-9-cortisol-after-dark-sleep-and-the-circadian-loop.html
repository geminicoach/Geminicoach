Chapter 9 : Cortisol After Dark

One loop, two directions: low nighttime cortisol permits deep sleep, and lost sleep raises the next evening's cortisol.

The Reciprocal Loop

Two-way streetThe 24-hour cortisol waveform is shaped by the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) clock, the ultradian pulse generator, AND sleep/behavior. Sleep is a hand on the wheel, not a passenger (Oster et al., 2017).
Trough permitsLow nighttime cortisol is permissive for slow-wave sleep (SWS). It is low because a restorative brain state requires it, not because low is intrinsically good.
Peak endsThe pre-waking cortisol rise lightens sleep, mobilizes glucose, and readies the cardiovascular system. Its job is to end the night; high is not intrinsically bad.
Reverse arrowSleep loss impairs glucocorticoid negative feedback, raising cortisol the FOLLOWING evening (Leproult et al., 1997). That closes the two facts into one self-reinforcing loop.

Key Terms

  • SCN : suprachiasmatic nucleus, the light-entrained master clock.
  • HPA axis : hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis.
  • CRH : corticotropin-releasing hormone; centrally an alerting peptide that suppresses deep sleep.
  • SWS : slow-wave sleep, the deep, delta-dominated early-night stage.
  • CAR : cortisol awakening response, the peak 30 to 45 min after waking.
  • Zeitgeber : a time-giver; cortisol resets peripheral clocks.

Tired but Wired

  • Homeostatic sleep drive is high (hence tired).
  • The arousal/HPA system runs an evening program that belongs to the morning (hence wired).
  • It is not too little or too much cortisol overall; it is a morning signal at a nighttime hour.
  • Lying there longer or trying harder fails; effort is itself arousing.

Circadian Misalignment

  • Shift work and jet lag attack timing, not amount.
  • Misalignment worsens glucose, insulin, leptin, and blood pressure even at equal sleep duration (Scheer et al., 2009).
  • Cortisol acts as a zeitgeber; peripheral clocks (liver, pancreas) re-entrain at different rates, so "many clocks disagree."
  • The SCN drags back only about one hour per day, so a big shift takes over a week to resolve.

Ties to Metabolism & Load

  • Six nights of four-hour sleep worsened glucose tolerance in healthy young men (Spiegel et al., 1999).
  • Poor sleep and elevated cortisol are the same road to metabolic disease, traveled both ways (Chapter 6).
  • Chronic insomnia shows 24-hour HPA activation and central nervous system hyperarousal (Vgontzas et al., 2001).
  • Sleep disruption is both a cause and a symptom of allostatic load; the nightly reset is what is lost (McEwen, 2006).
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the sleep-cortisol loop works, not a license to diagnose or treat. Chronic insomnia and shift-work disorder are clinical conditions; a single home evening cortisol reading during a misaligned week cannot support a diagnosis. Recognize the pattern, educate, and refer. Work within your role, whatever it is.
