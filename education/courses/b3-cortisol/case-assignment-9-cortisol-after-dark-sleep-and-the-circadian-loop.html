Chapter 9: Reason Through a Real Complaint

Take the sleep-cortisol loop and use it to explain a real complaint, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Della, 44
Consider Della, who works as an emergency-department nurse and rotates onto a week of night shifts every month, spending the other three weeks on a daytime schedule. For most of the past year she has described the same thing: on her night weeks she lies down exhausted at 9 a.m. in a darkened room and cannot drop off, her mind racing and her heart faintly too fast, and her daytime sleep stays short and broken. She has also noticed she feels physically "off" after those weeks, sluggish and puffy, even when she manages a full eight hours. Curious and a little alarmed, she ordered an at-home saliva cortisol kit, took one sample in the evening after a night shift, saw a value the kit flagged as high, and now wants to announce that she has "hormonal insomnia from cortisol dysregulation" and to ask which supplement will bring the number down.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what is happening across Della's night week using the loop from the chapter: how her forced schedule puts cortisol's morning program at her bedtime, why the trough that should permit deep sleep is missing, and how one short day's sleep feeds the next evening's cortisol (Leproult et al., 1997).
  2. Account for why Della can feel metabolically "off" even after a full eight hours of daytime sleep, using circadian misalignment as a timing-based harm independent of sleep duration (Scheer et al., 2009), and connect it to the metabolic and allostatic-load themes of earlier chapters.
  3. Assess Della's conclusion. Which parts of her reasoning rest on sound physiology, and where exactly does she cross from education into a clinical act? Address specifically why one evening home-kit reading, taken during a misaligned week, cannot support what she wants to claim.
  4. State clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins for someone in Della's position, and what a responsible, non-diagnosing response to her would actually say.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Insomnia and shift-work disorder are defined clinical conditions; recognizing the pattern is education, while labeling it or recommending treatment belongs to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Explains the reciprocal loop: low nighttime cortisol permits deep sleep, the pre-waking rise ends it, and sleep loss raises the next evening's cortisol through impaired feedback.
  • Accounts for circadian misalignment as a timing-based harm independent of sleep duration, using shift-work physiology.
  • Connects the loop to metabolism and allostatic load (glucose and insulin handling; McEwen's framing).
  • Frames the "tired but wired" experience as structural physiology, not a personal failing or a self-evident diagnosis.
  • Explains why a single evening home cortisol reading during a misaligned week cannot support a diagnosis.
  • Identifies what warrants professional evaluation and refers; does not diagnose or recommend a supplement.
