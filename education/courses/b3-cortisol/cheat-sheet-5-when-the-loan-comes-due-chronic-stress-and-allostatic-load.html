Cheat-Sheet · B3 Cortisol

Chapter 5 : When the Loan Comes Due

One keystone: a response built for minutes, run for decades, becomes a debt, and the heart is often the first ledger to show it.

The Core Ideas

HomeostasisThe fixed targets the body cannot bargain over (blood pH near 7.4, core temperature near 37°C, oxygen tension). Defended at almost any cost.
Allostasis"Stability through change." The active, anticipatory adjustment of regulated variables (pressure, glucose, cortisol, heart rate) so the fixed ones stay defended. The mechanism of homeostasis, not its enemy.
Allostatic loadThe "price of adaptation": cumulative wear that builds when the body adapts too often, too long, or without recovery. The unpaid principal on the loan.
Allostatic overloadLoad grown large enough to produce disease. Overload, not load, is where pathology begins.

The Loan, Without Metaphor

  • Each stress response borrows: pressure rises, glucose floods, clotting ticks up, the anti-inflammatory brake is applied.
  • Recovery reverses it and repairs the small costs.
  • Take recovery away and the small costs stop being reversed. That is the whole mechanism.
  • Adaptation is cheap when followed by recovery, expensive when it is not.

Two Types of Overload

  • Type 1 : genuine energy deficit (famine, injury, hard winter). The response is correct and it ends.
  • Type 2 : sufficient energy, chronic social and psychological stress that never sounds an "all clear." The dominant human pattern and the rest of the course.
  • The lever is not the presence of stressors but the presence of recovery.

The Four Modes of Overload (one machine, four failures)

1. Repeated hitsEach response is normal, but they arrive so often the body never gets a clean recovery window. Density generates load. Cardiovascular: pressure surges dozens of times a day stress the arterial wall.
2. No habituationThe system fails to learn a benign-but-uncomfortable stressor is survivable, so it keeps firing full-size responses. The commute stays an emergency.
3. No shut-offThe negative-feedback off-switch is faulty (receptor downregulation or feedback damage), so the response continues after the stressor is gone. The path to sustained hypertension, visceral fat, insulin resistance.
4. Inadequate responseToo little cortisol removes its brake on inflammation, so cytokines overshoot. "Low cortisol" is not automatically the opposite of a problem. Unrestrained inflammation drives atherosclerosis through the back door.

The Deformed Rhythm

  • Healthy diurnal curve: sharp rise after waking, early-morning peak, long decline to a near-floor at night.
  • Chronic stress: the peak flattens, the trough rises, amplitude collapses. This is the flattened diurnal slope.
  • Blunted feedback: chronic elevation downregulates glucocorticoid receptors, so the off-signal is heard less well and the axis gets harder to switch off the longer it runs.
  • Flatter slopes predict worse outcomes across most health domains (Adam et al., 2017).

The Cardiovascular Bill

  • The stress response is at its core a cardiovascular event: raise pressure, speed the heart, redistribute flow.
  • A single mental stressor impairs the vessel lining (endothelium) for hours (Ghiadoni et al., 2000).
  • Chronic job strain raises coronary risk across populations (Kivimäki et al., 2012).
  • A persistently active stress-processing brain predicts hard cardiac events (Tawakol et al., 2017; Steptoe & Kivimäki, 2012).
  • The spike is not the injury. The failure to recover from it, over and over, is.

Measuring It, and the Central Correction

  • 10-parameter index (Seeman et al., 1997, 2001): cardiovascular, metabolic, and neuroendocrine markers scored together. No single marker tells the story; the signal is in the multi-system accumulation. Predicts mortality, cognitive and functional decline independent of baseline health.
  • The keystone: the pathology is dysregulation and accumulated load, not a toxic molecule and not "exhausted glands." Cortisol harms by wrong timing, pattern, and absent recovery, not by simple quantity.
  • Every later chapter (metabolism, immunity, gut, sleep, mood, training) is the same four modes across a new system, all reporting back to the cardiovascular account.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how chronic stress and allostatic load work, not a license to diagnose or treat. Computing or interpreting an allostatic-load score in a real person is clinical work. A blood pressure that will not come down, chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or any acute or worsening symptom warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
