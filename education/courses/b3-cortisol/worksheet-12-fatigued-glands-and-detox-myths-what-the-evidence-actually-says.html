Part A · Why "Adrenal Fatigue" Is Not Evidence-Based

State the target fairly before you dismantle it, then explain where it breaks.

  1. In two or three sentences, state the "adrenal fatigue" hypothesis at its strongest: what is chronic stress supposed to do to the gland, and what cluster of complaints is said to follow?
  2. The chapter says the load-bearing error is a single category mistake. Explain how the pitch quietly reclassifies cortisol from a regulated signal built on demand into a depletable substance, and why that switch is the whole problem.

  4. Cadegiani and Kater (2016) ran a systematic review of 58 studies and found no substantiation for the diagnosis. Name two recurring methodological flaws the review catalogued, and explain why a large pile of studies can still point at nothing.

Part D · Blood, Saliva, Urine, and the Direct-to-Consumer Frontier

  1. Salivary cortisol has a legitimate place in real endocrine testing. Explain what it measures that a total serum value does not, and why that makes the failure of mail-order kits a failure of interpretation rather than of the fluid.
  2. The same saliva sample can return two different values from two laboratories. Name the reason (assay-method bias) and explain why a reference range from one method cannot be casually applied to a result from another.
  3. List two reasons a direct-to-consumer (DTC) cortisol kit or wearable sensor cannot deliver the consistency that would make its numbers comparable to a validated reference range. Draw on protocol heterogeneity and the limited validation of consumer devices.

Where education ends
This worksheet builds cortisol literacy, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how the physiology works, correct the myths, and treat anything that looks clinical (profound fatigue, unexplained weight loss, darkening skin, salt craving, dizziness on standing, or recent withdrawal from steroid medication) as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician. Interpreting a cortisol test to diagnose or exclude disease is clinical practice, not education, no matter how well you understand the diurnal rhythm. Work within your role, whatever it is: educate and refer, never diagnose or treat.
