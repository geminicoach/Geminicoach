Chapter 7: Reason Through a Real Complaint

Take the paradox of the same hormone with two faces and use it to explain a real pattern, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Selina, 38
Consider Selina, who has noticed a pattern she cannot shake. Every time a brutal stretch at work finally ends, the quiet that follows arrives with a head cold, sometimes a cold sore, once a flu that flattened her for a week. During the crunch itself she feels sharp, even a little invincible; it is the letdown afterward that makes her sick. She has absorbed the usual explanation, that stress "uses up" her immune reserve and leaves her empty, and it never quite fit, because she feels fine while the pressure is on. There is no persistent fever, no swollen lymph nodes, no unexplained weight loss, and the colds resolve on their own. Selina wonders: "Is my stress destroying my immune system? And why do I only get sick once things calm down?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what happens to Selina's immunity during the crunch and then in the letdown, using the acute-versus-chronic distinction, immune-cell redistribution, and the suppression of cellular, antiviral immunity from the chapter.
  2. Resolve the apparent paradox in her worry: name glucocorticoid receptor resistance (GCR) and use it to explain how cortisol can be high while inflammation is not held in check, so illness in the letdown feels worse rather than better.
  3. Assess whether "stress is destroying my immune system" is an accurate framing. State clearly what the evidence does and does not support, keeping the register at raised susceptibility and altered regulation rather than simple causation.
  4. Give one practical, general-education suggestion within scope, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins for someone in Selina's position.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, keeps the language at association and raised susceptibility rather than certainty, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Distinguishes acute redistribution and modulation from chronic suppression, with stressor duration setting the sign of the effect.
  • Names glucocorticoid receptor resistance (GCR) and uses it to resolve how cortisol can stay high while inflammation is no longer restrained.
  • Explains why illness surfaces in the letdown: blunted antiviral surveillance lets a virus take hold, and a disconnected brake makes the symptoms run unrestrained.
  • Keeps the register honest: chronic stress shifts probabilities and raises susceptibility; it is not a toxin that guarantees illness.
  • Stays within scope: does not diagnose, offers a general-education suggestion, and identifies what warrants professional evaluation.
