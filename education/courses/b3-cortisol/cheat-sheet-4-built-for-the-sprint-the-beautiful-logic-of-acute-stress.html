Cheat-Sheet · B3 Cortisol

Chapter 4 : Built for the Sprint

One emergency response, two arms on two clocks, borrowed against the future and built to repay on time.

The Two Arms

Sympathetic-adrenal-medullary (SAM) systemFast neural route. The brain fires down the sympathetic nervous system to the adrenal medulla, which releases catecholamines (adrenaline / epinephrine and noradrenaline / norepinephrine) within seconds. The pounding chest and jolt of alertness before conscious thought.
Hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axisSlower hormonal cascade. Hypothalamus releases corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH), pituitary releases adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), adrenal cortex releases cortisol over minutes. Peaks about 15 to 30 minutes in, clears over the next hour or two.

Cortisol's Four Adaptive Actions

  • Mobilize energy : raises glucose via gluconeogenesis, frees fatty acids, and makes tissues transiently insulin-resistant so fuel is reserved for muscle and brain.
  • Support the surge : permissively enables catecholamines to raise heart rate and blood pressure.
  • Sharpen the mind : boosts arousal, focus, and memory consolidation; blunts pain.
  • Dampen the non-essential : throttles digestion, growth, and reproduction; restrains inflammation over hours.

The Four Modes (Sapolsky)

  • Permissive : baseline cortisol sets the stage before the alarm.
  • Stimulatory : the surge actively pushes a response higher.
  • Suppressive : restrains a response so it does not overshoot.
  • Preparative : today's cortisol tunes tomorrow's response.
  • Cortisol is a conductor, not a lone cause.

The Immune Plot Twist

  • Acute stress enhances immunity (Dhabhar) : mobilizes leukocytes forward to skin and tissues, staged for a likely wound.
  • Chronic stress suppresses immunity : sustained glucocorticoids blunt and derail defense.
  • Same hormone, same tissue, opposite outcome. The only variable that changed is duration.

The Loan Architecture

  • Principal : resources pulled from digestion, growth, repair, immunity, reproduction.
  • Interest : the small cost of running the machinery and clearing the mediators.
  • Repayment : the recovery period after the threat resolves.
  • Rational only if the emergency is brief and self-limiting. Rests on homeostasis and an off-switch.

Why It Works, and Where It Breaks

  • Ancestral threats were short, physical, and had a clear resolution. The design fits that world exactly.
  • Off-switch : cortisol feeds back on the hippocampus, hypothalamus, and pituitary to shut down CRH and ACTH. The trembling after a near escape is the system powering down on schedule, not the emergency continuing.
  • Allostatic load (McEwen) : the same mediators that protect in the short term damage the body when overused. Acute stress is healthy because it is time-limited.
  • Tourniquet rule : life-saving on the ninety-second timescale, harmful on the ninety-day one. The mechanism does not change, only its duration.
  • Selye's general adaptation syndrome: alarm, resistance, then exhaustion if the stressor never ends.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of how the acute stress response is built, not a license to diagnose or treat. Explaining that a strong surge and a lingering buzz are normal features of a near miss is general education; judging whether a specific reaction in a specific body is a problem is a clinical judgment for a qualified clinician. Chest pain, breathlessness, fainting, reactions without a trigger, or a fear that disrupts daily life warrant evaluation. Work within your role, whatever it is.
