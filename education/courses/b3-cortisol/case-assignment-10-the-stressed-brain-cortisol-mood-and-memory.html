Case Assignment · B3 Cortisol

Chapter 10: Reason Through a Real Complaint

Take the stressed brain and use it to explain a real complaint, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 25 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Rourke, 39
Consider Rourke, who signed up for this course after a stretch he could not name. For roughly a year and a half of relentless, unremitting pressure he has noticed several things at once: he misplaces appointments he used to keep effortlessly, he snaps at small provocations and feels persistently on edge, and he struggles to hold a plan in his head long enough to carry it out. Lately he also describes low mood, a loss of pleasure in things he used to enjoy, fatigue, and pulling away from friends. He mentions, almost in passing, a recurrent inflammatory condition he has lived with for years. He read online that this was "cortisol damaging his brain," and he wants either to be reassured that it is nothing or to be told plainly that he has "stress-induced depression" so he can start on a supplement to fix it.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, how sustained cortisol could plausibly produce Rourke's cluster of experiences, using the divergent remodeling of the chapter: dendritic atrophy in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex (PFC) versus dendritic growth in the amygdala, and map each change onto what he actually reports. Bring in reduced brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) support where it fits.
  2. Account for why this can become self-reinforcing rather than self-correcting, using the hippocampus as an inhibitory brake on the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, the weakened brake, and the blunted feedback of earlier chapters. Then connect his recurrent inflammatory history to the inflammation-mood link.
  3. Assess Rourke's two requests. Which parts of his reasoning rest on sound physiology, and where exactly does confirming "stress-induced depression" or recommending a supplement cross from education into a clinical act? Explain why a coherent mechanistic narrative is not a diagnosis.
  4. State clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins for someone in Rourke's position, and what a responsible, non-diagnosing response would actually say, avoiding both reassuring it away and over-claiming a verdict.

Your Response

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Depression and anxiety disorders are defined by clinical criteria and assessed by qualified mental-health professionals; recognizing the pattern is education, while labeling it or recommending treatment belongs to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Lays out the divergent remodeling of the hippocampus, amygdala, and prefrontal cortex, and maps each change onto what Rourke actually reports.
  • Explains the weakened hippocampal brake and blunted feedback as a self-reinforcing loop in which a sensor is also a target of the signal it senses.
  • Connects the inflammation-mood link to Rourke's recurrent inflammatory history without overstating it.
  • Frames the account as a mechanistic narrative of how systems can drift, not a diagnosis, and distinguishes association from clinical verdict.
  • Avoids both errors: does not reassure it away and does not diagnose or recommend a protocol or supplement.
  • Identifies what warrants professional evaluation and refers to a qualified clinician; does not diagnose or treat.
