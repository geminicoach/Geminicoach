Part D · Fat and Muscle: The Chronic Cost

This is where the chronic story gets its shape. Answer each in a few sentences.

  1. State the lipolysis paradox: cortisol is acutely fat-mobilizing, yet chronic excess produces central fat gain. Resolve it using the two parts the chapter gives, depot-specific redistribution toward visceral fat, and the company cortisol keeps (high insulin).
  2. Explain the 11-beta-HSD1 twist and why it means "the cortisol level" is not a single number in the blood. What does this do to any product that claims to measure your cortisol with a kit and then block it?
  3. Cortisol is both catabolic and anti-anabolic toward muscle. Name the two atrogenes it upregulates through the ubiquitin-proteasome system, name the two building signals it works against (one it suppresses, one it upregulates), and say which fiber type is preferentially wasted.
  4. Tie fat and muscle together: explain why "fat up and muscle down" is one hormonal environment producing two faces of a single process rather than two separate problems.

Part E · Take Apart the "Cortisol Detox for Belly Fat" Pitch

A person shows you an advertisement: a capsule that will "flush your stress hormone" and "melt belly fat." Do not simply say it is a scam. Use the mechanisms to say exactly what is wrong and exactly what is right.

  1. Claim one: cortisol is a toxin to be flushed. Explain in a sentence or two why this is a category error before you even reach the evidence.
  2. Claim two: measure your cortisol, then block it. Explain why the biologically relevant exposure is largely invisible to a blood or saliva number, and what that does to the whole product logic.
  3. Claim three: lower cortisol and the belly fat vanishes. Explain why there is no single lever to pull, referring to how sleep, energy balance, and activity interact with cortisol dynamics.
  4. Now give the honest constructive flip side. Why do sleep, movement, and meal timing genuinely belong in a metabolic-health conversation, stated without the word "detox"?

Where education ends
This worksheet builds understanding of how cortisol shapes metabolism, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how the system works, and treat anything that looks clinical (rapid central weight change, unexplained fatigue, rising thirst, muscle loss you cannot account for, or other alarm signs) as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician. Constructing a plausible mechanism is education; declaring which mechanism is operating in a particular person is diagnosis, and that is not yours to do. Work within your role, whatever it is: refer when a situation calls for diagnosis or treatment, and do not self-diagnose.
