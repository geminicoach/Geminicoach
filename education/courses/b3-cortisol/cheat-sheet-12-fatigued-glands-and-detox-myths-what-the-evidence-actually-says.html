Chapter 12 : Fatigued Glands and Detox Myths

The fatigue is real; the explanation is not. Take the exhaustion seriously and take the story apart.

The One Category Error

  • Both the "fatigue" pitch and the "detox" pitch treat cortisol as a depletable substance that runs low or piles up.
  • The physiology says cortisol is a regulated signal: built on demand from cholesterol, governed by feedback, cleared continuously by the liver.
  • Get that category straight and the whole marketing edifice, diagnosis and cure alike, loses its foundation.

Why "Adrenal Fatigue" Fails

  • Cortisol is made on demand, not stored in vesicles like insulin; there is no warehouse to empty.
  • A cortex truly falling behind releases the pituitary from restraint, so ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone) climbs: a loud, measurable disease signature, not quiet "exhaustion."
  • "A little fatigued" is not a stable resting state the feedback loop permits.
  • Cadegiani & Kater (2016): systematic review of 58 studies found no substantiation; no endocrine society recognises the diagnosis.

The Real Disease: Insufficiency

  • Primary (Addison's): cortex destroyed, feedback collapses, ACTH high, skin darkens.
  • Secondary: pituitary/hypothalamus under-signals, ACTH low.
  • Diagnosed by the short corticotropin (250 microgram) stimulation test plus ACTH, renin, aldosterone (Bornstein et al., 2016).
  • The test challenges the gland and watches it answer; a healthy but tired gland responds normally.

The "Cortisol Detox" Myth

  • Cortisol is cleared continuously (half-life about one to two hours), largely by the liver; the disposal path is never backed up.
  • Nothing to "flush": it is a signalling hormone, not a toxin or waste.
  • Driving cortisol lower would move toward insufficiency, not health. The target is an intact rhythm, not a smaller number.
  • Adaptogens may modestly affect how someone feels; that does not validate a fabricated deficit.

Two Axes of Every Test

Free vs totalOnly about 3 to 5 percent of cortisol is free and active; the rest binds CBG (corticosteroid-binding globulin) and albumin. Shifted binding proteins make total mislead.
TimingA value is meaningless without its timestamp; the diurnal rhythm decides the meaning.

Matching the Modality to the Question

Single-point serumAnswers a precise, clock-anchored question (for example an 8 a.m. insufficiency workup); nearly useless as a freestanding "cortisol level."
Diurnal profile (saliva)The only strategy that captures rhythm, the slope and awakening response; trades single-draw precision for pattern.
24-hour urinary free cortisolAnswers "is total daily output elevated?" (the Cushing's question); discards all timing information by design.
Saliva strengths / limitsReflects the free, active fraction and tracks rhythm, but assay-method bias means the same sample can read differently across labs.

Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) Cautions

  • Protocol heterogeneity: even 78 randomised trials could not agree on how to collect saliva or what to compute (Ryan et al., 2016).
  • Kitchen-table kits self-report timing and control none of the variables a reference range assumes.
  • Wearable and sweat sensors have limited validation and inconsistent benchmarking (Juliana et al., 2025).
  • A readout is not the same as a meaningful number.

Spot the Sales Move

  • Every true-adjacent sentence, one false assembly: a signal reclassified as fuel.
  • Invented "stages" and reference ranges; a single value read as a diagnosis.
  • Avoids the one test (stimulation) that would refute the claim.
  • A comforting wrong answer ends the search for a real, often treatable cause.
Where education ends   This builds understanding, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Interpreting a cortisol test to diagnose or exclude disease is clinical practice, not education, wherever the number came from. Red flags such as profound fatigue, unexplained weight loss, darkening skin, salt craving, dizziness on standing, or recent steroid withdrawal warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Educate and refer; work within your role, whatever it is.
