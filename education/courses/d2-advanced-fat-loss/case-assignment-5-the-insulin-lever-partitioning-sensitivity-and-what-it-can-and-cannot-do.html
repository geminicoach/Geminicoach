The Scenario

Scenario · Owen, 52
Owen works long hours as a data scientist and is sedentary outside of that. A routine physical six weeks ago flagged a rising fasting glucose number. After watching a video about insulin and fat storage, he adopted a near-zero-carbohydrate diet with an 18-hour daily fasting window, believing that keeping insulin as low as possible for as long as possible would finally move his stalled weight. He sits at roughly 28 percent body fat, sleeps about five broken hours most nights because of work stress, and does no structured exercise. Six weeks in, he feels foggy and tired, his weight has barely moved, and he does not understand why "crushing his insulin" has not worked. He asks: "If insulin is what's making me store fat, why isn't cutting carbs to almost nothing fixing it?"

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanically, what insulin actually does and does not control, and why a near-zero-carbohydrate approach does not guarantee more fat loss on its own.
  2. Identify which of the four honest levers for insulin sensitivity are most neglected in Owen's current routine, and explain the mechanism connecting each gap to his stalled progress.
  3. Decide whether targeting insulin sensitivity is actually a useful lever for someone with his profile, and contrast that with a lean, already-sensitive athlete for whom it would not be.
  4. Address his elevated fasting glucose finding and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Correctly rejects the strong carbohydrate-insulin model and explains that controlling insulin does not drive fat loss independent of energy balance.
  • Explains insulin's brake-on-lipolysis role accurately without overstating it as the sole cause of his stall.
  • Identifies sleep as a major neglected lever and explains the mechanism connecting short sleep to reduced glucose tolerance and increased hunger, along with the missing exercise and muscle levers.
  • Applies population-fit reasoning correctly, distinguishing his profile from a lean, already-sensitive athlete.
  • Identifies the elevated fasting glucose as warranting a clinician conversation and does not diagnose or prescribe a fix.
