Chapter 7 : The Hunger You Have to Outlast

Appetite is a negotiation between short-term meal signals and the long-term leptin fuel gauge. Adherence is where that negotiation gets spent.

The Signals

GhrelinThe pre-meal drumbeat; the only major gut hormone that reliably increases hunger. Rises before meals, falls after.
CCKReleased as fat and protein reach the small intestine; contributes to meal-by-meal fullness.
GLP-1 & PYYReleased from the lower gut; slow gastric emptying and signal fullness directly to the brain.
AmylinCo-secreted with insulin; adds a further layer to the fullness signal.
LeptinThe long-term fuel gauge. Secreted roughly in proportion to fat mass; falls faster than fat mass during a deficit, setting the overall gain on the whole hunger system.

Setpoint Defense

  • Two arms : expenditure falls (adaptive thermogenesis) while appetite rises. Same defense, opposite directions.
  • Asymmetric : the body fights fat loss far harder than it fights fat gain, an old threat model meeting a modern deficit.
  • Persistent : Sumithran et al. (2011), appetite hormones still favored regain a full year after a completed diet.
  • Leptin proportionality : a leaner body has a smaller reserve, so the same absolute fat loss produces a louder defense.

Two Hunger Systems

  • Homeostatic : ghrelin, leptin, and the gut peptides set the baseline pressure to eat.
  • Reward : dopamine and reward circuitry decide, in the moment, how hard that pressure is to resist, and can override a technically full stomach.
  • Short sleep weakens the prefrontal brake on the reward system, so poor sleep drives eating through more than one route at once.

The Five Honest Levers

  • Protein : most reliable lever; full hormonal effect needs roughly 35g or more per meal (Kohanmoo et al., 2020).
  • Fiber : real but modest; viscous, soluble fiber (oats, psyllium, legumes) works best.
  • Sleep : a direct appetite lever, not a wellness nicety; short sleep raises intake with no offsetting rise in expenditure.
  • Meal structure : match food to a person's own hunger peaks to flatten the daily curve.
  • Planned diet break : the MATADOR study (Byrne et al., 2017); partially restores leptin and eases the defense at its source. Strongest for lean, long cuts.

Reading the Curve

  • Large fat reserve : mild defense, deficit is easy to absorb.
  • Mid-range : moderate defense; levers start to matter.
  • Near the floor : leptin low, ghrelin high, felt hunger near maximum; adherence breaks down without support.
  • Adherence is a physiological output that can be engineered, not a character trait to demand more of.
Where education ends   This builds literacy in appetite physiology, not a license to diagnose or treat disordered eating. Persistent hunger dysregulation, loss of control around food, or significant distress are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
