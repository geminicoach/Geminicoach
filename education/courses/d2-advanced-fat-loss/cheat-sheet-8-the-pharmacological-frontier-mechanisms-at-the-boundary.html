Chapter 8 : The Pharmacological Frontier

Four drug classes, mapped onto physiology you already know. Mechanism and intended benefit only, no dosing, no protocols, no sourcing.

The Four Classes

Thyroid hormoneSystemic metabolic-rate signal, via nuclear receptors and local T4-to-T3 conversion (type 2 deiodinase). Raises expenditure everywhere, not only in fat; monitored with TSH, free T4, and sometimes free T3.
Beta-agonistsPress the beta-receptor ignition step of lipolysis. Beta1: heart rate and cardiac workload. Beta2: fat and muscle, the repartitioning effect. Beta3: brown and beige fat thermogenesis.
Metabolic peptides & mimeticsAct on appetite and metabolic signaling. GLP-1 receptor agonists are approved medicines with strong human trial evidence. MOTS-c (a mitochondrial peptide) and SLU-PP-332 (a small molecule, not a peptide) are exercise-mimetic candidates, mostly preclinical.
Partitioning agentsBias nutrients toward lean tissue, via insulin-related signaling or, for anabolic-androgenic steroids, the androgen receptor. The sharpest boundary in the chapter.

The Evidence Ladder

  • Highest : large human trials (GLP-1 receptor agonists).
  • Middle : animal and cell-model data (MOTS-c, SLU-PP-332).
  • Lowest : a marketing claim borrowing real vocabulary and stretching it past what evidence supports.
  • Mechanistic reasoning alone is a hypothesis, not proof.

Why More Is Not Better

  • No lever touches fat cells only; every class reaches tissue far from the one you care about.
  • A hyperthyroid state raises heart rate, bone turnover, and catabolism alongside expenditure.
  • Beta receptors sit throughout the cardiovascular system, not just in fat.
  • Manipulating insulin signaling means manipulating blood glucose control.

The Three Questions That Keep You on the Right Side of the Line

  • Education or decision? You can explain mechanism. You cannot make or endorse a pharmacological decision.
  • Symptom or sign? Possible dysfunction routes to a qualified clinician, never to your own interpretation.
  • Inside or outside educational territory? Nutrition, training, sleep, and behavior are in scope. Prescribing, dosing, monitoring, and diagnosis are not.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of mechanism and intended benefit, not a license to dose, source, or recommend any compound. Decisions about pharmacology belong to a qualified prescribing clinician inside a monitored medical relationship.
