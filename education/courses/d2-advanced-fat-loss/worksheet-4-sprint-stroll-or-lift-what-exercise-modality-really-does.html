Part C · Two Distinctions Worth Holding

  1. Distinguish fuel used during a session from fat lost over time. Why does low-intensity exercise burn a higher percentage of fat without necessarily winning the weekly fat-balance contest?
  2. Explain what EPOC actually is, in plain terms, and why the marketing claim of an all-day metabolic furnace overstates it. Cite the approximate size of the advantage found in a controlled comparison.
  3. What does the roughly 500 kcal per day deficit threshold from Murphy and Koehler mean for how you set a training goal, muscle growth versus muscle preservation?

Part D · Trace a Stalled Program

A trainee at roughly 12 percent body fat, moderate recovery capacity, and cranky knees has been running three hard interval sessions, one long steady jog, and two rushed strength sessions every week, for months, without progress.

  1. Which modality is most over-relied on here, and what recovery cost is it quietly taking from the rest of the week?
  2. Which lever is most neglected, and why does neglecting it matter more at this body-fat level than it would for someone with more fat to lose?
  3. Propose one specific change to each of the three modalities in this plan.

Where education ends
This worksheet builds exercise-physiology literacy, not a license to diagnose or treat, in yourself or anyone else. Explain how the modalities work and what each one trades off, and treat anything that looks clinical (persistent or worsening joint pain, chest symptoms, unusual shortness of breath, or other alarm signs) as warranting evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role: a coach refers, a clinician works within their license, and a self-learner does not self-diagnose.
