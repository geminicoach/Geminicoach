Chapter 4 : Sprint, Stroll, or Lift

Three tools, three separate levers. No single modality is universally superior; match the tool to the person.

The Three Signatures

HIITModest in-session fat use, but strong adrenergic drive. Time-efficient, boosts insulin sensitivity, higher recovery cost.
Steady stateLow adrenergic spike, high proportional fat use, low friction. Its lever is duration, not intensity, and it barely taxes recovery.
ResistanceUnremarkable calorie burn; its job is the mechanical signal that defends lean mass under a deficit. Not on the same dial as cardio.

Key Findings

  • Crossover concept: low-moderate intensity maximizes fat percentage burned, but that is not the same as weekly fat balance (Romijn et al., 1993).
  • EPOC: real, exponential with intensity, small. About 66 vs 54 kcal HIIT vs continuous in one controlled trial (Jiang et al., 2024; LaForgia et al., 2006).
  • HIIT vs steady state: equivalent fat-mass and waist outcomes; HIIT gets there in about 40% less time (Wewege et al., 2017).

Defending Lean Mass

  • Fat-free mass sets resting metabolic rate; resistance training attenuates lean-tissue loss in a deficit (Stiegler & Cunliffe, 2006).
  • STRRIDE: cardio moved the scale more, but only programs with resistance training added lean mass (Willis et al., 2012).
  • Deficits impair lean-mass gains but not strength; the threshold sits near 500 kcal/day (Murphy & Koehler, 2021).

The Four Governing Variables

  • Recovery capacity: limited recovery favors steady state, the low-friction default.
  • Joint tolerance: history of joint trouble steers volume toward lower-impact choices.
  • Training history: novices have more margin; advanced trainees have less to gain and more to protect.
  • Body-fat level: leaner people have more lean tissue at risk, making resistance training closer to essential.

Myths to Retire

  • Low intensity does not win the weekly fat-balance contest just because it burns a higher session percentage.
  • The afterburn is a rounding error, not a metabolic bonfire.
  • Neither HIIT nor steady state is superior; the difference is time and recovery cost.
  • Stacking every modality at maximum does not stack benefits; it stacks recovery costs.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of what each modality does, not a license to diagnose or treat. Persistent or worsening joint pain, chest symptoms, or unusual shortness of breath are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
