Chapter 1 : Where the Easy Wins End

Beginner fat loss is forgiving; advanced fat loss is the same physics with the body's defenses fully engaged.

The Two Regimes

Early gameMore easily mobilized fat, larger absolute deficit for the same behavior, opposition not yet ramped up. Results feel almost linear.
Advanced gameFuel reserves scarcer, defenses fully engaged, same effort meets rising resistance. Results feel like wading through concrete.
Why it slowsHall's modeling: the system has inertia, with a half-time on the order of a year, so a fixed deficit produces a shrinking rate of loss purely as math.

The Defended Setpoint

  • Leibel, Rosenbaum & Hirsch (1995) : reduced-weight maintenance suppresses expenditure beyond what the smaller body predicts; overfeeding does the mirror opposite.
  • Adaptive thermogenesis : the mechanism, reversible by restoring leptin, built from SNS activity, thyroid (T3), muscle efficiency, and NEAT.
  • Fothergill et al. (2016) : suppression persisted six years post-loss, averaging about 500 kcal/day, largest in those who regained the most.
  • Müller & Bosy-Westphal (2013) : average magnitude around 120 kcal/day, begins within weeks, varies a great deal person to person.

Setpoint, or Settling Point?

  • Strict setpoint : one tight target, defended symmetrically. Useful first approximation, not the whole story.
  • Settling-point model : weight stabilizes wherever intake and expenditure passively balance, no active target.
  • Dual intervention point : strong defense only at an upper and lower boundary, drifts freely between them (Speakman et al., 2011).
  • The asymmetry : the lower boundary is defended far harder, an evolutionary legacy of famine risk mattering more than abundance risk.

Individual Variation

  • Harper et al. (2021) : roughly 49 percent of the variance in weight-loss response is genetic. "Diet-resistant" people show more adaptation than "diet-sensitive" ones.
  • Bouchard twin study (1990) : identical overfeeding produced roughly threefold variation in gain across twin pairs, but tight agreement within a pair.
  • Same protocol on paper, honestly different bodies, honestly different results.

The Grading Habit

  • Statistical significance : the effect is probably not zero.
  • Honest effect size : how big the effect actually is, in units you can act on, plus its spread.
  • Three questions : Magnitude · Reliability · Opportunity cost.
  • Population fit : which bodies was this tested in, and does that match the body using it now.
Where education ends   This builds understanding of why a plateau happens, not a license to diagnose or treat. Persistent fatigue that does not lift with sleep, menstrual disruption, marked mood change, or disordered-eating signals are signs that warrant evaluation by a qualified clinician. Work within your role, whatever it is.
