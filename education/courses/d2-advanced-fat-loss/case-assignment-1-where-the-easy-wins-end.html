Chapter 1: Reason Through a Real Stall

Take the physiology of the defended setpoint and use it to explain a real plateau, in plain language, within the bounds of general education.

Name: ____________________________ Date: __________ Est. time: 20 min

The Scenario

Scenario · Marcus Ferreira, 29
Marcus has been in a steady calorie deficit for ten weeks, training four days a week and logging his food precisely. His body fat dropped from about 22 percent to 15 percent over the first seven weeks, losing at a comfortable, predictable rate. For the last three weeks his weight has not moved at all, even though his food log, training log, and daily weigh-ins show nothing has changed on his end. He has started reading online that his "metabolism must be damaged" from dieting, and he found a supplement marketed with the line "clinically shown to reverse metabolic damage and reignite fat loss." He wants to know whether to buy it, and whether something has actually gone wrong with his body.

Your Tasks

  1. Explain, mechanistically, why Marcus's rate of loss would be expected to slow the way it has, even with perfect adherence, using the defended setpoint and adaptive thermogenesis from the chapter.
  2. Explain what the chapter's individual-variation evidence does, and does not, say about whether Marcus's own metabolism is "damaged."
  3. Grade the supplement's claim honestly using the three-question habit: magnitude, reliability, and population fit.
  4. Decide whether this reads as normal physiology or something to worry about, give one practical general-education suggestion, and state clearly where general education ends and clinical care begins.

Where education ends
You are demonstrating understanding and plain-language communication, not diagnosing. A responsible response explains the mechanism, identifies what if anything warrants professional evaluation, and does not diagnose. Diagnosis and treatment belong to a qualified clinician.
Instructor / self-check rubric
  • Identifies the defended fat-mass setpoint and adaptive thermogenesis as the mechanism behind the stall.
  • Explains that roughly half the variance in weight-loss response is genetic, and correctly concludes this is not evidence of a broken metabolism.
  • Grades the supplement claim on magnitude, reliability, and population fit, rather than accepting or rejecting it on the headline alone.
  • Frames the plateau as normal, expected physiology rather than personal failure.
  • Gives a practical, general-education suggestion and correctly states where general education ends, without diagnosing or inventing symptoms that are not in the scenario.
