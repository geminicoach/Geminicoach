# Gemini Education — D-track deep syllabi: AAS & Peptides (for PJ red-line)

*These two are built LOCALLY (not ClassBuild), mechanism-first, with every efficacy/risk claim citation-verified (PubMed, ChEMBL, clinical trials). Same content pipeline output as the rest: CE-depth chapters, homework, exam, images/widgets, published.*

## LOCKED FRAMING (applies to every chapter of both courses)
- Educational + harm-reduction. Teach WHAT each compound is, its BIOCHEMISTRY/MECHANISM, its RISKS and benefits, and WHY it is used in the bodybuilding/PED context, descriptively.
- INTENDED-USE STATED (plainly, as description not endorsement, still no dosing). For every compound, name its intended/claimed purpose in plain terms right next to the mechanism, and pair the claim with its evidence grade. Examples: semax as a nootropic for focus (neurotrophic/BDNF and dopaminergic action); selank for anxiety (GABAergic tone and its effect on sympathetic/autonomic balance); kisspeptin and gonadorelin for fertility / HPG-axis stimulation; KPV for gut and skin healing (anti-inflammatory, plus its role in skin-support stacks like KLOW); GHK-Cu for skin, hair, and wound repair. "Used for X, by way of mechanism Y, with evidence grade Z" is exactly the level, never a protocol.
- NO dosing, NO cycle/stack designs, NO post-cycle-therapy (PCT) protocols, NO sourcing. Combinations are discussed only as PHARMACOLOGY (why classes interact) with the risk attached, never as regimens to run.
- Evidence-graded: state plainly when something is approved, off-label, animal-only, or unproven/grey-market.
- Role-neutral ("a learner"). Strong, repeated clinician-and-legal boundary. Red flags framed as "signs that warrant medical evaluation." "Where education ends" box every chapter.
- Standard house rules: ~6,300-7,600 words/chapter, named persona, ~8 callouts, 2 widgets, References, 0 em/en-dashes, Parker voice.

## D1 — Anabolic-Androgenic Steroids & Ancillaries: Education & Harm Reduction (13 chapters)

1. Foundations: Testosterone as the Parent Molecule. The androgen receptor; anabolic vs androgenic; how "anabolic ratings" are derived and why they are crude; what "steroidal" means structurally.
2. Ester Theory and Delivery. Propionate/enanthate/cypionate/undecanoate and blends (Sustanon) as kinetics, not different hormones; injectable vs oral; half-life logic (no dosing).
3. The Testosterones and the Aromatization Pathway. Test esters as the base; conversion to estradiol; why estrogen matters for males (bone, brain, libido, lipids) and the estrogenic side-effect logic.
4. The DHT-Derivatives. Masteron/drostanolone, primobolan/methenolone, anavar/oxandrolone, winstrol/stanozolol, superdrol/methasterone, halodrol. 5-alpha-reductase context, non-aromatizing profiles, "drying," and their lipid/hepatic signatures.
5. The 19-Nortestosterones and Progestins. Deca/nandrolone, trenbolone, trestolone. Progestogenic activity, prolactin, neurological and cardiac considerations, why they behave unlike test.
6. Boldenone/EQ and the Erythropoiesis Story. Red-cell/hematocrit effects and cardiovascular meaning; oral vs injectable distinctions across the catalog.
7. The 17-Alpha-Alkylated Orals as a Class. Dbol, tbol, anadrol, winstrol, superdrol. The hepatotoxicity mechanism, first-pass metabolism, and lipid impact, why orals share a risk signature.
8. Managing Estrogen: Aromatase Inhibitors. Anastrozole, letrozole, exemestane. Mechanism, why they are used, and the real cost of crashing estrogen (lipids, bone, joints, mood, neuro).
9. SERMs and the HPTA. Tamoxifen, raloxifene, clomiphene, enclomiphene. Selective estrogen-receptor modulation, gynecomastia management, and the hypothalamic-pituitary-testicular-axis recovery concept (mechanism, not protocol).
10. Non-AAS Ergogenics I: Clenbuterol and Beta-Agonists. Beta-2 agonism, thermogenic and anticatabolic effects, and cardiac/tremor/hypokalemia/downregulation risks.
11. Non-AAS Ergogenics II: Thyroid Hormones (T3/T4). Metabolic mechanism, why they appear in fat-loss and PED contexts, and the muscle-loss, cardiac, and axis-suppression risks.
12. Additive Risk and Biochemical Class (PJ's harm-reduction chapter). Why risk concentrates within a class: doubling a base multiplies that base's signature harm (e.g., two hepatotoxic orals compound liver stress; over-managing one axis creates new problems). Teaches the learner to reason about their own risk picture from biochemistry, no named stacks, principle only.
13. Systemic Risk, Monitoring, and the Boundary. Cardiovascular/lipid, hepatic, endocrine/HPTA suppression, fertility, mental health; what bloodwork gets watched (ties to B4); harm-reduction, legality, and the educator/clinician line.

## D2 - Advanced Fat Loss: Physiology, Pathways, Supplements & Pharmacology (integrative applied course, ~9 chapters)

This is the applied capstone that ties the physiology to the tools. It descriptively references PEDs and peptides (mechanism and intended benefit, NO dosing) and cross-links the deep D1/D3/D4 chapters. Population-fit is the through-line: the same lever is not equally useful for a lean body vs an obese one.

1. Why "Advanced" Fat Loss Is Different. Where calories-in / calories-out stops being the whole story: metabolic adaptation, individual variation, diminishing returns, and honest effect sizes. Sets the "advanced does not mean magic" bar.
2. The Adipocyte and the Lipolysis Machinery. Catecholamines, cyclic AMP, hormone-sensitive lipase and adipose triglyceride lipase, alpha-2 vs beta-adrenergic receptors, and why regional / "stubborn" fat behaves differently (blood flow, receptor density).
3. Uncoupling and Thermogenesis: UCP1 and Brown / Beige Fat. The uncoupling-protein mechanism; how cold exposure and high-intensity interval training (HIIT) drive browning; and the population nuance PJ flagged, cold-shock beige-fat recruitment as a low-yield but real "advanced" lever for leaner bodies, vs HIIT's larger metabolic return in higher-body-fat populations.
4. Exercise Modality and the Fat-Loss Response. HIIT vs steady-state vs resistance training; excess post-exercise oxygen consumption; substrate use; matching modality to the starting body-composition (why HIIT tends to pay off more for obese populations, and where lean bodies chase smaller margins).
5. Insulin Sensitivity as a Fat-Loss Lever. Insulin resistance vs sensitivity, nutrient partitioning, lean-mass preservation; how training, sleep, and nutrition move sensitivity, and why that matters for advanced fat loss.
6. Adrenergic and Thermogenic Supplements. Caffeine, yohimbine hydrochloride (alpha-2 antagonism, the fasted-state rationale), synephrine, green-tea catechins (EGCG), capsaicinoids. Realistic effect sizes and the cardiovascular / anxiety caveats.
7. Appetite, Satiety, and Adherence Physiology. Protein, fiber, the gut-brain axis, and why adherence is the real limiter; the conceptual bridge to GLP-1 / incretin pharmacology (deep coverage in D3).
8. Pharmacological Levers, Descriptively (bridge to D1, D3, D4). Where PED and peptide tools sit in the fat-loss picture as mechanism and intended benefit, no dosing: thyroid hormone (T3/T4) and metabolic rate, beta-2 agonists (clenbuterol) for thermogenesis / anticatabolism, the metabolic-mitochondrial peptides (MOTS-c, the exercise-mimetic SLU-PP-332, 5-amino-1MQ), and the AAS nutrient-partitioning / lean-mass-preservation angle. Every item cross-references its deep chapter.
9. Putting It Together: Population Fit, Risk, and the Boundary. Matching tools to the person and their body-fat / health context; diminishing returns and risk-benefit reasoning; monitoring and the clinician boundary.

## Peptides: split into TWO modules (the library is too large and too deep for one)

Rationale: at the depth we are giving AAS, a single course either goes shallow or crams. Splitting by mechanism family (the GH-split approach PJ liked) lets each module stand alone as its own CE course, and it makes room for the compounds missing from the first draft: GHK-Cu, KPV, LL-37, SS-31, thymosin alpha-1 (TA-1), plus AOD-9604 / fragment 176-191, PEG-MGF, follistatin, kisspeptin / gonadorelin, epithalon, humanin, ARA-290, thymulin, cerebrolysin, VIP, and others. Foundations + grey-market/boundary chapters are repeated (condensed) in each module so either can be taken independently.

### D3 - Peptides I: Growth, Metabolic & Body Composition (9 chapters)

1. What Peptides Are, and Why the Category Is Messy. Definitions; peptide vs small molecule; approved drugs vs grey-market research chemicals; the evidence ladder (human RCT vs animal vs anecdote vs vendor marketing). Shared foundation.
2. Growth Hormone Secretagogues I: GHRH Analogs. Sermorelin, CJC-1295 / modified GRF (1-29), tesamorelin. Mechanism at the GHRH receptor; pulsatile vs tonic GH.
3. Growth Hormone Secretagogues II: Ghrelin-Receptor Agonists and Orals. Ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6, hexarelin, oral MK-677. The ghrelin / GH-secretagogue receptor; and the two-receptor SYNERGY (why a GHRH analog plus a ghrelin agonist produce a larger GH pulse), as pharmacology, with risk.
4. Growth Hormone, IGF-1, and the Growth-Factor Offshoots. Exogenous GH and IGF-1 (and LR3/DES variants); AOD-9604 and fragment 176-191; PEG-MGF. What they claim vs what is shown; risks (insulin resistance, edema, acromegalic change, cancer-signaling concern).
5. Myostatin and Muscle-Regulation Peptides. Follistatin, ACE-031 / ActRIIB pathway. Mechanism of myostatin inhibition and the honest (mostly preclinical, safety-flagged) evidence status.
6. GLP-1 and Incretin Peptides. Semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide. Mechanism, the strong human evidence base, and the side-effect / risk profile.
7. Metabolic and Mitochondrial Compounds. MOTS-c, SS-31 / elamipretide (cardiolipin-targeted), SLU-PP-332 (a small molecule, not a peptide), 5-amino-1MQ. Mechanisms and the "mostly preclinical" honesty.
8. HPG-Axis Peptides. Kisspeptin, gonadorelin (GnRH). Mechanism, endocrine-axis and fertility context, why they appear alongside AAS discussions (ties to D1 SERM/HPTA chapter).
9. Grey Market, Contamination, Legality, and the Boundary. Sterility, mislabeling, dosing-error and contamination risk, legal status, clinician boundary. Shared close.

### D4 - Peptides II: Repair, Immune, Neuro & Systemic (9 chapters)

1. What Peptides Are, and Why the Category Is Messy. Condensed shared foundation so this module stands alone (definitions, evidence ladder, grey-market intro).
2. Tissue Repair and Healing. BPC-157, thymosin beta-4 / TB-500. Claimed mechanisms (angiogenesis, tendon/ligament, gut) vs actual (largely animal) evidence; unknown long-term safety.
3. Cosmetic and Regenerative Peptides. GHK-Cu (copper tripeptide) and the copper-peptide family. Collagen/wound-signaling mechanism, skin and hair claims, topical vs injectable evidence.
4. Anti-Inflammatory and Antimicrobial Peptides. KPV (alpha-MSH C-terminal fragment) and LL-37 (cathelicidin). Host-defense and gut/anti-inflammatory mechanisms; the thin human evidence and the infection/immune caveats.
5. Immune Modulators. Thymosin alpha-1 (TA-1 / Zadaxin), thymulin, thymalin. Approved-elsewhere context, mechanism of immune modulation, and the important distinction from thymosin beta-4 (TB-500).
6. Neuro and Cognitive Peptides. Semax, selank, DSIP, cerebrolysin. Proposed mechanisms and the thin evidence base.
7. Melanocortins. PT-141 / bremelanotide, melanotan II. Melanocortin-receptor mechanism and specific risks (blood pressure, pigmentation, melanoma concern); note KPV's melanocortin lineage.
8. Longevity and Systemic Miscellany. Epithalon, humanin, ARA-290 / cibinetide, VIP. Proposed mechanisms (telomerase, mitochondrial, tissue-protective) and honest, mostly-preclinical evidence.
9. Grey Market, Contamination, Legality, and the Boundary. Shared close (sterility, mislabeling, legal status, clinician boundary).
