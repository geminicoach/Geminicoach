# Gemini Education — Accreditation & Pricing Research

*Compiled 2026-07. Current-market data with sources. This is guidance, not final numbers; fees marked "contact" are not published publicly.*

## 1. The accreditation path (this is the important part)

There are TWO very different things, and you want the first one now, not the second:

**A. Become an "Approved CE Provider" (realistic, near-term, weeks).**
This is how your courses grant CEUs toward *other* trainers' existing certifications (NASM, ACE, AFAA, ISSA, NCCPT, ACSM, NASM). You apply to each org's provider program, they assign your courses a CEU value, you get listed on their site and may use their "Approved Provider" seal. This is exactly the "rival NASM's CE, and partner with them" goal.
- **NASM Provider Program:** download the Continuing Education Provider Application, email all docs to providerprogram@nasm.org, approval takes up to ~30 days, annual renewal. Benefits: listing on nasm.org, exposure to 1.9M+ certified pros, use of the NASM Approved Provider seal. **Application/annual fee is NOT published** — you must email the provider team (1-800-460-6276). NASM also owns AFAA, so NASM approval often covers AFAA.
- **Same model exists at ACE, ISSA, NCCPT, NSCA, ACSM** — each has its own provider application. Being approved by several = your CEC counts for more trainers' recerts = bigger addressable market. (Reference: Fitness Mentors is a NASM Approved Provider whose courses each carry 1.9 NASM CEUs — that is the model to copy.)

**B. Build your OWN NCCA-accredited standalone certification (long-term, expensive).**
This is what NASM-CPT itself is (NCCA-accredited). It requires a psychometrically validated exam, a job-task analysis, an accreditation body review, and org infrastructure. This is a multi-year, high-cost undertaking. **Do NOT target this for launch.** Position the CEC as *approved continuing education*, not a rival standalone accredited credential, until the brand and revenue justify pursuing NCCA.

**Recommendation:** apply to the NASM Provider Program first (email them for the fee), then ACE/ISSA. That gets you legitimate "CE credit" status fast and is the credible way to partner with NASM.

## 2. Competitor pricing (current)

**Full certifications (for context, these are the ceiling):**
- NASM CPT: self-study ~$599; guided ~$999–1,299; premium ~$1,699. NCCA-accredited.
- NASM Certified Nutrition Coach (CNC): ~$700–1,200, 8–12 weeks.
- Precision Nutrition Level 1: ~$1,000–1,500, ~12 months (industry gold standard, NOT NCCA-accredited).
- Payment plans across the board: ~$50–100/mo.

**Continuing education (this is your real comparison set):**
- CE "specialist" courses = ~1.9–2.0 CEUs each (one full 2-year recert cycle = 2.0 CEU / 20 hrs). Typical price **~$50–500 per course/bundle** depending on provider (budget providers cheap, premium ~$300–500).
- NASM recert fee itself: **$99 member / $129 non-member**, every 2 years; ~$25–30 petition/late fees.

**No-credential professional content libraries (your GELL comparison):**
- MedBridge (all-access pro CE library, courses + webinars): **~$299–455 per YEAR** (~$25–38/mo).
- Consumer education (MasterClass-type): ~$120–240/yr (~$10–20/mo).

## 3. Pricing guidance for Gemini

**CEC (your continuing-education certs):** your instinct of $500–1,000 is at the very TOP of the CE market — that's specialization/credential territory, not single-course territory. As a new, unknown brand you should undercut to win early adopters, then raise as reputation builds. Suggested:
- Single CEC course (one subject, ~2.0 CEU, a certificate): **$99–299**.
- Bundled CEC "track" (e.g., all of Nutrition as one long cert, several courses, high CEU value): **$399–799**.
- Reserve **$800–1,000+** for a truly comprehensive, multi-track specialization once you have NASM/ACE approval + brand trust. Launching a no-name cert at $1,000 will be a hard sell against NASM's own $500–900 specializations.
- **Retake fee (after the 2 free attempts):** keep it small and fair — **$25–75** per additional attempt (in line with NASM's ~$25–30 petition fees). This covers cost without feeling punitive.

**GELL library:** $100–200/mo is well above market and will be very hard to sell with no credential attached (MedBridge's full pro library is ~$25–38/mo). Suggested: **$19–39/mo** (or ~$179–349/yr). A $29/mo sweet spot reads premium-but-fair. Consider making GELL subscribers get a discount on CEC certs (drives upsell), and/or a free-tier teaser.

**Why undercut NASM:** you noted NASM feels overpriced for what it delivers. That is your wedge — deeper, better-written, fairly-priced content. Win on value and price as the new entrant; do not anchor to NASM's number.
